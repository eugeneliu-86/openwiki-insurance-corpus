# Liability Claim Handling Guidance







## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** This guidance supports consistent handling of liability claims and provides internal direction for evaluating reported matters. It does not create coverage, alter policy terms, or establish a contractual obligation because the policy's Insuring Agreement and conditions govern the parties' rights.

**H.0.2** You should use this guidance with the applicable policy language, endorsements, and claim facts when deciding how to investigate or respond. This approach is necessary because the policy's Insuring Agreement controls whether an alleged loss falls within the grant of coverage.

**H.0.3** You must not represent that this guidance expands, restricts, or replaces the policy. That limitation follows from the policy's Entire Contract condition, which identifies the materials that form the insurance contract.

**H.0.4** You should document material coverage questions, factual disputes, and communications in the claim file. Complete documentation supports a reasoned decision under the Duties In The Event Of Occurrence, Offense, Claim Or Suit condition.

**H.0.5** You may seek assistance from claims leadership, legal resources, or underwriting when the facts present an unusual exposure or a material coverage issue. Escalation is appropriate because the policy's Insuring Agreement and exclusions can require careful application to disputed facts.

**H.0.6** You should distinguish between an allegation, a verified fact, and a coverage position in all claim communications. This distinction is important because the policy's Duties In The Event Of Occurrence, Offense, Claim Or Suit condition requires prompt and accurate reporting of claim information.

**H.0.7** You must evaluate each reported matter under the policy in force for the claimed event and under any applicable endorsements. That review is required because the policy's Declarations and endorsements identify the coverage terms applicable to the insured risk.

**H.0.8** You should not treat a cash sublimit as a substitute for determining whether the claim is otherwise covered. This is necessary because the applicable endorsement or limitation provision applies only after the coverage analysis is completed.

**H.0.9** You should not apply a coinsurance threshold when handling liability claims unless the applicable policy language expressly makes it relevant. This preserves accurate claim handling because liability coverage is determined under the policy's liability coverage provisions and endorsements.

**H.0.10** You must refer questions concerning notice for nonpayment to the appropriate policy administration resource before communicating a coverage position. This is necessary because cancellation and nonrenewal rights are governed by the applicable cancellation condition and related legal requirements.

**H.0.11** You should treat the backwater valve requirement for finished areas below grade as a property underwriting issue unless policy language makes it relevant to the liability claim. This separation is important because the liability Insuring Agreement governs the defense and indemnity analysis.

**H.0.12** You must not rely on a binding authority limit to resolve a claim without confirming the applicable policy terms and claim authority. This safeguard is necessary because binding authority concerns underwriting authority, while the policy's Insuring Agreement governs claim coverage.

**H.0.13** You should provide timely, clear, and factual communications to insureds, claimants, and representatives. Clear communication supports compliance with the policy's Duties In The Event Of Occurrence, Offense, Claim Or Suit condition and helps preserve the insurer's ability to investigate.

**H.0.14** You must apply this guidance in good faith and in a manner consistent with the facts, policy language, and applicable claim-handling obligations. This standard follows from the policy's claim conditions, which require cooperation and permit a meaningful investigation and response.

## H.1 — In Appetite

**H.1.1** You may handle a reported liability matter within appetite when the allegations present a conventional negligence theory and the insured’s operations match the described business activity. This approach rests on the Liability Coverage Grant because the claim must arise from covered operations before liability evaluation is useful.

**H.1.2** You may retain a matter within appetite when the claimant alleges bodily injury, property damage, or personal and advertising injury that is facially connected to the insured’s business. This approach rests on the Insuring Agreement because the alleged harm must fall within the coverage grant.

**H.1.3** You may proceed with routine investigation when the named insured, claimant, occurrence location, and alleged conduct are reasonably identifiable from the initial report. This approach rests on the Duties in the Event of Occurrence, Offense, Claim, or Suit Condition because usable claim facts support a timely coverage decision.

**H.1.4** You may treat a premises allegation as within appetite when the loss location is an insured location or a location where the insured was performing covered operations. This approach rests on the Coverage Territory provision because the policy responds only to loss events within its territorial scope.

**H.1.5** You may handle a completed-operations allegation within appetite when the claimed damage is tied to work the insured actually performed. This approach rests on the Products-Completed Operations Hazard provision because the claim must arise from the insured’s completed work rather than unrelated conduct.

**H.1.6** You may handle an ongoing-operations allegation within appetite when the reported event occurred while the insured was performing ordinary business work. This approach rests on the Premises and Operations coverage grant because active operations are evaluated differently from completed work.

**H.1.7** You may keep a slip, trip, or fall claim within appetite when the asserted condition is identifiable and the insured’s relationship to the location is clear. This approach rests on the Bodily Injury definition because the claimed injury must be connected to an occurrence.

**H.1.8** You may handle a customer injury allegation within appetite when the report describes an accidental event rather than deliberate conduct by the insured. This approach rests on the Occurrence definition because liability coverage is intended for accidental injury-producing events.

**H.1.9** You may handle a property damage allegation within appetite when the damaged property can be identified and the claimed mechanism of damage is plausible. This approach rests on the Property Damage definition because the policy requires qualifying physical injury or qualifying loss of use.

**H.1.10** You may investigate a loss-of-use allegation within appetite when the claimant identifies property that was rendered unavailable by the insured’s alleged negligence. This approach rests on the Property Damage definition because loss of use must be tied to covered damaged property or covered loss of use.

**H.1.11** You may handle a claim involving an independent contractor when the insured’s alleged responsibility is based on ordinary premises, hiring, supervision, or contractual allegations. This approach rests on the Separation of Insureds provision because each insured’s alleged conduct requires its own coverage analysis.

**H.1.12** You may investigate a claim involving an additional insured when the tender identifies the endorsement basis and the alleged liability arises from the named insured’s work or operations. This approach rests on the Additional Insured endorsement because the endorsement controls the scope of any added protection.

**H.1.13** You may handle a contractual tender within appetite when the agreement and tender materials are available for review and the liability allegation is understandable. This approach rests on the Contractual Liability exclusion because assumed liability requires careful comparison to the policy’s insured contract language.

**H.1.14** You may handle a claim involving damage to rented premises when the insured’s occupancy and the damaged area are clear from available information. This approach rests on the Damage to Premises Rented to You provision because that coverage has terms distinct from general property damage coverage.

**H.1.15** You may handle a claim involving incidental damage to another party’s property when the insured did not own, occupy, rent, or control the property in a manner that triggers a coverage concern. This approach rests on the Damage to Property exclusion because property-control issues can narrow or eliminate coverage.

**H.1.16** You may continue routine handling when the alleged damage developed from a readily understandable accidental event. This approach rests on the Occurrence definition because an identifiable accidental event supports efficient fact development.

**H.1.17** You may handle a claim involving negligent supervision when the claimant alleges that the insured failed to oversee ordinary business activity. This approach rests on the Bodily Injury and Property Damage Liability Coverage Grant because negligent supervision may be covered when it results in qualifying injury or damage.

**H.1.18** You may handle a claim involving negligent hiring or retention when the alleged wrongdoing is connected to the insured’s ordinary operations and does not appear intentional. This approach rests on the Expected or Intended Injury exclusion because the insured’s alleged conduct must remain within accidental liability exposure.

**H.1.19** You may handle a claim involving a delivery activity when the claimant alleges ordinary negligence by the insured or its employee during business operations. This approach rests on the Auto exclusion because you must distinguish delivery-related liability from liability arising out of vehicle use.

**H.1.20** You may handle a claim involving loading or unloading when the allegations concern handling of property apart from the use of an auto. This approach rests on the Auto exclusion because loading and unloading allegations may require close review of the claimed cause of loss.

**H.1.21** You may investigate a customer property claim when the customer identifies the damaged item and the insured’s alleged involvement is direct. This approach rests on the Property Damage definition because the file must establish qualifying physical injury to tangible property.

**H.1.22** You may handle a claim for damage caused by an employee when the employee was acting within the insured’s ordinary business activity. This approach rests on the Who Is An Insured provision because employee status and assigned duties affect the coverage analysis.

**H.1.23** You may handle a claim involving a temporary worker when the worker’s relationship to the insured is adequately established. This approach rests on the Who Is An Insured provision because the policy’s treatment of workers depends on the applicable insured-status language.

**H.1.24** You may handle a claim involving leased workers when the available facts identify who directed the work and where the work was performed. This approach rests on the Employee definition because worker classification can affect insured status and applicable exclusions.

**H.1.25** You may handle a claim involving a volunteer when the alleged injury arose from activities the insured authorized or directed. This approach rests on the Who Is An Insured provision because the volunteer’s status must be evaluated under the policy language.

**H.1.26** You may handle a claim involving incidental food or beverage service when the allegation concerns ordinary negligence in the insured’s operations. This approach rests on the Liquor Liability exclusion because the facts must show whether the claim arises from furnishing or selling alcohol.

**H.1.27** You may handle a claim involving incidental event activity when the event is consistent with the insured’s stated operations and the reported injury is accidental. This approach rests on the Designated Operations provision because the insured’s actual activities define the intended exposure.

**H.1.28** You may handle a claim involving signage, displays, or store fixtures when the allegation identifies a physical condition that allegedly caused injury or damage. This approach rests on the Bodily Injury and Property Damage Liability Coverage Grant because the condition must be linked to covered injury or damage.

**H.1.29** You may handle a claim involving ordinary maintenance when the allegation concerns upkeep of the insured’s premises or business equipment. This approach rests on the Premises and Operations coverage grant because routine maintenance activities are part of ordinary operational exposure.

**H.1.30** You may handle a claim involving a minor repair activity when the work was incidental to the insured’s usual business operations. This approach rests on the Designated Operations provision because incidental work remains subject to the insured’s described operations.

**H.1.31** You may handle a claim involving alleged failure to warn when the claimant identifies the hazard, the warning issue, and the resulting injury or damage. This approach rests on the Occurrence definition because the claimed failure must result in accidental injury or damage.

**H.1.32** You may handle a claim involving alleged failure to maintain when the condition and the insured’s responsibility for it can be determined from available evidence. This approach rests on the Duties in the Event of Occurrence, Offense, Claim, or Suit Condition because prompt evidence gathering preserves the condition analysis.

**H.1.33** You may handle a claim involving negligent security allegations when the facts concern ordinary premises safety measures rather than intentional wrongdoing by the insured. This approach rests on the Expected or Intended Injury exclusion because intentional conduct allegations require separate scrutiny.

**H.1.34** You may handle a claim involving assault by an unknown person when the insured is alleged to have negligently maintained or supervised the premises. This approach rests on the Bodily Injury Liability Coverage Grant because the insured’s alleged negligence, rather than the assailant’s conduct alone, is the coverage focus.

**H.1.35** You may handle a claim involving a claimant’s emotional distress when the allegation is tied to claimed bodily injury or covered personal and advertising injury. This approach rests on the Bodily Injury definition because emotional harm must be evaluated within the policy’s defined injury terms.

**H.1.36** You may handle a claim involving personal and advertising injury when the asserted offense is specifically described and appears connected to the insured’s business communications or conduct. This approach rests on the Personal and Advertising Injury Liability Coverage Grant because coverage applies only to listed offenses.

**H.1.37** You may handle a defamation allegation within appetite when the claimant identifies the statement, speaker, publication context, and claimed harm. This approach rests on the Personal and Advertising Injury definition because the alleged offense must match the covered offense wording.

**H.1.38** You may handle an invasion-of-privacy allegation within appetite when the claim identifies the alleged disclosure or intrusion and the insured’s role. This approach rests on the Personal and Advertising Injury definition because the applicable offense depends on the nature of the alleged privacy violation.

**H.1.39** You may handle a wrongful-eviction or wrongful-entry allegation when the insured’s relationship to the premises and claimant is clear. This approach rests on the Personal and Advertising Injury definition because the policy applies only to the specifically listed occupancy-related offenses.

**H.1.40** You may handle a copyright-style advertising allegation when the claimant identifies the protected material and the insured’s allegedly infringing advertisement. This approach rests on the Personal and Advertising Injury exclusion because intellectual-property allegations may fall outside the covered offense language.

**H.1.41** You may handle a claim involving advertising material when the material is available and the alleged offense is understandable from its content. This approach rests on the Personal and Advertising Injury Liability Coverage Grant because the coverage analysis depends on what was actually communicated.

**H.1.42** You may handle a claim involving reputational harm when the reported facts identify a covered personal and advertising injury offense rather than a general business dispute. This approach rests on the Personal and Advertising Injury definition because reputational damage alone does not establish a covered offense.

**H.1.43** You may handle a claim involving a demand letter when the demand describes alleged injury or damage and seeks relief from the insured. This approach rests on the Duties in the Event of Occurrence, Offense, Claim, or Suit Condition because a demand may trigger reporting and defense considerations.

**H.1.44** You may handle a claim involving a lawsuit when the pleadings identify the insured, claimant, alleged conduct, and requested relief. This approach rests on the Suit definition because the defense obligation depends on whether the matter qualifies as a suit.

**H.1.45** You may begin defense review when the pleading alleges facts that potentially fall within the liability coverage grant. This approach rests on the Defense provision because the duty to defend is determined by the allegations and known facts.

**H.1.46** You may assign counsel through ordinary procedures when the venue, allegations, and requested relief present familiar liability issues. This approach rests on the Defense provision because counsel selection must support the insurer’s right and duty to defend.

**H.1.47** You may continue routine litigation handling when the insured cooperates with counsel and provides requested claim information. This approach rests on the Cooperation Condition because the defense depends on the insured’s participation and truthful information.

**H.1.48** You may request additional facts from the insured when the initial report does not identify the accident mechanism or the claimant’s theory of liability. This approach rests on the Duties in the Event of Occurrence, Offense, Claim, or Suit Condition because incomplete facts can prevent an accurate coverage decision.

**H.1.49** You may seek photographs, video, incident reports, and witness information when the evidence is likely to clarify fault or damages. This approach rests on the Cooperation Condition because timely evidence development protects the insured’s defense.

**H.1.50** You may obtain recorded statements when the available facts leave material uncertainty about how the event occurred. This approach rests on the Cooperation Condition because consistent accounts help evaluate liability and preserve defenses.

**H.1.51** You may evaluate comparative fault when the evidence indicates that the claimant’s conduct may have contributed to the alleged injury or damage. This approach rests on the Defense provision because liability handling should account for available factual defenses.

**H.1.52** You may consider settlement within ordinary authority when liability, damages, and coverage are sufficiently developed for reasoned evaluation. This approach rests on the Supplementary Payments provision because defense and settlement activity must be managed in connection with covered claims.

**H.1.53** You may communicate directly with an unrepresented claimant about factual investigation and claim resolution. This approach rests on the Voluntary Payments provision because communications must not create unauthorized commitments or admissions.

**H.1.54** You may communicate through claimant counsel when representation is known and the matter remains within routine handling. This approach rests on the Defense provision because counsel-directed communication helps preserve orderly claim resolution.

**H.1.55** You may issue a reservation of rights when covered and potentially excluded allegations are presented together. This approach rests on the Exclusions provision because the file must preserve the insurer’s ability to evaluate all applicable coverage limitations.

**H.1.56** You may issue a coverage position after the material facts and relevant policy language have been reviewed. This approach rests on the Conditions provision because coverage determinations require compliance with policy duties as well as the insuring terms.

**H.1.57** You may handle a claim within appetite when no exclusion is plainly implicated by the known facts. This approach rests on the Exclusions provision because exclusions can alter the handling path even when the claim initially appears routine.

**H.1.58** You may continue ordinary handling when the insured did not make a voluntary payment, assume an obligation, or incur expense without consent. This approach rests on the Voluntary Payments provision because unauthorized actions can impair the insurer’s control of defense and settlement.

**H.1.59** You may handle a claim involving medical expenses when the alleged injury arose from ordinary premises or operations exposure and the required facts are available. This approach rests on the Medical Payments Coverage provision because that coverage has separate triggering requirements.

**H.1.60** You may consider medical payments handling when fault is unresolved but the reported injury otherwise fits the medical payments coverage language. This approach rests on the Medical Payments Coverage provision because that coverage may apply without establishing the insured’s legal liability.

**H.1.61** You may handle a claim involving a tenant, vendor, customer, or visitor when the claimant’s status is known and the insured’s duty can be assessed. This approach rests on the Bodily Injury Liability Coverage Grant because liability analysis depends on the insured’s alleged responsibility to the claimant.

**H.1.62** You may handle a claim involving ordinary business equipment when the equipment’s use is consistent with the insured’s described operations. This approach rests on the Designated Operations provision because the exposure must remain connected to the business activity contemplated by the policy.

**H.1.63** You may treat a claim as within appetite when the file can be investigated using available witnesses, records, physical evidence, and routine expert support if needed. This approach rests on the Cooperation Condition because claim resolution depends on accessible and reliable evidence.

**H.1.64** You may retain a matter within appetite when the insured’s alleged conduct presents a manageable liability dispute rather than a specialized coverage issue. This approach rests on the Insuring Agreement because ordinary liability handling begins with a clear connection between the claim and covered conduct.

**H.1.65** You should document the facts supporting the within-appetite decision and identify any developing issue that could affect coverage, liability, damages, or defense. This approach rests on the Duties in the Event of Occurrence, Offense, Claim, or Suit Condition because a clear file record supports consistent handling and timely escalation.

## H.2 — Roof Age and Condition

**H.2.1** You must obtain available information about the roof’s apparent condition before evaluating a liability allegation involving water entry, falling materials, or unsafe premises. This preserves a reliable factual record and follows the duties after loss provision.

**H.2.2** You may request photographs, inspection records, repair invoices, maintenance records, and correspondence concerning the roof. These materials help distinguish an alleged liability-producing condition from ordinary deterioration under the claims cooperation provision.

**H.2.3** You must not treat roof age alone as proof that an insured knew of a hazardous condition. Knowledge depends on the available facts, and this approach follows the liability insuring agreement and notice condition.

**H.2.4** You should identify whether the reported condition involves visible damage, concealed deterioration, active leakage, detached materials, or an alleged structural defect. Clear identification supports an accurate liability analysis under the occurrence and property damage provisions.

**H.2.5** You must document the source of each statement about the roof’s age, condition, maintenance, or repair history. Source documentation protects the file from unsupported assumptions and follows the claim investigation condition.

**H.2.6** You may rely on qualified inspection findings when the condition of the roof is material to liability, causation, or damages. Qualified findings provide a defensible basis for claim handling under the duties after loss provision.

**H.2.7** You should compare the reported loss location with the roof area described in photographs or inspection materials. This helps confirm whether the alleged condition could have contributed to the claimed injury or damage under the causation requirement of the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.8** You must preserve photographs showing the roof surface, drainage areas, flashing, edges, penetrations, and nearby affected property when available. Preservation is necessary because conditions may change after repairs or weather exposure, as contemplated by the duties after loss provision.

**H.2.9** You should record whether temporary repairs were made before inspection and who authorized them. This identifies potential changes to the condition at issue and follows the claim investigation condition.

**H.2.10** You may request an explanation for repairs that appear unrelated to the reported occurrence. The explanation may reveal preexisting conditions or unrelated work, which is relevant under the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.11** You must separate observed roof conditions from conclusions about fault. This prevents a condition report from being mistaken for an admission of liability and follows the no-voluntary-payment condition.

**H.2.12** You should note whether the claimed damage is consistent with long-term wear, deferred maintenance, sudden failure, or an external event. This distinction assists with causation review under the occurrence provision.

**H.2.13** You must not characterize ordinary aging as negligence without evidence of a duty, notice, unreasonable conduct, and causal connection. Liability requires more than a worn condition, as reflected in the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.14** You may ask the insured what inspections, maintenance, or repair decisions were made after learning of a roof concern. This inquiry helps evaluate notice and response under the duties after loss provision.

**H.2.15** You should obtain relevant communications concerning complaints, leaks, falling debris, or access restrictions. Prior communications may bear on notice and foreseeable harm under the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.16** You must identify whether any person had responsibility for inspection, maintenance, repair, or control of the roof area. Responsibility and control are central to a premises liability evaluation under the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.17** You should distinguish between an owner’s responsibility and the responsibility of a tenant, contractor, manager, or other party. This avoids attributing conduct to the insured without support and follows the insured-status provision.

**H.2.18** You may seek contracts, work orders, and certificates relevant to roof repairs or maintenance performed by others. These records may identify additional insureds, indemnity obligations, or responsible parties under the contractual liability provision.

**H.2.19** You must document whether the insured retained control over the work area while roof work was underway. Control may affect the liability analysis, consistent with the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.20** You should preserve evidence of warnings, barriers, access controls, or posted restrictions associated with the roof or affected area. Such evidence may address reasonable precautions and comparative responsibility under the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.21** You must not direct the insured to admit fault, promise payment, or accept responsibility for a claimant’s allegation. Claim handling authority remains subject to the no-voluntary-payment condition.

**H.2.22** You may advise the insured to preserve relevant roof materials, photographs, records, and communications. Preservation supports a fair investigation and follows the duties after loss provision.

**H.2.23** You should evaluate whether the alleged harm arose from the roof itself or from a related condition such as drainage, runoff, debris, interior moisture, or access equipment. Proper identification of the alleged source is necessary under the causation requirement of the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.24** You must identify any intervening event that may have caused or worsened the reported damage. Intervening facts may affect causation and damages under the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.25** You may consider weather information when it helps evaluate the reported sequence of events. Weather evidence can clarify whether the alleged condition is consistent with the claimed occurrence under the occurrence provision.

**H.2.26** You should avoid treating a weather event as the sole cause when maintenance, construction, occupancy, or drainage facts remain unresolved. A complete causation review is required by the claim investigation condition.

**H.2.27** You must describe the roof’s observable condition in neutral language. Neutral descriptions reduce the risk of prejudging coverage or fault and follow the good-faith claim handling obligation within the claims condition.

**H.2.28** You should record material discrepancies between the claimant’s account, the insured’s account, and physical evidence. Discrepancies may require further investigation under the duties after loss provision.

**H.2.29** You may obtain statements from persons who observed the roof condition, the occurrence, or resulting damage. Witness information can confirm timing, notice, and causation under the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.30** You must evaluate claimed damages separately from evidence of the roof condition. A damaged roof does not establish legal responsibility, as reflected in the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.31** You should identify whether the claimant seeks damages for bodily injury, property damage, loss of use, or another alleged harm. Accurate classification supports application of the applicable liability coverage provision.

**H.2.32** You must not use the roof age from which the actual cash value roof schedule applies to determine liability, fault, or the value of a third-party claim. That concept concerns roof settlement administration and does not replace the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.33** You should keep the roof settlement floor separate from the evaluation of alleged negligence or third-party damages. The settlement concept does not control liability claim handling under the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.34** You must not apply the coinsurance threshold when evaluating fault, causation, or damages asserted by a third party. Coinsurance concepts do not alter the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.35** You may note property coverage information when it provides useful background, but you must maintain a separate liability analysis. Separate analysis prevents property settlement considerations from controlling the liability claim under the applicable liability coverage provision.

**H.2.36** You should refer a claim for further coverage review when the alleged damage may involve an exclusion, endorsement, or condition affecting liability coverage. Referral supports a consistent coverage determination under the applicable liability coverage provision.

**H.2.37** You must document the basis for any reservation of rights arising from unresolved roof-condition facts. Clear documentation supports the insurer’s position and follows the coverage conditions provision.

**H.2.38** You may pursue recovery from a responsible contractor, vendor, tenant, or other party when the facts support that action. Recovery efforts protect available claim resources and follow the transfer-of-rights provision.

**H.2.39** You should preserve evidence before authorizing disposal of removed roof materials or related components. Physical evidence may be necessary to evaluate causation and responsibility under the duties after loss provision.

**H.2.40** You must coordinate with qualified experts when the roof condition presents issues outside ordinary claim handling expertise. Expert review supports an informed determination under the claim investigation condition.

**H.2.41** You should explain to the insured what information is needed and why it is material to the liability investigation. Clear communication promotes cooperation and follows the duties after loss provision.

**H.2.42** You must maintain a file record showing how roof condition evidence affected the liability evaluation. A complete record supports consistent claim handling under the liability insuring agreement.

**H.2.43** You may close the roof-condition inquiry when the available evidence reasonably resolves the relevant liability issues. Closure should reflect documented facts, applicable coverage terms, and the duties after loss provision.

## H.3 — Wind and Hail

**H.3.1** You must identify whether reported damage arose from wind, hail, wind-driven rain, or a combination of those causes. This distinction is required by the applicable cause-of-loss provision because coverage, deductibles, and defenses may depend on the initiating peril.

**H.3.2** You must preserve the reported date, location, weather conditions, and description of the event in the claim file. The Duties After Loss provision supports this requirement because prompt and reliable facts are needed to evaluate the insured’s liability.

**H.3.3** You must review the applicable windstorm deductible provision before discussing payment responsibility with an insured or claimant. This review is necessary because a wind or named storm deductible may apply differently from the deductible applicable to other property damage.

**H.3.4** You must not characterize an event as a named storm without support from reliable weather information and the applicable policy terms. The named storm deductible provision controls because the designation can affect the deductible and the scope of the coverage analysis.

**H.3.5** You must document whether the reported loss occurred within the named storm window. The applicable named storm deductible provision governs this determination because storm-related deductible treatment may depend on the event’s timing.

**H.3.6** You must apply the hurricane deductible floor when the applicable policy provision requires hurricane deductible treatment. The deductible provision controls because the deductible selected or imposed for hurricane losses may not fall below the required floor.

**H.3.7** You must confirm that any applicable named storm deductible does not exceed the maximum named storm deductible. The named storm deductible provision supports this review because deductible limitations must be honored when resolving a covered claim.

**H.3.8** You must verify that the insured received notice before a windstorm deductible increase takes effect when the file reflects an increased windstorm deductible. The deductible change provision governs because enforceability may depend on required notice.

**H.3.9** You must obtain a wind mitigation inspection when Coverage A exceeds $1,000,000. The underwriting inspection requirement supports this step because verified mitigation features may affect the risk evaluation and claim investigation.

**H.3.10** You must retain the wind mitigation inspection and related supporting materials in the underwriting or claim file when they are available. The inspection provision supports retention because the documents may clarify the building’s protective features at the time of loss.

**H.3.11** You must distinguish preexisting deterioration from sudden wind or hail damage. The wear and tear exclusion applies because liability coverage does not extend to damage caused solely by maintenance-related conditions.

**H.3.12** You must inspect for evidence of prior repairs, repeated leaks, surface wear, and unrelated damage. The concealment, misrepresentation, and fraud provision supports this inquiry because inconsistent physical evidence may affect the reliability of the reported claim.

**H.3.13** You must obtain photographs or other reliable visual evidence showing the claimed wind or hail damage. The Duties After Loss provision supports this request because physical documentation assists in determining cause, extent, and timing.

**H.3.14** You must request repair records when prior work may explain the reported condition. The Cooperation provision applies because repair history can help separate covered storm damage from preexisting defects.

**H.3.15** You must evaluate whether wind-driven rain entered through an opening created by wind or hail. The applicable wind-driven rain provision controls because resulting water damage may depend on whether a covered peril created the opening.

**H.3.16** You must not assume that interior water damage establishes wind-created damage to the exterior. The causation requirement applies because water intrusion may result from wear, faulty maintenance, or an uncovered source.

**H.3.17** You must investigate whether hail damage is functional, cosmetic, or unrelated to the reported storm. The direct physical loss requirement supports this investigation because visible marks alone may not establish covered damage.

**H.3.18** You must obtain an expert evaluation when the cause or extent of roof, siding, glazing, or structural damage remains unclear. The Claims Settlement provision supports expert review because a qualified opinion may be needed for a fair coverage determination.

**H.3.19** You must provide the expert with complete and neutral background information, including photographs, weather data, repair records, and the reported loss history. The good-faith claims handling obligation supports this practice because incomplete information can produce an unreliable opinion.

**H.3.20** You must review weather information that reasonably corresponds to the reported location and claimed date of loss. The proof-of-loss requirement supports this review because local conditions may confirm or contradict the claimed event.

**H.3.21** You must document competing causes when wind or hail may have combined with flood, surface water, faulty construction, or maintenance conditions. The concurrent causation provision governs because coverage may depend on the relationship among multiple causes.

**H.3.22** You must evaluate whether a third party may be legally responsible for creating or worsening the damage. The subrogation and transfer-of-rights provision supports this inquiry because the insurer may seek recovery from a responsible party.

**H.3.23** You must preserve evidence that may be needed for a potential recovery action against a contractor, property manager, manufacturer, or other responsible party. The subrogation provision applies because altered or discarded evidence can impair recovery rights.

**H.3.24** You must not authorize destructive testing without confirming that the testing is reasonably necessary and appropriately documented. The Duties After Loss provision supports this restriction because unnecessary alteration can compromise the claim investigation.

**H.3.25** You must communicate clearly when the insured has a duty to protect property from further damage. The Duties After Loss provision requires reasonable mitigation because preventable additional damage may not be covered.

**H.3.26** You must distinguish reasonable emergency measures from permanent repairs that may alter evidence before inspection. The preservation-of-property requirement supports this distinction because mitigation is appropriate while the cause and extent of loss must remain verifiable.

**H.3.27** You must review invoices for emergency services, tarping, water removal, and debris handling for connection to the reported wind or hail event. The reasonable and necessary expense provision governs because only loss-related mitigation costs may be considered.

**H.3.28** You must evaluate allegations that the insured failed to maintain the property before the storm. The neglect exclusion applies because damage caused by a failure to protect or maintain property may be outside coverage.

**H.3.29** You must avoid attributing all observed damage to the reported storm merely because a storm occurred nearby. The direct physical loss requirement controls because causation must be supported by the facts of the individual claim.

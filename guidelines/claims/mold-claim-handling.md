# Fungi and Mold Claim Handling Guidance







## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** You should use this guidance to support consistent handling of claims involving fungi, mold, wet rot, dry rot, or related microbial matter. It reflects the Fungi and Mold coverage provision and applicable claims conditions, which control the scope of coverage.

**H.0.2** This guidance is internal operational material and is not part of any policy contract. You must apply the policy language as issued because the policy, endorsements, and applicable law govern the claim.

**H.0.3** You should identify potential fungi or mold involvement as soon as the reported facts suggest moisture, water intrusion, concealed damage, odor, staining, or microbial growth. Early identification supports timely investigation and helps preserve information needed to evaluate causation.

**H.0.4** You should distinguish direct physical loss from damage caused by fungi or mold. This distinction is required by the Fungi and Mold coverage provision because coverage may depend on the cause of loss and the applicable grant of coverage.

**H.0.5** You should review all applicable forms and endorsements before communicating a coverage position. The controlling coverage form may modify exclusions, additional coverage, duties after loss, or conditions applicable to fungi and mold claims.

**H.0.6** You should document the reported source of moisture and all available evidence concerning when the condition developed. The claims conditions require a reasonable investigation because the duration and origin of moisture may affect the coverage analysis.

**H.0.7** You should obtain photographs, inspection findings, invoices, remediation records, and relevant repair estimates when available. These materials support a reasoned claim decision under the proof-of-loss and cooperation conditions.

**H.0.8** You should explain that reasonable emergency measures may be necessary to protect covered property from further damage. The duties after loss condition supports mitigation because preventable additional damage may complicate the adjustment.

**H.0.9** You should not characterize remediation, testing, cleaning, repair, or replacement as covered until the applicable coverage analysis is complete. The Fungi and Mold coverage provision requires you to connect each claimed expense to covered loss.

**H.0.10** You should evaluate whether the claimed damage resulted from a covered water event, long-term seepage, maintenance issues, construction defects, or another excluded cause. The exclusions provision requires cause-based analysis because similar visible conditions can arise from materially different sources.

**H.0.11** You should keep the fungi and mold analysis separate from the jewelry theft sublimit. The property coverage provisions treat these subjects independently because each addresses a different category of loss.

**H.0.12** You should not use the coinsurance threshold to resolve a fungi or mold coverage question. The valuation conditions serve a separate purpose because they do not establish whether the claimed damage falls within the Fungi and Mold coverage provision.

**H.0.13** You should not treat a notice for nonpayment as evidence bearing on the cause, extent, or handling of fungi and mold damage. Policy status issues must be evaluated under the applicable cancellation and reinstatement provisions because they are distinct from the loss investigation.

**H.0.14** You should not apply the roof age from which the actual cash value roof schedule applies when evaluating fungi or mold damage unless the relevant roof provision directly affects the claimed property. That schedule concerns roof valuation because it does not independently determine fungi or mold coverage.

**H.0.15** You should obtain appropriate authority before making commitments that may exceed the binding authority limit. Authority controls protect the company from unauthorized claim commitments and support consistent handling.

**H.0.16** You should provide clear, fact-based communications to insureds, claimants, and representatives throughout the investigation. The claims conditions support prompt cooperation because clear communication helps obtain the information needed for an accurate coverage determination.

## H.1 — In Appetite

**H.1.1** You may consider a risk in appetite when the reported operations do not create an apparent ongoing moisture condition. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision by focusing acceptance on risks with controllable sources of water intrusion.

**H.1.2** You should obtain a clear description of the premises, occupancy, and intended operations before making an appetite decision. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because moisture exposure often follows the way a location is used.

**H.1.3** You may accept well-maintained premises where the applicant demonstrates prompt attention to leaks, seepage, and visible dampness. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision by reducing the likelihood that a minor water condition develops into fungal growth.

**H.1.4** You should treat unresolved roof leakage as an adverse appetite indicator. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because roof intrusion can affect concealed building materials before damage becomes visible.

**H.1.5** You should treat recurring plumbing leakage as an adverse appetite indicator unless the source has been identified and repaired. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because recurring leakage can create persistent moisture within walls, floors, or ceilings.

**H.1.6** You may consider premises with documented repairs to prior water damage when the repairs appear complete and the affected area has been dried. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because completed remediation reduces the chance that moisture remains within building components.

**H.1.7** You should seek clarification when an applicant reports staining, odors, peeling finishes, warped materials, or visible growth. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because these conditions may indicate an unresolved moisture source.

**H.1.8** You should treat visible fungal growth as outside ordinary appetite until the cause, scope, and remediation status are understood. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because visible growth may reflect continuing water exposure or incomplete cleanup.

**H.1.9** You may accept a location with prior fungal growth only when the file supports that the moisture source was corrected and the affected materials were appropriately addressed. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because past growth can recur if moisture controls are not durable.

**H.1.10** You should not rely solely on cosmetic repairs when the submission suggests prior water intrusion. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because paint, flooring, and surface finishes can conceal damp or damaged materials.

**H.1.11** You should consider the building envelope when evaluating appetite for a property exposure. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because defects involving exterior walls, windows, flashing, or roofing can permit repeated moisture intrusion.

**H.1.12** You may view recently completed building-envelope repairs favorably when the repair scope identifies the source of water entry. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because source-focused repairs are more reliable than repairs limited to damaged finishes.

**H.1.13** You should treat poorly maintained gutters, downspouts, and drainage features as adverse conditions. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because unmanaged exterior water can enter foundations, walls, or lower-level spaces.

**H.1.14** You should evaluate grading and drainage conditions where the premises includes lower-level or partially below-grade areas. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because surface water accumulation can contribute to dampness in enclosed spaces.

**H.1.15** You may consider a lower-level exposure in appetite when the space is dry, maintained, and used in a manner compatible with its moisture susceptibility. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because appropriate use and maintenance help limit concealed water damage.

**H.1.16** You should obtain details regarding any seepage, dampness, or odor in crawl spaces, utility areas, or storage areas. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because these areas can retain moisture without prompt detection.

**H.1.17** You should treat inadequate ventilation in moisture-producing areas as an adverse appetite factor. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because stagnant humid air can support fungal growth even without a significant leak.

**H.1.18** You may accept residential risks with kitchens, bathrooms, and laundry areas that appear maintained and adequately ventilated. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because routine moisture-producing activities require reliable drying conditions.

**H.1.19** You should seek additional information when a submission identifies frequent condensation on windows, walls, ceilings, or mechanical equipment. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because condensation can signal humidity, insulation, or ventilation concerns.

**H.1.20** You should consider the condition of heating, cooling, and ventilation equipment in the appetite review. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because malfunctioning equipment can create condensate leakage or elevated interior humidity.

**H.1.21** You may view maintained condensate drainage systems favorably when the applicant describes regular inspection and prompt repair practices. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because blocked or leaking condensate components can introduce water into concealed areas.

**H.1.22** You should treat reported water damage near heating, cooling, or ventilation equipment as an issue requiring explanation. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because the damage may indicate an unresolved mechanical moisture source.

**H.1.23** You should consider occupancy characteristics that generate steam, humidity, or repeated water use. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because moisture-intensive operations require controls that may not be present in ordinary occupancies.

**H.1.24** You may accept moisture-producing operations when the submission demonstrates suitable ventilation, housekeeping, and maintenance practices. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because these controls reduce the potential for persistent damp conditions.

**H.1.25** You should treat operations involving food preparation, laundering, bathing, aquatic activity, or plant cultivation as requiring closer review. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because these operations can create elevated moisture conditions within enclosed spaces.

**H.1.26** You should obtain details concerning ventilation, drainage, and cleanup practices for moisture-producing operations. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because operational controls may determine whether ordinary moisture becomes a fungal exposure.

**H.1.27** You may consider vacant premises only when the applicant can explain how water systems, inspections, and maintenance will be managed. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because water damage can remain undetected when a location is unoccupied.

**H.1.28** You should treat extended lack of supervision as an adverse appetite condition for a vacant or lightly occupied property. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because unnoticed leaks can affect a larger portion of the premises before corrective action occurs.

**H.1.29** You should seek clarification when utilities remain active at an unoccupied location. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because active plumbing, heating, and cooling systems can create water-related damage without occupants present.

**H.1.30** You may view a documented property-monitoring practice favorably for a vacant location. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because active oversight improves the likelihood that moisture conditions are discovered promptly.

**H.1.31** You should consider the age and apparent condition of plumbing systems without treating age alone as a declination basis. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because maintenance history and visible condition may be more relevant than the original installation era.

**H.1.32** You should treat corroded piping, repeated backups, low-pressure complaints, or prior supply-line failures as adverse indicators. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because these conditions can signal an elevated likelihood of future water release.

**H.1.33** You may accept premises with updated plumbing when the submission identifies the work performed and the reason for the update. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because targeted replacement can reduce exposure from known plumbing deficiencies.

**H.1.34** You should obtain details about prior water losses, even when the applicant describes the damage as minor. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because repeated minor events can indicate an ongoing maintenance or construction issue.

**H.1.35** You should distinguish between an isolated accidental discharge and a pattern of moisture-related conditions. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because a pattern may indicate a continuing source that is more likely to produce fungal damage.

**H.1.36** You may consider a prior water loss in appetite when the cause was accidental, corrected, and not suggestive of recurring seepage or neglect. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because a resolved event presents a different exposure from continuing moisture.

**H.1.37** You should treat prior claims alleging mold, mildew, odor, dampness, or indoor-air concerns as requiring careful review. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because these allegations may involve unresolved building conditions or heightened claimant sensitivity.

**H.1.38** You should obtain the applicant’s explanation of remediation activities following any prior fungal condition. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because the adequacy of source correction is central to recurrence potential.

**H.1.39** You may view professional remediation favorably when the file indicates that the underlying water source was also corrected. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because removal of affected materials alone may not resolve continuing moisture.

**H.1.40** You should avoid accepting a risk based solely on an applicant’s statement that mold was removed. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because removal does not establish that the cause of growth has been eliminated.

**H.1.41** You should evaluate housekeeping and cleanliness as part of the risk review where moisture-sensitive materials are stored or used. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because poor housekeeping can delay discovery of leaks and limit airflow around damp materials.

**H.1.42** You may view orderly storage practices favorably when materials are kept away from apparent damp surfaces. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because accessible areas are easier to inspect for water intrusion and fungal growth.

**H.1.43** You should seek clarification when inventory, furnishings, or building materials are stored directly against exterior walls or on damp floors. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because stored materials can conceal moisture conditions and sustain damage.

**H.1.44** You should consider whether the applicant has responsibility for building maintenance or relies on another party to perform repairs. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because delayed responsibility can permit water conditions to continue unresolved.

**H.1.45** You may consider tenant-occupied premises when maintenance responsibilities are clearly understood and reported conditions are addressed promptly. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because prompt communication and repair reduce the duration of moisture exposure.

**H.1.46** You should treat unclear maintenance responsibility as an adverse appetite factor. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because uncertainty about who will repair a leak can delay mitigation and increase fungal potential.

**H.1.47** You should consider whether the applicant has received complaints involving odors, dampness, leaks, stains, or visible growth. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because occupant complaints can provide early evidence of a moisture condition.

**H.1.48** You should seek explanation when complaints appear unresolved or repeatedly reported. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because recurring complaints may indicate that prior corrective measures did not address the source.

**H.1.49** You may view a documented repair-response practice favorably when the applicant promptly investigates moisture-related complaints. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because early investigation can limit damage before fungal growth develops.

**H.1.50** You should consider renovation activity when evaluating current appetite. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because demolition, plumbing changes, roof work, and enclosure changes can expose or create moisture pathways.

**H.1.51** You should obtain details about unfinished repairs following water damage or building-envelope work. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because incomplete construction can leave building materials exposed to rain or interior humidity.

**H.1.52** You may consider recently renovated premises when the work appears complete and no continuing moisture concerns are reported. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because completed work is less likely to leave exposed or damp construction materials.

**H.1.53** You should treat active construction moisture, wet building materials, or unfinished drying work as outside ordinary appetite. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because retained construction moisture can support fungal growth after finishes are installed.

**H.1.54** You should consider local environmental conditions only as they affect the applicant’s ability to control moisture at the premises. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because climate conditions alone do not establish exposure without a building or maintenance vulnerability.

**H.1.55** You may view effective dehumidification, ventilation, and drainage practices favorably in humid environments. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because these controls can reduce moisture accumulation within enclosed areas.

**H.1.56** You should treat reliance on portable drying equipment without a stated moisture-control practice as an adverse factor. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because temporary equipment may not address the source of recurring dampness.

**H.1.57** You should document the factual basis for any appetite determination involving prior water or fungal concerns. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because the file should show why the risk was considered controllable or unsuitable.

**H.1.58** You should record the reported moisture source, corrective action, and current condition when accepting a previously affected risk. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because clear documentation supports consistent handling if a related loss is later reported.

**H.1.59** You may request photographs, repair descriptions, maintenance records, or inspection information when the submission raises a moisture concern. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because objective information helps distinguish resolved damage from an active condition.

**H.1.60** You should not assume that the absence of a prior claim confirms the absence of moisture damage. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because fungal conditions may be repaired, concealed, or tolerated without a reported insurance loss.

**H.1.61** You should treat incomplete or inconsistent responses about prior leaks, odors, staining, or remediation as an adverse underwriting condition. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because unreliable information limits your ability to assess whether moisture controls are effective.

**H.1.62** You may accept a risk after material moisture concerns are clarified and supported by credible corrective information. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because appetite depends on the present condition and the likelihood of recurrence.

**H.1.63** You should decline or refer risks where the moisture source remains active, unknown, or disputed. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because unresolved causation prevents a reliable assessment of future fungal exposure.

**H.1.64** You should apply the same moisture-focused review to new business and renewal business when new information indicates changed conditions. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because appetite can change when occupancy, maintenance, construction, or loss experience changes.

**H.1.65** You should communicate material fungal or moisture concerns clearly within the underwriting file. This supports the Fungi and Mold limitation provision because clear internal documentation promotes consistent risk selection and claim handling.

## H.2 — Roof Age and Condition

**H.2.1** You must obtain available roof installation, repair, and replacement records before evaluating a fungi or mold claim involving roof leakage. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because roof history helps distinguish a sudden covered opening from deterioration or deferred maintenance.

**H.2.2** You must document the roof’s observed condition as close as practical to the time of inspection. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because later repairs may obscure evidence of the condition that permitted moisture intrusion.

**H.2.3** You may use contractor invoices, permits, photographs, inspection reports, and credible insured statements to establish roof history. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because no single source may fully show the roof’s prior condition.

**H.2.4** You must record when the insured cannot provide records supporting the roof’s installation or maintenance history. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because the claim file must show the basis for any age or condition conclusion.

**H.2.5** You must apply the roof surfacing age threshold when the applicable coverage terms make roof age relevant to settlement. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because roof surfacing age may affect the valuation method available for damaged materials.

**H.2.6** You must identify the roof surfacing material before applying any age-related settlement guidance. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because different materials may have different service characteristics and replacement methods.

**H.2.7** You should distinguish roof surfacing from underlying decking, framing, insulation, flashing, and drainage components. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because age or wear of one component does not by itself establish the condition of another.

**H.2.8** You must identify whether the claimed moisture entered through roof surfacing, a penetration, flashing, drainage equipment, a structural opening, or an undetermined pathway. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because the path of water may determine whether fungi or mold resulted from a covered cause.

**H.2.9** You must photograph visible roof defects, including worn surfacing, displaced materials, open seams, damaged flashing, and blocked drainage paths. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because clear condition evidence supports consistent causation and settlement decisions.

**H.2.10** You should obtain photographs showing the relationship between the alleged entry point and the reported interior moisture damage. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because proximity alone does not establish that a roof condition caused the claimed fungi or mold.

**H.2.11** You must note signs of long-term water exposure, including staining, decay, corrosion, biological growth, and material breakdown. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because those conditions may indicate that moisture was present before the reported loss event.

**H.2.12** You should not treat visible fungi or mold as proof that roof damage resulted from a covered cause. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because fungi or mold may develop from repeated leakage, humidity, condensation, or other non-roof sources.

**H.2.13** You must evaluate whether the reported roof damage is consistent with the claimed cause of loss. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because a claimed event should reasonably explain the observed opening and resulting moisture path.

**H.2.14** You should request a qualified roof inspection when field observations do not establish whether damage is sudden, accidental, wear-related, or maintenance-related. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because specialized findings may be necessary to separate covered damage from excluded conditions.

**H.2.15** You must preserve any inspection findings that identify prior repairs, patching, temporary coverings, or recurring leak locations. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because prior repair activity may show that the moisture source existed before the reported claim.

**H.2.16** You should ask the insured about known leaks, prior water staining, prior fungi or mold, and earlier roof repairs. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because the insured’s knowledge may clarify whether the claimed damage developed gradually.

**H.2.17** You must document any inconsistency between the reported date of loss and the condition shown by the roof or interior materials. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because unexplained inconsistencies may require further causation investigation.

**H.2.18** You must separate emergency mitigation from permanent roof repair in the claim estimate and file notes. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because necessary steps to protect property do not establish coverage for all repair work performed.

**H.2.19** You may authorize reasonable protective measures when supported by the reported loss and applicable coverage terms. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because prompt protection can limit additional moisture intrusion and resulting fungi or mold damage.

**H.2.20** You must review mitigation invoices for work related to active moisture control, removal of affected materials, drying, and containment. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because the scope of mitigation should correspond to documented moisture conditions.

**H.2.21** You should obtain moisture findings and material condition observations before authorizing broad fungi or mold remediation. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because remediation scope should be supported by evidence of affected areas and a covered source of water.

**H.2.22** You must distinguish damage caused by the reported roof event from damage caused by seepage, repeated leakage, condensation, or inadequate ventilation. This handling direction rests on the Roof Condition and Settlement Provision because coverage analysis depends on the cause of moisture rather than the presence of damage alone.

**H.2.23** You should inspect attic and ceiling areas for signs that moisture originated away from the alleged roof opening. ## H.3 — Wind and Hail

**H.3.1** Confirm that wind or hail directly caused the reported physical damage before addressing any resulting fungi or mold condition.  
This distinction prevents payment for moisture conditions that arose from an unrelated source.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision and the Fungi and Mold provision.  

**H.3.2** Document the reported storm event, the location of the loss, and the observed damage condition as early as practical.  
Contemporaneous documentation helps distinguish sudden storm damage from deterioration, seepage, or long-term moisture exposure.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.3** Obtain a wind mitigation inspection when Coverage A exceeds $1,000,000.  
The inspection helps identify construction features that may affect the expected path and extent of wind-related damage.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.4** Review the wind mitigation inspection for information that may explain whether wind entered through a covered opening or through a pre-existing defect.  
This review supports a reasoned causation determination where fungi or mold is reported after the storm.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.5** Identify the applicable wind deductible before issuing any payment for direct wind or hail damage.  
Accurate deductible handling prevents the deductible from being applied to unrelated mold remediation costs.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.6** Apply the named storm deductible minimum when the reported event qualifies as a named storm loss.  
This protects consistent deductible treatment for losses arising from the same type of storm event.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.7** Do not apply a wind deductible that exceeds the wind deductible ceiling.  
This limitation prevents deductible handling that conflicts with the applicable wind-loss terms.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.8** Confirm whether the loss occurred during the named storm period before applying a named storm deductible.  
This determination is necessary because storm-related deductible treatment depends on the applicable designation.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.9** Determine whether the loss falls within the hours after a named storm designation ends that the named storm period lasts.  
This review prevents premature removal of named storm deductible treatment when the designation has ended.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.10** Preserve weather information that supports or contradicts the reported date and cause of loss.  
Reliable weather evidence helps establish whether wind or hail was capable of causing the reported opening or damage.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.11** Inspect the exterior for wind or hail damage before attributing interior moisture damage to the reported event.  
Exterior evidence may confirm a direct path for water intrusion or may indicate that another source caused the moisture.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.12** Document all claimed openings, including damage to roofing, siding, glazing, vents, and exterior penetrations.  
Clear identification of the opening helps determine whether resulting water intrusion was a consequence of covered wind or hail damage.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.  

**H.3.13** Distinguish storm-created openings from openings caused by wear, deterioration, construction defects, or prior damage.  
This distinction is important because fungi or mold may develop from moisture entering through an uncovered condition.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision and the Fungi and Mold provision.  

**H.3.14** Ask the insured when water was first observed and what reasonable protective actions were taken after discovery.  
The timing of discovery and mitigation may help determine whether later fungi or mold resulted from avoidable continued moisture exposure.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision and the Fungi and Mold provision.  

**H.3.15** Evaluate reasonable emergency measures separately from permanent repair work.  
Prompt drying, water extraction, and temporary protection may limit additional fungi or mold damage after a covered wind or hail loss.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision and the Fungi and Mold provision.  

**H.3.16** Request photographs showing the damaged area before repairs, during mitigation, and after drying efforts are completed.  
Photographs help establish the progression of water intrusion and the relationship between storm damage and fungi or mold.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision and the Fungi and Mold provision.  

**H.3.17** Separate damage caused by direct wind or hail from damage caused by ensuing rainwater intrusion.  
This separation supports accurate allocation of covered direct physical loss and any applicable fungi or mold limitation.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision and the Fungi and Mold provision.  

**H.3.18** Do not assume that the presence of fungi or mold establishes that wind or hail caused the condition.  
Fungi or mold may result from many sources, including prior leakage, humidity, plumbing failure, or delayed drying.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision and the Fungi and Mold provision.  

**H.3.19** Obtain repair records or other available evidence when the claimed damage may have existed before the reported storm.  
Prior-condition evidence helps prevent storm causation from being assigned to long-standing maintenance or moisture issues.  
This direction rests on the Wind and Hail provision.

**H.6.35** You must evaluate supplemental invoices and newly reported damage against the original cause-of-loss findings. This procedure rests on the Loss Settlement provision because additional charges are payable only to the extent they relate to covered damage.

**H.6.36** You should monitor the legal action deadline when handling an unresolved claim or responding to a coverage dispute. This procedure rests on the Suit Against Us provision because claim activity must not obscure the contractual conditions applicable to legal action.

**H.6.38** You should close the mold handling file only after documenting the coverage decision, payment basis, outstanding issues, and disposition of retained evidence. This procedure rests on the Loss Payment provision because the final file must support the claim outcome and any later review.

## H.7 — Authority Limits

**H.7.1** You must handle fungi and mold matters within the authority granted to your role. The governing fungi and mold coverage provision controls because authority does not create coverage, expand coverage, or alter an applicable limitation.

**H.7.2** You may investigate a reported fungi or mold condition before making a coverage commitment. The claim investigation provision supports this approach because factual development is necessary before a coverage position can be evaluated.

**H.7.3** You must refer a claim when the reported condition may involve coverage beyond the authority available to you. The fungi and mold limitation provision requires referral because a limitation must be applied consistently.

**H.7.4** You must not communicate that fungi or mold damage is covered merely because an inspection, mitigation effort, or vendor assignment has begun. The claim investigation provision supports this rule because investigation activity is not a coverage determination.

**H.7.5** You may authorize reasonable investigative activity that is necessary to identify the source, scope, and cause of the reported condition. The duties after loss provision supports this authority because the carrier may need reliable facts to evaluate the claim.

**H.7.6** You must obtain authority before approving work that could be characterized as fungi or mold remediation. The fungi and mold limitation provision supports escalation because remediation may involve restricted coverage or a separate coverage grant.

**H.7.7** You must distinguish emergency stabilization from remediation when reviewing vendor requests. The loss mitigation provision supports this distinction because reasonable steps to protect property do not automatically establish coverage for later remediation.

**H.7.8** You may authorize actions needed to prevent additional direct physical damage when those actions fall within your assigned authority. The duties after loss provision supports this approach because the insured is expected to protect covered property from further damage.

**H.7.9** You must refer any request for extensive environmental testing, intrusive sampling, or specialized analysis when the request may exceed your authority. The claim investigation provision supports referral because specialized work must have a clear claim purpose.

**H.7.10** You must not approve testing solely to establish the presence of fungi or mold where the result would not affect claim handling. The fungi and mold limitation provision supports this rule because testing expenses must relate to a covered evaluation.

**H.7.11** You may use qualified experts when expert involvement is necessary to resolve a material factual dispute. The claim investigation provision supports expert use because causation and damage scope may require specialized evaluation.

**H.7.12** You must refer an expert assignment when the proposed scope could influence a major coverage decision or settlement position. The fungi and mold coverage provision supports this requirement because expert findings may affect application of exclusions, limitations, and conditions.

**H.7.13** You must document the reason for any expert assignment and the question the expert is expected to address. The claim investigation provision supports documentation because the claim record must show why the expense was necessary.

**H.7.14** You may request photographs, invoices, repair records, and other available property information relevant to the reported condition. The duties after loss provision supports these requests because the insured must cooperate with reasonable claim investigation.

**H.7.15** You must not rely on a vendor conclusion as the sole basis for a coverage determination. The claim investigation provision supports independent evaluation because vendor findings may address damage conditions without resolving coverage issues.

**H.7.16** You must refer a claim when the reported fungi or mold condition may have resulted from long-term moisture, repeated seepage, deferred maintenance, or another excluded cause. The fungi and mold limitation provision supports referral because causation may determine whether any coverage applies.

**H.7.17** You may issue factual status communications while investigation remains open. The claim investigation provision supports status communications because clear updates help prevent misunderstanding without prejudging coverage.

**H.7.18** You must not make an oral or written promise of payment before confirming that you have authority to make that commitment. The claim authority provision supports this rule because unauthorized commitments can impair consistent claim handling.

**H.7.19** You must obtain appropriate approval before issuing a coverage denial, partial denial, or limitation notice involving fungi or mold. The fungi and mold limitation provision supports approval because the notice must accurately state the applicable coverage basis.

**H.7.20** You may prepare a reservation of rights for review when facts suggest that coverage may be limited, excluded, or subject to unresolved conditions. The reservation of rights provision supports this step because the carrier must preserve its ability to complete the investigation.

**H.7.21** You must refer a reservation of rights for review before it is issued when the claim presents unusual facts, competing causes, or uncertain application of a fungi or mold limitation. The reservation of rights provision supports referral because the notice must address the actual coverage issue.

**H.7.22** You must state the factual basis for a coverage position without overstating what the investigation has established. The claim investigation provision supports this practice because a coverage decision must rest on supported facts.

**H.7.23** You may negotiate resolution of covered direct physical damage within your assigned settlement authority. The loss settlement provision supports this authority because covered damage must be adjusted under the applicable valuation terms.

**H.7.24** You must refer any proposed settlement that includes fungi or mold remediation, testing, or related expenses outside your authority. The fungi and mold limitation provision supports referral because these expenses may be subject to restricted coverage.

**H.7.25** You must not combine covered repair costs with disputed fungi or mold expenses in a manner that obscures the basis for payment. The loss settlement provision supports separate evaluation because each payment must be traceable to the applicable coverage determination.

**H.7.26** You may issue payment for undisputed covered damage while a fungi or mold issue remains under review, when doing so is within your authority. The loss settlement provision supports this approach because unresolved issues should not delay payment for damage that is otherwise payable.

**H.7.27** You must clearly identify the basis for any payment involving cleanup, tear out, repair, testing, or remediation. The loss settlement provision supports this requirement because the payment record must show the coverage rationale.

**H.7.28** You must not use payment authority to resolve a coverage dispute that requires a formal coverage determination. The claim authority provision supports this rule because settlement authority does not replace required coverage analysis.

**H.7.29** You must refer any request for payment to an assignee, contractor, consultant, or other service provider when entitlement is disputed. The loss payment provision supports referral because payment direction must be consistent with the applicable claim obligations.

**H.7.30** You may communicate directly with a contractor or remediation vendor about scope, access, scheduling, and documentation needs. The claim investigation provision supports this communication because vendor coordination may be necessary to evaluate the reported loss.

**H.7.31** You must not instruct a vendor to perform work beyond the approved scope. The claim authority provision supports this restriction because vendor direction may create an unauthorized expense obligation.

**H.7.32** You must refer a claim when a vendor alleges that delay, denial, or limited authorization caused additional fungi or mold damage. The claim investigation provision supports referral because the allegation may require review of claim handling facts and causation.

**H.7.33** You must preserve information relevant to potential recovery from a responsible party. The transfer of rights provision supports this requirement because the carrier may seek recovery after payment of a covered loss.

**H.7.34** You must refer a claim when another party may be responsible for the moisture source, construction defect, maintenance failure, or remediation error. The transfer of rights provision supports referral because potential recovery rights should be evaluated before the claim is resolved.

**H.7.35** You must not sign a release, indemnity agreement, or similar undertaking outside your assigned authority. The claim authority provision supports this rule because those commitments may affect coverage obligations or recovery rights.

**H.7.36** You must refer any proposed settlement that requires a broad release of claims, confidentiality obligation, or nonstandard term. The loss settlement provision supports referral because nonstandard terms may create obligations beyond the loss payment itself.

**H.7.37** You may request a signed proof of loss or other required claim information when the facts warrant it. The duties after loss provision supports this request because the insured may be required to provide information needed to adjust the claim.

**H.7.38** You must refer a claim when the insured declines access, refuses reasonable information requests, or prevents necessary inspection. The duties after loss provision supports referral because cooperation issues may affect the ability to complete the investigation.

**H.7.39** You must not treat a hygiene concern, odor complaint, or occupant symptom as proof of covered fungi or mold damage. The fungi and mold coverage provision supports this rule because coverage depends on the applicable facts and terms.

**H.7.40** You must refer claims involving allegations of bodily injury, illness, contamination, or habitability concerns. The liability coverage provision supports referral because those allegations may involve coverage issues beyond property damage handling authority.

**H.7.41** You must refer claims involving governmental involvement, public health allegations, or regulatory demands. The claim authority provision supports referral because these matters may require coordinated handling and specialized review.

**H.7.42** You may explain the claim process, the information needed, and the status of the investigation to the insured or authorized representative. The claim investigation provision supports these communications because clear process information promotes cooperation and accurate expectations.

**H.7.43** You must not provide legal advice or interpret statutory obligations for the insured, contractor, or other party. The claim authority provision supports this restriction because claim handling communications must remain within authorized operational responsibilities.

**H.7.44** You must refer any threat of litigation, formal demand, appraisal request, mediation request, or attorney representation. The dispute resolution provision supports referral because disputed claim matters require coordinated authority and response.

**H.7.45** You must promptly record the authority obtained for material claim decisions. The claim authority provision supports this practice because the record must show that decisions were approved at the appropriate level.

**H.7.46** You must record the factual basis for a decision to approve, limit, deny, or defer fungi or mold-related expenses. The fungi and mold limitation provision supports this requirement because later review must be able to identify how the provision was applied.

**H.7.47** You may seek clarification when the claim facts do not fit a routine fungi or mold handling pattern. The claim authority provision supports consultation because unclear facts can create unauthorized handling risk.

**H.7.48** You must escalate promptly when new facts materially change the apparent cause, scope, or coverage basis of the fungi or mold condition. The claim investigation provision supports escalation because authority decisions must reflect the current facts.

**H.7.49** You must treat this guidance as operational direction rather than a coverage grant or modification. The governing fungi and mold coverage provision controls because only its applicable terms determine the claim outcome.

# Water Loss Claim Handling Guidance







## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** This guidance supports consistent handling of reported water-loss claims from initial notice through resolution. You should use it as an operational aid because the applicable coverage grant, exclusions, definitions, conditions, and endorsement terms govern the claim outcome.

**H.0.2** This guidance is not contractual and does not create, expand, restrict, or waive coverage. You must base coverage decisions on the applicable policy language because only the operative coverage provisions establish the insurer’s obligations.

**H.0.3** You should evaluate the reported facts before drawing conclusions about the source, duration, or extent of water damage. This approach follows the direct physical loss requirement and relevant exclusions because an accurate cause determination supports a defensible coverage decision.

**H.0.4** You should distinguish damage caused by water from damage caused by wear, deterioration, faulty maintenance, or another excluded cause. The applicable exclusion terms require this analysis because coverage may depend on the efficient cause of loss and any ensuing-loss language.

**H.0.5** You should document the insured’s description of the event, the observed conditions, and the information supporting your cause assessment. Claim conditions requiring cooperation and evidence of loss support this practice because contemporaneous documentation preserves the basis for the decision.

**H.0.6** You should acknowledge the claim, explain needed information, and communicate next steps in clear language. Controlling claim-handling requirements support prompt communication because the insured needs a meaningful opportunity to provide information relevant to coverage and valuation.

**H.0.7** You should request only information reasonably related to the reported water loss, the claimed damage, and the applicable coverage analysis. The cooperation condition supports focused requests because unnecessary demands can delay a fair investigation.

**H.0.8** You should inspect promptly when the reported facts or available evidence make an inspection useful. The loss-settlement and duties-after-loss provisions support timely inspection because conditions can change and evidence of causation can be lost.

**H.0.9** You should preserve photographs, estimates, invoices, statements, and expert opinions that materially support the handling decision. The proof-of-loss and claim-file requirements support retention because the file must show how the coverage and payment conclusions were reached.

**H.0.10** You should explain any reservation of rights in terms that identify the relevant issue without prematurely denying the claim. The applicable coverage provisions support this practice because further investigation may be needed before the insurer can determine whether a limitation or exclusion applies.

**H.0.11** You should not treat this guidance as a substitute for the controlling policy language when an endorsement modifies a standard water-loss provision. Endorsement terms control where applicable because they may change the scope of coverage, exclusions, conditions, or valuation.

**H.0.12** You should evaluate claimed repair costs against the applicable loss-settlement provision and the supported scope of covered damage. That provision governs valuation because payment is based on covered loss rather than every condition found at the property.

**H.0.13** You should separate mitigation expenses from permanent repair costs when the applicable coverage terms treat them differently. The duties-after-loss and loss-settlement provisions support this distinction because reasonable protective measures and restoration work may require separate analysis.

**H.0.14** You should consider whether damaged property can be repaired, restored, or requires replacement under the applicable valuation terms. The loss-settlement provision controls this assessment because the method of payment depends on the coverage language and supported damage scope.

**H.0.15** You should apply deductibles, limits, sublimits, and other policy restrictions only after confirming that the relevant coverage applies. The declarations, coverage grant, and limitation terms support this sequence because a payment calculation cannot replace a coverage determination.

**H.0.16** You should address the special limit on money and precious metals only when the claimed property and applicable coverage language make that issue relevant. The applicable special-limit provision controls because that topic does not determine coverage for unrelated water-damaged property.

**H.0.17** You should address the code upgrade percentage only when the reported repair scope raises a coverage issue under the applicable additional-coverage language. The ordinance-or-law provision controls because required upgrades may involve conditions separate from the direct water damage.

**H.0.18** You should manage the claim payment deadline in accordance with controlling claim-handling requirements and the completed investigation. Those requirements support timely action because payment, denial, or a request for material information should not be delayed without a claim-related reason.

**H.0.19** You should evaluate the backwater device condition only when the reported loss and applicable coverage language make that condition material. The relevant exclusion or condition controls because the presence or operation of a device may affect causation or coverage analysis.

**H.0.20** You should consider years of loss history considered only when prior losses are relevant to the investigation, underwriting information, or an applicable policy condition. The cooperation and concealment-or-fraud provisions support appropriate inquiry because prior events may clarify a reported cause without replacing the facts of the current loss.

**H.0.21** You should seek supervisory, legal, engineering, or other specialist support when the claim presents unresolved coverage, causation, valuation, or regulatory issues. The applicable claim-handling requirements support escalation because specialized review helps ensure that material issues are evaluated consistently.

**H.0.22** You should make the final claim decision understandable, supported by the file, and tied to the applicable coverage language. The coverage grant, exclusions, conditions, and loss-settlement provisions support a clear explanation because the insured must be able to understand the basis for the outcome.

## H.1 — In Appetite

**H.1.1** You may handle a reported water loss within appetite when the facts indicate direct physical loss from a covered accidental water event. This approach follows the Water Damage Coverage provision because coverage depends on the loss cause and resulting damage.

**H.1.2** Confirm the reported source before making a coverage decision, including whether water escaped from plumbing, heating, cooling, fire protection, or an appliance. The Policy Definitions provision supports this review because the meaning of water loss terms controls the scope of coverage.

**H.1.3** Treat a sudden discharge from an intact system as potentially within appetite when the reported facts support an accidental event. The Water Damage Coverage provision applies because an accidental discharge may produce covered direct physical loss.

**H.1.4** Refer a loss involving gradual leakage, repeated seepage, or long-term moisture conditions for additional review before accepting coverage. The Water Damage Exclusion applies because ongoing water conditions may fall outside the contemplated accidental event.

**H.1.5** Obtain a clear account of where the water began, how it traveled, and what property it contacted. The Duties After Loss provision supports this requirement because the claim file must establish a reliable cause of loss.

**H.1.6** Ask the insured to preserve accessible damaged components when practical, including failed pipes, hoses, valves, and appliance parts. The Duties After Loss provision supports preservation because the damaged item may establish whether the event was accidental and covered.

**H.1.7** You may authorize reasonable emergency mitigation when it is necessary to protect covered property from further damage. The Duties After Loss provision supports prompt protective action because the insured must take reasonable steps to prevent additional loss.

**H.1.8** Confirm that mitigation work addresses the reported water event rather than unrelated maintenance or renovation needs. The Loss Settlement provision applies because payment is limited to repair of covered physical damage.

**H.1.9** Review photographs, contractor observations, and moisture findings for consistency with the reported source. The Duties After Loss provision supports this review because claim evidence must reasonably connect the damage to the event.

**H.1.10** Evaluate damage to building materials separately from damage to personal property, even when both arose from the same water event. The Property Covered provision applies because covered property categories may be subject to different terms.

**H.1.11** Identify whether the affected property is owned by the insured, held for the insured’s use, or belongs to another party. The Property Covered provision governs because ownership and property classification affect the available coverage.

**H.1.12** You may consider resulting damage to walls, floors, ceilings, and contents when water escaped from a covered source. The Water Damage Coverage provision applies because direct physical damage caused by the discharge may be covered.

**H.1.13** Separate the cost to repair a failed plumbing component from the cost to repair ensuing water damage. The Wear and Tear Exclusion applies because deterioration of the failed component may not be covered even when resulting damage is covered.

**H.1.14** Do not assume that a blocked drain establishes a covered accidental discharge without confirming the manner in which water entered the property. The Water Damage Coverage provision requires a covered cause of loss rather than the presence of water alone.

**H.1.15** Refer losses involving water that entered from outside the structure for careful cause-of-loss review. The Flood Exclusion applies because surface water, overflow, and external water movement may be excluded.

**H.1.16** Distinguish water entering through an opening created by a covered event from water entering through defective maintenance conditions. The Water Damage Coverage provision applies because the source and pathway of entry determine whether resulting damage is covered.

**H.1.17** Refer sewer, drain, sump, or septic backup allegations for review under the applicable endorsement terms. The Water Backup provision governs because backup-related damage may require specific coverage.

**H.1.18** Confirm whether the reported loss arose from a plumbing discharge or from groundwater, surface water, or external runoff. The Policy Definitions provision supports this distinction because similar damage may arise from materially different causes.

**H.1.19** You may accept reasonable emergency drying, extraction, and stabilization expenses when they are related to a potentially covered water event. The Reasonable Repairs provision supports payment because protective measures may reduce further covered damage.

**H.1.20** Review emergency invoices for work descriptions that identify the affected area and the services performed. The Duties After Loss provision supports this review because claimed expenses must be tied to the reported loss.

**H.1.21** Evaluate demolition and access work only to the extent necessary to investigate or repair covered damage. The Loss Settlement provision applies because coverage does not extend to unrelated improvement work.

**H.1.22** Do not include upgrades, redesign, or elective remodeling in the covered scope unless required by applicable policy terms. The Loss Settlement provision limits payment because the purpose of the claim is to restore covered damage.

**H.1.23** Consider whether hidden moisture resulted from the reported discharge and caused physical damage to otherwise covered property. The Water Damage Coverage provision applies because concealed damage may still be direct physical loss from a covered event.

**H.1.24** Refer mold, fungi, wet rot, or related contamination concerns for review under the applicable limitation or exclusion. The Mold Limitation provision applies because microbial damage may be treated differently from the initial water event.

**H.1.25** Document pre-existing stains, deterioration, prior repairs, and unresolved moisture conditions that may affect the scope of damage. The Loss Settlement provision supports this documentation because payment applies only to damage caused by the reported occurrence.

**H.1.26** You may use contractor findings as claim evidence, but independently evaluate whether those findings establish a covered cause of loss. The Duties After Loss provision applies because the insurer must assess coverage from the available facts.

**H.1.27** Request clarification when the reported cause changes during the investigation or differs from physical evidence. The Concealment or Fraud provision supports careful review because material inconsistencies may affect claim handling.

**H.1.28** Maintain a clear claim file explanation for the accepted cause, excluded cause, or unresolved cause of damage. The Duties After Loss provision supports this practice because the coverage decision must be grounded in documented facts.

**H.1.29** You may issue payment for undisputed covered damage while continuing to investigate a separate disputed portion of the claim. The Loss Payment provision supports this approach because covered amounts should not be delayed solely by an unrelated coverage question.

**H.1.30** Explain any partial denial in plain terms that connect the decision to the reported cause and the applicable policy language. The Policy Conditions provision supports this communication because the insured should understand the basis for the claim outcome.

**H.1.31** Review personal property claims for direct water damage rather than replacement of undamaged property removed for convenience. The Property Covered provision applies because coverage requires physical loss to covered property.

**H.1.32** Consider cleaning, restoration, or replacement based on the condition of the affected property after the water event. The Loss Settlement provision governs because the appropriate method depends on the nature and extent of covered damage.

**H.1.33** Confirm whether damaged contents can be safely restored before approving replacement as the claimed remedy. The Loss Settlement provision supports this review because reasonable restoration may satisfy the covered loss.

**H.1.34** Refer claims involving vacant, unoccupied, or seasonally occupied property for review of the applicable condition. The Vacancy Condition provision applies because occupancy status may affect coverage for water-related loss.

**H.1.35** Evaluate freezing-related water damage by confirming whether the insured took reasonable steps to maintain heat or protect the plumbing system. The Freezing Condition provision applies because coverage may depend on compliance with protective duties.

**H.1.36** Do not characterize a maintenance failure as a sudden accidental event merely because resulting damage was discovered unexpectedly. The Wear and Tear Exclusion applies because discovery of damage does not establish a covered cause.

**H.1.37** Consider whether a covered event caused an opening that allowed later water intrusion into the property. The Water Damage Coverage provision applies because ensuing water damage may depend on the covered cause of the opening.

**H.1.38** Refer claims alleging damage from roof leakage for investigation of the initiating cause and the condition of the roof covering. The Water Damage Exclusion applies because leakage and maintenance-related conditions may be excluded.

**H.1.39** Obtain a reasonable repair scope that distinguishes damaged materials from undamaged matching materials. The Loss Settlement provision applies because the covered repair obligation is determined by the policy’s valuation terms.

**H.1.40** Consider necessary access and repair to concealed plumbing when the work is directly related to locating or repairing covered water damage. The Water Damage Coverage provision applies because access costs may be part of restoring covered direct physical loss.

**H.1.41** Do not pay for replacement of an entire system when the evidence supports repair of only the damaged portion. The Loss Settlement provision limits payment because coverage is based on the covered damage actually sustained.

**H.1.42** Identify subrogation potential when a product failure, contractor work, utility event, or another party may have caused the water loss. The Transfer of Rights of Recovery provision applies because recovery rights should be preserved after payment.

**H.1.43** Avoid disposing of failed components or releasing responsible parties before subrogation facts have been evaluated. The Transfer of Rights of Recovery provision supports preservation because recovery opportunities may depend on available evidence.

**H.1.44** Handle claims involving code-related repair requirements only after confirming that the requirement applies to covered repair work. The Ordinance or Law provision applies because code costs may be subject to separate coverage terms.

**H.1.45** Refer unusual water-loss facts, conflicting evidence, or potentially excluded causes for coverage consultation before issuing a final denial. The Policy Conditions provision supports referral because a complete factual record promotes consistent claim decisions.

**H.1.46** Close the claim only after documenting the covered cause, payment basis, unresolved issues, and any recovery action. The Loss Payment provision supports complete documentation because the claim file should show how the covered loss was resolved.

## H.2 — Roof Age and Condition

**H.2.1** You should identify the roof covering, roof slope, drainage features, and visible condition before determining whether a reported water loss is sudden or the result of deterioration. This supports application of the policy’s Covered Cause of Loss provision.

**H.2.2** You should obtain photographs showing the affected roof area, adjacent materials, and the interior water path when access and conditions permit. This supports the Duties After Loss provision by preserving evidence of the reported event.

**H.2.3** You may rely on inspection findings from a qualified roofing professional when the cause of water entry cannot be determined from available photographs. This supports the Loss Settlement provision by establishing the basis for a covered-damage evaluation.

**H.2.4** You should distinguish storm-created openings from openings caused by wear, inadequate maintenance, installation defects, or prior damage. This supports application of the Wear and Tear exclusion.

**H.2.5** You should document whether roof materials are missing, displaced, fractured, punctured, lifted, or otherwise altered by an identified covered event. This supports the Direct Physical Loss requirement.

**H.2.6** You should not treat interior staining alone as evidence that a covered event damaged the roof. This supports the Insuring Agreement requirement that direct physical loss be caused by a covered peril.

**H.2.7** You should review available weather information when the reported cause involves wind, hail, falling objects, or another weather-related event. This supports the Covered Cause of Loss provision by comparing the reported event with objective conditions.

**H.2.8** You should request the date the insured first observed water intrusion, roof damage, or related interior damage. This supports the Duties After Loss provision by clarifying when the condition was discovered.

**H.2.9** You should ask whether the roof had leaked, been repaired, or been inspected before the reported loss. This supports the Concealment or Fraud condition by identifying material prior-loss information.

**H.2.10** You should evaluate whether prior repairs address the same area from which water reportedly entered. This supports the Wear and Tear exclusion by separating recurring leakage from new accidental damage.

**H.2.11** You should preserve records of prior claims involving the roof or related interior water damage when those records are available. This supports the Other Insurance and Loss History conditions relevant to claim evaluation.

**H.2.12** You should not deny a claim solely because the roof is aged or shows general weathering. This supports the Covered Cause of Loss provision because age alone does not establish the cause of the reported water damage.

**H.2.13** You should determine whether the roof’s condition prevented it from performing its intended water-shedding function before the reported event. This supports application of the Maintenance condition and the Wear and Tear exclusion.

**H.2.14** You should document granule loss, brittle materials, exposed fasteners, failed sealant, open seams, and deteriorated flashing where present. This supports the Wear and Tear exclusion by identifying conditions that may permit gradual water entry.

**H.2.15** You should distinguish accidental water entry through a newly damaged roof component from seepage through long-standing deterioration. This supports the Water Damage limitation and the Direct Physical Loss requirement.

**H.2.16** You should assess whether the reported water path is consistent with the claimed point of roof damage. This supports the Loss Settlement provision by linking covered damage to resulting interior repairs.

**H.2.17** You should consider whether water could have entered through vents, skylights, chimneys, wall transitions, gutters, or flashing rather than through the roof surfacing. This supports the Covered Cause of Loss provision by identifying the actual source of intrusion.

**H.2.18** You should apply the roof surfacing age threshold only as directed by the applicable coverage terms and claim handling instructions. This supports consistent application of the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.2.19** You should not use roof condition as a substitute for determining whether a covered event caused direct physical loss. This supports the Insuring Agreement, which requires a causal connection between the event and the claimed damage.

**H.2.20** You should request repair invoices, inspection reports, and contractor communications when they may clarify preexisting roof conditions. This supports the Duties After Loss provision by obtaining information needed to evaluate the claim.

**H.2.21** You should evaluate whether repairs were temporary mitigation measures or permanent corrections to a deteriorated condition. This supports the Reasonable Repairs provision by separating emergency protection from maintenance work.

**H.2.22** You should allow reasonable emergency measures that protect covered property from further damage when the circumstances support prompt action. This supports the Reasonable Repairs provision.

**H.2.23** You should not include the cost to correct unrelated maintenance deficiencies in the covered repair scope. This supports the Loss Settlement provision and the Wear and Tear exclusion.

**H.2.24** You should identify whether damaged materials can be repaired without disturbing undamaged roof areas. This supports the Loss Settlement provision by limiting payment to direct physical loss.

**H.2.25** You should consider matching issues only when required by applicable coverage terms and supported by the repair scope. This supports the Loss Settlement provision by distinguishing necessary replacement from aesthetic preference.

**H.2.26** You should document whether replacement materials are available and whether their use will restore the roof’s pre-loss function. This supports the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.2.27** You should not approve replacement of an entire roof solely because localized covered damage is present. This supports the Loss Settlement provision, which limits payment to covered direct physical loss.

**H.2.28** You should evaluate whether code-required work results directly from covered repairs rather than from preexisting roof conditions. This supports the Ordinance or Law provision where that coverage applies.

**H.2.29** You should obtain the applicable code authority’s written requirement when code-related costs are claimed. This supports the Ordinance or Law provision by confirming that the work is required.

**H.2.30** You should separate roof replacement costs from interior drying, repair, and mitigation costs in the estimate. This supports the Loss Settlement provision by maintaining a clear record of covered damage categories.

**H.2.31** You should evaluate interior damage independently when roof coverage is limited or excluded. This supports the Insuring Agreement because covered interior water damage may require a separate causation analysis.

**H.2.32** You should determine whether wind-driven rain entered through an opening created by a covered event or through a preexisting defect. This supports the Windstorm or Hail limitation and the Water Damage limitation.

**H.2.33** You should not assume that roof leaks during rainfall are caused by windstorm damage. This supports the Covered Cause of Loss provision because rainfall may reveal an existing maintenance-related condition.

**H.2.34** You should record the insured’s description of the event without treating that description as the sole proof of causation. This supports the Duties After Loss provision and preserves the reported facts for evaluation.

**H.2.35** You should explain any roof-related coverage decision using the observed condition, the reported event, and the applicable policy provision. This supports the Claims Settlement Practices provision by providing a clear claim rationale.

**H.2.36** You should apply the roof payment floor only when the applicable coverage terms call for that treatment. This supports consistent administration of the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.2.37** You should evaluate the insured-to-value threshold for replacement cost settlement under the applicable coverage terms before finalizing the payment basis. This supports the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.2.38** You should not reduce payment for betterment unless the applicable policy provision requires that adjustment. This supports the Loss Settlement provision by preventing unsupported depreciation or replacement-cost changes.

**H.2.39** You should document depreciation assumptions when actual cash value applies to roof damage. This supports the Actual Cash Value provision by showing how the valuation was determined.

**H.2.40** You should issue payment for covered emergency mitigation separately from disputed roof replacement costs when the available evidence supports that approach. This supports the Loss Payment provision by allowing undisputed covered amounts to be handled promptly.

**H.2.41** You should refer uncertain causation issues for additional technical review rather than relying on unsupported conclusions about roof condition. This supports the Claims Settlement Practices provision by promoting a reasonable investigation.

**H.2.42** You should maintain a complete claim file containing inspection materials, estimates, photographs, weather information, and the coverage analysis. This supports the Recordkeeping condition and provides the basis for the claim decision.

## H.3 — Wind and Hail

**H.3.1** Identify the reported weather event before evaluating resulting water damage. This follows the Windstorm or Hail Coverage provision because the cause of entry determines whether wind, hail, flood, or another coverage analysis applies.

**H.3.2** Obtain weather records, photographs, and witness information that place the alleged wind or hail event at the insured location. The Windstorm or Hail Coverage provision requires a supported link between the event and the claimed damage.

**H.3.3** Inspect for a wind-created opening before accepting rainwater damage as ensuing wind loss. This follows the Wind-Driven Rain provision because rain entering without a covered opening may be subject to different coverage treatment.

**H.3.4** Treat a wind mitigation inspection as required when Coverage A exceeds $1,000,000. This follows the Wind Mitigation Inspection provision because the inspection may affect the applicable underwriting information and claim review.

**H.3.5** Confirm whether the reported damage involves direct physical loss from wind, hail, or both. The Windstorm or Hail Coverage provision applies only when the evidence supports the reported peril.

**H.3.6** Separate damage caused by wind from damage caused by long-term leakage, deterioration, or faulty maintenance. This follows the Exclusions provision because excluded conditions do not become covered merely because they are discovered after a storm.

**H.3.7** Document all visible roof, wall, window, door, and flashing damage before authorizing permanent repairs. The Duties After Loss provision supports prompt documentation because repairs can remove evidence needed to determine cause and scope.

**H.3.8** Ask the insured when and how water was first observed inside the building. This follows the Duties After Loss provision because the timing of water entry may distinguish storm damage from prior seepage.

**H.3.9** Review prior claims, inspections, repair records, and underwriting information for the same areas of damage. The Concealment or Fraud provision and Duties After Loss provision support this review because prior damage may affect causation and valuation.

**H.3.10** Preserve samples or photographs of damaged materials when hail impact or wind uplift is disputed. The Loss Settlement provision requires reliable evidence because the repair scope must reflect covered physical damage.

**H.3.11** Do not treat cosmetic marks as covered hail damage without evidence of functional impairment or direct physical loss. This follows the Direct Physical Loss requirement because surface appearance alone may not establish covered damage.

**H.3.12** Evaluate roof damage together with interior water staining, moisture readings, and the reported path of entry. The Wind-Driven Rain provision supports a connected analysis because interior damage must be tied to a covered opening or other covered cause.

**H.3.13** Confirm whether any temporary protective measures were reasonable and necessary. This follows the Reasonable Repairs provision because emergency efforts may prevent additional covered damage.

**H.3.14** Instruct the insured to retain damaged property when practical until inspection is complete. The Duties After Loss provision supports retention because the damaged property may be necessary to confirm cause, age, and condition.

**H.3.15** Distinguish emergency mitigation from permanent restoration work in the claim file. The Reasonable Repairs provision supports this distinction because mitigation may be necessary before the full repair scope is established.

**H.3.16** Review invoices for drying, tarping, extraction, and debris removal against the documented storm conditions. This follows the Reasonable Repairs provision because only reasonable measures related to covered damage should be considered.

**H.3.17** Confirm whether water entered through an opening created by wind or hail rather than through a preexisting defect. The Wind-Driven Rain provision requires this determination because the source and condition of the opening affect coverage.

**H.3.18** Refer suspected construction defects, wear, corrosion, rot, or installation failures for appropriate review. This follows the Faulty Workmanship and Wear and Tear Exclusions because these conditions may limit or exclude coverage.

**H.3.19** Apply the wind percentage deductible minimum when calculating the applicable wind deductible. The Windstorm or Hail Deductible provision controls because the deductible must be applied as stated for the reported peril.

**H.3.20** Do not apply a deductible greater than the maximum windstorm and hail deductible percentage. This follows the Windstorm or Hail Deductible provision because deductible application must remain within the policy limitation.

**H.3.21** Verify the deductible shown for the loss date rather than relying on a later policy record. The Declarations and Deductible provision governs because the applicable deductible depends on the coverage in force when the loss occurred.

**H.3.22** Escalate any asserted deductible change for review under the deductible increase notice period. This follows the Deductible Change provision because a changed deductible may require confirmation that notice requirements were satisfied.

**H.3.23** Determine whether the reported event meets the policy definition of hurricane before applying hurricane-specific terms. The Hurricane Deductible provision controls because not every wind or rain event is a hurricane occurrence.

**H.3.24** Apply the hurricane occurrence tail only when the event and reported damage fall within the applicable hurricane occurrence analysis. This follows the Hurricane Deductible provision because related storm damage may be handled as part of the same occurrence.

**H.3.25** Avoid assigning a hurricane deductible solely because the insured describes the event as a hurricane. The Hurricane Deductible provision requires verification because the policy definition controls the deductible decision.

**H.3.26** Review official weather information and claim evidence together when the storm classification is unclear. The Hurricane Deductible provision supports this review because event classification must be supported by reliable information.

**H.3.27** Explain the cause-of-loss decision in plain language when denying or limiting water damage. The Loss Payment and Exclusions provisions support a clear explanation because the insured must understand the basis for the coverage determination.

**H.3.28** Identify covered damage and excluded damage separately in the estimate and claim notes. The Loss Settlement provision requires an accurate scope because payment must be limited to covered physical loss.

**H.3.29** Do not reduce a covered repair scope merely because unrelated deterioration is present elsewhere on the property. This follows the Loss Settlement provision because the estimate should address the covered damage while separately documenting unrelated conditions.

**H.3.30** Consider matching issues only after confirming that replacement of the damaged material is covered. The Loss Settlement provision controls because matching does not create coverage for damage that is otherwise excluded.

**H.3.31** Obtain a qualified expert review when cause, scope, or repair method cannot be resolved from the available evidence. The Loss Settlement provision supports expert review because complex damage requires a reliable basis for adjustment.

**H.3.32** Refer conflicting contractor opinions for further evaluation rather than selecting an opinion without support. This follows the Duties After Loss and Loss Settlement provisions because the claim decision must rest on documented facts.

**H.3.33** Record the basis for each reserve change involving wind, hail, or ensuing water damage. The Loss Payment provision supports complete file documentation because the reserve should reflect the supported exposure.

**H.3.34** Issue payment only after confirming the applicable deductible, covered scope, and loss settlement basis. The Loss Payment provision governs because payment must reflect the policy terms and supported damage.

**H.3.35** Preserve all weather reports, photographs, estimates, correspondence, and expert findings in the claim file. The Duties After Loss provision supports complete retention because these materials establish the basis for the claim outcome.

**H.3.36** Refer any potential misrepresentation, altered evidence, or inconsistent loss account for special investigation review. The Concealment or Fraud provision applies because intentional misrepresentation may affect coverage for the claim.

## H.4 — Water Backup

**H.4.1** You should confirm that the reported damage resulted from water backing up through a sewer, drain, or sump. The Water Backup provision controls this review because water backup coverage depends on the manner in which the water entered the property.

**H.4.2** You should obtain a clear description of where the water was first observed and which fixture, drain, or equipment component was involved. The Water Backup provision requires source identification because similar damage may arise from causes that are not water backup.

**H.4.3** You should record whether notice was received within 30 days after the loss was discovered. The Water Backup provision supports this inquiry because timely reporting helps preserve evidence of the source and extent of damage.

**H.4.4** You should distinguish water that backed up from a sewer or drain from water entering through a roof, wall, foundation, or opening. The Water Backup provision applies only to the described backup peril, so the claimed cause must be evaluated separately from other water causes.

**H.4.5** You should ask whether the water emerged from a plumbing fixture, floor drain, toilet, utility sink, or similar opening. The Water Backup provision supports this investigation because the point of emergence may identify whether backup is the operative cause.

**H.4.6** You should obtain photographs showing the affected area, the water path, and any visible source conditions. The Water Backup provision supports evidence collection because conditions may change quickly after cleanup or repairs begin.

**H.4.7** You should preserve information about any plumbing work, drain clearing, or mitigation performed after the loss. The Water Backup provision requires a reliable cause evaluation, and post-loss work may remove evidence needed to determine coverage.

**H.4.8** You should ask whether a public sewer, private sewer, septic system, or interior drain system may have contributed to the loss. The Water Backup provision supports this distinction because the coverage analysis turns on backup, not on the ownership of the system alone.

**H.4.9** You should review whether the claimed water came from a sump, sump pit, or sump discharge system. The Water Backup provision addresses sump-related water differently from other water causes, so the reported mechanism must be documented.

**H.4.10** You should apply the sewer backup limit to covered damage arising from a qualifying sewer or drain backup. The Water Backup provision contains this limitation because the endorsement or coverage grant may restrict the amount available for this peril.

**H.4.11** You should apply the sump overflow deductible when the facts establish an otherwise covered sump overflow loss. The Water Backup provision supports the deductible application because the deductible allocates the insured’s share of the covered loss.

**H.4.12** You should determine whether a backwater device was present, operational, and maintained before the loss. The Water Backup provision supports this inquiry because the backwater device condition may affect the applicability of policy terms or underwriting conditions.

**H.4.13** You should not assume that standing water near a drain establishes a covered backup. The Water Backup provision requires evidence of the covered mechanism because surface water, seepage, and plumbing leaks may produce similar conditions.

**H.4.14** You should inspect for signs that water entered from outside the building rather than through a sewer, drain, or sump. The Water Backup provision limits the analysis to covered backup causes, so exterior entry must be separated from an internal backup event.

**H.4.15** You should obtain statements from occupants, contractors, and service providers who observed the water event. The Water Backup provision supports witness development because those observations may establish the sequence and source of the discharge.

**H.4.16** You should identify all damaged building materials, personal property, and improvements claimed from the event. The Water Backup provision applies only to damage caused by the covered water source, so the damage inventory must be tied to that source.

**H.4.17** You should separate damage caused by the backup from damage caused by preexisting deterioration, repeated leakage, or unrelated moisture. The Water Backup provision supports allocation because coverage does not extend merely because covered and noncovered conditions appear in the same area.

**H.4.18** You should review mitigation invoices for extraction, drying, cleaning, demolition, and debris handling. The Water Backup provision supports this review because reasonable mitigation may be covered only when related to a covered backup loss.

## H.5 — Prior Losses and Referral

**H.5.1** You must identify prior water-related losses, repairs, and reported damage when the available claim information indicates a recurring condition. This helps you distinguish a new accidental event from an unresolved or continuing source of damage.

**H.5.2** Obtain available loss-history information early and record the source of that information in the claim file. Reliable history supports consistent coverage analysis and prevents reliance on unsupported assumptions.

**H.5.3** Compare the reported location, damaged property, water source, and claimed repairs with available prior-loss information. Material similarities may indicate that the current claim involves prior damage, incomplete remediation, or a recurring cause.

**H.5.4** Ask the insured to explain any prior water damage that appears related to the current report. The insured’s explanation may clarify whether repairs were completed and whether the current damage arose from a separate event.

**H.5.5** Obtain records of prior mitigation, drying, reconstruction, plumbing work, and mold-related work when those records may affect the current evaluation. These records can establish the condition of the property before the reported loss.

**H.5.6** Do not treat the existence of a prior loss as a basis to deny or limit the current claim without supporting evidence. A prior loss alone does not establish that the reported damage was present before the current event.

**H.5.7** Refer for additional review when prior damage cannot be separated from newly reported damage using available evidence. A clear allocation is necessary to evaluate the claimed loss accurately.

**H.5.8** Document observable differences between older damage and recently occurring water damage. Physical distinctions may help support a causation decision and a reasonable repair scope.

**H.5.9** Refer any water loss with a claimed, incurred, or reasonably anticipated total above $25,000 to the designated claim authority. Escalation at that level helps ensure appropriate oversight of significant exposure.

**H.5.10** Do not delay referral while the full value of the claim remains uncertain. Early referral allows the designated claim authority to guide investigation, reserves, and communication.

**H.5.11** Refer a claim when emergency mitigation charges appear disproportionate to the reported event or condition of the property. Unusual mitigation activity may require prompt review of necessity, causation, and reasonableness.

**H.5.12** Refer a claim when mitigation began before the reported water event and the relationship between the work and current damage is unclear. Earlier work may indicate a pre-existing condition or a separate source of loss.

**H.5.13** Refer a claim when the reported water source cannot be identified after reasonable investigation. An unresolved source may materially affect coverage, repair scope, and recovery potential.

**H.5.14** Refer a claim when evidence suggests repeated leakage, seepage, overflow, backup, or intrusion from the same area. Repetition may indicate a condition that requires specialized causation review.

**H.5.15** Refer a claim when the reported damage appears related to deferred maintenance, deterioration, wear, corrosion, or construction defects. These conditions may affect whether the claimed damage resulted from a covered accidental event.

**H.5.16** Refer a claim when prior repairs appear incomplete, ineffective, or unrelated to the reported source of water. Incomplete repairs can complicate the distinction between prior damage and current damage.

**H.5.17** Refer a claim when the insured’s account conflicts materially with inspection findings, repair records, photographs, or other claim information. Material inconsistencies require careful review before you make a coverage or payment decision.

**H.5.18** Preserve records that show when prior damage was reported, observed, repaired, or left unrepaired. A complete chronology supports a defensible evaluation of causation and damages.

**H.5.19** Request photographs showing the property before repairs whenever prior-loss concerns exist. Pre-repair images may reveal the extent, condition, and apparent age of the claimed damage.

**H.5.20** Ask for invoices, estimates, work orders, and proof of payment associated with prior repairs when relevant to the current damage. Repair records may establish whether the affected area was restored before the current report.

**H.5.21** Refer a claim when an estimate includes replacement of materials that appear unaffected by the reported water event. Expanded repair scopes may require review to separate direct damage from unrelated improvements.

**H.5.22** Establish and update the claim reserve based on the known facts, anticipated investigation, mitigation exposure, and potential repair scope. Accurate reserving supports appropriate claim authority and operational planning.

**H.5.23** Refer a claim when the anticipated repair scope includes extensive access work, hidden damage, structural concerns, or competing repair recommendations. Complex repair conditions may increase uncertainty about cause and reasonable cost.

**H.5.24** Refer a claim when water damage affects property shared by multiple parties or occupied by parties other than the insured. Shared interests can create additional responsibility, access, payment, and recovery issues.

**H.5.25** Refer a claim when a landlord, tenant, association, contractor, neighbor, or other outside party may have caused or contributed to the water loss. Potential responsibility of another party should be evaluated before claim resolution.

**H.5.26** Preserve information that may support recovery from a responsible party, including photographs, damaged components, repair records, and contact information. Timely preservation helps protect potential recovery rights.

**H.5.27** Refer a claim when a contractor, mitigation vendor, or repair vendor is alleged to have caused additional damage. Vendor-related allegations may require separate review of scope, responsibility, and communications.

**H.5.28** Refer a claim when there is evidence of altered documents, duplicate invoices, unsupported charges, or unusual billing practices. These indicators may require specialized review before payment is issued.

**H.5.29** Refer a claim when the condition of the property prevents a reliable inspection or when access is repeatedly restricted. Limited access may prevent you from confirming the cause and extent of damage.

**H.5.30** Document all unsuccessful attempts to obtain material information, access, or records from the insured or another involved party. Clear documentation supports later claim decisions and explains any investigation limitations.

**H.5.31** Refer a claim when the insured reports health concerns, contamination concerns, or conditions that may require specialized handling. Specialized conditions can affect inspection practices, vendor selection, and communication.

**H.5.32** Refer a claim when mold, microbial growth, or similar conditions are alleged to result from the water event. These allegations may require careful separation of current conditions from prior moisture-related damage.

**H.5.33** Refer a claim when the claimed water damage appears to have developed gradually rather than from a reported accidental event. Gradual damage concerns require close review of the available evidence and applicable claim terms.

**H.5.34** Refer a claim when the insured seeks payment for damage that was known, visible, or previously repaired before the reported loss. Known prior damage may not be attributable to the current reported event.

**H.5.35** Refer a claim when the loss involves a vacant, unoccupied, undergoing renovation, or otherwise atypical property condition. These conditions may affect the investigation of source, timing, discovery, and damages.

**H.5.36** Refer a claim when a public authority, utility, municipality, or emergency service agency may possess relevant information. Independent records may help confirm the reported event and identify a potential source.

**H.5.37** Do not communicate a final coverage position while a material prior-loss issue remains unresolved. Premature conclusions can create inconsistent claim handling and require later correction.

**H.5.38** Explain to the insured what information is needed to evaluate prior damage and the current reported loss. Clear communication helps obtain relevant evidence and reduces misunderstanding about the investigation.

**H.5.39** Keep communications focused on the facts needed to assess the current claim and any related prior damage. Neutral, fact-based communication supports fair handling and avoids unsupported conclusions.

**H.5.40** Record the basis for any determination that damage is new, prior, recurring, unrelated, or not reasonably distinguishable. A documented rationale supports consistency, quality review, and claim-file clarity.

**H.5.41** Follow the designated claim authority’s direction after referral and document material guidance received. Consistent execution ensures that significant water-loss exposures receive appropriate oversight.

**H.5.42** Treat this guidance as internal claim-handling direction and not as a policy promise, coverage grant, or limitation of applicable claim obligations. The claim decision must remain grounded in the facts developed during the investigation.

## H.6 — Handling Procedures

## H.7 — Authority Limits

**H.7.3** You may investigate facts, obtain estimates, and document damage without treating those actions as approval authority. This keeps fact gathering separate from decisions that affect claim payment.

**H.7.4** You must refer a claim when the anticipated exposure may exceed your available authority. This allows the appropriate decision maker to review the full claim impact before a commitment is made.

**H.7.5** You must refer a claim when coverage depends on an unresolved exclusion, limitation, condition, or endorsement issue. This protects against payment decisions based on an incomplete coverage analysis.

**H.7.6** You may authorize reasonable emergency mitigation within your authority when action is needed to prevent additional damage. This supports loss control while preserving review of the underlying cause and coverage.

**H.7.8** You must document the reason for any emergency authorization in the claim record. This shows that the action was taken to protect property rather than to resolve coverage.

**H.7.9** You may approve reasonable investigation expenses within your authority when they are necessary to determine cause, scope, or responsibility. This supports an informed claim decision without expanding settlement authority.

**H.7.10** You must refer investigation expenses that could materially affect the claim exposure or require specialized analysis. This ensures that unusual expense commitments receive appropriate oversight.

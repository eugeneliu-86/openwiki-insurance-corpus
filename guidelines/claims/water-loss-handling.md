# Water Loss Claim Handling Guidance

Internal claims guidance for homeowners water losses. This document tells an adjuster how to determine the cause of a water loss, which provisions govern, and when to refer. It is not part of any policy and must never be quoted to an insured or a claimant.

Last revised 2026-05-02 by Claims Technical Services.

## C.1 Determine the Water Source First

Every water loss decision turns on where the water came from, not on what it damaged. Establish the source before evaluating the damage, and document the basis for that determination in the file.

There are four sources that produce four different outcomes under HO-3, and confusing them is the single most common cause of a wrongful denial in this line.

## C.2 Surface Water and Flood

Water that reached the property across the ground — rising water, storm surge, overflow of a body of water — is excluded by HO-3 Section I A.1, and that exclusion applies whether or not the water was driven by wind. No endorsement in our portfolio writes this exclusion back. If the source is surface water or flood, the loss is not covered and the file should be closed with a denial citing A.1 directly.

Do not treat the presence of a water backup endorsement as changing this outcome. HO 04 90 W.4 states expressly that Section I A.1 continues to apply in full.

## C.3 Subsurface Water

Water that came from below grade — hydrostatic pressure, seepage through a foundation or slab — is excluded by HO-3 Section I A.2. As with surface water, no endorsement writes this back, and HO 04 90 W.4 preserves A.2 in full.

Foundation seepage claims frequently arrive described as "flooding in the basement." The insured's description is not the determination. Inspect for the entry path.

## C.4 Sewer and Drain Backup

Water that backed up through a sewer or drain, or that overflowed or discharged from a sump or sump pump, is excluded by HO-3 Section I A.3 unless the water backup endorsement is attached to the policy.

Where HO 04 90 is attached, coverage applies to the sublimit in W.2, which is five thousand dollars unless the Declarations show a higher amount, and the separate five hundred dollar deductible in W.3 applies instead of the Section I deductible.

Before paying, confirm the maintenance condition in W.5. Coverage does not apply where the backup resulted from a maintenance failure the insured knew about and a reasonable person would have remedied. A prior service call reporting a slow drain, without evidence of repair, is the typical fact pattern here and requires a documented determination either way.

Note that personal property under this endorsement settles at actual cash value under W.6 regardless of any replacement cost personal property endorsement on the policy. This surprises insureds and should be explained at first contact rather than at payment.

## C.5 Sudden and Accidental Discharge

Water that escaped from within a plumbing, heating, or air conditioning system, or from a household appliance, is a covered peril under HO-3 Section I P.2 where the discharge was sudden and accidental as defined in Definitions 5.

The line between this and an excluded loss is duration. HO-3 Section I C.3 excludes continuous or repeated seepage over a period of weeks, months, or years. A pipe that burst is covered. A supply line that had been weeping behind a wall for months is not, and the exclusion applies even though the insured only discovered it suddenly. Definitions 5 makes discovery irrelevant — what matters is the onset of the condition.

Document the duration finding with physical evidence: staining patterns, corrosion, material degradation. A duration determination resting only on the adjuster's opinion will not survive a coverage dispute.

## C.6 Resulting Mold

Where a covered water loss produces mold, HO 04 81 provides limited coverage if attached, subject to the ten thousand dollar aggregate limit in M.3 and to the mitigation condition in M.5.

Confirm the underlying water loss is covered before evaluating the mold. HO 04 81 M.2 provides coverage only where the moisture came from a covered event, so mold arising from surface water, subsurface water, or long-term seepage is not covered regardless of the endorsement, because the underlying loss was already excluded.

The limit in M.3 is an annual aggregate, not a per-occurrence limit. Check for prior mold payments in the same policy period before reserving.

## C.7 Referral Triggers

Refer to a technical claims specialist where the water source cannot be established from physical evidence, where two or more of C.2 through C.5 could plausibly apply, where the claim involves a foundation, or where the insured has retained a public adjuster or counsel.

Refer any claim where the loss exceeds twenty-five thousand dollars, or where a denial would rest primarily on the duration finding in C.5.

## C.8 Reservation of Rights

Issue a reservation of rights letter before investigating any claim where coverage may turn on C.5 duration or on the C.4 maintenance condition. Investigating a questionable claim without reserving rights risks waiver of the defense.

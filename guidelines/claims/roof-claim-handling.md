# Roof Claim Handling Guidance







## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** This guidance supports consistent handling of roof claims from initial report through resolution. You should use it to identify relevant facts, apply the applicable policy language, and document the basis for the claim decision.

**H.0.2** This guidance is internal underwriting and claims direction, not part of any insurance contract. The policy form, endorsements, declarations, and applicable law control because they establish the rights and obligations of the parties.

**H.0.3** You should not treat this guidance as expanding, restricting, or replacing coverage. That approach follows the policy’s insuring agreement and conditions, which govern only the coverage purchased.

**H.0.4** You should evaluate each roof claim on its own reported facts, inspection findings, and policy terms. This is necessary because roof damage may arise from covered and excluded causes within the same reported loss.

**H.0.5** You should confirm the policy status before making a coverage representation or issuing payment. This follows the Cancellation provision because a nonpayment cancellation notice may affect whether coverage remained in force.

**H.0.6** You should identify all potentially applicable coverages, limitations, deductibles, exclusions, and endorsements before communicating a final position. This follows the Declarations and Coverage provisions because the money limit and applicable endorsements may affect the available payment.

**H.0.7** You should distinguish direct physical loss from maintenance concerns, wear, deterioration, workmanship issues, and other noncovered conditions. This follows the Perils Insured Against provision because the cause of loss determines whether the reported damage falls within coverage.

**H.0.8** You should obtain sufficient information to determine the cause, scope, and location of roof damage. This follows the Duties After Loss provision because a complete investigation supports a timely and supportable claim decision.

**H.0.9** You should document visible damage, reported conditions, repair history, weather information, and relevant communications in the claim file. This supports the Loss Settlement provision because the file must show how the scope of covered damage was determined.

**H.0.10** You should evaluate whether interior damage, detached structures, personal property damage, or loss of use may also be implicated by the reported roof event. This follows the applicable Coverage provisions because related damage may require separate analysis under different policy terms.

**H.0.11** You should address ordinance or law coverage as a percentage of Coverage A only when the applicable policy language and claimed work support that analysis. This follows the Ordinance or Law provision because required upgrades may be treated differently from repairs to covered direct physical damage.

**H.0.12** You should not assume that local building requirements create covered loss. This follows the Ordinance or Law provision because coverage depends on the policy terms and the connection between the requirement and covered damage.

**H.0.13** You should review prior claims, repairs, inspections, and disclosed conditions when they may bear on causation or scope. This follows the Concealment or Fraud and Duties After Loss provisions because the prior loss period may help distinguish new damage from preexisting conditions.

**H.0.14** You should avoid directing repair methods, contractors, or building-system modifications unless the policy language or claim facts require a position. This is important because a check valve requirement may involve code compliance, maintenance, or property protection issues outside the roof loss itself.

**H.0.15** You should communicate coverage decisions clearly, accurately, and in a manner consistent with the claim file. This follows the Loss Payment and Conditions provisions because the insured must be able to understand the basis for payment, partial payment, or denial.

**H.0.16** You should escalate unusual facts, competing causes, policy interpretation questions, or potential coverage disputes through the appropriate internal process. This supports consistent application of the policy form because complex roof claims may require specialized review before a final determination.

## H.1 — In Appetite

**H.1.1** You may retain a roof claim in standard handling when the reported damage presents as a direct physical loss rather than a maintenance concern. This approach rests on the Direct Physical Loss provision because coverage requires damage caused by a covered event.

**H.1.2** Confirm that the reported roof area is part of the insured property before addressing cause, scope, or settlement. This approach rests on the Covered Property provision because only insured property is eligible for payment.

**H.1.3** Obtain a clear description of the reported event and the damage observed after that event. This approach rests on the Duties After Loss provision because a reliable cause assessment depends on prompt, complete loss information.

**H.1.4** Preserve the reported cause of loss in the claim record using the insured’s description and available observations. This approach rests on the Duties After Loss provision because later handling must be supported by a consistent factual record.

**H.1.5** Inspect the roof whenever the reported facts do not clearly establish the source, extent, or character of damage. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because payment must reflect covered damage rather than assumed conditions.

**H.1.6** Evaluate exterior roof surfaces, related flashings, penetrations, drainage components, and visible interior effects as part of the reported loss. This approach rests on the Direct Physical Loss provision because covered damage may extend beyond the area initially identified.

**H.1.7** Distinguish fresh damage from prior deterioration, staining, granule loss, surface wear, and installation defects. This approach rests on the Wear and Tear Exclusion because coverage does not apply to conditions arising from ordinary aging or maintenance needs.

**H.1.8** Identify whether the claimed damage is consistent with wind, hail, falling objects, water entry, or another reported cause. This approach rests on the Covered Causes of Loss provision because the claimed event must be a covered cause of loss.

**H.1.9** Keep the claim in appetite when the evidence reasonably supports a covered cause and the roof damage can be evaluated through ordinary claim handling. This approach rests on the Claim Settlement provision because routine covered losses should be resolved without unnecessary escalation.

**H.1.10** Refer the claim for specialized review when the reported facts suggest a cause that cannot be reliably determined through available observations. This approach rests on the Duties After Loss provision because uncertain causation can affect the coverage decision.

**H.1.11** Refer the claim for specialized review when the roof condition appears materially altered, concealed, or unavailable for meaningful inspection. This approach rests on the Concealment or Fraud provision because altered conditions may prevent a reliable evaluation of the loss.

**H.1.12** Confirm whether water damage resulted from a covered opening created by the reported event or from a preexisting condition. This approach rests on the Water Damage Limitation because the source and path of water determine whether resulting damage is covered.

**H.1.13** Do not treat interior staining alone as proof of a covered roof loss. This approach rests on the Direct Physical Loss provision because visible effects must be connected to covered physical damage.

**H.1.14** Consider the condition of adjoining roof areas when evaluating whether observed damage is isolated or part of a broader event pattern. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the repair scope must reflect the actual covered extent of damage.

**H.1.15** Record visible evidence of prior repairs, patching, uneven surfaces, missing materials, and workmanship concerns. This approach rests on the Faulty Workmanship Exclusion because damage resulting from defective construction or repair may not be covered.

**H.1.16** Separate damage caused by the reported event from conditions caused by deferred maintenance. This approach rests on the Wear and Tear Exclusion because maintenance-related conditions do not become covered merely because they are discovered during a loss.

**H.1.17** Evaluate whether the claimed roof material can be repaired without creating a functional deficiency. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because payment should address covered damage using a reasonable method of repair or replacement.

**H.1.18** Consider visible differences in material appearance only after confirming that covered physical damage requires repair or replacement. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because cosmetic variation alone does not establish a covered loss.

**H.1.19** Do not expand the repair scope solely to achieve uniform appearance where the covered damage can be reasonably repaired. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because settlement is based on covered damage rather than elective improvement.

**H.1.20** Refer matching concerns for review when required repairs may affect compliance with an applicable building requirement. This approach rests on the Ordinance or Law provision because increased costs arising from building requirements may depend on separate coverage terms.

**H.1.21** Confirm whether the claim includes damage to detached structures, attached features, or roof-mounted equipment. This approach rests on the Covered Property provision because different property categories may be subject to different coverage terms.

**H.1.22** Evaluate roof-mounted solar equipment, antennas, skylights, and similar features according to their ownership and attachment to insured property. This approach rests on the Covered Property provision because eligibility depends on whether the item falls within the insured property definition.

**H.1.23** Identify emergency measures taken to prevent further damage after the reported event. This approach rests on the Reasonable Repairs provision because necessary protective actions may be covered when they mitigate additional loss.

**H.1.24** Allow reasonable temporary protection of exposed property when the reported facts support a covered roof event. This approach rests on the Reasonable Repairs provision because the insured is expected to protect property from further damage.

**H.1.25** Do not treat permanent upgrades or elective improvements as emergency protective work. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because payment is limited to work necessary to address covered damage.

**H.1.26** Evaluate contractor opinions as evidence rather than as the coverage determination. This approach rests on the Claims Conditions provision because the carrier remains responsible for determining coverage and the appropriate settlement.

**H.1.27** Compare repair recommendations with observed damage and the reported cause of loss. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the recommended work must be supported by covered physical damage.

**H.1.28** Request clarification when a repair proposal includes unrelated maintenance, code upgrades, or broad replacement work. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the claim payment must be limited to covered repair needs.

**H.1.29** Apply the applicable deductible after determining the covered amount of the roof loss. This approach rests on the Deductible provision because the deductible reduces the amount payable for an otherwise covered claim.

**H.1.30** Do not advise that any payment is owed until coverage, damage scope, and applicable limitations have been evaluated. This approach rests on the Loss Payment provision because payment obligations arise only after the claim is adjusted under the policy terms.

**H.1.31** Explain the basis for any partial denial in clear claim language tied to the observed condition and reported cause. This approach rests on the Exclusions provision because excluded damage must be distinguished from covered damage.

**H.1.32** Explain the basis for any limitation of payment without characterizing excluded conditions as covered damage. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because settlement must accurately reflect the covered portion of the loss.

**H.1.33** Preserve photographs, observations, and claim notes that support the cause and scope conclusions. This approach rests on the Duties After Loss provision because the claim decision should be supported by a clear factual record.

**H.1.34** Use neutral descriptions when documenting disputed facts, contractor findings, or competing cause opinions. This approach rests on the Claims Conditions provision because fair handling requires an objective evaluation of available information.

**H.1.35** Refer the claim when the insured alleges improper prior claim handling, prior roof repairs, or unresolved damage from an earlier event. This approach rests on the Concealment or Fraud provision because prior loss circumstances can affect the evaluation of the present claim.

**H.1.36** Refer the claim when evidence suggests intentional damage, material misrepresentation, or inconsistent reporting of the event. This approach rests on the Concealment or Fraud provision because intentional acts and material misrepresentations may affect coverage.

**H.1.37** Refer the claim when the claimed damage involves structural instability, unsafe access, or conditions requiring specialized safety assessment. This approach rests on the Duties After Loss provision because the loss must be evaluated without exposing claim personnel or others to avoidable hazards.

**H.1.38** Refer the claim when the roof claim includes suspected collapse, earth movement, or another cause subject to distinct coverage analysis. This approach rests on the Collapse provision because those causes may be governed by specialized coverage terms.

**H.1.39** Refer the claim when damage appears related to repeated seepage, leakage, or chronic moisture intrusion. This approach rests on the Water Damage Limitation because gradual water-related conditions may be treated differently from sudden covered damage.

**H.1.40** Keep communication focused on the facts needed to evaluate cause, damage, mitigation, and repair scope. This approach rests on the Claims Conditions provision because efficient fact gathering supports timely and accurate handling.

**H.1.41** Avoid directing the insured to use a particular contractor unless a service arrangement is available and the insured remains free to choose. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the claim decision must remain independent of contractor selection.

**H.1.42** Confirm that any repair recommendation addresses the damaged roof system rather than unrelated property improvements. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because covered settlement does not include betterment unrelated to the loss.

**H.1.43** Treat material availability concerns as part of the repair evaluation rather than as automatic support for full replacement. This approach rests on the Loss Settlement provision because replacement is appropriate only when repair is not a reasonable response to covered damage.

**H.1.44** Identify any known lienholder, mortgage interest, or other protected interest before issuing payment. This approach rests on the Loss Payment provision because payment handling may require recognition of parties with an insurable interest.

**H.1.45** Close the claim only after the coverage determination, damage evaluation, and payment decision are clearly supported in the claim record. This approach rests on the Claims Conditions provision because the file must demonstrate a complete and fair handling process.

**H.1.46** Reopen the claim for review when material new information affects the reported cause, observed damage, or settlement scope. This approach rests on the Loss Payment provision because the payment decision must reflect the covered loss supported by the available facts.

## H.2 — Roof Age and Condition

**H.2.1** You must evaluate roof age and condition from the claim file before selecting a settlement approach. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the roof’s condition may affect the covered measure of loss.

**H.2.2** You should obtain clear photographs of the roof surface, edges, penetrations, drainage areas, and visible damaged locations. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because photographs support a consistent distinction between sudden damage and preexisting deterioration.

**H.2.3** You should document the reported cause of loss in the file using the insured’s description and available physical evidence. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the cause of loss must be evaluated separately from the roof’s age or appearance.

**H.2.4** You may request installation records, invoices, warranties, or prior inspection materials when roof age is material to the claim. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because reliable age evidence supports the applicable loss settlement evaluation.

**H.2.5** You should not rely solely on the insured’s estimate of roof age when records or observable construction features indicate a different age. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because settlement decisions require support that can be explained in the claim file.

**H.2.6** You should record the source of each roof-age conclusion, including records, contractor information, inspection findings, or underwriting information available in the file. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the basis for the conclusion must remain clear for review.

**H.2.7** You should distinguish roof surfacing age from the age of the underlying roof structure. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the damaged component may not share the same installation history as the supporting structure.

**H.2.8** You should identify the roof surfacing material and describe its observable condition. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because different materials may show wear, weathering, and damage in different ways.

**H.2.9** You should note missing, fractured, displaced, curled, worn, or otherwise compromised roof surfacing. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because visible condition may affect whether claimed damage resulted from a covered cause of loss.

**H.2.10** You should document evidence of patching, repairs, overlays, or mismatched materials. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because prior work may affect both the damage assessment and the scope of reasonable repair.

**H.2.11** You should identify signs of long-term moisture exposure, rot, corrosion, algae growth, or biological deterioration where visible. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because gradual deterioration must not be treated as direct physical loss from a sudden covered event.

**H.2.12** You should separate weather-related damage from ordinary wear, maintenance concerns, and construction defects in your estimate notes. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because only covered direct physical loss belongs in the covered repair scope.

**H.2.13** You may consult a qualified roofing professional when the cause, extent, or condition of the damage cannot be determined from the available evidence. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because specialized observations may be needed to support a sound coverage decision.

**H.2.14** You should ask the roofing professional to identify observed conditions rather than to make coverage conclusions. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because coverage determinations remain the insurer’s responsibility under the Roof Condition provision.

**H.2.15** You should preserve photographs and reports that show both damaged and undamaged roof areas. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because comparison of conditions helps establish the extent and likely cause of the claimed damage.

**H.2.16** You should evaluate whether observed conditions are localized or broadly present across the roof. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the distribution of conditions may help distinguish event damage from aging or deterioration.

**H.2.17** You should describe the condition of flashings, vents, skylight surrounds, valleys, ridges, and roof transitions when they relate to the reported loss. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because failure at these locations may have a cause different from damage to the roof surfacing.

**H.2.18** You should inspect accessible interior areas for staining, moisture, or other evidence that may corroborate the reported roof damage. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because interior evidence may assist in determining whether water entered through a covered opening.

**H.2.19** You should not assume that interior staining establishes the timing or cause of roof damage. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because staining may result from prior leakage, condensation, maintenance issues, or unrelated causes.

**H.2.20** You should note whether temporary repairs were made before inspection and obtain available photographs of the condition before those repairs. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because temporary measures can obscure evidence needed to evaluate the claimed damage.

**H.2.21** You should evaluate the roof surfacing age threshold when determining the applicable settlement treatment. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the applicable roof settlement terms may depend on the roof surfacing condition and age determination.

**H.2.22** You should apply the roof payment floor when the roof settlement terms call for that treatment. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the claim payment must follow the applicable roof loss settlement terms.

**H.2.23** You should evaluate the insured-to-value threshold for replacement cost settlement when replacement cost treatment is requested. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because eligibility for replacement cost settlement must be supported by the applicable valuation requirements.

**H.2.24** You should explain the settlement basis in plain language when communicating the coverage decision to the insured. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because a clear explanation reduces misunderstanding about age, condition, and covered damage.

**H.2.25** You should identify the observed roof condition without characterizing normal weathering as damage unless the evidence supports damage from a covered cause of loss. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because ordinary aging is not established as covered damage merely by its appearance.

**H.2.26** You should not deny a claim solely because a roof is aged or shows maintenance concerns. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because you must still evaluate whether a covered cause of loss produced separate direct physical damage.

**H.2.27** You should not include undamaged roof areas in the repair scope unless the applicable settlement terms support their inclusion. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the estimate should reflect covered direct physical loss rather than unrelated improvement.

**H.2.28** You should identify any code-related requirements asserted by a contractor and obtain support for their application to the covered repair. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because code-related work must be evaluated under the applicable coverage terms and supported by the repair scope.

**H.2.29** You should distinguish between repair needed to address covered damage and work recommended to improve the roof’s overall condition. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because betterment and maintenance work may not be part of the covered loss.

**H.2.30** You should review contractor estimates for assumptions about material availability, matching, tear-off, and replacement scope. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because estimate assumptions can expand beyond the damage supported by the inspection evidence.

**H.2.31** You should request clarification when a contractor estimate describes broad replacement but does not identify corresponding covered damage. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the estimate must connect proposed work to the claimed direct physical loss.

**H.2.32** You should document any material-matching concern raised by the insured or contractor. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because matching issues require a fact-based evaluation of the available materials and the covered repair area.

**H.2.33** You should retain samples, photographs, and supplier information when material availability is relevant to the repair decision. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because contemporaneous evidence supports the basis for the selected repair method.

**H.2.34** You should identify whether damage was present before the reported event when prior inspections, photographs, or claim history provide relevant evidence. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because preexisting damage must be separated from loss caused by the reported event.

**H.2.35** You should consider prior repairs or prior claims only as evidence of roof condition and damage history. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because prior activity does not replace an independent evaluation of the current reported loss.

**H.2.36** You should document weather information when it is reasonably available and relevant to the reported cause of loss. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because weather information may corroborate or call into question the claimed event.

**H.2.37** You should avoid treating weather information as conclusive when physical evidence indicates a different cause or timing of damage. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because weather occurrence alone does not establish covered direct physical loss.

**H.2.38** You should explain any depreciation or condition-based settlement adjustment in the claim notes. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the file must show how roof age and condition affected the settlement calculation.

**H.2.39** You should ensure that the estimate description matches the coverage decision and the documented roof condition. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because inconsistent file documentation can obscure the basis for payment or denial.

**H.2.40** You should obtain supervisory review when roof age, condition, causation, or settlement treatment remains unclear after reasonable investigation. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because complex roof claims require consistent application of the applicable settlement terms.

**H.2.41** You should keep claim communications factual and avoid suggesting that an inspection conclusion is a finding of fraud or neglect. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because the claim decision must be based on supported coverage facts and respectful communication.

**H.2.42** You should treat this guidance as internal claim-handling direction rather than as part of any policy contract. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because coverage is determined by the applicable policy terms and the facts of the reported loss.

## H.3 — Wind and Hail

**H.3.1** You may apply wind and hail claim handling procedures only after confirming that direct physical loss is alleged. This rule rests on the Windstorm or Hail Cause of Loss provision because coverage depends on a covered cause producing covered damage.

**H.3.2** You should obtain prompt photographs, weather information, and insured statements describing the reported event. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because timely evidence helps distinguish recent storm damage from prior conditions.

**H.3.3** You may inspect the roof and related exterior components for signs of wind uplift, hail impact, displaced materials, and resulting openings. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the scope of covered repair depends on the actual storm-caused damage.

**H.3.4** You must obtain a wind mitigation inspection where Coverage A exceeds $1,000,000. This rule rests on the Wind Mitigation Inspection requirement because the inspection may affect risk evaluation and claim documentation.

**H.3.5** You must identify the applicable wind or hail deductible before calculating the payable loss. This rule rests on the Windstorm or Hail Deductible provision because the deductible may differ from the deductible otherwise applicable to the claim.

**H.3.6** You may apply a named storm deductible only when the reported loss falls within the applicable named storm designation. This rule rests on the Named Storm Deductible provision because the special deductible depends on the stated storm trigger.

**H.3.7** You should retain reliable weather records supporting the named storm status of the reported event. This rule rests on the Named Storm Deductible provision because the claim file must show why the applicable deductible was selected.

**H.3.8** You must use the applicable claim handling setting for hours after a named storm designation ends that the named storm period lasts. This rule rests on the Named Storm Deductible provision because designation status may continue to control deductible application after the official designation changes.

**H.3.9** You must validate the named storm deductible minimum before applying the deductible to a covered loss. This rule rests on the Named Storm Deductible provision because the deductible must be administered consistently with its stated limitation.

**H.3.10** You must not apply a wind deductible that exceeds the wind deductible ceiling. This rule rests on the Windstorm or Hail Deductible provision because deductible administration must remain within the applicable policy limitation.

**H.3.11** You should address any renewal deductible notice inquiry using the claim file and available underwriting information. This rule rests on the Deductible Change Notice provision because the insured may need an explanation of the deductible shown for the loss.

**H.3.12** You must document the deductible calculation in the claim file, including the basis for the selected deductible. This rule rests on the Deductible provision because the payment record must support the amount withheld from covered damages.

**H.3.13** You should evaluate whether reported damage arose from a single weather event or from separate causes. This rule rests on the Occurrence provision because deductible application and loss adjustment may depend on the cause of damage.

**H.3.14** You must separate wind or hail damage from flood, surface water, and other excluded water damage. This rule rests on the Water Damage Exclusion because covered roof damage does not convert excluded water causes into covered loss.

**H.3.15** You may consider interior water damage when wind or hail created an opening that allowed rain to enter. This rule rests on the Windstorm or Hail Cause of Loss provision because resulting interior damage requires a covered pathway for water entry.

**H.3.16** You should identify the location and condition of every claimed roof opening. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because the insured must provide information needed to determine whether the opening resulted from the reported event.

**H.3.17** You must not treat ordinary wear, aging, or deterioration as storm damage merely because a storm occurred near the reported date. This rule rests on the Wear and Tear Exclusion because gradual conditions are not covered accidental direct physical loss.

**H.3.18** You should distinguish hail impacts from blistering, granule loss, manufacturing defects, and mechanical damage. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because payment must be based on damage caused by the covered peril.

**H.3.19** You must evaluate whether inadequate maintenance contributed to the reported condition. This rule rests on the Maintenance Exclusion because neglected conditions may affect the scope of covered damage.

**H.3.20** You may pay for repairs that restore damaged property to its condition immediately before the covered loss, subject to applicable terms. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the policy provides repair or replacement treatment for covered direct physical loss.

**H.3.21** You should consider reasonable repair feasibility before approving replacement of an entire roof covering. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because replacement is not automatically owed when covered damage can be repaired.

**H.3.22** You must document the condition of undamaged roof areas when matching is asserted. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the claim decision must distinguish covered physical damage from aesthetic preference.

**H.3.23** You may seek a contractor estimate when the scope, method, or cost of repair is unclear. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because reliable repair information supports an accurate evaluation.

**H.3.24** You should compare contractor estimates with inspection findings before adopting any repair scope. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because estimates may include work unrelated to covered storm damage.

**H.3.25** You must apply actual cash value treatment when required by the applicable loss settlement terms. This rule rests on the Actual Cash Value provision because depreciation may affect the amount presently payable.

**H.3.26** You may consider replacement cost benefits only after confirming that the applicable loss settlement conditions are met. This rule rests on the Replacement Cost provision because additional payment depends on compliance with the stated conditions.

**H.3.27** You should review claimed code-related work separately from direct storm repairs. This rule rests on the Ordinance or Law provision because code upgrades may require separate coverage consideration.

**H.3.28** You may allow reasonable emergency measures that protect covered property from further damage. This rule rests on the Reasonable Repairs provision because prompt protective action can reduce the severity of the loss.

**H.3.29** You must distinguish temporary protection costs from permanent roof repair costs. This rule rests on the Reasonable Repairs provision because emergency measures and final restoration may be evaluated under different policy terms.

**H.3.30** You should obtain invoices, receipts, and photographs for claimed emergency services when available. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because the claim file must support the necessity and scope of the expense.

**H.3.31** You must preserve photographs showing alleged hail impacts before destructive testing or roof removal occurs. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because physical evidence may be needed to confirm the cause and extent of damage.

**H.3.32** You may retain an appropriate expert when the cause, scope, or timing of roof damage cannot be resolved from the available evidence. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because a qualified assessment may be necessary for an accurate coverage decision.

**H.3.33** You should evaluate possible recovery from a contractor, manufacturer, or other responsible party when evidence supports that possibility. This rule rests on the Transfer of Rights of Recovery provision because payment of a covered loss may preserve the insurer’s recovery rights.

**H.3.34** You must explain the coverage decision in clear claim correspondence, including the basis for any deductible, limitation, or exclusion applied. This rule rests on the Loss Payment provision because the insured needs a clear explanation of the adjusted loss.

**H.3.35** You should maintain a complete claim file showing weather evidence, inspection findings, estimates, communications, and the final payment analysis. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss and Loss Settlement provisions because the file must support the handling decision.

## H.4 — Water Backup

**H.4.1** You may treat water backup as a covered cause of loss only when the applicable Water Backup Endorsement is shown as part of the coverage. This approach follows the Water Backup Endorsement because the base policy water damage exclusion does not grant backup coverage by itself.

**H.4.2** You should obtain notice of a suspected water backup loss within 30 days of the reported event. The Duties After Loss condition supports prompt reporting because early inspection helps preserve evidence of the water source and resulting damage.

**H.4.3** You should identify whether water entered through a drain, sewer, sump, or similar system before assigning the loss to water backup coverage. The Water Backup Endorsement controls because coverage depends on the source and path of the water.

**H.4.4** You should distinguish water that backed up from a covered system from water that entered directly through an opening in the roof. The Water Damage exclusion and the Water Backup Endorsement require this distinction because separate coverage provisions may apply to each source.

**H.4.5** You may request photographs showing the affected roof area, interior staining, drains, downspouts, and any visible overflow. The Duties After Loss condition permits reasonable investigation because the physical evidence may establish whether backup caused the claimed damage.

**H.4.6** You should document the insured’s description of the event using the insured’s own words where possible. The Duties After Loss condition supports a recorded loss history because later accounts may need to be compared with inspection findings.

**H.4.7** You should confirm whether the reported water originated from a plumbing system, drainage system, roof drain, sump system, or public sewer. The Water Backup Endorsement applies only to the sources described in its coverage grant.

**H.4.8** You should not classify rainwater collecting on a roof as water backup without evidence that a covered system overflowed or discharged improperly. The Water Backup Endorsement requires a qualifying backup event rather than water accumulation alone.

**H.4.9** You should evaluate whether a blocked roof drain caused water to pond and enter the building through a roof opening or failed roof component. The Water Damage exclusion is relevant because direct rain entry may be subject to different coverage analysis than backup.

**H.4.10** You may inspect the roof drainage layout, including drains, scuppers, gutters, downspouts, and discharge points. The Duties After Loss condition supports this inspection because the drainage configuration can identify the point of failure.

**H.4.11** You should obtain available maintenance records when blockage, deterioration, or deferred cleaning is alleged. The policy Conditions and exclusions are relevant because maintenance information may affect the cause-of-loss determination.

**H.4.12** You should separate damage caused by the backup event from damage caused by wear, corrosion, deterioration, or prior leakage. The policy exclusions require separation because excluded conditions do not become covered merely because water damage is also present.

**H.4.13** You may consider invoices, service reports, and emergency mitigation records as evidence of the source and timing of the loss. The Duties After Loss condition supports review of these records because they may show what was observed before repairs altered the condition.

**H.4.14** You should preserve the failed drain component, pump component, or blockage material when practical and when ownership permits. The Duties After Loss condition supports preservation because physical evidence may establish whether the claimed event was a backup or a maintenance issue.

**H.4.15** You should not approve replacement of an entire roof solely because water backup caused localized interior damage. The Loss Settlement provision governs because payment is limited to covered direct physical loss and necessary related repair.

**H.4.16** You may allow reasonable emergency measures to stop active water intrusion, protect property, and reduce additional damage. The Duties After Loss condition requires reasonable protection of property because avoidable subsequent damage may not be attributable to the reported event.

**H.4.18** You should apply the drain backup sublimit to covered loss that falls within the Water Backup Endorsement. The Water Backup Endorsement controls this handling because it may limit payment for the covered water backup loss.

**H.5.15** Refer conflicting evidence about the age or cause of roof damage for technical review. This rule rests on the direct physical loss requirement because causation must be supported before coverage is extended.

**H.5.16** Consider prior inspections from underwriting, maintenance vendors, contractors, and lenders when they are available in the claim file. **H.5.21** Request clarification from the insured when prior-loss information is incomplete, unclear, or inconsistent with inspection findings. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because the insured must cooperate with reasonable investigation requests.

**H.5.22** Explain the information needed without suggesting that the insured must prove coverage beyond the policy requirements. This rule rests on the Claims Handling provision because communications must be fair, accurate, and reasonably clear.

**H.5.23** Avoid directing the insured to obtain unnecessary expert opinions about prior damage. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because requests should be reasonably related to the investigation.

**H.5.24** You may use a qualified field adjuster, engineer, roofing consultant, or other appropriate expert when available evidence does not reliably separate prior damage from current damage. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because a supported scope is necessary to value covered repair or replacement.

**H.5.25** Document the reason for any expert referral and the specific questions the referral is intended to answer. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because the claim record should show why further investigation was necessary.

**H.5.26** Do not allow a contractor’s statement alone to establish that all observed roof damage resulted from the reported event. This rule rests on the direct physical loss requirement because causation must be supported by the full claim investigation.

**H.5.27** Evaluate contractor evidence alongside inspection findings, weather information, prior records, and the insured’s statements. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because coverage decisions require a balanced review of relevant evidence.

**H.5.28** Refer any roof loss with an incurred amount above $10,000 before authorizing final payment. This rule rests on the Loss Payment provision because elevated claim exposure requires appropriate claim oversight.

**H.5.29** Include the current estimate, photographs, prior-loss records, coverage analysis, and recommended action with the referral. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because the reviewer needs a complete factual record to assess the claim.

**H.5.30** State clearly in the referral whether prior damage appears repaired, unrepaired, previously paid, or unresolved. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the distinction may affect the covered scope and valuation.

**H.5.31** Refer claims involving suspected duplicate recovery even when the current damage estimate appears otherwise supportable. This rule rests on the Concealment or Fraud provision because duplicate recovery concerns require review before payment is issued.

**H.5.32** Refer claims where prior-loss information suggests a potential contribution, subrogation, or recovery issue. This rule rests on the Transfer of Rights of Recovery provision because responsible parties and recovery rights should be preserved.

**H.5.33** Do not waive available recovery rights through communications, settlement language, or disposal of relevant evidence. This rule rests on the Transfer of Rights of Recovery provision because the carrier’s recovery interest may depend on preserving the claim evidence.

**H.5.34** Refer claims involving prior repairs that may have failed because of workmanship, material defects, or contractor actions. This rule rests on the policy exclusions because the cause of loss may involve conditions not covered by the policy.

**H.5.35** When prior repairs are incomplete, identify the incomplete work separately from any current covered repair work. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the estimate must not include non-covered work as part of the current loss.

**H.5.36** Explain any prior-loss limitation in plain language when discussing the claim with the insured. This rule rests on the Claims Handling provision because the insured should understand the basis for the claim decision.

**H.5.37** Do not tell the insured that prior damage automatically eliminates coverage for later covered damage. This rule rests on the direct physical loss requirement because new covered damage may still be payable when it can be separately identified.

**H.5.38** Issue a written coverage explanation when prior loss conditions affect payment, scope, depreciation, or denial. This rule rests on the Claims Handling provision because the file and the insured require a clear statement of the decision basis.

**H.5.39** Quote or accurately summarize the applicable policy language in the coverage explanation without overstating exclusions or limitations. This rule rests on the Claims Handling provision because communications must accurately reflect the policy terms.

**H.5.40** Maintain respectful communications when prior-loss questions create concern or disagreement. This rule rests on the Claims Handling provision because a thorough investigation should remain fair and professional.

**H.5.41** Update claim notes as new prior-loss information is received, reviewed, or disputed. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because the claim record must show the progression of the investigation.

**H.5.42** Obtain referral approval or direction before resolving a referred loss when the facts materially change. This rule rests on the Loss Payment provision because the final payment decision must reflect the complete and current claim record.

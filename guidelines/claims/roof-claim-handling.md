# Roof Claim Handling Guidance

Internal claims guidance for homeowners roof losses. Not part of any policy and not to be quoted to an insured or a claimant.

Last revised 2026-03-20 by Claims Technical Services.

## K.1 Establish the Peril

Roof settlement depends on the peril, not only on the damage. HO 23 74 R.2 applies the actual cash value schedule only to loss caused by windstorm or hail. Loss to roof surfacing from any other covered peril — fire, falling object, aircraft, vehicle — settles at replacement cost under HO-3 Section I A.3, and the roof schedule endorsement does not apply.

Document the peril determination before pricing the loss. Reversing a settlement basis after payment is a complaint driver and, in some states, a market conduct finding.

## K.2 Establish the Age

HO 23 74 R.5 determines roof age from the documented date of original installation or of the most recent full replacement, whichever is later. Where the insured cannot document that date, the age is presumed to be the age of the dwelling.

That presumption is rebuttable in practice and frequently disputed. Where the insured produces a permit, an invoice, or a prior inspection establishing a later date, accept it and note the source in the file. Where the insured produces nothing, state the presumption and its basis in the denial or reduction letter explicitly.

Partial repair does not reset the age under R.5. Replacement of a full slope resets the age for that slope only, which means a single roof can carry two ages. Price each slope separately where this occurs.

## K.3 Apply the Schedule

Where HO 23 74 is attached and the peril is windstorm or hail, apply the depreciation table in R.3 by material and age. Composition shingle depreciates fastest — twenty-five percent payable at twenty years — while architectural shingle, metal, and tile retain forty percent at thirty years and older.

Confirm the material from inspection rather than from the application. Material misclassification is the most common error in this category and always favors the insured on appeal.

Apply the minimum payment floor in R.4 before the deductible. In no event is the payable amount less than twenty-five percent of the replacement cost of the surfacing.

## K.4 Scope Is Surfacing Only

HO 23 74 R.1 limits the endorsement to roof surfacing, defined as shingles, tiles, shakes, metal panels, membrane, and the underlayment and flashing directly beneath them. Decking, trusses, rafters, sheathing, and interior finish are not roof surfacing and settle at replacement cost under HO-3 Section I A.3.

Separate these line items in the estimate. A single depreciated figure covering both surfacing and decking will not survive review.

## K.5 Ordinance or Law

Where code requires replacement of undamaged surfacing in order to repair the damaged portion, HO 23 74 R.6 excludes that cost from the roof endorsement and routes it to HO-3 Section I D.1, which excludes it entirely unless HO 04 16 is attached.

Where HO 04 16 is attached, that cost is covered under O.3 subject to the ten percent of Coverage A limit in O.2. Check for the endorsement before denying a code upgrade — this is a frequent miss.

## K.6 Deductibles

Where a windstorm or hail loss occurs in a state with a separate windstorm and hail deductible, apply that deductible and not the all-other-perils deductible. In Texas this is governed by HO 01 45 T.1, which amends HO-3 Section I S.5 so that only the windstorm and hail deductible is applied.

In Florida, a hurricane deductible and a separate roof deductible may not both be applied to the same loss; where both would apply, only the larger is deducted, per Florida bulletin OIR-2023-04 F.6.

## K.7 Referral Triggers

Refer where the roof age is disputed and the outcome would move the payable amount by more than ten thousand dollars, where the insured has retained a public adjuster or counsel, where more than one slope age applies, or where a code upgrade is claimed and no ordinance or law endorsement is attached.

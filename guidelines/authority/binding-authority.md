# Binding Authority and Exceptions







## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** This guidance states the internal purpose of Binding Authority and Exceptions. You use it to apply underwriting judgment within the authority granted to the company.

**H.0.2** This guidance is not part of any policy contract. It supports consistent decisions because policy obligations arise from the applicable policy language, not from internal direction.

**H.0.3** You may bind business only when the risk fits the authority available to you. This preserves disciplined selection because authority is intended to match the risk characteristics you are authorized to evaluate.

**H.0.4** You must refer a risk when material facts fall outside the expected underwriting profile. Referral protects the company because exceptions may require broader review, pricing judgment, or risk-control direction.

**H.0.5** You should obtain clear and complete applicant information before making a binding decision. Complete information supports sound authority use because undisclosed conditions can alter eligibility and loss potential.

**H.0.6** You may not treat this guidance as permission to disregard policy language or established underwriting requirements. Those requirements control because internal authority cannot expand contractual coverage or alter approved terms.

**H.0.7** You should consider the special limit on theft of jewelry, watches and precious stones when reviewing personal-property exposures. This review matters because the applicant’s expectations may differ from the coverage available under the policy language.

**H.0.8** You should confirm the all-other-perils deductible minimum before offering terms. This confirmation is necessary because deductible selection affects the level of risk retained by the insured and the company.

**H.0.9** You should address notice of cancellation for other reasons when eligibility concerns may affect continuation of coverage. Clear handling is important because cancellation activity must follow the applicable policy language and company process.

**H.0.10** You should evaluate ACV roof age when roof condition or settlement basis affects the risk. This evaluation supports appropriate underwriting because roof characteristics may influence expected loss severity.

**H.0.11** You should verify maximum protection class written before binding a location with fire-protection concerns. This verification matters because protection characteristics can materially affect property-loss exposure.

**H.0.12** You must document the facts supporting any exception decision. Documentation is required because the underwriting file must show why the risk was accepted, referred, modified, or declined.

**H.0.13** You should state the exception clearly enough for another reviewer to understand the risk concern. Clear records promote consistent handling because later actions may depend on the same underwriting facts.

**H.0.14** You may request additional underwriting information when available facts do not support a reliable decision. Additional review is appropriate because assumptions can result in terms that do not reflect the actual exposure.

**H.0.15** You should not bind based on an expectation that missing information will later confirm eligibility. This practice is prohibited because authority decisions must rest on facts available at the time of binding.

**H.0.16** You must refer conditions that require approval beyond your authority. Referral is necessary because only the authorized reviewer may accept responsibility for an exception.

**H.0.17** You should communicate any binding conditions in a manner that can be verified in the underwriting file. Verifiable communication is important because conditions must be understood and satisfied before coverage is finalized.

**H.0.18** You may withdraw a tentative indication when later information changes the underwriting assessment. This protects the company because an indication does not override eligibility requirements or authority limits.

**H.0.19** You should apply this guidance consistently to comparable risks. Consistent application is important because similar underwriting facts should receive similar treatment unless a documented distinction supports a different result.

**H.0.20** You must avoid making representations that conflict with policy language or approved underwriting terms. This restriction applies because informal statements cannot create coverage or change the company’s obligations.

**H.0.21** You should seek underwriting support when the practical effect of an exception is unclear. Consultation is appropriate because uncertain authority application can create avoidable coverage, pricing, or compliance concerns.

**H.0.22** You should use this guidance as an operational aid rather than as a substitute for careful underwriting judgment. Its purpose is to support accountable decisions because each binding action remains subject to the authority available to you.

## H.1 — In Appetite

**H.1.3** You must confirm the legal name of the insured before binding. This prevents coverage from being issued to an unintended party and applies the Named Insured provision.

**H.1.4** You may bind only for a risk located within the approved coverage territory. This keeps the risk within the jurisdiction contemplated by the coverage and applies the Coverage Territory provision.

**H.1.5** You must confirm that the insured has an insurable interest in the property or operations presented. This avoids issuing coverage where no covered financial interest exists and applies the Insurable Interest provision.

**H.1.6** You may bind only when the risk classification matches an approved class. This supports accurate underwriting treatment and applies the Classification provision.

**H.1.7** You must obtain a clear description of the insured’s operations before binding. This helps identify whether the operations create an excluded or unapproved exposure and applies the Operations provision.

**H.1.8** You may bind when the disclosed operations do not include prohibited activities. This prevents acceptance of hazards outside appetite and applies the Exclusions provision.

**H.1.9** You must confirm the occupancy or operational use of the premises before binding. This helps ensure that the rating and eligibility assumptions match the actual exposure and applies the Occupancy provision.

**H.1.10** You may bind only when the property is used for a lawful and disclosed purpose. This reduces the risk of an undisclosed use changing the nature of the exposure and applies the Use provision.

**H.1.11** You must confirm that the property appears to be in acceptable physical condition. This helps avoid binding a risk with apparent maintenance concerns that may affect loss potential and applies the Property Condition provision.

**H.1.12** You may bind when required protective features are present and operational. This supports the risk characteristics assumed by the coverage and applies the Protective Safeguards condition.

**H.1.13** You must confirm that essential utilities are safely maintained where the risk requires them. This helps identify conditions that may create avoidable property or liability exposure and applies the Property Conditions provision.

**H.1.14** You may bind only after reviewing disclosed prior losses. This helps determine whether loss experience remains consistent with appetite and applies the Loss History provision.

**H.1.15** You must obtain disclosure of known incidents, circumstances, or claims that may affect the risk. This helps prevent binding without awareness of a developing exposure and applies the Known Loss provision.

**H.1.16** You may bind when there is no undisclosed cancellation, nonrenewal, or adverse underwriting action affecting the risk. This helps identify concerns that may signal an unapproved exposure and applies the Prior Coverage provision.

**H.1.17** You must not bind when information supplied by or for the insured appears materially inaccurate or incomplete. This protects the integrity of the underwriting decision and applies the Concealment, Misrepresentation or Fraud condition.

**H.1.18** You may rely on information supplied by the insured when it is reasonably supported by the submission. This permits efficient handling while preserving the need for credible underwriting information and applies the Representations provision.

**H.1.19** You must obtain all required acknowledgments before binding. This confirms that material information has been presented for underwriting consideration and applies the Representations provision.

**H.1.20** You may bind only when the requested premium basis is available for the risk. This helps ensure that the coverage can be issued using an approved rating approach and applies the Premium provision.

**H.1.21** You may bind only when the requested limits fall within your delegated authority. This keeps the insurer’s retained exposure within approved underwriting parameters and applies the Limits of Insurance provision.

**H.1.22** You may bind only when the requested deductible is available for the class of business. This ensures that the insured retains the portion of loss contemplated by the rating and applies the Deductible provision.

**H.1.23** You may bind approved optional coverages when they are compatible with the base coverage. This avoids creating inconsistent coverage intent and applies the Optional Coverages provision.

**H.1.24** You must not add an unapproved coverage grant, exception, or special condition. This prevents changes that could expand coverage beyond delegated authority and applies the Endorsements provision.

**H.1.25** You may use approved endorsements that match the disclosed exposure. This allows the coverage to address anticipated conditions without altering the underlying coverage intent and applies the Endorsements provision.

**H.1.26** You must not revise required wording without underwriting approval. This preserves consistent contract terms and applies the Changes provision.

**H.1.27** You may bind only through a producer with appropriate authority for the transaction. This helps confirm that the placement is made through an authorized distribution channel and applies the Producer Authority provision.

**H.1.28** You must confirm that the transaction is handled in a jurisdiction where binding authority is available. This avoids issuing coverage where the placement cannot be properly supported and applies the Jurisdiction provision.

**H.1.29** You must provide required notices and disclosures at binding when they apply to the risk. This helps ensure that the insured receives material information associated with the coverage and applies the Notice condition.

**H.1.30** You must retain underwriting information that supports the binding decision. This creates a clear record of the basis for acceptance and applies the Records provision.

**H.1.31** You may bind coverage to begin only when the requested inception is acceptable under your authority. This prevents coverage from taking effect outside approved binding parameters and applies the Inception provision.

**H.1.32** You must not backdate coverage to address a known loss or known circumstance. This avoids creating coverage after the risk has already changed and applies the Known Loss provision.

**H.1.33** You may bind when premium payment arrangements are available for the requested coverage. This helps ensure that the issued coverage aligns with approved payment practices and applies the Payment provision.

**H.1.34** You must confirm that any required deposit or payment condition has been satisfied before binding. This supports proper issuance of the coverage and applies the Payment condition.

**H.1.35** You may bind subject to an inspection requirement when the risk otherwise fits appetite. This allows underwriting to verify conditions that cannot be reasonably confirmed from the submission and applies the Inspection condition.

**H.1.36** You must identify inspection concerns that could materially affect eligibility before binding. This helps prevent acceptance of a risk with apparent conditions outside appetite and applies the Inspection condition.

**H.1.37** You may bind only when the stated property value has reasonable support. This helps reduce the risk of material underinsurance or overinsurance and applies the Valuation provision.

**H.1.38** You must confirm ownership, tenancy, or management responsibility when it affects the requested coverage. This helps match the coverage to the party bearing the exposure and applies the Ownership provision.

**H.1.39** You may bind a vacant or unoccupied risk only when that condition is eligible. This is necessary because reduced occupancy may materially change loss potential and applies the Vacancy condition.

**H.1.40** You must confirm the construction characteristics of property risks where construction affects eligibility. This helps identify hazards that require different underwriting treatment and applies the Construction provision.

**H.1.41** You may bind only when catastrophe exposure remains within approved appetite. This helps manage concentration of similar risks that could be affected by the same event and applies the Catastrophe Accumulation provision.

**H.1.42** You must consider surrounding exposures when the risk may be affected by neighboring property or operations. This helps identify external hazards that may alter the expected loss potential and applies the Adjacent Exposure provision.

**H.1.43** You may bind only when the risk does not require unapproved reinsurance support. This keeps the acceptance decision within the insurer’s available risk-transfer arrangements and applies the Reinsurance provision.

**H.1.44** You must refer any material deviation from approved appetite before binding. This ensures that exceptions receive authority appropriate to the changed exposure and applies the Referral provision.

**H.1.45** You must issue binding confirmation that accurately reflects the coverage authorized. This helps avoid misunderstandings about the scope of coverage placed and applies the Binder provision.

**H.1.46** You must report material changes discovered after binding through the applicable underwriting process. This allows the insurer to evaluate whether the risk remains acceptable and applies the Change in Risk condition.

**H.1.47** You must treat this guidance as an underwriting instruction rather than a coverage term. This preserves the controlling effect of the applicable coverage provisions and applies the Policy Terms provision.

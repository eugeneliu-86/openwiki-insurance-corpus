# Underwriting Referral and Authority Matrix







## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** You must treat this Matrix as internal underwriting and claims guidance rather than as an insurance contract. This distinction prevents an operational instruction from being represented as a coverage promise and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.2** You may use this Matrix to identify when an account, transaction, or claim decision requires referral. This promotes consistent escalation of matters that exceed ordinary handling judgment and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.3** You must not use this Matrix to create, expand, restrict, or waive any coverage obligation. This preserves the controlling effect of the applicable contract terms and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.4** You may bind, decline, endorse, settle, or otherwise act only within the authority assigned to your role. This prevents an unauthorized action from creating an avoidable operational or customer dispute and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.5** You must refer a matter when the facts do not support a confident authority determination. This ensures that uncertainty is resolved by the appropriate decision maker and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.6** You must not describe an internal referral outcome as a guarantee of coverage, payment, renewal, or claim resolution. This avoids creating expectations that may conflict with the controlling terms and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.7** You should obtain sufficient facts before seeking approval or making a decision within your authority. This allows the referral recipient or decision maker to evaluate the matter on a complete and reliable record and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.8** You should clearly document the facts, requested action, and reason for referral in the file. This creates an auditable explanation for the decision path and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.9** You must raise material inconsistencies in the available information rather than selecting the interpretation that supports the preferred outcome. This protects the integrity of underwriting and claim decisions and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.10** You may seek clarification from the appropriate business function when a matter involves both underwriting and claims considerations. This helps ensure that the final action reflects the relevant expertise and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.11** You must treat a referral as a request for direction, not as a transfer of your responsibility to maintain an accurate file. This preserves accountability for the information supplied and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.12** You should refer questions involving the jewelry theft sublimit when the available facts do not permit a confident application of the controlling terms. This helps avoid an incorrect representation of the available coverage and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.13** You should refer issues concerning the coinsurance threshold when valuation information is incomplete, inconsistent, or disputed. This supports a careful assessment of the applicable handling position and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.14** You must refer actions involving the general cancellation notice rather than treating this Matrix as notice language. This avoids using internal guidance in place of controlling notice requirements and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.15** You should refer files where the roof age from which the actual cash value roof schedule applies is material to the proposed action. This ensures that the relevant facts receive appropriate review and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.16** You must not infer approval from silence, delay, informal discussion, or an incomplete response. This prevents an unconfirmed understanding from being treated as an authorized decision and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.17** You should obtain express direction when a proposed action could materially affect acceptance, pricing, terms, handling, or settlement. This ensures that significant decisions receive the review contemplated by the authority framework and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.18** You must preserve all material communications and supporting information in the file when a referral is made. This enables later review of the facts considered and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.19** You may ask for supervisory guidance when the assigned authority is unclear or competing considerations cannot be reconciled. This supports prompt resolution without exceeding your role and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.20** You must apply this Matrix consistently while recognizing that unusual facts may require referral. This balances efficient routine handling with appropriate review of exceptions and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.21** You should communicate a referral need promptly and describe the decision that requires approval. This allows the appropriate reviewer to provide usable direction and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

**H.0.22** You must not treat this Matrix as a substitute for sound professional judgment. This preserves the need to escalate matters whose facts or consequences warrant additional review and rests on the Purpose and Status provision.

## H.1 — In Appetite

**H.1.1** You may bind a submission when the disclosed operations fit the intended class of business and present no material hazard outside ordinary underwriting expectations.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because clear operational fit allows consistent risk selection.

**H.1.2** You may bind when the named insured’s business purpose is plainly described and aligns with the requested coverage.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because an identifiable business purpose permits meaningful evaluation of the exposure.

**H.1.3** You may bind when the applicant provides complete ownership information and no ownership concern affects control of the insured operations.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because responsible ownership is necessary to assess management quality.

**H.1.4** You may bind when the insured location is actively used for the operations disclosed in the submission.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because actual use determines the hazards presented to the carrier.

**H.1.5** You may bind when the premises appear maintained and free of visible conditions that suggest deferred repairs or unsafe occupancy.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because routine maintenance reduces the likelihood of preventable loss.

**H.1.6** You may bind when building condition, occupancy, and use are consistent with the risk characteristics described by the applicant.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because consistent information supports reliable underwriting judgment.

**H.1.7** You may bind when access to the premises is suitable for ordinary business activity and emergency response.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because practical access can reduce property damage and liability severity.

**H.1.8** You may bind when the applicant identifies any neighboring activity that could create smoke, fire, chemical, traffic, or similar exposure.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because known surrounding hazards must be considered in selecting an acceptable risk.

**H.1.9** You may bind when the premises do not rely on an unaddressed condition that could impair fire protection or emergency response.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because dependable protective conditions are fundamental to property acceptability.

**H.1.10** You may bind when safeguards represented by the applicant appear appropriate for the operations and premises.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because represented safeguards form part of the risk basis used for approval.

**H.1.11** You may bind when the applicant’s housekeeping practices appear suitable for the type of property and operations involved.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because orderly premises management helps control common fire and liability hazards.

**H.1.12** You may bind when storage practices do not create an apparent accumulation, obstruction, ignition, or access concern.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because safe storage reduces avoidable damage and injury exposures.

**H.1.13** You may bind when the applicant’s equipment use is customary for the disclosed operations and does not introduce an unusual hazard.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because customary equipment hazards can be evaluated within delegated authority.

**H.1.14** You may bind when any machinery or operational process is described clearly enough to determine its effect on the risk.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because incomplete process information can conceal a material exposure.

**H.1.15** You may bind when the insured does not conduct hazardous manufacturing, processing, or handling outside the stated appetite.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because specialized hazards require review beyond ordinary binding authority.

**H.1.16** You may bind when hazardous materials, if present, are incidental to the disclosed operations and are managed appropriately.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because material handling can create environmental, fire, and injury exposures.

**H.1.17** You may bind when the applicant does not perform work that creates a substantial uncontrolled injury or property damage exposure.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because uncontrolled work activities can exceed the intended risk profile.

**H.1.18** You may bind when the applicant’s services are provided within the ordinary scope of its stated operations.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because service activities outside the stated scope may alter the liability exposure.

**H.1.19** You may bind when subcontracted activity, if used, is consistent with the applicant’s operations and is subject to reasonable oversight.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because unmanaged subcontracting can transfer unknown loss potential to the insured.

**H.1.20** You may bind when the applicant maintains reasonable control over employees, visitors, customers, and other persons at the premises.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because premises control is a key factor in evaluating liability risk.

**H.1.21** You may bind when public access is ordinary for the disclosed business and does not create a heightened crowd, security, or injury concern.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because unusual public access can materially change expected liability exposure.

**H.1.22** You may bind when the applicant’s operations do not involve a known activity that is incompatible with the requested coverage.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because incompatible activities may require specialized terms or authority.

**H.1.23** You may bind when the risk does not depend on an exception, accommodation, or underwriting assumption that has not been documented.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because undocumented assumptions can undermine consistent risk selection.

**H.1.24** You may bind when the submission contains no unresolved discrepancy regarding ownership, occupancy, operations, or premises condition.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because unresolved discrepancies may indicate incomplete or unreliable risk information.

**H.1.25** You may bind when the applicant’s loss information does not reveal an unresolved pattern of preventable or recurring events.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because recurring losses may signal inadequate controls or a changing exposure.

**H.1.26** You may bind when prior loss circumstances, if disclosed, have been addressed through credible corrective action.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because corrective action helps demonstrate that the underlying cause has been managed.

**H.1.27** You may bind when the applicant provides a reasonable explanation for any material loss, complaint, or claim concern.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because a credible explanation assists in determining whether the exposure remains acceptable.

**H.1.28** You may bind when there is no indication of misrepresentation, concealment, or unwillingness to provide material underwriting information.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because reliable disclosure is necessary for valid risk assessment.

**H.1.29** You may bind when requested coverage corresponds to the property, operations, and liability exposure actually presented.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because coverage must reflect the risk being evaluated rather than an assumed risk.

**H.1.30** You may bind when the described property values appear internally consistent with the size, use, and condition of the premises.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because inconsistent values can indicate an inaccurate basis for coverage selection.

**H.1.31** You may bind when the applicant’s business income exposure, if applicable, is reasonably supported by the disclosed operations.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because business income coverage depends on an understandable operational basis.

**H.1.32** You may bind when the requested liability exposure is customary for the applicant’s stated business activities.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because customary liability exposures remain within the intended underwriting class.

**H.1.33** You may bind when the applicant does not seek coverage for an activity that is not clearly connected to its disclosed operations.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because unrelated activities may create an unreviewed exposure.

**H.1.34** You may bind when the insured has not requested a coverage arrangement that materially changes the ordinary risk transfer contemplated by this matrix.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because nonstandard risk transfer may require broader authority review.

**H.1.35** You may bind when any additional insured request is incidental to the disclosed business relationship and does not expand the insured’s operations.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because additional insured arrangements can create obligations beyond the underlying risk.

**H.1.36** You may bind when contractual obligations described by the applicant are ordinary for the operations and do not create an unusual assumption of liability.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because contractual risk transfer can materially affect expected loss exposure.

**H.1.37** You may bind when the applicant’s management practices indicate reasonable attention to safety, maintenance, and operational controls.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because management quality is a practical indicator of loss prevention capability.

**H.1.38** You may bind when the insured’s personnel practices do not present a known employment-related concern outside the contemplated coverage.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because employment practices can introduce exposures not reflected in the basic submission.

**H.1.39** You may bind when the applicant’s use of vehicles, mobile equipment, or delivery activity is incidental to the disclosed operations.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because transportation-related activity may create exposures requiring separate evaluation.

**H.1.40** You may bind when the insured does not store, transport, or distribute products that introduce an unusual product liability hazard.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because product hazards can differ substantially from premises and operations exposure.

**H.1.41** You may bind when any product sold, supplied, or installed by the applicant is consistent with its ordinary business purpose.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because products outside the ordinary business purpose may not have been contemplated in pricing or selection.

**H.1.42** You may bind when the premises and operations are not subject to a known regulatory, environmental, or safety concern that remains unresolved.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because unresolved compliance concerns may signal a heightened likelihood of loss.

**H.1.43** You may bind when the applicant cooperates with reasonable underwriting inquiries and provides information that supports the requested action.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because cooperative disclosure permits an informed and defensible binding decision.

**H.1.44** You may bind when the submission can be documented clearly enough for another underwriter to understand the basis for acceptance.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because clear documentation supports consistency, auditability, and sound file handling.

**H.1.45** You may bind when no fact in the submission creates doubt that the risk falls within your delegated authority.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because doubt regarding authority should be resolved before coverage is committed.

**H.1.46** Refer the submission when a material fact cannot be reconciled with the stated operations, premises condition, loss information, or requested coverage.  
The In Appetite provision supports this rule because unresolved material facts prevent you from confirming that the risk is within appetite.

## H.2 — Roof Age and Condition

**H.2.1** You may bind a risk only when the roof information supports a reasonable expectation of continued weather resistance. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision, which requires the property to be maintained against preventable loss.

**H.2.2** Obtain the roof installation date, covering type, repair history, and current condition before making an eligibility decision. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because roof condition directly affects expected loss frequency and severity.

**H.2.3** Refer a risk when the roof age is at or above 25 years years. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because a roof at that age falls outside the standard appetite.

**H.2.4** Do not infer roof age from the construction date when the applicant reports replacement or substantial reroofing. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because the relevant exposure is the age and condition of the existing roof covering.

**H.2.5** Request reliable support for a reported replacement roof when the reported condition is inconsistent with the apparent age or wear. This rule rests on the Concealment or Misrepresentation provision because accurate underwriting depends on material property information.

**H.2.6** Accept records such as invoices, permits, contractor statements, inspection reports, or clear dated photographs when they reasonably establish roof work. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because documentation reduces uncertainty about the roof exposure.

**H.2.7** Refer a risk when available information does not establish whether the roof was fully replaced or only repaired. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because repair work may not restore the condition or serviceability of the full roof system.

**H.2.8** Treat a roof covering replacement as distinct from structural deck repair, flashing work, or isolated patching. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because each type of work addresses a different source of potential water intrusion.

**H.2.9** Review the roof as a system, including covering, flashing, penetrations, gutters, drainage, and visible deck condition. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because failure at any of these components can produce a covered water loss.

**H.2.10** Refer a risk with visible curling, cracking, cupping, blistering, exposed substrate, missing material, or widespread staining. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because these conditions indicate diminished protection from weather.

**H.2.11** Do not bind a risk with an active roof leak, active interior water staining, or an unrepaired opening in the roof envelope. This rule rests on the Maintenance provision because insurance is not intended to fund correction of known ongoing deterioration.

**H.2.12** Refer a risk when the applicant reports a leak but states that repairs were completed without providing support for the repair scope. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because the reported repair may not resolve the underlying cause.

**H.2.13** You may consider a repaired roof only when the available information shows that the repair addressed the source of water entry. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because cosmetic interior repair does not establish exterior weather resistance.

**H.2.14** Refer a risk with recurring leak reports, repeated patching, or a history of water entry from the same roof area. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because repeated failures suggest unresolved deterioration or design concerns.

**H.2.15** Review aerial imagery and inspection observations for consistency with the applicant's roof description. This rule rests on the Concealment or Misrepresentation provision because material discrepancies require resolution before binding.

**H.2.16** Do not treat unclear aerial imagery as proof that the roof is sound. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because imagery may not reveal wear, concealed defects, or damage at penetrations.

**H.2.17** Refer a risk when aerial imagery shows apparent tarping, open areas, uneven coloration, displaced material, or other signs of possible damage. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because those conditions may indicate compromised weather protection.

**H.2.18** Obtain clarification when the roof material identified by the applicant differs from the material visible in inspection information. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because construction type affects durability, repairability, and loss performance.

**H.2.19** Apply the ACV roof age approach where the applicable loss settlement terms call for it. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because valuation treatment depends on the roof's reported age and condition.

**H.2.20** Do not represent that ACV roof age changes eligibility unless the underwriting decision independently supports that conclusion. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because claim valuation and underwriting acceptance are separate determinations.

**H.2.21** Explain the minimum payable percentage of roof surfacing replacement cost only as required by the applicable loss settlement terms. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because the payable amount must be determined under the policy terms in effect at the time of loss.

**H.2.22** Do not use the minimum payable percentage of roof surfacing replacement cost as a substitute for condition review. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because limited claim payment does not make a deteriorated roof acceptable for new business.

**H.2.23** Consider eighty percent condition when the applicable roof evaluation standard requires that assessment. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because the standard is intended to identify whether the roof remains serviceable.

**H.2.24** Refer a risk when the available evidence does not permit a reliable eighty percent condition assessment. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because uncertain condition information prevents a sound acceptance decision.

**H.2.25** Review the roof slope and design when evaluating visible wear or reported water intrusion. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because drainage performance and material suitability depend on roof configuration.

**H.2.26** Refer a risk with flat, low-slope, mixed-material, or otherwise specialized roof construction when condition cannot be confirmed. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because these roofs may require specialized inspection and maintenance.

**H.2.27** Obtain evidence of professional inspection when roof access is limited and material condition cannot be observed. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because inaccessible roofs cannot be reliably evaluated from applicant statements alone.

**H.2.28** You may accept a qualified inspection report when it identifies the roof covering, observed condition, and any recommended corrective work. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because a meaningful inspection addresses both present condition and needed maintenance.

**H.2.29** Refer a risk when an inspection report recommends replacement, substantial repair, or prompt corrective work. This rule rests on the Maintenance provision because recommended corrective action signals a known condition that may lead to loss.

**H.2.30** Do not disregard a contractor recommendation merely because the applicant disputes it. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because conflicting information must be resolved before you rely on the more favorable account.

**H.2.31** Consider prior roof claims together with the reported repair history and current condition. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because prior loss may reveal recurring vulnerability or incomplete repair.

**H.2.32** Refer a risk with prior roof damage when the repair documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or unavailable. This rule rests on the Concealment or Misrepresentation provision because material loss and repair information must be verified.

**H.2.33** Do not assume that a paid roof claim resulted in full replacement of the roof covering. This rule rests on the Loss Settlement provision because claim payment may reflect repair, depreciation, deductible application, or other policy terms.

**H.2.34** Request documentation of completed work when the applicant reports a roof claim followed by repairs. This rule rests on the Duties After Loss provision because records of repairs help establish the present condition of the property.

**H.2.35** Refer a risk with unrepaired storm damage, impact marks, lifted material, or compromised flashing. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because weather-related damage can permit later water intrusion.

**H.2.36** Do not bind based on an applicant's intent to repair or replace a deficient roof after policy issuance. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because eligibility must be supported by the condition at the time of binding.

**H.2.37** You may reconsider a referred risk after completed repairs or replacement are documented and the resulting condition is acceptable. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because completed work may materially change the roof exposure.

**H.2.38** Review repair documentation for the work description, property identification, and evidence that the work was completed. This rule rests on the Concealment or Misrepresentation provision because documents that cannot be tied to the insured location do not verify the risk.

**H.2.39** Refer a risk when photographs appear outdated, altered, unrelated to the property, or insufficient to show the reported work. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because reliable visual evidence is necessary to support the acceptance decision.

**H.2.40** Treat interior staining as a roof-condition concern unless credible evidence identifies another source. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because interior water evidence may indicate an exterior envelope failure.

**H.2.41** Obtain clarification when interior water damage is attributed to plumbing, condensation, or another nonroof source. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because the cause of damage affects both roof evaluation and loss exposure.

**H.2.42** Refer a risk with mold, rot, sagging, or soft areas associated with roof or attic moisture. This rule rests on the Maintenance provision because these conditions may indicate longstanding water intrusion or structural deterioration.

**H.2.43** Review attic observations when available, including staining, daylight, wet insulation, and signs of poor ventilation. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because attic evidence can reveal concealed roof failure.

**H.2.44** Do not require attic access when safe, reliable exterior evidence sufficiently supports the condition decision. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because the goal is a reasonable assessment of roof risk rather than a destructive inspection.

**H.2.45** Refer a risk when vegetation contacts the roof, obstructs drainage, or prevents condition assessment. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because contact and debris can retain moisture and conceal deterioration.

**H.2.46** Require removal of accumulated debris when it blocks roof drainage or conceals visible roof defects. This rule rests on the Maintenance provision because trapped moisture and impaired drainage can accelerate damage.

**H.2.47** Consider gutters, downspouts, and drainage paths as part of the roof review where their condition affects water handling. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because roof runoff that is not properly directed may contribute to water damage.

**H.2.48** Refer a risk when roof drainage appears to discharge against the structure or into areas of recurring water accumulation. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because poor drainage may increase the likelihood of water-related loss.

**H.2.49** Obtain clarification for solar equipment, satellite equipment, skylights, or other roof-mounted installations that may affect roof integrity. This rule rests on the Roof Condition and Maintenance provision because penetrations and attachments can create water-entry points.

**H.2.50** Refer a risk when roof-mounted equipment appears damaged, improperly sealed, or unsupported by installation information. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because improper installation may compromise both the roof and the attached equipment.

**H.2.51** Review roof alterations for consistency with local construction practices and the observed building design. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because unusual alterations may affect durability and repair quality.

**H.2.52** Do not rely solely on a seller disclosure, listing description, or verbal representation for roof condition. This rule rests on the Concealment or Misrepresentation provision because these sources may not provide sufficient detail for underwriting reliance.

**H.2.53** You may use a property inspection ordered for underwriting when its findings are current, property-specific, and sufficiently detailed. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because an objective inspection can support a consistent risk decision.

**H.2.54** Refer a risk when inspection findings conflict with application information and the conflict cannot be resolved. This rule rests on the Concealment or Misrepresentation provision because unresolved material discrepancies require further review.

**H.2.55** Document the basis for any roof-related exception, referral, declination, or acceptance decision. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because the file must show why the risk was treated outside the usual authority.

**H.2.56** Record the source of roof age, condition information, and any repair confirmation relied upon. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because later review must be able to distinguish verified facts from applicant representations.

**H.2.57** Do not override a roof referral based solely on premium, account relationship, or anticipated future improvements. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because roof acceptability is determined by the present risk characteristics.

**H.2.58** Escalate unusual roof construction, disputed condition evidence, or material uncertainty to the designated underwriting authority. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because risks outside routine evaluation require a documented authority decision.

**H.2.59** Advise the applicant of any required roof information or corrective action without promising coverage for a known condition. This rule rests on the Maintenance provision because underwriting communication must not alter policy obligations.

**H.2.60** Apply these roof rules consistently to new business, changes in exposure, and renewal review. This rule rests on the Underwriting Eligibility provision because roof condition remains material whenever the risk is evaluated.

## H.3 — Wind and Hail

**H.3.1** You may bind wind and hail coverage only when the risk fits the filed appetite and the requested deductible is available for the territory. This control rests on the Wind and Hail Underwriting Authority provision because local storm exposure can materially affect expected loss.

**H.3.2** You must obtain a wind mitigation inspection when Coverage A exceeds $1,000,000. This requirement rests on the Wind Mitigation Inspection provision because verified construction features support more reliable wind-loss selection.

**H.3.3** You may rely on a wind mitigation inspection only when it is complete, legible, and consistent with the risk information supplied for binding. This control rests on the Wind Mitigation Inspection provision because incomplete findings cannot support an underwriting decision.

**H.3.4** You must refer a risk when the inspection identifies conflicting roof, opening, or attachment information. This requirement rests on the Wind Mitigation Inspection provision because unresolved construction details may change the expected wind-loss outcome.

**H.3.5** You may apply available mitigation treatment only when the supporting inspection addresses the insured location being quoted. This control rests on the Wind Mitigation Inspection provision because mitigation characteristics may differ among locations.

**H.3.6** You must refer a risk when photographs, inspection findings, and application statements do not reasonably align. This requirement rests on the Wind and Hail Underwriting Authority provision because inconsistent evidence may indicate unrecognized property condition concerns.

**H.3.7** You may bind a risk with a roof in sound apparent condition when the roof information supports the requested coverage. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because roof performance is a principal driver of wind and hail severity.

**H.3.8** You must refer a risk with visible roof deterioration, temporary repairs, active leakage, or unexplained surface damage. This requirement rests on the Roof Condition provision because these conditions can increase susceptibility to storm-related loss.

**H.3.9** You may accept roof documentation from a reliable source when it clearly identifies the insured property and the work performed. This control rests on the Roof Condition provision because underwriting must be able to connect the documentation to the risk.

**H.3.10** You must refer a risk when roof materials, roof shape, or roof age information cannot be reasonably established. This requirement rests on the Roof Condition provision because uncertain roof characteristics limit accurate wind and hail evaluation.

**H.3.11** You may bind properties with protected openings when the application and supporting evidence demonstrate the protection is functional and applicable. This control rests on the Wind Mitigation Inspection provision because opening protection may reduce wind-driven damage.

**H.3.12** You must refer a property with unprotected or damaged openings in an area subject to meaningful wind exposure. This requirement rests on the Wind and Hail Underwriting Authority provision because opening failure can contribute to extensive interior damage.

**H.3.13** You may use the requested windstorm and hail deductible only when it complies with the minimum windstorm and hail deductible percentage. This control rests on the Wind and Hail Deductible provision because the deductible must preserve the intended insured participation in storm losses.

**H.3.14** You must refer any request to reduce, waive, or otherwise alter the applicable windstorm and hail deductible. This requirement rests on the Wind and Hail Deductible provision because deductible changes can materially affect catastrophe exposure.

**H.3.15** You may offer a hurricane deductible only when the selected option remains within the hurricane deductible cap. This control rests on the Hurricane Deductible provision because deductible availability is limited by the approved catastrophe structure.

**H.3.16** You must provide advance notice of deductible change when the applicable policy condition requires it. This requirement rests on the Wind and Hail Deductible provision because the insured must receive the notice required for a deductible revision.

**H.3.17** You may bind a replacement risk when prior wind and hail loss information is fully disclosed and does not exceed your authority. This control rests on the Prior Loss Review provision because prior storm losses may signal unresolved exposure conditions.

**H.3.18** You must refer a risk with recurring wind, hail, roof, water-intrusion, or exterior damage claims. This requirement rests on the Prior Loss Review provision because recurring losses may indicate a continuing maintenance or construction concern.

**H.3.19** You may bind a property after documented repairs when the repairs reasonably address the cause of prior wind or hail damage. This control rests on the Prior Loss Review provision because repaired conditions may restore the property to an acceptable risk profile.

**H.3.20** You must refer a risk when prior storm damage remains open, disputed, unrepaired, or insufficiently documented. This requirement rests on the Prior Loss Review provision because unresolved damage can impair evaluation of current property condition.

**H.3.21** You may bind a property with mature vegetation when trees and limbs do not present an apparent threat to the structure. This control rests on the Property Protection provision because falling vegetation can increase wind-related damage severity.

**H.3.22** You must refer a property with overhanging limbs, unstable trees, unsecured exterior features, or debris accumulation near the structure. This requirement rests on the Property Protection provision because these conditions can create preventable storm damage.

**H.3.23** You may accept a property with detached structures when the location schedule clearly identifies the structures and their construction. This control rests on the Location Description provision because wind and hail exposure must be evaluated for all insured property.

**H.3.24** You must refer a risk when detached structures, outdoor equipment, or exterior improvements are not adequately described. This requirement rests on the Location Description provision because incomplete descriptions can result in unassessed storm exposure.

**H.3.25** You may bind a vacant or unoccupied property only when its condition, maintenance, and protection measures meet the available appetite. This control rests on the Vacancy and Unoccupancy provision because unattended properties may sustain greater storm damage before mitigation occurs.

## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** This guide states the Florida homeowners appetite and supports consistent
underwriting decisions. It is internal guidance only and does not alter the Insuring
Agreement, because the policy form controls the insurance provided.

**H.0.2** You may use this guide to identify risks that fit the company’s intended
homeowner profile. This promotes consistent selection under the Eligibility provision,
which governs whether a risk may be considered for coverage.

**H.0.3** You must not treat this guide as an offer, binder, endorsement, or policy
promise. The Declarations and Insuring Agreement establish the terms of any issued
policy, because those provisions identify the insured risk and coverage granted.

**H.0.4** You should evaluate the dwelling, applicant, occupancy, construction,
maintenance, loss history, and exposure information as a whole. The Underwriting
Conditions provision supports this approach because material risk characteristics affect
acceptability and pricing.

**H.0.5** You may request clarification or additional documentation when submitted
information is incomplete, inconsistent, or suggests an unaddressed hazard. The
Concealment or Fraud condition supports verification because inaccurate material
information can affect underwriting decisions.

**H.0.6** You should rely on the current policy form language when describing coverage
to an applicant or producer. The Definitions provision controls the meaning of policy
terms, because common usage may differ from the policy meaning.

**H.0.7** You must not describe a dwelling as eligible solely because it appears to meet
a general appetite description. The Eligibility provision requires evaluation of the
actual risk presented, because each location may have conditions that change expected
loss potential.

**H.0.8** You should consider coastal, wind, flood, wildfire, lightning, water, and
liability exposures that may affect a Florida property. The Perils Insured Against
provision supports this review because covered causes of loss and exclusions determine
the scope of the insurance request.

**H.0.9** You may discuss the named storm window only by using the applicable policy
language and approved underwriting materials. The Deductible provision controls how a
named storm loss is adjusted, because deductible application depends on the policy terms
in force.

**H.0.10** You should confirm that requested property coverages align with the dwelling
characteristics and the applicant’s stated insurance needs. The Coverage A provision
supports this review because dwelling coverage is tied to the insured residence
premises.

**H.0.11** You should address the Coverage B percentage through the applicable policy
language rather than through informal descriptions. The Coverage B provision controls
related private structures coverage, because the policy determines what property
qualifies and how coverage applies.

**H.0.12** You may explain the fire department service charge limit only as stated in
the policy form. The Additional Coverages provision governs that protection, because it

**H.0.13** You should address the backup and sump overflow limit using the applicable

may affect whether coverage remains acceptable.

determination. The Insuring Agreement and Exclusions provisions govern coverage, because
underwriting acceptance does not expand the policy’s stated protections.

**H.0.18** You must not imply that an inspection, valuation, recommendation, or
loss-control observation guarantees safety or coverage. The Inspection provision governs
the company’s inspection rights, because an inspection is not a representation that a
property is safe or insurable.

**H.0.19** You should document the basis for any referral, exception request, or adverse
underwriting outcome in clear and neutral terms. The Underwriting Conditions provision
supports documentation because the file should show the material facts considered.

**H.0.20** You may use approved underwriting tools and verified third-party information
to assess the risk. The Application provision supports use of reliable information
because the application and supporting records inform the company’s acceptance decision.

**H.0.21** You should communicate coverage questions through approved channels when
policy language, endorsements, or risk facts create uncertainty. The General Conditions
provision supports escalation because only authorized interpretation can resolve a
material coverage issue.

**H.0.22** This guide remains subject to the policy form, applicable endorsements, and
controlling law. The Policy Period and Conditions provisions govern the insurance
relationship, because internal underwriting guidance is not part of the contract.

## H.2 — Roof Age and Condition

**H.2.1** You may consider a risk only when the roof information is complete enough to
evaluate condition, material, installation, and maintenance. This requirement rests on
the roof condition eligibility provision because incomplete roof information prevents a
reliable assessment of expected loss.

**H.2.2** Obtain the reported roof age from a credible source, such as installation
records, contractor documentation, or an inspection report. This practice rests on the
underwriting representation provision because unsupported age information can misstate
roof eligibility.

**H.2.3** You must obtain a roof inspection before binding when the reported roof age is
15 years or greater. This requirement rests on the roof inspection eligibility provision
because age alone may not reveal deterioration, repairs, or deferred maintenance.

**H.2.4** Do not bind a risk when the reported roof age is 20 years or greater. This
limitation rests on the roof condition eligibility provision because roof performance
may be less predictable beyond the stated appetite boundary.

**H.2.5** Do not rely on an applicant’s estimate of roof age when available records

**H.2.8** Confirm that the inspection addresses the primary roof surface and all

## H.3 — Wind and Hail

**H.3.1** You must obtain a wind mitigation inspection when Coverage A exceeds $500,000.
This supports accurate wind pricing under the Wind Mitigation provision.

**H.3.2** Accept a wind mitigation inspection only when the report is complete, legible,
and supports each credited construction feature. This prevents unsupported credits under
the Wind Mitigation provision.

**H.3.3** Refer an inspection that contains conflicting descriptions, photographs, or
construction details for underwriting review. This protects rating accuracy under the
Wind Mitigation provision.

**H.3.4** Do not apply a mitigation credit based solely on an applicant statement or
listing description. The Wind Mitigation provision requires reliable evidence of the
qualifying feature.

**H.3.5** Verify that roof-covering information in the inspection agrees with the

**H.3.6** Refer a risk when the inspection indicates prior wind damage, incomplete

**H.3.7** You may rely on current underwriting evidence when it clearly identifies the

**H.3.9** Request updated evidence when roof replacement, opening replacement,
structural alteration, or other work may affect credited wind features. This maintains

**H.3.10** Confirm that any claimed opening protection applies to the openings required

qualifying protection under the Wind Mitigation provision.

the Wind Mitigation provision.

**H.3.12** Do not treat temporary panels, stored materials, or unverified coverings as
qualifying opening protection. The Wind Mitigation provision requires evidence that the
protection meets the applicable standard.

## H.7 — Authority Limits

**H.7.1** You may bind Coverage A at or below $600,000 when the risk otherwise meets the
applicable underwriting requirements. The Authority Limits provision supports this rule
because delegated authority must match the exposure accepted.

**H.7.2** You must refer a risk with Coverage A above your authority to a senior
underwriter, who may bind Coverage A at or below $900,000. The Authority Limits
provision supports this rule because broader authority requires additional underwriting
judgment.

**H.7.3** You must refer any risk that exceeds senior underwriter authority for approval
before quoting, binding, or changing coverage. The Authority Limits provision supports
this rule because risks outside delegated authority require management review.

**H.7.4** You may not split a risk, adjust a valuation, or use a coverage structure to
avoid an authority referral. The Authority Limits provision supports this rule because
authority applies to the actual exposure presented.

**H.7.5** You must use the completed replacement cost estimate when determining whether
Coverage A falls within your authority. The Authority Limits provision supports this
rule because binding authority depends on a supportable valuation.

**H.7.6** You must refer a risk when the replacement cost estimate appears unreliable,
incomplete, or inconsistent with the property characteristics. The Authority Limits
provision supports this rule because an unsupported value can place the exposure outside
delegated authority.

# Texas Homeowners Appetite Guide






## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** This guide states the underwriting appetite and handling approach for Texas homeowners business. You use it to support consistent decisions because similar risks should receive similar consideration.

**H.0.2** This guide is internal operating guidance and is not part of any policy contract. You must rely on the applicable coverage language when determining what is insured because the contract controls the insurer’s obligations.

**H.0.3** You may use this guide when evaluating new business, renewals, endorsements, and risk changes. The guidance supports sound selection because eligibility can change when the property or occupancy changes.

**H.0.4** You should apply the guidance with reasonable underwriting judgment. A risk that appears acceptable on limited information may require further review because complete facts can affect the exposure.

**H.0.5** You must obtain information that is material to the risk before making a binding decision. Accurate information is necessary because underwriting decisions depend on the condition, use, and characteristics of the insured property.

**H.0.6** You should treat statements made in an application or supporting materials as information requiring verification where appropriate. This approach is necessary because incomplete or conflicting information can affect eligibility and pricing.

**H.0.7** You may request photographs, inspection information, repair details, or other evidence of property condition. Supporting evidence is useful because visible hazards may not be adequately described in an application.

**H.0.8** You should identify conditions that could increase the chance or severity of loss. This review is important because property maintenance, construction features, and occupancy patterns can materially affect risk quality.

**H.0.9** You must refer a risk when the available information does not support a clear underwriting decision. Referral is appropriate because exceptions require review within the insurer’s approved authority.

**H.0.10** You should not represent that this guide expands, restricts, or changes coverage. Coverage determinations must follow the applicable policy language because internal guidance cannot alter contractual terms.

**H.0.11** You may explain general underwriting requirements to an applicant or producer without characterizing them as policy coverage. Clear communication is important because eligibility requirements and coverage obligations serve different purposes.

**H.0.12** You should consider the Coverage B limit as a percentage of Coverage A when reviewing the property profile. This consideration is relevant because related property exposures should be evaluated consistently with the insured location.

**H.0.13** You must review fire service charge coverage when the risk information raises questions about emergency-response exposure. This review supports accurate handling because local response arrangements can affect the insured’s potential loss experience.

**H.0.14** You should identify the drain backup sublimit when discussing water-related loss scenarios. Clear identification is needed because a sublimit may affect how the insured understands the available protection.

**H.0.15** You must recognize the proof of loss period as a claims-handling requirement rather than an underwriting promise. This distinction matters because claim duties are governed by the applicable coverage language.

**H.0.16** You should document material underwriting information and the basis for any referral or exception decision. Documentation is necessary because the file should show how the risk was evaluated.

**H.0.17** You may rely on information from authorized sources when it is relevant and reasonably credible. Source review is important because risk decisions should be based on information that can be supported in the file.

**H.0.18** You must escalate suspected misrepresentation, undisclosed hazards, or unusual loss information. Escalation protects the underwriting process because these conditions may require specialized review.

**H.0.19** You should apply this guidance without making assumptions about protected characteristics or other improper factors. Consistent risk-based review is required because underwriting decisions must rest on permissible information.

**H.0.20** You may decline, nonrenew, or condition a risk only within your assigned authority and applicable procedures. Authority controls are necessary because risk actions must receive the required level of review.

**H.0.21** You should communicate underwriting requirements clearly and promptly when additional action is needed. Timely communication is important because applicants and producers need an opportunity to provide relevant information.

**H.0.22** You must treat this guide as operational direction that may be updated as underwriting needs change. Current guidance should be followed because appetite decisions depend on the insurer’s present risk-management approach.

## H.1 — In Appetite

**H.1.1** You may bind only when the Coverage A limit is at least $150,000 and does not exceed $1,200,000. This range supports adequate reconstruction funding and rests on the Declarations provision.

**H.1.2** You may bind only when the protection class does not exceed 8. Fire-response capability materially affects expected severity, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.3** You may bind a dwelling used principally as a private residence by the named insured. Personal occupancy supports the homeowners risk assumption and rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.4** You may bind only when the named insured has an insurable interest in the dwelling. Clear ownership supports proper settlement and rests on the Insurable Interest provision.

**H.1.5** You may bind a dwelling with construction characteristics that permit reliable replacement-cost valuation. Reliable valuation supports appropriate Coverage A selection and rests on the Valuation provision.

**H.1.6** You may bind only when the dwelling is structurally sound and free of known unrepaired damage. Existing damage can obscure the cause of later loss, and this rule rests on the Concealment provision.

**H.1.7** You may bind only when the roof is serviceable and shows no known active leakage. A sound roof reduces weather-related loss potential and rests on the Property Not Covered provision.

**H.1.8** You may bind only when exterior walls, siding, windows, and doors are maintained against weather intrusion. Maintained exterior components reduce gradual deterioration claims and rest on the Property Not Covered provision.

**H.1.9** You may bind only when plumbing systems are operational and free of known leaks. Undisclosed leakage can produce progressive damage, and this rule rests on the Concealment provision.

**H.1.10** You may bind only when electrical systems are safely maintained and suitable for residential use. Unsafe electrical conditions increase fire potential and rest on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.11** You may bind only when heating equipment is properly installed and maintained. Heating defects can create fire or carbon-monoxide exposure, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.12** You may bind only when the dwelling has a functional water supply and sanitary drainage system. Basic utility function supports normal residential occupancy and rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.13** You may bind only when the foundation is stable and shows no known unresolved movement or failure. Foundation impairment can affect the entire dwelling and rests on the Property Not Covered provision.

**H.1.14** You may bind only when the dwelling is not vacant or abandoned. Lack of occupancy increases the chance that damage will remain undiscovered, and this rule rests on the Vacancy provision.

**H.1.15** You may bind only when the applicant discloses material facts concerning the dwelling and its use. Complete disclosure permits accurate risk selection and rests on the Concealment provision.

**H.1.16** You may bind only when the applicant provides information sufficient to verify the location, occupancy, and construction of the dwelling. Verifiable underwriting information supports sound eligibility decisions and rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.17** You may bind only when prior property losses are accurately disclosed. Loss history may indicate unresolved conditions, and this rule rests on the Concealment provision.

**H.1.18** You may bind only when prior cancellation, nonrenewal, or declination information is accurately disclosed. Prior coverage action may identify underwriting concerns, and this rule rests on the Concealment provision.

**H.1.19** You may bind only when the dwelling is not used primarily for commercial activity. Commercial use creates exposures outside the homeowners risk assumption and rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.20** You may bind only when transient lodging activity is not a principal use of the dwelling. Lodging activity can increase liability and property exposure, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.21** You may bind only when the dwelling is not used for manufacturing, repair, storage, or distribution activity that materially changes the risk. Business operations can introduce hazards not contemplated by homeowners coverage and rest on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.22** You may bind only when agricultural operations do not materially alter the residential nature of the location. Agricultural activity can create liability and property exposures outside appetite, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.23** You may bind only when the premises are reasonably accessible for inspection and emergency response. Access supports property verification and loss mitigation, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.24** You may bind only when the location does not present a known severe brush, wildfire, or wildfire-interface exposure beyond appetite. Vegetation-driven fire spread can produce concentrated property loss, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.25** You may bind only when the location does not have known recurring flood conditions that materially impair the risk. Recurring flood conditions may indicate an exposure not addressed by homeowners coverage, and this rule rests on the Water Damage provision.

**H.1.26** You may bind only when the dwelling is maintained to reduce windborne-debris exposure. Secure exterior features reduce the potential for preventable storm damage and rest on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.27** You may bind only when detached structures are safe, maintained, and incidental to residential use. Incidental structures must remain consistent with the homeowners risk assumption, and this rule rests on the Other Structures provision.

**H.1.28** You may bind only when swimming pools and similar water features are secured and maintained. Water hazards can create significant liability exposure, and this rule rests on the Liability provision.

**H.1.29** You may bind only when recreational equipment is maintained and does not present an unmanaged liability hazard. Unsafe equipment can lead to preventable bodily injury claims, and this rule rests on the Liability provision.

**H.1.30** You may bind only when animals kept at the location do not create an unacceptable liability exposure. Animal behavior can materially affect personal liability risk, and this rule rests on the Liability provision.

**H.1.31** You may bind only when fences, gates, walkways, and stairs are maintained in safe condition. Unsafe premises conditions increase bodily injury potential and rest on the Liability provision.

**H.1.32** You may bind only when known sinkhole, subsidence, erosion, or earth-movement conditions are disclosed. Earth-movement conditions may involve excluded or restricted causes of loss, and this rule rests on the Earth Movement provision.

**H.1.33** You may bind only when the dwelling is not subject to active construction that materially changes its condition or occupancy. Construction activity can alter valuation and loss exposure, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.34** You may bind only when renovations are competently managed and do not leave the property exposed to weather or theft. Incomplete work can create avoidable damage, and this rule rests on the Property Not Covered provision.

**H.1.35** You may bind only when the dwelling is not condemned or subject to a governmental order affecting habitability. A governmental restriction may prevent normal residential use, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.36** You may bind only when the location is not used to store hazardous materials beyond ordinary household use. Hazardous materials can create contamination and liability exposures, and this rule rests on the Exclusions provision.

**H.1.37** You may bind only when fuel storage and heating fuel equipment are safely installed and maintained. Improper fuel handling increases fire and pollution potential, and this rule rests on the Exclusions provision.

**H.1.38** You may bind only when the applicant accepts inspection where underwriting conditions warrant verification. Inspection confirms material risk characteristics and rests on the Inspection provision.

**H.1.39** You may bind only when inspection findings do not reveal conditions outside appetite. Unacceptable findings can materially alter expected loss potential, and this rule rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.40** You may bind only when required repairs are completed or otherwise resolved before coverage is issued. Unresolved repair needs can lead to known-loss disputes, and this rule rests on the Concealment provision.

**H.1.41** You may bind only when the requested deductible and coverage arrangement are consistent with the dwelling and applicant exposure. Suitable policy structure supports proper risk transfer and rests on the Declarations provision.

**H.1.42** You may bind only when personal property coverage is reasonably related to the residential occupancy presented. Coverage that does not fit the occupancy may signal undisclosed use, and this rule rests on the Personal Property provision.

**H.1.43** You may bind only when liability coverage is appropriate for the disclosed premises activities. Liability exposure depends on the conditions and activities at the location, and this rule rests on the Liability provision.

**H.1.44** You may bind only when all material underwriting questions receive complete and consistent answers. Consistency helps identify misrepresentation before issuance and rests on the Concealment provision.

**H.1.45** You may decline risks with characteristics that cannot be adequately evaluated from available information. Inability to evaluate material facts prevents sound underwriting selection and rests on the Eligibility provision.

**H.1.46** You may refer any risk presenting unusual ownership, occupancy, construction, or hazard characteristics. Referral allows review of exposures that may fall outside ordinary homeowners appetite and rests on the Eligibility provision.

## H.2 — Roof Age and Condition

**H.2.1** You must verify the roof covering type, visible condition, and reported age before offering terms. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because roof performance depends on both material condition and exposure history.

**H.2.2** You may rely on applicant information only when it is consistent with the property facts available to you. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because unsupported roof information can conceal deterioration or prior loss exposure.

**H.2.3** You must refer a risk when the roof age cannot be established with reasonable confidence. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because an unknown age prevents reliable evaluation of remaining service life.

**H.2.4** You must obtain current roof condition evidence when visible images do not clearly show the roof surface. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because distant or obstructed images may not reveal material damage.

**H.2.5** You must obtain a roof inspection before binding when the roof age is at or above 15 years. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because aging materials require an independent assessment of present condition.

**H.2.6** You may not bind a risk when the roof age is at or above 25 years. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because roofs beyond the age ceiling present unacceptable uncertainty regarding failure potential.

**H.2.7** You must apply the roof surfacing age threshold when determining whether age-related review is warranted. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because roof surfacing can deteriorate before other building components show concern.

**H.2.8** You may bind a risk with an older roof only when the available condition evidence supports continued serviceability. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because age alone does not establish whether a roof remains weather resistant.

**H.2.9** You must refer a risk when roof condition evidence shows active leakage, staining, or moisture intrusion. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because these conditions may indicate failed weather protection or concealed deterioration.

**H.2.10** You may not bind a risk with an actively leaking roof. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because an active leak indicates a current maintenance or repair need.

**H.2.11** You must refer a risk when the roof surface shows missing, lifted, cracked, curled, or deteriorated materials. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because compromised surfacing can allow wind or water entry.

**H.2.12** You must refer a risk when roof images show exposed underlayment, substrate, or fasteners. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because exposed roof components suggest the weather barrier may no longer perform as intended.

**H.2.13** You may not treat cosmetic discoloration as acceptable when it appears alongside material deterioration. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because discoloration may accompany moisture retention, algae growth, or surface breakdown.

**H.2.14** You must refer a risk when roof images show sagging, uneven planes, or visible structural deflection. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because these signs may reflect deck, framing, or moisture damage.

**H.2.15** You must refer a risk when roof drainage appears obstructed or ineffective. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because standing water and poor runoff can shorten roof service life.

**H.2.16** You may bind a risk with minor debris only when the debris does not obscure roof condition or impair drainage. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because debris can hide damage and retain moisture against roof materials.

**H.2.17** You must refer a risk when tree limbs contact, overhang closely, or visibly abrade the roof surface. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because vegetation contact can damage surfacing and increase wind-related loss potential.

**H.2.18** You must verify that recent roof work addressed the condition that prompted the work. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because isolated repairs may not resolve broader deterioration.

**H.2.19** You may accept repaired roof areas only when the repair appears complete and compatible with the surrounding roof system. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because incomplete or mismatched repairs can create weak points.

**H.2.20** You must refer a risk when roof repairs appear temporary, improvised, or unsupported by durable materials. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because temporary repairs may fail during ordinary weather events.

**H.2.21** You must verify the roof replacement history when the reported roof age conflicts with visible material condition. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because the inconsistency may indicate inaccurate risk information.

**H.2.22** You may rely on replacement information when it identifies the roof area addressed and is consistent with the visible condition. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because partial replacement can leave older materials in service.

**H.2.23** You must refer a risk when only part of the roof appears to have been replaced and the remaining areas show deterioration. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because mixed-condition roof areas can create uneven loss exposure.

**H.2.24** You must evaluate attached structures with separate roof surfaces as part of the overall risk review. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because a failed roof on an attached structure can contribute to covered property damage.

**H.2.25** You must identify flat, low-slope, or otherwise drainage-sensitive roof areas during review. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because these areas can retain water and require closer condition evaluation.

**H.2.26** You must refer a risk when flat or low-slope roof areas show ponding, blistering, open seams, or membrane separation. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because these conditions can permit water intrusion.

**H.2.27** You may bind a risk with a specialty roof material only when its condition and maintenance characteristics are clear. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because unfamiliar or poorly documented materials can limit reliable exposure evaluation.

**H.2.28** You must refer a risk when roof material identification is uncertain and the available evidence does not resolve the uncertainty. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because material type affects expected durability and loss susceptibility.

**H.2.29** You must consider roof geometry when evaluating wind exposure and visible wear. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because complex roof lines can create vulnerable transitions and concealed drainage concerns.

**H.2.30** You must refer a risk when valleys, penetrations, flashings, or transitions appear damaged or poorly maintained. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because these locations are common points of water entry.

**H.2.31** You may accept sealed roof penetrations only when the seals appear intact and properly maintained. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because failed seals can allow moisture intrusion around vents and other openings.

**H.2.32** You must refer a risk when flashing appears loose, corroded, displaced, or absent. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because flashing protects roof intersections from water entry.

**H.2.33** You must evaluate skylights, solar equipment, and roof-mounted devices for their effect on roof condition. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because roof attachments can create additional penetration and maintenance exposure.

**H.2.34** You must refer a risk when roof-mounted equipment appears improperly secured or poorly sealed. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because improper installation can damage roof materials or create leak paths.

**H.2.35** You may not assume a roof is acceptable merely because no prior roof claim is reported. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because unreported deterioration may still create a material exposure.

**H.2.36** You must consider reported prior roof repairs when reviewing current roof condition. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because prior repairs may identify recurring damage or unresolved maintenance issues.

**H.2.37** You must refer a risk when reported prior roof damage is inconsistent with current roof appearance. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because the difference may indicate incomplete repairs or inaccurate reporting.

**H.2.38** You may request additional condition evidence when roof images are outdated, incomplete, or obscured by weather conditions. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because clear current evidence is necessary to assess present roof integrity.

**H.2.39** You must use clear roof images that show the material surface and relevant roof features. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because broad exterior views may not reveal roof defects.

**H.2.40** You must refer a risk when available roof evidence does not permit a reasonable determination of condition. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because uncertainty about the roof can materially affect expected loss frequency.

**H.2.41** You may bind a risk with normal weathering when there is no indication of compromised roof function. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because ordinary wear is distinguishable from deterioration that affects weather resistance.

**H.2.42** You must distinguish normal granule variation from widespread surface loss or exposed substrate. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because widespread surface loss may indicate reduced protection from weather.

**H.2.43** You must refer a risk when shingles or roof panels appear brittle, fractured, or unable to lie flat. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because brittle materials may be more susceptible to wind and water damage.

**H.2.44** You may not bind a risk when roof deterioration appears extensive or unresolved. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because extensive deterioration indicates a substantial likelihood of near-term damage.

**H.2.45** You must consider the roof payment floor when evaluating whether the requested coverage approach is compatible with the risk. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because roof condition can affect the appropriate settlement treatment.

**H.2.46** You must consider the insured-to-value threshold for replacement cost settlement when reviewing a risk with roof condition concerns. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because adequate valuation supports appropriate settlement expectations following a covered loss.

**H.2.47** You must refer a risk when the roof condition is inconsistent with the requested replacement cost settlement approach. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because settlement expectations should align with the property’s maintained condition.

**H.2.48** You may offer terms only after roof-related underwriting concerns are resolved or accepted through referral authority. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because unresolved roof concerns can materially affect eligibility.

**H.2.49** You must document the basis for any roof condition exception approved through referral authority. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because the underwriting file must show why the risk remains acceptable.

**H.2.50** You must apply roof requirements consistently to owner-occupied and tenant-occupied risks. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because roof condition affects property damage exposure regardless of occupancy.

**H.2.51** You must evaluate detached structures when their roof condition could contribute to a broader property loss. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because windborne materials or collapsing roof components can damage nearby insured property.

**H.2.52** You may not disregard roof concerns because the dwelling exterior otherwise appears well maintained. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because roof deterioration may not be apparent from ground-level maintenance indicators.

**H.2.53** You must refer a risk when the roof is covered, concealed, or inaccessible and no reliable condition evidence is available. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because inaccessible roof areas may contain material defects that cannot be evaluated.

**H.2.54** You must assess visible roof condition separately from the condition of gutters, siding, and trim. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because maintained exterior features do not confirm roof integrity.

**H.2.55** You may use maintenance history as supporting information but not as a substitute for roof condition evidence. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because reported maintenance may not identify hidden deterioration.

**H.2.56** You must refer a risk when the applicant declines to provide requested roof information or condition evidence. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because incomplete information prevents a reliable eligibility determination.

**H.2.57** You must communicate roof repair expectations clearly when binding is contingent on correction of a condition concern. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because clear expectations reduce uncertainty about the risk accepted.

**H.2.58** You may not treat a stated intent to repair as evidence that the roof is currently acceptable. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because the condition remains unresolved until the work is complete.

**H.2.59** You must obtain confirmation that required roof work has been completed before removing a roof-related underwriting concern. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because completed work must resolve the condition that created the concern.

**H.2.60** You must decline or refer any risk that presents roof conditions outside available underwriting authority. This control rests on the Roof Age and Condition Provision because roof condition remains a material factor in Texas homeowners eligibility.

## H.3 — Wind and Hail

**H.3.1** You may consider wind and hail exposure only after confirming the insured location, roof characteristics, and surrounding conditions. This review supports consistent risk selection under the Wind and Hail Underwriting provision.

**H.3.2** You must obtain a wind mitigation inspection when Coverage A exceeds $500,000. The inspection supports evaluation of construction features that may affect wind and hail loss potential under the Wind and Hail Underwriting provision.

**H.3.3** You should obtain the inspection from a qualified inspector and retain the report in the underwriting file. This documentation supports reliance on reported mitigation features under the Wind Mitigation Inspection provision.

**H.3.4** You may rely on mitigation features only when the inspection report clearly identifies the feature and its location. This avoids crediting conditions that cannot be verified under the Wind Mitigation Inspection provision.

**H.3.5** You must refer a risk when the inspection report conflicts with the application, photographs, or other underwriting information. Referral is required because unresolved differences may materially affect eligibility under the Wind and Hail Underwriting provision.

**H.3.6** You should review the roof covering, visible condition, and apparent maintenance before binding. This review helps identify roofs with increased susceptibility to wind or hail damage under the Roof Condition eligibility provision.

**H.3.7** You may require current photographs when available images do not show the roof, exterior walls, or attached structures clearly. Clear images support accurate assessment under the Property Inspection condition.

**H.3.8** You must not treat cosmetic roof wear as proof of wind or hail damage without supporting evidence. The Wind and Hail Loss Settlement provision requires damage evaluation based on direct physical loss.

**H.3.9** You should refer risks with visible roof deterioration, unrepaired openings, loose materials, or structural concerns. These conditions may increase the likelihood or severity of loss under the Roof Condition eligibility provision.

**H.3.10** You may consider roof material and installation characteristics when evaluating wind and hail susceptibility. This is permitted because the Wind and Hail Underwriting provision recognizes construction as a material exposure factor.

**H.3.11** You should review nearby trees, unsecured exterior features, and surrounding debris hazards. These conditions may contribute to wind-related property damage under the Property Maintenance condition.

**H.3.12** You must obtain a satisfactory explanation for prior wind, hail, roof, or water losses before binding. Prior losses may indicate unresolved conditions under the Loss History underwriting provision.

**H.3.13** You should verify that repairs from reported losses are complete and supported by available records. This helps avoid accepting property with unrepaired damage under the Property Condition eligibility provision.

**H.3.14** You may bind a risk with prior wind or hail losses only when the loss history and current condition support the risk. This determination rests on the Wind and Hail Underwriting provision.

**H.3.15** You must apply the minimum windstorm and hail deductible percentage when required by the applicable deductible provision. The requirement preserves the intended insured participation for wind and hail losses under the Windstorm and Hail Deductible provision.

**H.3.16** You should confirm that the selected deductible is shown consistently on the application, quote, and issued policy records. Consistent records reduce disputes concerning deductible application under the Windstorm and Hail Deductible provision.

**H.3.17** You must explain that the windstorm and hail deductible may apply to covered damage caused by those perils. This disclosure is required by the Windstorm and Hail Deductible provision.

**H.3.18** You should not describe a deductible as waived unless the applicable policy provision expressly provides for that result. Deductible handling must follow the Windstorm and Hail Deductible provision.

**H.3.19** You must apply the hurricane deductible cap where required by the applicable hurricane deductible provision. The cap controls deductible treatment for qualifying hurricane-related losses under the Hurricane Deductible provision.

**H.3.20** You should distinguish hurricane deductible handling from other wind and hail deductible handling when discussing coverage with an applicant. This distinction helps prevent inaccurate expectations under the Hurricane Deductible provision.

**H.3.21** You must provide advance notice of deductible change when the policy change process requires it. Notice supports informed policyholder decisions under the Policy Change provision.

**H.3.22** You should document the delivery method and policyholder response for a deductible change. Documentation supports administration of the change under the Policy Change provision.

**H.3.23** You must not process a deductible reduction or other wind-related coverage change when binding restrictions apply. Restrictions are necessary to prevent adverse selection under the Binding Authority provision.

**H.3.24** You should review pending weather conditions before processing requested changes to wind or hail coverage. This review supports compliance with the Binding Authority provision.

**H.3.25** You must apply the storm period duration as defined by the applicable policy provision when determining whether a binding restriction remains in effect. The defined period supports consistent handling under the Binding Authority provision.

**H.3.26** You should refer requests received during an active weather event or related binding restriction. Referral is required because authority may be limited under the Binding Authority provision.

**H.3.27** You may accept emergency protective actions that are reasonable and necessary to prevent further damage. This approach follows the Duties After Loss condition.

**H.3.28** You must advise the insured to preserve damaged property, photographs, invoices, and repair information when a wind or hail loss is reported. Preservation supports investigation under the Duties After Loss condition.

**H.3.29** You should distinguish temporary protective work from permanent repair work when reviewing a reported loss. The distinction supports proper evaluation under the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.3.30** You must not direct permanent repairs before the claim investigation supports the scope of covered damage. Premature repair direction may impair evaluation under the Duties After Loss condition.

**H.3.31** You should inspect for damage caused by wind-driven debris, hail impact, and resulting openings in the building envelope. These sources may establish direct physical loss under the Wind and Hail coverage grant.

**H.3.32** You must evaluate interior water damage for evidence that wind or hail created an opening through which water entered. Coverage depends on the cause and pathway of damage under the Wind and Hail coverage grant.

**H.3.33** You should not assume that water damage is covered merely because a storm occurred near the insured location. The Wind and Hail coverage grant requires a covered cause of direct physical loss.

**H.3.34** You must evaluate roof damage separately from preexisting wear, deterioration, faulty maintenance, and prior repairs. Separate evaluation is necessary under the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.3.35** You should obtain available weather information when it may help establish the timing or cause of reported damage. Weather information supports causation analysis under the Wind and Hail coverage grant.

**H.3.36** You may request contractor estimates, invoices, and repair records when the claimed damage or scope is unclear. These records support valuation under the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.3.37** You must review contractor submissions for consistency with observed damage and the insured's report of loss. This review helps prevent payment for unsupported work under the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.3.38** You should refer claims involving suspected misrepresentation, altered documentation, or inconsistent damage reports. Referral is required because coverage may be affected under the Concealment or Fraud condition.

**H.3.39** You must not deny a wind or hail claim solely because damage is difficult to observe from the ground. A complete investigation is required under the Duties After Loss condition.

**H.3.40** You should consider appropriate inspection methods when exterior conditions do not permit a reliable ground-level assessment. Appropriate inspection supports accurate scope evaluation under the Loss Settlement provision.

**H.3.41** You may require additional information when the cause of loss cannot be determined from the available evidence. Additional information supports a fair coverage determination under the Wind and Hail coverage grant.

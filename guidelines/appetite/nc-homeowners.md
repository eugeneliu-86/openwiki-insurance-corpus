## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** You may use this guide to assess whether a North Carolina homeowners risk fits
the company’s current underwriting appetite. This guidance rests on the applicable
policy form’s Insuring Agreement and is intended to promote consistent risk selection.

**H.0.2** You must treat this guide as internal underwriting guidance rather than as an
offer of insurance or a statement of coverage. This status rests on the policy form’s
Entire Contract provision, which controls the terms available to an insured.

**H.0.3** You may bind only risks that satisfy applicable authority, filing, and
underwriting requirements. This rule rests on the policy form’s Declarations provision
because the issued declarations identify the coverage actually provided.

**H.0.4** You must not represent that a risk is covered merely because it appears
acceptable under this guide. This rule rests on the policy form’s Insuring Agreement,
which determines coverage only after the policy is issued.

**H.0.5** You should obtain complete and accurate application information before making
an eligibility decision. This practice rests on the policy form’s Concealment or Fraud
condition because material inaccuracies can affect underwriting and claim handling.

**H.0.6** You may request supporting information when the risk characteristics, prior
loss information, occupancy, or property condition are unclear. This practice rests on
the policy form’s Duties After Loss condition, which requires reliable information for
proper evaluation.

**H.0.7** You must apply this guidance consistently while considering the facts of the
individual risk. This approach rests on the policy form’s Definitions provision because
coverage terms depend on the circumstances that meet those definitions.

**H.0.8** You should refer unusual, incomplete, or conflicting information for
underwriting review before binding. This rule rests on the policy form’s Concealment or
Fraud condition because unresolved discrepancies may be material to the risk.

**H.0.9** You may not alter policy terms, conditions, exclusions, or endorsements
through correspondence, application notes, or verbal statements. This rule rests on the
policy form’s Changes condition, which limits how policy terms may be changed.

**H.0.10** You should explain that limits, deductibles, and endorsements shown on the
declarations govern the insured’s protection. This guidance rests on the policy form’s
Declarations provision because the declarations identify the applicable coverage
selections.

**H.0.11** You must not describe the loss of use limit as broader than the applicable
policy wording allows. This rule rests on the policy form’s Additional Coverages
provision because that provision defines the circumstances for loss of use payments.

**H.0.12** You should confirm the building code coverage percentage through the
applicable declarations and endorsement wording. This practice rests on the policy
form’s Ordinance or Law provision because eligibility and payment depend on the coverage
selected.

## H.2 — Roof Age and Condition

**H.2.1** You must evaluate roof age, roof surfacing condition, and evidence of
maintenance before accepting the risk. This guidance applies the Roof Age and Condition
provision because roof condition materially affects expected weather loss severity.

**H.2.2** You must obtain a roof inspection before binding when roof age is at or above
18 years. This requirement rests on the Roof Age and Condition provision because age
alone may not show whether the roof remains serviceable.

**H.2.3** You may rely on a recent inspection that clearly identifies the roof
surfacing, observed condition, and any recommended repairs. The Roof Age and Condition
provision supports this approach because underwriting needs evidence that the roof can
protect the dwelling.

**H.2.4** You must obtain clarification when the application, inspection, photographs,
or public records show conflicting roof information. This requirement follows the Roof
Age and Condition provision because inconsistent information prevents a reliable
eligibility decision.

**H.2.5** You may bind a risk with an older roof only when the available evidence
supports acceptable condition and remaining serviceability. The Roof Age and Condition
provision permits condition-based review because proper maintenance may affect loss
potential.

**H.2.6** You must not accept a risk when the roof shows active leakage, widespread
deterioration, or unrepaired storm damage. This restriction rests on the Roof Age and
Condition provision because existing damage is not an accidental future loss exposure.

**H.2.7** You must review roof photographs for missing, lifted, curled, cracked, or
deteriorated surfacing. The Roof Age and Condition provision requires this review
because visible defects may indicate susceptibility to wind and water intrusion.

**H.2.8** You should request photographs from more than one roof slope when available.
The Roof Age and Condition provision supports broader review because deterioration can
vary across the roof.

**H.2.9** You must evaluate the condition of flashing, valleys, penetrations, edges, and
other vulnerable roof details. This requirement follows the Roof Age and Condition
provision because water intrusion often begins at transitions rather than across the
field of the roof.

**H.2.10** You must consider whether repairs appear complete, compatible with the
existing roof, and professionally performed. The Roof Age and Condition provision
supports this review because poor repairs can create continuing water-entry exposure.

**H.2.11** You may accept localized repairs when the surrounding roof remains sound and
the repair does not indicate broad deterioration. This approach rests on the Roof Age
and Condition provision because limited damage may be manageable when the roof otherwise
performs as intended.

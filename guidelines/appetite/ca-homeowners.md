# California Homeowners Appetite Guide






## H.0 — Purpose and Status

**H.0.1** You use this Guide as internal underwriting and claims guidance for California homeowners business. It supports consistent decisions because the applicable policy provisions control the coverage outcome.

**H.0.2** You may rely on this Guide when evaluating whether a risk fits the intended appetite. It does not alter the insuring agreement, exclusions, conditions, or endorsements because those policy provisions govern the contract.

**H.0.3** You do not represent this Guide as a promise of coverage, a commitment to bind, or a claim settlement position. That restriction follows from the Policy Conditions provision because coverage depends on the issued policy and the facts presented.

**H.0.4** You apply the Guide together with the underwriting information available for the risk. This approach supports sound risk selection because the Declarations and policy provisions apply to the specific insured location and coverage selection.

**H.0.5** You may request clarification or supporting information when the submission does not show whether the risk meets the intended appetite. This practice follows the Concealment or Fraud condition because accurate information is necessary for a reliable underwriting decision.

**H.0.6** You do not use a general appetite statement to override a specific eligibility restriction or underwriting instruction. A specific rule controls because the applicable endorsement and policy provision may narrow the coverage offered.

**H.0.7** You review the Coverage B percentage with the property characteristics and coverage selection shown in the submission. That review is appropriate because the Other Structures provision determines how that coverage applies.

**H.0.8** You confirm the loss assessment additional coverage limit when evaluating condominium, cooperative, or association-related exposures. This review follows the Loss Assessment Additional Coverage provision because the assessment must arise from a covered circumstance.

**H.0.9** You refer claim questions involving a sworn statement deadline to the Duties After Loss provision. The timing requirement matters because the insured’s compliance with policy duties can affect claim handling.

**H.0.10** You evaluate the backup and sump overflow limit under the applicable Water Backup and Sump Overflow provision. This review is necessary because the endorsement or policy provision defines the covered source of loss and the available coverage.

**H.0.11** You do not infer coverage from the presence of a risk characteristic in this Guide. Coverage must be determined under the Perils Insured Against provision and applicable exclusions because eligibility review and claim coverage analysis serve different purposes.

**H.0.12** You document material underwriting observations in the underwriting record when a risk is accepted, declined, or referred. Clear documentation supports consistent application because the Underwriting Information condition relies on complete and accurate risk information.

**H.0.13** You refer unusual, incomplete, or potentially conflicting information for review rather than resolving uncertainty through assumptions. Referral is appropriate because the policy application and Concealment or Fraud condition require dependable underwriting facts.

**H.0.14** You use plain, noncontractual language when communicating the Guide’s purpose to producers or insureds. This practice avoids creating unintended coverage expectations because the policy’s Insuring Agreement and conditions state the contractual obligations.

**H.0.15** You apply the Guide as an operational aid and not as a substitute for professional judgment. Judgment remains necessary because the applicable policy provisions must be applied to the actual risk facts.

**H.0.16** You preserve the distinction between underwriting acceptance and claim coverage determination. That distinction follows from the Insuring Agreement, exclusions, and conditions because a risk may be acceptable for underwriting while a particular loss remains uncovered.

**H.2.1** You must obtain an acceptable roof inspection before binding when the roof age is 20 years or greater. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because roof aging may not be apparent from application information alone.

**H.2.2** You may rely on a recent inspection only when it identifies the roof covering, visible condition, and material defects. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because a general property observation may not address roof-specific loss potential.

**H.2.3** You must review roof photographs with the inspection whenever photographs are available. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because photographs help confirm whether the written observations match visible conditions.

**H.2.4** You must decline a risk with an actively leaking roof unless documented repairs are completed before binding. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because an active leak indicates unresolved exposure to ensuing water damage.

**H.2.5** You must not bind where roof repair work is incomplete, unless underwriting approves the risk after reviewing the scope and expected completion. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because unfinished work can leave the dwelling exposed to weather-related loss.

**H.2.6** You must refer a roof with missing, lifted, broken, curled, or displaced surfacing for underwriting review. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because these conditions may allow wind or water intrusion.

**H.2.7** You must refer a roof showing widespread granule loss, surface cracking, splitting, or exposed substrate. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because surface deterioration can reduce the roof’s ability to shed water.

**H.2.8** You must refer a roof with visible sagging, deflection, or irregular roof planes. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because these conditions may indicate structural concerns or long-term moisture damage.

**H.2.9** You must not rely solely on a homeowner’s statement that the roof is in good condition. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because condition determinations require objective underwriting evidence.

**H.2.10** You may accept a contractor statement when it identifies the observed condition and any recommended repairs. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because a meaningful contractor statement provides support for the underwriting decision.

**H.2.11** You must refer contractor statements that recommend replacement, substantial repair, or further investigation. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because a recommendation for additional work may identify an unresolved loss exposure.

**H.2.12** You must verify that repairs described in inspection materials appear complete before binding. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because proposed repairs do not remove an existing condition concern.

**H.2.13** You may accept repair evidence that clearly identifies the affected roof area and completed work. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because vague repair descriptions may not establish that the reported defect was corrected.

**H.2.14** You must refer roofs with patchwork repairs that suggest recurring leaks or deferred maintenance. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because repeated repairs can indicate broader deterioration beneath the visible surface.

**H.2.20** You must refer roofs with evidence of ponding water, blocked drainage, or persistent moisture accumulation. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because standing water may accelerate deterioration and contribute to leakage.

The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because these roof systems may deteriorate in ways not visible from ground-level observations.

**H.2.22** You must not treat a roof replacement estimate as evidence that replacement has been completed. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because an estimate reflects anticipated work rather than the current insurable condition.

**H.2.23** You may bind after a documented roof replacement when the evidence identifies the completed installation and supports acceptable condition. The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because completed replacement can resolve prior condition concerns when adequately documented.

The Roof Condition underwriting provision supports this requirement because age uncertainty combined with limited condition evidence prevents a reliable eligibility assessment.

## H.4 — Water Backup

**H.4.1** You may consider water backup exposure only when the applicant provides a clear description of the dwelling’s drainage and sump arrangements. This review is required by the Water Backup endorsement insuring agreement because covered loss depends on the source and path of the water.

**H.4.2** You must distinguish water that enters through a sewer, drain, sump, or similar system from water that enters through an opening in the building envelope. This distinction follows the Water Backup endorsement definition of covered backup because surface-water and flood causes are addressed separately.

**H.4.3** You may bind the risk when the dwelling has ordinary plumbing and drainage features that can be reasonably inspected and maintained. This approach rests on the Water Backup endorsement conditions because preventable maintenance failures create adverse loss potential.

**H.4.4** You must obtain underwriting review when the applicant reports recurring drainage obstruction, sewage discharge, or sump malfunction. This referral follows the Water Backup endorsement loss-causation provision because repeated events may indicate an ongoing condition rather than an accidental occurrence.

**H.4.5** You should document whether the dwelling is served by a public sewer, private septic system, or other wastewater arrangement. This information is material under the Water Backup endorsement insuring agreement because the service arrangement can affect the probable source of a backup.

**H.4.6** You may request information about recent plumbing repairs, drain cleaning, sewer-line work, and sump replacement. This inquiry is supported by the Water Backup endorsement conditions because recent work may reveal unresolved drainage concerns.

**H.4.7** You must refer a risk with known sewage odor, persistent slow drains, or visible wastewater staining for additional review. This rule rests on the Water Backup endorsement loss-causation provision because those conditions can indicate a preexisting drainage impairment.

**H.4.8** You should identify finished living areas that are below grade and determine whether they contain susceptible flooring, walls, or personal property. This review follows the Water Backup endorsement insuring agreement because backup losses often concentrate in below-grade improvements.

**H.4.9** Apply the backwater valve requirement for finished areas below grade when the risk presents drainage conditions that warrant protective equipment. This requirement rests on the Water Backup endorsement conditions because a properly functioning valve can reduce reverse flow into vulnerable areas.

**H.4.10** You must verify that any reported backwater valve is maintained and capable of operation. This verification is required by the Water Backup endorsement conditions because an inoperative protective device does not reduce the expected loss exposure.

This decision rests on the Water Backup endorsement underwriting basis because a complete file supports consistent risk selection.

**H.5.1** You must evaluate prior loss information before offering terms or confirming eligibility. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because prior losses may indicate unresolved conditions or a recurring source of damage.

**H.5.2** Obtain available loss history from the applicant, prior carrier information, consumer reports, and underwriting records. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because complete information supports a consistent eligibility decision.

**H.5.3** You must review loss history for 5 years years before determining whether referral is needed. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because recent experience is relevant to the condition and maintenance of the insured property.

**H.5.4** You must refer a risk when the count of paid property claims reaches 2. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because paid property losses may reflect a material underwriting concern.

**H.5.5** Treat paid claims for dwelling, other structures, personal property, loss of use, or related property coverages as property claims. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because the damaged interest may reveal conditions affecting future property exposure.

**H.5.6** Do not treat an inquiry, a report without payment, or a withdrawn notice as a paid property claim unless the loss record confirms payment. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because a claim inquiry alone does not establish incurred property damage.

**H.5.7** Review the cause of every reported property loss, even when the loss does not require referral based on claim count. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because cause of loss may identify an unacceptable hazard or needed corrective action.

**H.5.8** Obtain a clear explanation for losses involving water, fire, theft, vandalism, wind, or other recurring damage causes. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because the explanation helps determine whether the underlying condition remains present.

**H.5.9** Request repair documentation when prior damage could affect the current condition of the dwelling or insured location. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because repaired damage may still present exposure if work was incomplete or inadequate.

**H.5.10** Confirm whether repairs were completed by a qualified contractor when the loss involved structural, electrical, plumbing, roofing, or wildfire-related damage. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because these conditions can materially affect future loss potential.

**H.5.11** Document the insured’s explanation of each prior loss in the underwriting file. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because referral review requires a clear record of the facts considered.

**H.5.12** Refer losses with an unclear cause, incomplete repair information, or inconsistent reporting. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because uncertainty can prevent an accurate evaluation of the property condition.

**H.5.13** Do not assume that a weather-related loss is isolated without support from the loss record or property information. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because repeated weather losses may reflect maintenance, location, or construction concerns.

**H.5.14** Refer wildfire-related losses when available information suggests unresolved vegetation, access, construction, or protection concerns. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because wildfire exposure must be evaluated based on the current property condition.

**H.5.15** Review water losses for the source of water, affected areas, completed repairs, and any mitigation work. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because water damage may recur when the source or repair remains unresolved.

**H.5.16** Refer water losses involving repeated leakage, seepage, backup, overflow, or moisture damage. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because these losses may indicate continuing maintenance or plumbing issues.

**H.5.17** Review fire losses for ignition source, extent of damage, repair status, and changes to the risk after the loss. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because fire losses may expose electrical, heating, occupancy, or maintenance concerns.

**H.5.18** Refer theft or vandalism losses when the circumstances suggest an ongoing vacancy, security, access, or neighborhood concern. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because the same conditions may increase the likelihood of future loss.

**H.5.19** Consider losses occurring at the insured location separately from losses at another property owned or occupied by the applicant. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because location-specific losses may be more relevant to the present risk.

**H.5.20** Do not disregard losses at another location when the loss cause reflects the applicant’s maintenance practices or occupancy pattern. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because applicant behavior can remain relevant across properties.

**H.5.21** Verify whether the applicant owned, occupied, rented, or otherwise controlled the property at the time of a reported loss. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because responsibility for maintenance and repairs may affect the underwriting assessment.

**H.5.22** Refer a risk when the applicant cannot explain a reported paid property loss or cannot provide requested supporting information. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because missing information prevents confirmation of the current exposure.

**H.5.23** Do not bind a referred risk unless underwriting provides an approval or other documented disposition. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because referral authority is required before accepting a risk outside routine handling.

**H.5.24** Include the loss date, cause, payment status, damaged property, repair status, and applicant explanation in the referral submission. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because underwriting needs sufficient facts to assess the risk without speculation.

**H.5.25** Include photographs, inspection findings, contractor invoices, repair receipts, or mitigation evidence when available. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because supporting records can confirm that corrective work has addressed the loss cause.

**H.5.26** Explain any discrepancy between the application, loss report, inspection information, and applicant statement. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because inconsistent information may indicate an incomplete understanding of the exposure.

**H.5.27** Refer suspected nondisclosure of prior losses or material loss details. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because accurate disclosure is necessary for proper risk selection.

**H.5.28** Do not alter loss descriptions to avoid referral or to make a risk appear eligible. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because underwriting decisions must rely on the actual loss circumstances.

**H.5.29** You may request additional information when the reported loss appears unrelated to the current property condition but the record is incomplete. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because an apparently isolated loss may require verification before it can be treated as such.

**H.5.30** Refer losses involving unrepaired damage, open claims, pending recovery, or unresolved disputes affecting the property. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because unresolved matters can change the condition, value, or insurability of the risk.

**H.5.31** Consider whether prior loss repairs introduced new materials, systems, or construction features requiring further review. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because changes made after a loss may alter the property exposure.

**H.5.32** Confirm that required permits, inspections, or approvals were obtained when repairs involved work subject to local requirements. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because unverified work may create safety or property condition concerns.

**H.5.33** Refer losses involving foundation movement, slope damage, drainage issues, earth movement, or other site conditions. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because these conditions may continue to affect the dwelling and surrounding structures.

**H.5.34** Refer losses involving roof damage when the roof condition, repair quality, or remaining vulnerability cannot be confirmed. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because roof condition is material to protection against future weather damage.

**H.5.35** Review claims involving service lines, appliances, heating equipment, or electrical systems for evidence of completed correction. This rule rests on the Prior Losses and Referral provision because failure of these systems may recur if the cause was not remedied.

## H.6 — Handling Procedures

**H.6.1** Open a claim file promptly when you receive notice of a potentially covered loss. The Notice of Loss provision supports prompt intake because early handling preserves facts and helps prevent avoidable damage.

**H.6.2** Record the reported cause of loss, location, date of discovery, affected property, and the insured’s initial description in the claim file. The Duties After Loss provision supports complete documentation because coverage analysis depends on reliable loss facts.

**H.6.3** Confirm the named insured, risk address, policy status, and applicable coverages before providing coverage direction. The Insuring Agreement supports this review because only an insured loss at an insured location may proceed under the policy.

**H.6.4** Provide the insured with clear contact information and explain the claim-handling process in plain language. The Claim Handling provision supports clear communication because the insured must understand what information and cooperation are needed.

**H.6.5** Track the acknowledgment of claim deadline from the date notice is received. The Claim Handling provision supports timely acknowledgment because delayed contact may impair mitigation, investigation, and customer service.

**H.6.6** Advise the insured to protect the property from further damage and to retain damaged property when practical for inspection. The Duties After Loss provision supports this direction because the condition of the property may be material to both mitigation and causation.

**H.6.7** You must begin reasonable mitigation within 7 days after discovery of damage. The Duties After Loss provision supports this requirement because preventable worsening damage is not handled as though it resulted from the original event.

**H.6.8** Distinguish emergency protective work from permanent repair work when discussing mitigation with the insured. The Duties After Loss provision supports this distinction because reasonable emergency measures may be necessary before coverage and scope are fully resolved.

**H.6.9** Request photographs, videos, invoices, receipts, and other available evidence of the loss and mitigation efforts. The Duties After Loss provision supports this request because supporting materials help establish the timing, cause, extent, and reasonableness of claimed damage.

**H.6.10** Arrange an inspection when the reported facts, claimed damage, or available evidence require field evaluation. The Inspection provision supports inspection because a sound coverage decision must rest on an adequate investigation.

**H.6.11** Use a qualified representative for inspections involving complex damage, disputed causation, specialty construction, or potential safety concerns. The Inspection provision supports appropriate expertise because unsupported conclusions can produce inaccurate claim outcomes.

**H.6.12** Do not direct a contractor, vendor, or mitigation firm unless the applicable claim-handling process authorizes that involvement. The Claim Handling provision supports neutral vendor communication because the insured remains responsible for selecting service providers unless the company assumes that role.

**H.6.13** Document all material communications with the insured, representatives, vendors, and witnesses in the claim file. The Claim Handling provision supports complete notes because the file must show the basis for material decisions and communications.

**H.6.14** Explain that emergency repairs should be reasonable and supported by available records. The Duties After Loss provision supports this guidance because the insurer may need to evaluate whether the work was necessary to protect covered property.

**H.6.15** Review the cause of loss before authorizing payment for repair, replacement, cleanup, or additional living expense. The Perils Insured Against provision supports this review because the reported condition may arise from an excluded, limited, or noncovered cause.

**H.6.16** Separate resulting damage from the condition that caused the loss when evaluating coverage. The Perils Insured Against provision supports this analysis because coverage may differ between ensuing property damage and an excluded source condition.

**H.6.17** Consider wear, deterioration, maintenance conditions, construction defects, and repeated seepage when the facts indicate those issues. The Exclusions provision supports this review because excluded conditions may affect some or all of the claimed damage.

**H.6.18** Obtain a clear chronology when the loss may involve gradual damage, intermittent leaks, prior repairs, or earlier notices of loss. The Duties After Loss provision supports chronology development because timing may determine whether damage resulted from a covered event.

**H.6.19** Review prior claims, underwriting information, inspections, and known property conditions that are available in the file. The Concealment or Fraud provision supports this review because inconsistent information may require further investigation before coverage is determined.

**H.6.20** Refer suspected material misrepresentation, altered documentation, inflated invoices, or intentional damage through the established escalation process. The Concealment or Fraud provision supports escalation because coverage may be affected when material information is false or intentionally concealed.

**H.6.21** Do not accuse an insured or claimant of fraud without documented facts and appropriate internal review. The Concealment or Fraud provision supports measured communication because an allegation must be based on evidence rather than suspicion.

**H.6.22** Request a signed proof of loss when the claim circumstances or policy conditions require it. The Proof of Loss provision supports this request because the insured’s sworn statement may be necessary to evaluate the amount and basis of the claim.

**H.6.23** Explain the proof of loss period when requesting a proof of loss from the insured. The Proof of Loss provision supports this explanation because the insured must have a clear opportunity to satisfy applicable policy conditions.

**H.6.24** Evaluate repair estimates for scope, labor, materials, code-related work, and depreciation treatment when applicable. The Loss Settlement provision supports estimate review because payment must reflect the policy’s applicable valuation method.

**H.6.25** Confirm whether damaged property is repaired, replaced, or not yet restored before applying any replacement cost conditions. The Loss Settlement provision supports this confirmation because payment eligibility may depend on the insured’s completed or intended restoration.

**H.6.26** Apply the minimum Section I deductible to covered property losses as required by the policy. The Deductible provision supports consistent application because the deductible is the insured’s retained portion of a covered loss.

**H.6.27** Do not apply a deductible to an excluded loss as though coverage existed. The Deductible provision supports this approach because a deductible adjusts covered loss payments rather than creating coverage for an excluded condition.

**H.6.28** Evaluate additional living expense only after confirming that a covered loss made the residence unfit to live in. The Loss of Use provision supports this review because inconvenience alone does not establish a covered loss of use.

**H.6.29** Request reasonable documentation of temporary housing, meals, transportation, and other claimed increased living costs when applicable. The Loss of Use provision supports documentation because payment is limited to covered increases caused by the loss.

**H.6.30** Evaluate personal property claims by identifying ownership, condition, age, and the applicable settlement basis. The Coverage C provision supports this evaluation because personal property payment depends on covered ownership and valuation.

**H.6.31** Ask the insured to identify any property that may belong to another person, be used for business, or be subject to a special limit. The Coverage C provision supports this inquiry because ownership, use, and property type can affect coverage.

**H.6.32** Preserve subrogation opportunities by identifying responsible parties, retaining relevant evidence, and avoiding releases without review. The Transfer of Rights of Recovery provision supports this practice because payment may create recovery rights against a responsible party.

**H.6.33** Refer losses involving possible liability, injury, third-party property damage, or a demand for defense to the appropriate liability handler. The Coverage E provision supports referral because liability coverage requires separate evaluation of defense and indemnity obligations.

**H.6.34** Issue coverage communications that identify the facts reviewed, applicable policy language, and the basis for the decision. The Claim Handling provision supports clear written decisions because the insured must be able to understand the outcome and any remaining requirements.

**H.6.35** Explain partial denials and limitations separately from covered portions of a claim. The Exclusions provision supports separate explanation because a mixed claim may contain both covered damage and damage that is not covered.

**H.6.36** Do not delay undisputed payment while investigating unrelated disputed portions of the claim. The Loss Settlement provision supports prompt payment of covered amounts because unresolved issues should not prevent settlement of amounts already established.

**H.6.37** Maintain a diary for outstanding documents, inspections, estimates, communications, and coverage decisions. The Claim Handling provision supports active follow-up because an unattended file can delay a fair and complete investigation.

**H.6.38** Explain the legal action deadline when communicating a final coverage position or claim resolution. The Suit Against Us provision supports this communication because the insured should understand conditions affecting the ability to pursue an action.

**H.6.39** Close the claim only after documenting the final payment, denial, withdrawal, settlement, or other disposition. The Claim Handling provision supports complete closure documentation because the file must show that all material claim obligations were addressed.

## H.7 — Authority Limits

**H.7.1** You may bind a risk when Coverage A does not exceed $1,000,000 under the Line Authority provision. This limit preserves referral control for risks that may require broader underwriting review.

**H.7.2** You must refer a risk when the requested Coverage A exceeds your binding authority under the Authority Limits provision. Referral allows the carrier to evaluate exposures outside delegated authority.

**H.7.3** You must evaluate the requested Coverage A before offering any bindable terms under the Coverage A Valuation provision. This review prevents an authority decision based on incomplete replacement-cost information.

**H.7.4** You may rely on supported valuation information when applying the Line Authority provision. Reliable valuation supports consistent decisions and reduces avoidable referral activity.

**H.7.5** You must not reduce a stated Coverage A solely to bring a risk within your authority under the Coverage Adequacy provision. Adequate insurance remains necessary even when the requested limit exceeds your authority.

**H.7.6** You must refer a risk with valuation information that appears incomplete, inconsistent, or unsupported under the Underwriting Information provision. Referral protects the carrier from binding on an unreliable exposure estimate.

**H.7.7** You may bind only the coverages and endorsements authorized by the Authority Limits provision. This restriction ensures that delegated authority matches the carrier's approved product scope.

**H.7.8** You must refer any requested coverage change that is not clearly authorized under the Endorsement Authority provision. Referral prevents an unauthorized alteration of the intended policy terms.

**H.7.9** You must apply underwriting eligibility rules before determining whether a risk is within your authority under the Eligibility provision. A risk within the Coverage A limit may still require review because of its underlying characteristics.

**H.7.10** You must not bind an ineligible risk merely because its Coverage A falls within your authority under the Eligibility provision. Binding authority does not override the carrier's eligibility requirements.

**H.7.11** You may use approved underwriting tools to confirm whether a risk fits delegated authority under the Underwriting Verification provision. Verification helps identify material conditions that are not apparent from the application alone.

**H.7.12** You must refer a risk when available information conflicts with the application under the Underwriting Verification provision. Referral is necessary because unresolved conflicts can materially affect acceptance or terms.

**H.7.13** You must document the basis for each authority decision under the File Documentation provision. Clear documentation allows later review of the underwriting judgment and supporting facts.

**H.7.14** You must retain the valuation basis and relevant risk information supporting a bind decision under the File Documentation provision. The file must show why the risk was eligible for delegated handling.

**H.7.15** You may request clarification from the producer before making an authority decision under the Producer Information provision. Clarification reduces the risk of binding on assumptions rather than verified facts.

**H.7.16** You must not treat missing information as favorable information under the Producer Information provision. Unknown conditions may affect eligibility, coverage, or the proper referral path.

**H.7.17** You must refer any risk involving an exception to ordinary underwriting requirements under the Exception Authority provision. Exception handling requires approval beyond routine delegated authority.

**H.7.18** You must recognize that ceilings are set by the state exception ruleceilings are set by the state exception rule (see Personal Lines Underwriting Manual, R520 Rule 520 — California State Exceptions). This rule ensures that exception decisions remain within California-specific underwriting controls.

**H.7.19** You may bind a risk with ordinary characteristics when all required information supports eligibility under the Standard Risk provision. Routine risks may be handled efficiently when no exception is involved.

**H.7.20** You must refer a risk with an unusual occupancy, construction feature, or loss concern under the Risk Characteristics provision. These characteristics may alter the carrier's expected exposure beyond standard handling.

**H.7.21** You must not divide related property interests or coverage requests to avoid a referral under the Aggregation provision. Related exposures must be evaluated together because their combined risk may exceed delegated intent.

**H.7.22** You must consider all material information known at the time of binding under the Material Facts provision. A complete review prevents authority decisions that omit relevant exposure details.

**H.7.23** You must refer a risk when material facts change before policy issuance under the Change in Risk provision. Changed facts may affect whether the prior authority decision remains appropriate.

**H.7.24** You may withdraw unissued terms when new information shows that the risk is outside your authority under the Change in Risk provision. Withdrawal prevents issuance based on superseded underwriting facts.

**H.7.25** You must obtain required approvals before representing that a referred risk is accepted under the Approval Communication provision. This control prevents producers or applicants from relying on unapproved indications.

**H.7.26** You must communicate referral status accurately under the Approval Communication provision. Accurate communication avoids creating expectations that exceed underwriting authority.

**H.7.27** You must not alter required underwriting statements, valuation data, or eligibility responses to obtain a bindable result under the Integrity provision. Accurate information is essential to a valid authority decision.

**H.7.28** You must refer any situation in which application information appears intentionally misleading under the Integrity provision. Suspected misrepresentation requires review because it may affect acceptability and coverage terms.

**H.7.29** You may bind only after confirming that required underwriting conditions are satisfied under the Binding Conditions provision. Conditions protect the carrier from accepting a risk before essential controls are in place.

**H.7.30** You must refer a risk when a required condition cannot be confirmed under the Binding Conditions provision. Unconfirmed conditions leave the carrier without support for delegated acceptance.

**H.7.31** You must use current approved guidance when applying authority limits under the Current Guidance provision. Current guidance promotes consistent treatment of California homeowners risks.

**H.7.32** You must not rely on informal direction that conflicts with approved authority guidance under the Current Guidance provision. Informal direction may not reflect the carrier's authorized underwriting position.

**H.7.33** You must escalate uncertainty about authority before binding under the Escalation provision. Escalation protects the carrier when the available guidance does not clearly resolve the issue.

**H.7.34** This guidance is an internal underwriting control and does not create, amend, or interpret a policy contract under the Authority Limits provision. The rule supports disciplined underwriting decisions rather than policyholder rights.

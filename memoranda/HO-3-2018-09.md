## M.2 — Changes to Definitions

**M.2.1** The prior definition of “residence premises” could apply differently where ownership and occupancy were not aligned. The definition was revised to describe the required relationship between the insured and the dwelling, which clarifies when the premises qualify and reduces uncertainty in claim handling.

**M.2.2** The prior definition of “insured” did not fully distinguish persons whose status arises from residency from persons whose status arises from legal responsibility. The definition was revised to identify those categories separately, which clarifies the scope of persons entitled to coverage and the reason their status applies.

**M.2.3** The prior definition of “you” could be read without regard to the named insured’s status under the policy. The definition was revised to connect the term to the named insured and the named insured’s spouse where applicable, which clarifies who is subject to policy duties and entitled to policy benefits.

**M.2.4** The prior definition of “bodily injury” did not expressly describe the treatment of related consequences arising from physical harm. The definition was revised to address those consequences within the defined term, which clarifies the types of injury-related claims evaluated under the policy.

**M.2.5** The prior definition of “property damage” did not clearly differentiate physical injury to tangible property from loss of use of that property. The definition was revised to state both circumstances, which clarifies when damage to property or the inability to use property falls within the term.

**M.2.6** The prior definition of “occurrence” could be interpreted inconsistently when damage resulted from repeated exposure to similar conditions. The definition was revised to address accidental events and continuous or repeated exposure, which clarifies the event or condition used to evaluate coverage.

**M.2.7** The prior definition of “business” did not clearly identify activities conducted for compensation or economic gain. The definition was revised to describe those activities more directly, which clarifies the distinction between personal activities and activities subject to business-related policy provisions.

**M.2.8** The prior definition of “business” could leave uncertainty regarding incidental activities performed away from the residence premises. The definition was revised to address the character of the activity rather than its location alone, which supports consistent application of the definition.

**M.2.9** The prior definition of “insured location” did not describe all locations associated with the residence premises in consistent terms. The definition was revised to organize the included locations by their relationship to the insured premises, which clarifies where applicable coverages may apply.

**M.2.10** The prior definition of “insured location” could be read to include locations without a sufficient connection to the insured’s residential use. The definition was revised to specify the required connection, which clarifies the intended scope of covered locations.

**M.2.11** The prior definition of “motor vehicle” did not clearly distinguish vehicles intended for land use from other conveyances. The definition was revised to describe the relevant vehicle characteristics, which clarifies the property subject to motor vehicle provisions.

**M.2.12** The prior definition of “motor vehicle” did not expressly address vehicles used in connection with residential activities. The definition was revised to identify the circumstances relevant to that use, which clarifies whether the vehicle remains subject to the definition.

**M.2.13** The prior definition of “watercraft” could be interpreted differently based on the watercraft’s method of propulsion or use. The definition was revised to use consistent descriptive language, which clarifies the watercraft evaluated under applicable policy provisions.

**M.2.14** The prior definition of “aircraft” did not clearly address devices capable of flight that may not be traditionally operated aircraft. The definition was revised to describe the relevant characteristics of those devices, which clarifies the property included within the term.

**M.2.15** The prior definition of “vacant” did not adequately distinguish a dwelling that is unoccupied from a dwelling lacking customary contents. The definition was revised to address both occupancy and contents, which clarifies when vacancy-related provisions apply during a vacant dwelling period.

**M.2.16** The prior definition of “collapse” could be read to include conditions involving deterioration without a substantial failure of the structure. The definition was revised to describe the degree of structural failure required, which clarifies when collapse-related coverage may be considered.

**M.2.17** The prior definition of “fungi” did not consistently group related organic conditions within the same term. The definition was revised to identify the related conditions addressed by the policy, which clarifies the scope of provisions that use the term.

**M.2.18** The prior definition of “pollutant” did not clearly describe the materials treated as contaminants or irritants. The definition was revised to use broader descriptive language for those materials, which clarifies the application of pollution-related provisions.

**M.2.19** The prior definition of “remodeling” could be interpreted to include routine maintenance activities. The definition was revised to distinguish alterations from ordinary upkeep, which clarifies the work considered when applying provisions involving construction or repair.

**M.2.20** The prior definition of “rebuilding cost” did not clearly identify the basis used to evaluate restoration of damaged property. The definition was revised to describe restoration using comparable materials and workmanship, which clarifies the valuation concept applied to eligible property damage.

**M.2.21** The prior wording did not identify the eighty percent condition within the definitions in a manner that clearly connected it to applicable valuation provisions. The definition was revised to identify the eighty percent condition, which clarifies the condition’s meaning when the related provisions are applied.

**M.2.22** The prior wording did not clearly describe the circumstances used to determine the storm period duration. The definition was revised to identify the event-related circumstances relevant to the storm period duration, which supports consistent evaluation of related claims.

**M.2.23** The prior definitions used terms that could overlap when describing damage, loss, and resulting conditions. The definitions were revised to use coordinated terminology throughout, which clarifies the meaning of those terms wherever they are used in the policy.

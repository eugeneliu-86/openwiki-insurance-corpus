# North Carolina Bulletin NCDOI-2021-06 — Claims Handling Standards




## B.1 — Purpose and Applicability

**B.1.1** This section states claims handling standards applicable to an admitted insurer conducting insurance business in North Carolina. An insurer must administer covered claims in a manner consistent with the duties described in this section.

**B.1.2** These standards apply to claim handling activity undertaken by an insurer, including activity performed through an employee, adjuster, producer, administrator, or other representative acting for the insurer. An insurer remains responsible for compliance when claim handling functions are delegated.

**B.1.3** This section applies to first-party and third-party claims arising under insurance policies issued, delivered, or administered in North Carolina, to the extent the insurer is responsible for handling the claim. It also applies when a claimant communicates with the insurer through an authorized representative.

**B.1.4** An insurer must apply these standards throughout the claim handling process. The process includes receipt of a claim, investigation, evaluation, communication, settlement, denial, payment, and file documentation.

**B.1.5** These standards are effective upon issuance and apply prospectively to claim handling undertaken thereafter. An insurer must not treat this section as limiting a duty otherwise imposed by applicable law or by the insurance contract.

**B.1.6** This section establishes minimum conduct standards for an insurer. An insurer may adopt claim practices that provide greater protection to an insured or claimant when those practices are consistent with applicable law.

**B.1.7** An insurer must administer a claim in good faith and with reasonable care. An insurer must not use a claim practice that is unfair, deceptive, arbitrary, or intended to avoid a proper claim obligation.

**B.1.8** An insurer must provide communications that are reasonably clear and responsive to the matter presented. An insurer must give an insured or claimant information reasonably necessary to understand the claim handling process.

**B.1.9** An insurer must evaluate coverage based on the policy terms, available facts, and applicable law. An insurer must not misrepresent pertinent policy provisions or material facts relating to a claim.

**B.1.10** An insurer must maintain claim records sufficient to show the basis for material claim handling decisions. The records must reasonably identify communications, investigative activity, evaluations, and claim payments or denials.

**B.1.11** An insurer must apply policy deductibles, limits, conditions, exclusions, and endorsements as required by the insurance contract and applicable law. This section does not alter the coverage provided by a policy.

**B.1.12** Nothing in this section establishes the AOP deductible floor or determines how that concept is applied under a policy. An insurer must administer any applicable deductible according to the policy and applicable law.

**B.1.13** Nothing in this section establishes the replacement cost threshold percentage or determines eligibility for replacement cost treatment. An insurer must evaluate replacement cost provisions according to the policy and applicable law.

**B.1.14** Nothing in this section establishes the Coverage D limit as a percentage of Coverage A or alters any policy limit. An insurer must apply applicable coverage limits according to the policy and applicable law.

**B.1.15** An insurer must ensure that personnel responsible for claims have authority and training appropriate to their assigned functions. An insurer must provide reasonable supervision of claim handling activity performed on its behalf.

**B.1.16** An insurer may use automated systems, estimating tools, or other technology in claim handling. An insurer must ensure that use of such tools does not prevent a fair evaluation of the individual claim.

**B.1.17** The Department may review an insurer's claim practices for compliance with these standards. An insurer must make claim information available to the Department when required by applicable law.

## B.2 — Requirements

**B.2.1** An insurer authorized to transact insurance in this State must apply these requirements to the handling of claims arising under its policies. An insurer must administer claims practices fairly, promptly, and in good faith.

**B.2.2** An insurer must establish written claim-handling procedures that are reasonably designed to promote compliance with these requirements. The procedures must be available to personnel responsible for claim handling.

**B.2.3** An insurer must maintain adequate supervision over employees, adjusters, and representatives who handle claims on its behalf. Supervisory review must address compliance with policy terms and applicable law.

**B.2.4** An insurer must create and maintain a claim file that reasonably documents material claim activity. The file must identify communications, investigations, evaluations, payments, and material decisions.

**B.2.5** An insurer must acknowledge a claim communication with reasonable promptness. An insurer must provide the claimant with sufficient information concerning the next steps in the claim process.

**B.2.6** An insurer must respond to reasonable communications from a claimant, insured, beneficiary, or authorized representative. A response must be clear, accurate, and relevant to the matter raised.

**B.2.7** An insurer must identify applicable policy provisions before denying or limiting coverage. An insurer must not rely upon a policy provision that does not support the position taken.

**B.2.8** An insurer must conduct a reasonable investigation before refusing to pay a claim or any portion of a claim. The investigation must be appropriate to the nature of the loss and the information available.

**B.2.9** An insurer must not require an insured or claimant to provide information that is not reasonably necessary to evaluate the claim. An insurer must explain the purpose of requested information when the purpose is not apparent.

**B.2.10** An insurer must evaluate a claim using information that is reasonably reliable and relevant. An insurer must not disregard material information without documenting a reasonable basis for doing so.

**B.2.11** An insurer must promptly pay any undisputed claim amount when payment is due under the policy. An insurer must not withhold an undisputed amount solely because another portion of the claim remains under review.

**B.2.12** An insurer must provide a written explanation when it denies a claim, limits payment, or reserves rights based on a policy provision. The explanation must identify the relevant policy language and the material facts supporting the decision.

**B.2.13** An insurer must communicate a reservation of rights in a manner that does not mislead the insured or claimant. A reservation of rights must state the basis for the insurer's continuing investigation or coverage concern.

**B.2.14** An insurer must not misrepresent pertinent facts, policy provisions, or the status of a claim. An insurer must ensure that claim communications accurately describe the insurer's position.

**B.2.15** An insurer must not use forms, letters, or other communications that are reasonably likely to confuse or mislead a claimant. An insurer must review standardized claim communications for clarity and accuracy.

**B.2.16** An insurer must provide a reasonable explanation of the documents or information needed to complete its evaluation. An insurer must not repeatedly request the same information unless a reasonable need exists.

**B.2.17** An insurer must reasonably assist an insured or claimant who seeks information about the claim process. Assistance must not be conditioned upon acceptance of a settlement offer.

**B.2.18** An insurer must communicate settlement offers in a manner that clearly states the purpose and effect of the offer. An insurer must not represent that an offer is final unless the insurer has a reasonable basis for that representation.

**B.2.19** An insurer must not compel an insured or claimant to accept a settlement by engaging in conduct that is unfair or deceptive. An insurer must allow a reasonable opportunity to review a proposed settlement and seek advice.

**B.2.20** An insurer must not request a release that is broader than reasonably necessary to resolve the claim being settled. An insurer must explain any material limitation created by a release.

**B.2.21** An insurer must support property damage evaluations with a reasonable basis in the claim file. An insurer must identify material differences between an insurer estimate and information submitted by an insured or claimant.

**B.2.22** An insurer must not require repair of damaged property by a particular vendor as a condition of payment, except as permitted by applicable law. An insurer must not misrepresent an insured's ability to select a repair provider.

**B.2.23** An insurer must communicate material repair requirements in clear language. An insurer must not direct repairs that are inconsistent with applicable policy obligations.

**B.2.24** An insurer must explain the basis for any deduction from a property payment. The explanation must identify the information used to calculate the deduction.

**B.2.25** An insurer must not apply depreciation unless the policy permits the deduction and the deduction is reasonably supported. The claim file must document the basis for material depreciation decisions.

**B.2.26** An insurer must handle salvage in a manner consistent with the policy and the interests of the insured. An insurer must explain any transfer of salvage rights that affects payment or possession.

**B.2.27** An insurer must not delay payment of a covered loss solely to preserve a potential subrogation interest. An insurer may protect a subrogation interest through reasonable claim procedures that do not unfairly burden the insured.

**B.2.28** An insurer must evaluate liability claims based upon a reasonable assessment of available facts, damages, and defenses. An insurer must not reject a liability claim without documenting the basis for its position.

**B.2.29** An insurer must treat communications from third-party claimants fairly and accurately. An insurer must not advise a third-party claimant against seeking independent advice.

**B.2.30** An insurer must handle bodily injury claims with appropriate regard for the claimant's reported condition and available supporting information. An insurer must not minimize a reported injury without a reasonable factual basis.

**B.2.31** An insurer must handle claims involving a deceased person with reasonable care and sensitivity. An insurer must identify the information reasonably needed to determine the proper recipient of payment.

**B.2.32** An insurer must continue to evaluate a claim reasonably after litigation begins. Litigation must not be used as a basis to discontinue communications or claim activity that remain appropriate.

**B.2.33** An insurer that retains counsel must provide counsel with material claim information relevant to the assigned matter. An insurer must maintain responsibility for claim-handling conduct performed on its behalf.

**B.2.34** An insurer may use electronic communications when permitted by applicable law and consistent with the preferences of the recipient. Electronic communications must be retained in the claim file when material to claim handling.

**B.2.35** An insurer must protect nonpublic personal information obtained during claim handling. Access to claim information must be limited to persons with a legitimate business need.

**B.2.36** An insurer must take reasonable steps to communicate effectively with persons who have limited ability to understand the insurer's customary claim communications. An insurer must not use communication barriers to delay or discourage a claim.

**B.2.37** An insurer must maintain procedures for handling claims arising from widespread loss events. Those procedures must support consistent evaluations and clear communications with affected policyholders.

**B.2.38** An insurer must allocate personnel and resources reasonably when claim volume increases. Resource allocation must not result in unfair discrimination among similarly situated claimants.

**B.2.39** An insurer remains responsible for claim-handling activities performed by a vendor or other representative. An insurer must maintain reasonable oversight of delegated claim functions.

**B.2.40** An insurer must ensure that persons handling claims on its behalf possess the authority, qualifications, and approvals required for their assigned duties. An insurer must address identified deficiencies through appropriate supervision or corrective action.

**B.2.41** An insurer must identify and manage conflicts of interest that may affect claim decisions. A claim decision must not be influenced by an undisclosed personal or financial interest.

**B.2.42** An insurer must not structure compensation or performance measures in a manner that encourages unfair claim practices. An insurer must reasonably monitor claim incentives for improper effects on claim outcomes.

**B.2.43** An insurer must conduct reasonable quality review of claim files and claim communications. Review findings must be used to identify and correct material claim-handling deficiencies.

**B.2.44** An insurer must correct a claim-handling error when the error is identified. The insurer must document the corrective action when the error materially affects coverage, payment, or claimant rights.

**B.2.45** An insurer must maintain claim data in a manner that supports accurate claim decisions and meaningful oversight. An insurer must address material data integrity concerns that affect claim handling.

**B.2.46** An insurer must maintain procedures for receiving and resolving complaints concerning claim handling. Complaint review must be conducted by personnel with sufficient authority to investigate and respond.

**B.2.47** An insurer must reasonably consider new material information submitted after a claim decision. An insurer must not refuse to reconsider a claim solely because the claim file was previously closed.

**B.2.48** An insurer must preserve claim records in a manner that permits review of material claim activity. Records must be reasonably accessible to personnel responsible for compliance and oversight.

**B.2.49** An insurer must cooperate with lawful examination or inquiry concerning its claim-handling practices. An insurer must provide claim information in an accurate and complete manner, subject to applicable confidentiality protections.

**B.2.50** An insurer must provide claim-handling training appropriate to the duties assigned to its personnel. Training must address fair claims practices, policy interpretation, communications, and documentation.

**B.2.51** An insurer must incorporate the carrier implementation in the underwriting manual state exceptioncarrier implementation in the underwriting manual state exception (see Personal Lines Underwriting Manual, R550 Rule 550 — North Carolina State Exceptions) into internal procedures when that exception affects claim-related policy administration. The insurer must ensure that relevant personnel understand the applicable exception.

**B.2.52** An insurer must apply policy provisions consistently to similarly situated claims, subject to material differences in facts or coverage. An insurer must document a reasonable basis for materially different treatment.

**B.2.53** An insurer must maintain internal escalation procedures for claims involving complex coverage questions or material consumer impact. Escalation must occur when the assigned personnel lack sufficient authority or expertise to resolve the matter.

**B.2.54** An insurer must ensure that claim-handling procedures are consistent with the policy contract. Internal practices must not narrow coverage or impose conditions beyond those supported by the policy.

**B.2.55** An insurer must document material oral communications in the claim file. The documentation must accurately state the substance of the communication and any material commitment made.

**B.2.56** An insurer must coordinate claim handling when more than one insurer representative is involved in the same matter. Coordination must avoid inconsistent instructions, duplicative requests, and conflicting coverage positions.

**B.2.57** An insurer must not retaliate against an insured, claimant, employee, or representative for raising a good-faith concern regarding claim handling. An insurer must permit reasonable reporting of potential noncompliance through its internal processes.

**B.2.58** An insurer must comply with these requirements upon issuance of this bulletin. An insurer must apply corrective measures when its existing procedures do not conform to these requirements.

## B.3 — Notice and Disclosure

**B.3.1** An insurer must apply these provisions to claim-related notices and disclosures provided to an insured, claimant, beneficiary, or authorized representative. An insurer must ensure that communications are clear, accurate, and consistent with the policy and the claim file.

**B.3.2** An insurer must provide required notices in writing unless the recipient has agreed to an electronic method of delivery permitted by applicable law. An insurer must retain evidence of the method and date of delivery in the claim file.

**B.3.3** An insurer must use language reasonably understandable to the intended recipient. An insurer must avoid misleading, ambiguous, or incomplete statements concerning coverage, benefits, duties, or claim handling.

**B.3.4** An insurer must identify itself in a claim-related notice and provide a means for the recipient to contact the insurer concerning the claim. An insurer must provide the name or title of the person or unit responsible for handling the claim when reasonably requested.

**B.3.5** An insurer must acknowledge material claim communications promptly. The acknowledgment must advise the sender of any information reasonably needed for the insurer to evaluate the claim.

**B.3.6** An insurer must notify an insured or claimant when the insurer requires additional information, records, or cooperation. The notice must describe the information sought in sufficient detail to permit a reasonable response.

**B.3.7** An insurer must not request information that is not reasonably related to the investigation, evaluation, or resolution of the claim. An insurer must explain the relevance of requested information when the recipient reasonably questions the request.

**B.3.8** An insurer must provide a copy of a policy or relevant policy provision to an insured upon reasonable request. An insurer may protect information that is confidential, privileged, or otherwise protected from disclosure by applicable law.

**B.3.9** An insurer must identify the policy provisions on which it relies when communicating a coverage position. The communication must fairly explain how the identified provisions apply to the known facts of the claim.

**B.3.10** An insurer must provide a written explanation when it reserves rights under a policy. The explanation must state the basis for the reservation and must not imply that coverage has been denied unless the insurer has made that determination.

**B.3.11** An insurer must provide a written explanation when it denies coverage in whole or in part. The explanation must identify the material facts, policy provisions, and other grounds supporting the denial.

**B.3.12** An insurer must not use a denial notice that misstates the policy, omits a material basis for the decision, or suggests that a claimant lacks rights available under applicable law. An insurer must correct a material error in a denial notice promptly after discovering the error.

**B.3.13** An insurer must notify the insured or claimant when it accepts coverage subject to unresolved issues that may affect payment. The notice must describe the unresolved issues without overstating their effect on the claim.

**B.3.14** An insurer must provide a written explanation of a partial payment or partial denial. The explanation must distinguish the portion being paid from the portion that remains disputed, unavailable, or under review.

**B.3.15** An insurer must disclose the basis for a claim valuation when the valuation is material to a settlement offer, payment, or denial. The disclosure must be sufficient to permit the recipient to understand the principal factors used by the insurer.

**B.3.16** An insurer must not represent that a valuation, estimate, or assessment is final when it remains subject to reasonable revision based on material information. An insurer must communicate any material revision and its basis to the affected recipient.

**B.3.17** An insurer must state the amount and purpose of a claim payment in a manner that permits the recipient to identify the covered loss or expense addressed by the payment. An insurer must not characterize a payment as a full resolution unless the payment is intended and documented as such.

**B.3.18** An insurer must provide a settlement offer in a manner that clearly states the terms proposed for resolution of the claim. An insurer must identify any release, condition, deduction, or limitation that the insurer expects the recipient to accept.

**B.3.19** An insurer must not require an insured or claimant to execute a release that is broader than reasonably necessary to resolve the matter addressed by the settlement. An insurer must provide a reasonable opportunity to review a proposed release before requesting execution.

**B.3.20** An insurer must not state or imply that an insured or claimant must accept a settlement offer to preserve coverage or receive an undisputed payment. An insurer must separately communicate any undisputed payment that is available without a release.

**B.3.21** An insurer must provide clear notice of any policy duty that may affect the handling or resolution of a claim. The notice must explain the action requested and the reasonably foreseeable consequence of noncompliance.

**B.3.22** An insurer must not assert noncooperation, late notice, or another policy condition without advising the affected insured of the basis for the insurer’s position. The insurer must provide the insured a reasonable opportunity to provide information relevant to that position.

**B.3.23** An insurer must communicate a request for examination, inspection, recorded statement, or other claim activity in a manner that reasonably describes its purpose. An insurer must accommodate a reasonable request to use an accessible communication method when practicable.

**B.3.24** An insurer must provide reasonable assistance to a person who indicates difficulty understanding a material claim communication. An insurer must not use a person’s limited ability to understand a communication as a basis to obtain an unfair advantage in claim handling.

**B.3.25** An insurer must protect nonpublic personal information contained in claim notices and disclosures. An insurer must limit disclosure of that information to persons with a legitimate claim-related need to receive it.

**B.3.26** An insurer must disclose to an insured or claimant when an outside person is acting on the insurer’s behalf in a material claim communication. The insurer remains responsible for the accuracy and fairness of the communication.

**B.3.27** An insurer must not discourage an insured or claimant from seeking advice, representation, or assistance concerning a claim. An insurer must not represent that an insured or claimant has no right to question a claim decision or provide additional information.

**B.3.28** An insurer must provide a meaningful response to a reasonable request for clarification of a material claim notice. The response must address the issue raised or explain why the insurer cannot provide the requested information.

**B.3.29** An insurer must notify an insured or claimant when a claim remains under review and the insurer cannot yet provide a final coverage or payment determination. The notice must describe the material issue preventing a determination and any information still needed.

**B.3.30** An insurer must not use a status notice to delay a claim determination when the information needed to decide the claim is reasonably available. An insurer must continue claim activity while awaiting information that does not prevent resolution of undisputed matters.

**B.3.31** An insurer must communicate material claim decisions to the insured or claimant without undue delay after the decision is made. An insurer must document the communication in the claim file.

**B.3.32** An insurer must preserve copies of material notices, disclosures, and responses sent or received in connection with a claim. The claim file must reasonably identify the recipient, method of delivery, and substance of each material communication.

**B.3.33** An insurer must supervise persons acting on its behalf to ensure compliance with these notice and disclosure requirements. An insurer must take appropriate corrective action when it identifies a material communication deficiency.

**B.3.34** An insurer may use standardized claim notices if the notices are reasonably designed for the coverage and claim circumstance involved. An insurer must modify a standardized notice when necessary to avoid a misleading or incomplete communication.

**B.3.35** An insurer must not include language in a claim notice that improperly limits rights, remedies, or benefits available to an insured or claimant. An insurer must not condition the handling of a claim on an agreement that is inconsistent with the policy or applicable law.

**B.3.36** An insurer must provide notices and disclosures in a manner reasonably calculated to reach the intended recipient. An insurer must use an alternative delivery method when the insurer knows that the selected method was ineffective.

**B.3.37** An insurer must ensure that claim notices and disclosures are consistent with communications made by its representatives. An insurer must promptly correct a material inconsistency that could affect an insured’s or claimant’s understanding of the claim.

**B.3.38** An insurer must construe these notice and disclosure requirements to promote fair, timely, and transparent claim handling. An insurer must not rely on a technical defect in a communication to defeat a claim unless the defect materially prejudices the insurer.

## B.4 — Claims Standards

**B.4.1** This subsection applies to each claim handled by an admitted insurer under a policy delivered or issued for delivery in this State. An insurer must apply this subsection as of its effective date.

**B.4.2** An insurer must acknowledge receipt of a claim within thirty (30) days after receipt. The acknowledgment must identify the claim and provide contact information for the person handling the claim.

**B.4.3** An insurer must establish and maintain procedures for recording the date a claim is received. The claim record must reflect material communications and actions taken during the handling of the claim.

**B.4.4** An insurer must conduct a reasonable investigation of each claim before denying coverage or refusing payment. The investigation must be appropriate to the information available and the issues presented by the claim.

**B.4.5** After receiving all requested items necessary to evaluate a claim, an insurer must accept or deny the claim within thirty (30) business days. An insurer must provide the claimant with a clear written notice of its decision.

**B.4.6** A denial notice must identify the policy provision, condition, exclusion, or other basis supporting the denial. The notice must state the material facts on which the insurer relied.

**B.4.7** An insurer must not deny a claim based upon an exclusion, condition, or limitation unless the insurer has determined that the provision applies to the facts of the claim. The insurer must retain documentation supporting that determination.

**B.4.8** An insurer must respond to reasonable communications from a claimant, insured, beneficiary, or authorized representative without undue delay. A response must address the substance of the communication or explain the information needed for a substantive response.

**B.4.9** An insurer must request only information reasonably necessary to investigate, evaluate, or resolve a claim. An insurer must explain the purpose of a requested item when the purpose is not apparent.

**B.4.10** An insurer must not repeatedly request information already provided unless the insurer reasonably requires clarification or verification. The claim record must state the reason for any repeated request.

**B.4.11** An insurer must promptly provide claim forms and instructions when a claimant requests them or when their use is necessary to process the claim. The forms and instructions must be reasonably understandable.

**B.4.12** An insurer must evaluate a claim fairly and in good faith. The insurer must not use an investigation, communication, or document request to avoid a reasonable claim determination.

**B.4.13** When coverage is accepted, an insurer must identify the benefits, damages, or other claim elements included in its evaluation. If any claimed element is not included, the insurer must explain the basis for its position.

**B.4.14** An insurer must not misrepresent pertinent facts, policy provisions, or applicable claim procedures. An insurer must correct a material misstatement when it becomes aware of the misstatement.

**B.4.15** An insurer must make claim payments in accordance with the policy and the insurer's claim determination. A payment communication must identify the claim to which the payment applies.

**B.4.16** If an insurer needs additional information after issuing a decision, the insurer must explain why the information is needed and how it may affect the claim. The insurer must reconsider its determination when material information warrants reconsideration.

**B.4.17** An insurer must preserve claim records sufficient to show compliance with this subsection. The records must be available to the Department upon request.

**B.4.18** An insurer must ensure that personnel involved in claim handling are instructed regarding the requirements of this subsection. An insurer remains responsible for claim handling performed by its employees, agents, and representatives.

**B.4.19** An insurer must apply claim handling practices consistently to similarly situated claimants. A difference in claim treatment must be supported by relevant differences in policy terms, claim facts, or available information.

**B.4.20** Compliance with this subsection does not limit an insurer's obligation to comply with any other applicable requirement. An insurer must not rely on an internal practice that conflicts with this subsection.

## B.5 — Filing and Effective Date

**B.5.1** This section applies to each admitted insurer that adjusts, investigates, evaluates, settles, or denies claims in this State. An insurer must maintain claims-handling practices that comply with this section when handling covered claims.

**B.5.2** An insurer must file with the Department its claims-handling procedures when the Department requires a filing. The filing must describe the insurer’s procedures in sufficient detail to permit review for compliance with this section.

**B.5.3** An insurer must include in its filing the standards used to acknowledge communications, investigate claims, evaluate coverage, make payment, and provide written explanations of claim determinations. The filing must identify the personnel or functions responsible for carrying out those standards.

**B.5.4** An insurer must file any material change to its claims-handling procedures before using the change in this State, unless the Department directs otherwise. An insurer must not represent that a changed procedure is approved unless the Department has approved it.

**B.5.5** A filing must be complete, accurate, and consistent with the insurer’s policy forms, underwriting practices, and claim communications. An insurer must provide supporting information requested by the Department to evaluate a filing.

**B.5.6** The Department may require an insurer to revise, supplement, or withdraw a filing that does not demonstrate compliance with this section. An insurer must comply with a Department request concerning a filing within the time directed by the Department.

**B.5.7** An insurer must not implement a claims-handling procedure that conflicts with a requirement of this section. A filing does not relieve an insurer of its duty to handle each claim fairly and in good faith.

**B.5.8** An insurer must retain records sufficient to demonstrate that its claims-handling practices conform to its filed procedures and this section. The records must be available for Department examination upon request.

**B.5.9** An insurer must ensure that persons acting on its behalf follow the insurer’s applicable claims-handling procedures. The insurer remains responsible for compliance when claims functions are performed by a managing general agent, administrator, adjuster, or other representative.

**B.5.10** This section becomes effective upon issuance and applies prospectively to claims-handling conduct occurring after that effective date. An insurer may continue to use an existing procedure only to the extent that the procedure complies with this section.

**B.5.11** A filing made before the effective date may be reviewed under this section when the filing concerns procedures to be used after that date. The Department may request revisions needed to bring the procedure into compliance.

**B.5.12** An insurer must promptly correct a claims-handling practice that the Department determines is inconsistent with this section. Correction of a practice does not limit the Department’s authority to take appropriate action for noncompliance.

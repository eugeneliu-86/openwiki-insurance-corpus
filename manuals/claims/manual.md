# Property Claims Handling Manual







## Chapter 1 — Purpose and Adjuster Authority

**1.A** Apply this manual to property claims assigned to the carrier. Exercise authority only within the assigned claim role.

This manual directs internal claim handling and authority controls. It does not alter coverage or create obligations to an insured.

Document the assigned claim role and authority status in the claim file. Record any authority restriction communicated by claim leadership.

**1.B** Confirm that the reported loss concerns covered property before directing adjustment activity. Identify the policyholder, claimant, and affected interest.

Accurate party identification prevents unauthorized disclosure and misdirected payments. It also supports proper ownership and insurable-interest review.

Document the reported parties and their relationship to the loss. Preserve available contact information in the claim file.

**1.C** Establish a claim file upon receipt of a report of loss. Record the source and substance of the report.

A complete file supports consistent handling and carrier oversight. It provides the record used to evaluate coverage, damages, and payment authority.

Document the reported date of loss, loss location, and loss description as received. Identify information that remains unverified.

**1.D** Make prompt claim contact consistent with the claim contact deadline. Explain the adjuster’s role and request information needed to evaluate the loss.

Timely contact supports mitigation, preserves evidence, and reduces avoidable dispute. Clear communication establishes appropriate expectations without making coverage commitments.

Document contact attempts, successful communications, and requested information. Record the method used and the party contacted.

**1.E** Verify policy status before extending coverage guidance or authorizing claim payments. Confirm that the reported loss involves the policy under review.

Policy status is necessary to determine whether the carrier has a claim-handling obligation. Early verification also prevents reliance on incomplete policy information.

Document the policy information reviewed and any discrepancy identified. Refer unresolved policy-status issues through the designated channel.

**1.F** Review the applicable policy terms before stating a coverage position. Use approved carrier interpretations and claim-handling practices.

Coverage determinations must rest on the policy record and verified facts. Informal or unsupported interpretations create inconsistent claim outcomes.

Document the policy provisions considered and the facts applied to them. Preserve relevant policy materials in the claim file.

**1.G** Do not waive policy conditions, alter policy terms, or create coverage through claim correspondence. Refer any request for such action to authorized personnel.

Adjuster authority is limited to investigation, evaluation, negotiation, and payment activity within delegated authority. Policy modification requires separate carrier approval.

Document the request and the referral action. Preserve all related communications in the claim file.

**1.H** Communicate with insureds, claimants, representatives, and vendors professionally and accurately. Avoid statements that exceed the confirmed claim facts or assigned authority.

Professional communication protects the carrier’s claim position and promotes fair handling. Accurate communication also reduces confusion regarding coverage and payment.

Document material communications and any representations made regarding the claim. Confirm significant discussions in the claim file.

**1.I** Explain that claim investigation does not constitute acceptance of coverage. Continue fact gathering until the record supports a coverage determination.

Investigation may reveal facts affecting coverage, valuation, responsibility, or payment. Premature conclusions may compromise the carrier’s position.

Document the investigative purpose of material requests and inspections. Record facts that support or challenge the reported loss.

**1.J** Preserve a clear distinction between coverage analysis and damage evaluation. Do not treat an estimate as a coverage decision.

An estimate may measure claimed damage without establishing that the policy responds. Separate analysis supports accurate communication and authority review.

Document coverage issues and valuation issues in distinct file entries. Identify any unresolved issue affecting payment.

**1.K** Inspect damaged property when inspection is necessary to evaluate the reported loss. Use qualified resources when the loss involves conditions outside assigned expertise.

Appropriate inspection supports fact development, scope accuracy, and mitigation review. Specialized conditions may require professional evaluation.

Document inspection findings, photographs, observations, and sources of information. Record the reason for using any specialized resource.

**1.L** Protect the carrier’s right to inspect, examine, and evaluate damaged property. Do not authorize disposal of material evidence before necessary review.

Physical evidence may establish cause, extent, repairability, and coverage applicability. Its loss may impair claim evaluation or recovery efforts.

Document evidence-preservation instructions and the condition of property when observed. Record any evidence that was unavailable or altered.

**1.M** Request reasonable information needed to establish cause, extent, ownership, and value. Tailor requests to the reported loss and known coverage issues.

Focused information requests promote efficient handling and reduce unnecessary burden. Relevant documentation supports defensible claim decisions.

Document requested materials and the reason each request is relevant. Record receipt, review, and follow-up activity.

**1.N** Evaluate the reported cause of loss using available facts rather than assumptions. Refer disputed or technically complex causation issues for appropriate review.

Cause-of-loss findings may control coverage, valuation, and recovery handling. Unsupported causation conclusions expose the carrier to inconsistent decisions.

Document the reported cause, observed conditions, and supporting evidence. Identify competing explanations and the basis for any referral.

**1.O** Address emergency mitigation without directing work beyond assigned authority. Confirm that mitigation activity is reasonable and related to preventing further damage.

Reasonable mitigation can limit additional property damage and preserve the condition of the risk. Uncontrolled work may create avoidable expense or coverage issues.

Document mitigation discussions, observed conditions, and work information received. Record the basis for any approval or referral.

**1.P** Do not guarantee repair results, contractor performance, or completion of work. Limit communications to the carrier’s claim evaluation and authorized payment position.

Repair performance is outside the adjuster’s authority unless the carrier has expressly assumed a defined obligation. Improper assurances may create disputes unrelated to covered loss.

Document any repair-related concern raised by a party. Refer vendor-performance disputes through the designated claim channel.

**1.Q** Identify all potentially affected property interests before issuing payment. Confirm whether lienholders, mortgagees, additional insureds, or other interested parties require consideration.

Payment handling must reflect verified interests and applicable policy requirements. Failure to identify an interest may delay payment or create competing demands.

Document the interests identified and the source of verification. Record any unresolved payment-direction issue.

**1.R** Apply the minimum Section I deductible when determining the payable amount. Do not waive or reduce a deductible without authorized approval.

Deductible application is part of the policy-based loss calculation. Unauthorized changes create inconsistent treatment and may exceed adjuster authority.

Document the deductible applied and the basis for the calculation. Refer any requested exception to authorized personnel.

**1.S** Evaluate damage using credible scope, pricing, and condition information. Distinguish observed damage from reported damage that remains unverified.

Reliable valuation depends on a supported scope of loss. Clear distinctions reduce overpayment risk and improve claim communication.

Document the damage scope, pricing source, and factual basis for valuation. Identify items requiring further verification.

**1.T** Consider repairability, replacement feasibility, and pre-loss condition when evaluating property damage. Do not assume replacement is warranted merely because damage is reported.

The proper valuation approach depends on policy terms and verified property condition. Unsupported replacement conclusions may produce inaccurate loss amounts.

Document the condition of the property and the basis for the selected valuation approach. Preserve photographs, estimates, and expert findings.

**1.U** Identify potential betterment, depreciation, salvage, and prior damage issues during valuation. Refer disputed valuation treatment when it exceeds assigned authority.

These issues may materially affect the amount payable. Early identification supports consistent calculation and negotiation.

Document the issue identified and the evidence supporting the evaluation. Record any referral and resulting direction.

**1.V** Refer any loss with an evaluated amount above $25,000 to the designated authority before committing the carrier to settlement or payment.

Authority limits protect the carrier from unauthorized financial commitments. Referral ensures that material claim decisions receive appropriate review.

Document the evaluated amount, referral date, and authority response in the claim file. Do not issue payment beyond delegated authority.

**1.W** Obtain required approval before making a settlement offer or payment that exceeds assigned authority. Do not divide claim activity to avoid an authority referral.

Authority applies to the claim exposure as evaluated, not merely to an individual transaction. Fragmented handling can obscure the carrier’s actual commitment.

Document the approval request and the authority granted. Record the claim exposure considered at the time of approval.

**1.X** Use approved payment methods and payees when issuing claim funds. Confirm that payment instructions match the verified claim record.

Controlled payment practices reduce the risk of misdirection, duplication, and unauthorized disbursement. They also support reconciliation and audit review.

Document payment authorization, payee information, and the basis for the amount issued. Preserve payment-related correspondence.

**1.Y** Do not issue duplicate payment for the same covered damage. Reconcile prior payments, advances, and recoveries before authorizing additional funds.

Payment duplication may create unnecessary claim expense and recovery complications. A current payment ledger supports accurate claim evaluation.

Document prior payments and their intended purpose. Record the review completed before any additional payment is released.

**1.Z** Explain the basis for a payment, partial payment, or denial in clear claim language. Do not use ambiguous statements that conceal the carrier’s position.

Clear explanations promote fair handling and allow parties to provide responsive information. They also create a record of the carrier’s decision process.

Document the communication and the policy basis for the position stated. Preserve supporting calculations and claim evidence.

**1.AA** Issue reservation or denial communications only with required review and approval. Refer coverage positions that present uncertainty, conflict, or material exposure.

Formal coverage communications may affect the carrier’s rights and obligations. Consistent review reduces the risk of unsupported or incomplete positions.

Document the issue referred, the reviewer’s direction, and the communication issued. Preserve the final approved correspondence.

**1.AB** Investigate potential fraud indicators without accusing any party of misconduct. Refer suspected fraud through the carrier’s designated process.

Fraud concerns require controlled handling to preserve evidence and avoid improper communication. Adjusters must not conduct investigations beyond assigned authority.

Document observed indicators and factual inconsistencies without conclusory language. Restrict file access and communications as directed.

**1.AC** Identify potential subrogation, salvage, contribution, or recovery opportunities. Preserve relevant evidence and avoid actions that may impair recovery rights.

Recovery activity can reduce net claim cost and protect the carrier’s financial interest. Prompt identification is necessary because evidence and responsible parties may become unavailable.

Document potential recovery sources and preservation actions taken. Refer the matter through the designated recovery channel.

**1.AD** Do not release responsible parties or compromise recovery rights without authorized approval. Review proposed settlements for recovery implications before payment.

An unauthorized release may eliminate the carrier’s ability to pursue reimbursement. Recovery rights require coordinated handling.

Document the proposed release or settlement language and the review completed. Record approval before taking action that affects recovery.

**1.AE** Coordinate with defense, recovery, special investigation, or claim leadership when a matter requires their involvement. Provide complete and accurate claim information.

Coordinated handling reduces conflicting direction and incomplete analysis. Shared claim information supports consistent carrier action.

Document the referral purpose and materials provided. Record instructions received and actions taken.

**1.AF** Protect confidential claim information and disclose it only for authorized claim purposes. Verify the identity and authority of any person requesting claim information.

Claim records may contain personal, financial, medical, or proprietary information. Improper disclosure may create legal, regulatory, and reputational exposure.

Document material disclosure requests and the basis for any release of information. Record restrictions applicable to the claim file.

**1.AG** Use claim notes that are factual, professional, and sufficient to explain material decisions. Do not include speculative, derogatory, or unnecessary commentary.

Claim notes may be reviewed by supervisors, auditors, counsel, or regulators. Clear documentation supports the carrier’s handling rationale.

Document facts, actions, decisions, and pending items contemporaneously. Identify the source of material information.

**1.AH** Maintain a current claim diary for pending actions and unresolved issues. Follow up on outstanding information without unnecessary delay.

Active diary management prevents missed obligations and stalled claim handling. It also supports timely escalation of barriers to resolution.

Document follow-up activity and the status of outstanding items. Record the reason when planned action cannot proceed.

**1.AI** Escalate complaints, threats of legal action, regulatory inquiries, and media contacts promptly. Do not provide unauthorized responses on behalf of the carrier.

These matters may require specialized review and controlled communication. Delayed escalation can increase claim, legal, and reputational exposure.

Document the communication received and the referral action. Preserve related correspondence and instructions.

**1.AJ** Refer claims involving coverage ambiguity, unusual facts, or significant dispute to claim leadership. Do not resolve material uncertainty through unsupported compromise.

Complex matters require review beyond routine field adjustment. Referral promotes consistent application of carrier practices.

Document the issue requiring escalation and the facts known at the time. Record the direction received and its implementation.

**1.AK** Treat represented parties through their authorized representative when representation is confirmed. Do not bypass counsel or another authorized representative without approval.

Improper direct communication may interfere with the representative relationship and create handling concerns. Confirmation of authority protects all parties.

Document the representative’s information and the basis for recognizing the representation. Record communications through the authorized channel.

**1.AL** Accommodate reasonable communication needs identified during claim handling. Refer requests requiring specialized assistance or legal review.

Appropriate communication supports fair access to the claim process. Some requests require coordination to protect confidentiality and accuracy.

Document the request and the accommodation provided or referred. Record any limitation affecting communication.

**1.AM** Use approved vendors and experts when vendor engagement is authorized. Do not select or direct a vendor in a manner that exceeds assigned authority.

Vendor controls support quality, cost management, and consistent claim practices. Unauthorized engagement may create unapproved expense or contractual obligations.

Document the reason for the engagement and the authority supporting it. Preserve vendor reports and material communications.

**1.AN** Review vendor invoices, estimates, and reports for consistency with the observed loss. Question unsupported charges or conclusions before authorizing payment.

Vendor materials inform the claim evaluation but do not replace adjuster judgment. Review protects the carrier from inaccurate or unrelated charges.

Document the review performed and any discrepancy identified. Record the resolution or referral of disputed vendor information.

**1.AO** Avoid conflicts of interest and disclose any actual or apparent conflict to claim leadership. Do not handle a claim when impartiality may reasonably be questioned.

Impartial claim handling protects the carrier and supports credible outcomes. Undisclosed conflicts may compromise claim decisions.

Document the conflict disclosure and reassignment direction. Do not retain unnecessary conflict details in routine claim notes.

**1.AP** Do not accept gifts, favors, or personal benefits connected to claim handling. Refer any attempted inducement to claim leadership.

Outside influence may impair independent claim judgment or create the appearance of impropriety. Carrier decisions must rest on policy terms and verified facts.

Document the attempted inducement and the referral action. Preserve relevant communications as directed.

**1.AQ** Maintain control of claim authority when working with independent adjusters or other field resources. Review material recommendations before adopting them as the carrier’s position.

External resources may assist investigation but do not replace carrier decision-making. The assigned adjuster remains responsible for appropriate escalation and documentation.

Document recommendations received and the review performed. Record the basis for accepting, modifying, or rejecting a recommendation.

**1.AR** Reassess the claim when new facts, estimates, or policy information become available. Update the claim position when the record materially changes.

Claim evaluation may evolve as evidence develops. A current assessment supports accurate reserves, communication, and authority decisions.

Document the new information and its effect on the claim evaluation. Record any revised action or referral.

**1.AS** Maintain appropriate claim financial information consistent with the current evaluation. Refer reserve changes or financial actions that exceed assigned authority.

Financial records support carrier planning and claim oversight. Inaccurate financial information may distort the claim’s reported exposure.

Document the basis for material financial updates. Record approval when required by assigned authority.

**1.AT** Do not close a claim while material coverage, payment, recovery, or complaint issues remain unresolved. Confirm that required claim actions are complete before closure.

Premature closure may delay resolution and impair the carrier’s ability to respond to later developments. Closure should reflect the actual status of the claim.

Document the closure rationale and outstanding matters, if any. Record final communications and payment status.

**1.AU** Reopen a claim when new information requires further carrier action. Review the prior file record before taking a new position.

Reopened matters may involve prior coverage, payment, or communication decisions. Review of the existing record promotes consistency and avoids duplication.

Document the reason for reopening and the information prompting it. Record the scope of renewed investigation or evaluation.

**1.AV** Preserve claim records in accordance with carrier retention practices. Do not alter, delete, or obscure material claim information.

The claim file is the carrier’s record of handling activity and decision-making. Record integrity supports audit, litigation, and regulatory review.

Document corrections through clear supplemental entries. Preserve original materials when correction or clarification is required.

**1.AW** Use approved claim systems and communication channels for claim activity. Do not store material claim information in unauthorized locations.

Approved systems support security, accessibility, and file continuity. Unapproved storage can impair confidentiality and record preservation.

Document material claim activity in the carrier claim file. Transfer relevant information received through another channel into the approved record.

**1.AX** Follow claim leadership direction unless it conflicts with law, regulation, policy terms, or carrier procedure. Escalate conflicting direction rather than acting independently.

Consistent direction supports coordinated claim handling. Escalation protects the carrier when instructions require clarification.

Document the direction received and any clarification requested. Record the final instruction followed.

**1.AY** Refer matters involving potential extra-contractual exposure, unfair claim practices, or bad-faith allegations to claim leadership. Do not attempt to resolve such allegations through informal assurances.

These allegations may require legal, compliance, or executive review. Controlled handling protects the carrier’s position and promotes appropriate response.

Document the allegation and the referral action. Preserve all related communications and instructions.

**1.AZ** Do not provide legal, tax, engineering, or public-adjusting advice. Limit guidance to claim process information and authorized carrier positions.

Specialized advice may exceed adjuster expertise and authority. Improper advice can create reliance and unnecessary claim exposure.

Document requests for specialized advice and the response provided. Refer the party to appropriate resources when directed.

**1.BA** Explain available claim-process options without directing an insured to select a particular contractor, representative, or legal adviser. Maintain neutrality unless carrier direction authorizes otherwise.

Neutral process communication supports fair handling and avoids the appearance of undue influence. Parties remain responsible for their independent choices.

Document material process guidance provided. Record any request for recommendation and the response given.

**1.BB** Verify authority before discussing claim information with a public adjuster, assignee, contractor, or other third party. Obtain required claim-file support for the asserted authority.

Third-party involvement may affect communications, payment, and release considerations. Verification prevents disclosure or payment to an unauthorized person.

Document the authority evidence reviewed and any limitation identified. Record the approved communication channel.

**1.BC** Identify assignments, payment directions, and repair agreements that may affect claim handling. Refer documents presenting legal uncertainty or payment conflict.

Such documents may affect who may receive information or payment. Adjusters must not independently determine legal effect when the issue is unclear.

Document the document received and the issue presented. Preserve the original or reliable copy in the claim file.

**1.BD** Maintain respectful handling when a claimant disputes the carrier’s evaluation. Invite relevant supporting information without conceding the disputed position.

Disagreement is not itself evidence that the carrier’s evaluation is incorrect. Respectful communication may develop facts needed for further review.

Document the dispute and the information requested or received. Record any reevaluation or referral.

**1.BE** Review supplemental claims for connection to the reported loss and prior payments. Do not assume that a supplemental request is covered or duplicative.

Supplemental information may reveal additional covered damage or may concern unrelated conditions. Careful review supports accurate payment decisions.

Document the supplemental request and the evidence reviewed. Record the coverage and valuation determination or referral.

**1.BF** Refer catastrophic, widespread, or operationally sensitive losses when claim leadership direction is required. Follow carrier deployment and communication controls.

Large-scale events may require coordinated authority, vendor, and communication management. Individual claim actions must remain consistent with carrier response direction.

Document the event-related direction received and the claim action taken. Record any operational barrier affecting handling.

**1.BG** Use plain language when explaining claim requirements and next actions. Avoid technical terms unless they are necessary and explained.

Plain communication helps parties understand what information is needed and why. Clear requests reduce avoidable delay and misunderstanding.

Document material explanations and instructions provided. Confirm important claim requirements in writing when appropriate.

**1.BH** Maintain professional boundaries with all claim participants. Do not engage in conduct that could compromise independent judgment or claim-file integrity.

Professional boundaries support fair, consistent, and defensible claim handling. Informal relationships may create actual or perceived bias.

Document any circumstance requiring supervisory review. Follow reassignment or communication direction promptly.

**1.BI** Report material claim-handling errors, missed actions, or unauthorized activity to claim leadership. Take corrective action as directed.

Early reporting allows the carrier to assess impact and reduce further exposure. Concealing an error impairs effective claim management.

Document the error, affected claim activity, and corrective steps taken. Preserve the record without alteration.

**1.BJ** Exercise judgment within delegated authority and seek guidance when the proper action is uncertain. Do not allow production pressure to override claim quality or authority controls.

Adjuster authority depends on disciplined application of policy terms, facts, and carrier practices. Escalation is required when informed judgment cannot resolve the issue.

Document the uncertainty, guidance requested, and direction received. Record the final action and its supporting basis.

## Chapter 2 — First Notice, Acknowledgement and Reservation of Rights

**2.A** Open a claim record upon receipt of loss information. Capture the source and substance of the notice.  
Prompt intake preserves the facts available when the loss is reported. It also establishes ownership of the handling activity.  
Document the receipt source, reported loss facts, and assigned claim identifier. Record the handler responsible for intake.

**2.B** Treat any communication asserting property damage, loss, or a request for benefits as a claim notice. Do not require formal wording before opening the claim record.  
Claimants may report losses through informal channels. Early recognition prevents avoidable delay.  
Document the communication method and the reported concern. Preserve the communication in the claim file.

**2.C** Identify the reporting party and the party's relationship to the claimed property. Obtain reliable contact information before ending the intake contact.  
Accurate party identification supports timely communication and authority review. It also reduces misdirected correspondence.  
Document the name, contact details, and stated relationship of the reporting party. Note any uncertainty requiring follow-up.

**2.D** Record the reported location of loss exactly as provided. Clarify any incomplete or conflicting location information.  
The location may affect risk identification, inspection routing, and coverage review. Unresolved location facts can impair the investigation.  
Document the reported location and any alternate location information. Record the source of each location detail.

**2.E** Record the reported date and circumstances of loss without characterizing coverage. Ask neutral questions necessary to understand the allegation.  
Early descriptions are often incomplete or imprecise. Neutral intake preserves the claimant's account without adopting conclusions.  
Document the claimant's description in the claim file. Identify facts that require confirmation.

**2.F** Identify any immediate condition that may increase property damage or create a safety concern. Direct the reporting party to take reasonable protective action where appropriate.  
Prompt mitigation can limit further loss and protect persons at the location. Claims handling must not interfere with emergency response.  
Document the reported condition and any protective guidance provided. Record the reporting party's response.

**2.G** Escalate reports involving suspected criminal activity, fire origin concerns, structural instability, or hazardous conditions. Notify the appropriate internal claim resource without delay.  
These conditions may require specialized handling and preservation of evidence. Delayed escalation can compromise the investigation.  
Document the escalation, recipient, and reason for referral. Preserve any available information concerning the condition.

**2.H** Check available claim records for related loss reports, prior activity, or duplicate notices. Consolidate handling only after confirming the matters concern the same loss.  
Related records may contain material facts or reveal duplicate reporting. Improper consolidation can confuse distinct claims.  
Document the search performed and the result. Record the basis for any consolidation decision.

**2.I** Establish the correct named claimant or claimant representative from the information available. Do not assume authority based solely on a caller's assertion.  
Authority affects communications, release of information, and claim direction. Verification protects confidential claim information.  
Document the claimed authority and any supporting information received. Note outstanding authority verification.

**2.J** Identify any representative, contractor, public adjuster, lender, or other interested party disclosed at intake. Route communications through authorized channels when authority is established.  
Interested parties may affect access, repair activity, or claim communications. Clear routing reduces conflicting instructions.  
Document the party's identity, role, and stated authority. Preserve any authorization received.

**2.K** Provide a clear acknowledgment that the loss report has been received. Do not express a coverage conclusion during the acknowledgment.  
Acknowledgment confirms that the carrier is acting on the report. Premature conclusions may misstate the claim position.  
Document the acknowledgment method and recipient. Preserve the communication record.

**2.L** Meet the acknowledgment of claim deadline. Escalate any circumstance that may prevent timely acknowledgment.  
Timely acknowledgment supports responsive claim handling and claimant confidence. Delay may increase complaints and handling risk.  
Document the acknowledgment date and delivery method. Record the reason for any escalation.

**2.M** Use plain language when describing the next claim-handling steps. Avoid statements that promise payment, repair approval, or coverage.  
Claimants need practical direction after reporting a loss. Clear language reduces misunderstanding while preserving the coverage review.  
Document the information provided and any claimant questions. Preserve written acknowledgment communications.

**2.N** Advise the reporting party to protect damaged property from further loss when circumstances support that guidance. Do not direct repairs beyond what is reasonably necessary to protect the property.  
Protective action may reduce additional damage while preserving claim options. Overly specific direction can create avoidable responsibility.  
Document the protective guidance and the facts supporting it. Record any stated inability to protect the property.

**2.O** Request preservation of damaged property, relevant materials, and loss-related evidence when practical. Do not require preservation where safety or lawful disposal requires otherwise.  
Physical evidence may assist cause, scope, and damage evaluation. Preservation requests must account for safety and practical conditions.  
Document the preservation instruction and the recipient's response. Record any reported disposal or alteration.

**2.P** Obtain a concise description of the damaged property and the claimed damage. Separate reported facts from the handler's observations.  
The reported scope directs early investigation and resource assignment. Accurate distinction supports later evaluation.  
Document the reported damaged items and conditions. Identify information that remains unverified.

**2.Q** Ask whether emergency services, restoration vendors, contractors, or public authorities have responded. Obtain available contact information for those parties.  
Third parties may hold relevant facts, photographs, or records. Early identification supports timely investigation.  
Document the parties identified and their stated involvement. Record available contact information.

**2.R** Identify whether the claimed property is occupied, vacant, inaccessible, or subject to access restrictions. Escalate access barriers that may delay inspection or mitigation.  
Access conditions affect inspection planning and protection of the property. Prompt escalation helps avoid unnecessary delay.  
Document the reported occupancy and access condition. Record the person authorized to provide access.

**2.S** Ask whether repairs, cleanup, demolition, or removal have begun or are planned. Obtain a description of work already performed or anticipated.  
Post-loss activity can affect evidence, scope evaluation, and mitigation review. Early notice permits appropriate claim direction.  
Document the reported work and the party performing it. Preserve available invoices, estimates, or photographs.

**2.T** Identify whether other insurance, service plans, warranties, or responsible parties may be involved. Do not make responsibility determinations during intake.  
Potentially involved sources may affect investigation and coordination. Intake identification is not a coverage conclusion.  
Document the reported alternate source or responsible party. Record the basis for the information.

**2.U** Screen the loss report for indicators requiring special investigation review. Refer concerns without accusing any party of wrongdoing.  
Objective referral protects the integrity of the investigation. Careful communication avoids improper claim handling.  
Document the indicators observed and the referral made. Restrict sensitive notes to authorized claim personnel.

**2.V** Confirm the preferred communication method and any stated communication accommodation. Use the confirmed method unless circumstances require another channel.  
Communication preferences support clear and accessible handling. Consistent contact reduces missed information.  
Document the preference and any accommodation requested. Record any communication barrier reported.

**2.W** Identify language needs at intake and arrange appropriate assistance when needed. Do not rely on an interested party to interpret material claim communications.  
Accurate communication supports informed participation in the claim process. Independent assistance reduces misunderstanding.  
Document the language need and assistance arranged. Preserve material translated communications.

**2.X** Review the loss report for facts that may require immediate reservation of rights consideration. Refer any uncertainty about coverage defenses or conditions to the assigned coverage resource.  
Early issue spotting preserves the carrier's ability to investigate while maintaining available rights. Delay may create avoidable handling risk.  
Document the facts prompting coverage review. Record the referral and response.

**2.Y** Issue the reservation of rights letter within 10 days. Use approved claim correspondence language and tailor the stated basis to known facts.  
A timely reservation permits continued investigation without implying that coverage has been accepted. Specific communication promotes fair claim handling.  
Document the issuance date, delivery method, recipient, and stated reservation basis. Preserve the final correspondence in the claim file.

**2.Z** Do not reserve rights using vague, unsupported, or irrelevant language. Identify the known issue without asserting facts not established by the investigation.  
Overbroad reservation language may confuse the claimant and weaken claim handling quality. The communication must reflect the actual claim concern.  
Document the facts supporting the reservation language. Record any issue that remains under investigation.

**2.AA** Send the reservation of rights letter to every party entitled to receive claim communications. Confirm authority before sending sensitive information to a representative.  
Proper delivery supports notice and protects confidential information. Incomplete distribution may cause avoidable disputes.  
Document all recipients and the method used for each delivery. Record the authority basis for representative delivery.

**2.AB** Continue claim investigation after issuing a reservation of rights unless a coverage resource directs otherwise. Do not suspend necessary mitigation or fact gathering solely because an issue exists.  
Reservation of rights preserves the claim position while facts are developed. Ongoing investigation supports a sound coverage decision.  
Document investigative activity performed after issuance. Record any direction to limit or pause activity.

**2.AC** Distinguish a reservation of rights from a denial, acceptance, or payment commitment. Do not describe the reservation as a final coverage decision.  
Claim communications must accurately state the carrier's current position. Mischaracterization can mislead the claimant.  
Document the language used to explain the reservation. Preserve records of material verbal communications.

**2.AD** Escalate any request for a coverage decision that cannot be supported by the known claim facts. Do not speculate about the likely outcome.  
Coverage conclusions require a complete and accurate factual basis. Speculation can create inconsistent handling.  
Document the request and the facts available at the time. Record the escalation recipient and response.

**2.AE** Manage the claim toward the coverage decision period. Identify missing facts or outstanding information that may affect the decision.  
Active file control supports timely and defensible claim resolution. Unidentified gaps can create unnecessary delay.  
Document outstanding information and planned follow-up. Record the status of the coverage review.

**2.AF** Explain any request for information in terms of the claim investigation. Do not request information that is unrelated to the reported loss or coverage issue.  
Focused requests reduce burden and improve the quality of information received. Unnecessary requests can delay handling.  
Document the information requested and the claim purpose. Preserve the request communication.

**2.AG** Request a sworn proof when needed to evaluate the claim or resolve material facts. Clearly communicate the sworn proof deadline.  
A sworn proof may establish the claimant's statement of loss facts and claimed damages. Clear communication supports meaningful compliance.  
Document the request, delivery method, and stated requirements. Preserve any sworn proof received.

**2.AH** Review any sworn proof for completeness and consistency with available claim facts. Refer material discrepancies for further investigation.  
The sworn proof is a claim statement that may affect evaluation. Inconsistencies require careful fact development rather than assumption.  
Document the review findings and any discrepancies identified. Record follow-up requested from the claimant.

**2.AI** Maintain a chronological activity record from notice through resolution. Enter material actions and communications promptly.  
A complete activity record supports continuity, supervision, and claim defensibility. Delayed entries can impair reliability.  
Document each material contact, decision, and referral. Identify the person recording the entry.

**2.AJ** Record material verbal communications in sufficient detail to show what was discussed. Include any guidance, request, or representation made during the contact.  
Verbal contacts may affect claimant expectations and claim direction. A clear record reduces later uncertainty.  
Document the date, participants, subject, and substance of the communication. Note any follow-up commitment.

**2.AK** Preserve material electronic communications received or sent during claim handling. Maintain them in the claim file in a retrievable form.  
Electronic communications may contain material claim facts or directions. Preservation supports accurate review and response.  
Document the communication source and subject. Retain any attachments with the related communication.

**2.AL** Verify the loss location before assigning an inspection where available information conflicts. Escalate unresolved location discrepancies.  
Inspection resources must be directed to the correct property. Location errors can delay evaluation and increase expense.  
Document the information reviewed and the verified location. Record the basis for any escalation.

**2.AM** Assign an appropriate claim handler based on loss characteristics and handling complexity. Refer specialized exposures to the applicable internal resource.  
Proper assignment supports effective investigation and claimant service. Complex matters may require specialized expertise.  
Document the assignment decision and reason. Record any specialized resource requested.

**2.AN** Arrange inspection promptly when inspection is necessary to evaluate the claim. Coordinate access without directing unsafe entry or work.  
Timely inspection supports assessment of cause, scope, and condition. Safe coordination protects all participants.  
Document inspection arrangements and the agreed access contact. Record any access limitation.

**2.AO** Provide the inspector with the reported loss facts and known access information. Do not instruct the inspector to reach a coverage conclusion.

**2.AQ** Review early loss facts for potential subrogation, contribution, or recovery opportunities. Preserve relevant information without making liability admissions.  
Potential recovery rights may depend on prompt fact preservation. Premature conclusions can compromise claim handling.

Vendor materials may be incomplete, inaccurate, or outside the claimed loss. Objective review supports sound evaluation.

**2.AV** Avoid directing repairs that exceed emergency protection needs before the claim facts are evaluated. Explain that repair activity may affect the ability to inspect damage.  
Early repairs can alter evidence and obscure the original condition. Claimants still may need to protect property from further loss.  
Document the guidance given concerning repairs. Record any repairs reported as completed.

**2.AW** Provide reasonable status information when requested by an authorized claimant or representative. Do not disclose protected claim information to an unauthorized party.  
Status communication supports transparency and reduces unnecessary contact. Authorization controls remain essential.  
Document the request, authority review, and response. Preserve material status correspondence.

**2.AX** Escalate complaints, threats of litigation, regulatory inquiries, or media contacts promptly. Do not debate the matter or make unauthorized statements.  
These matters require coordinated carrier handling. Uncontrolled responses may increase exposure.  
Document the communication and the escalation recipient. Preserve the original inquiry or complaint.

**2.AY** Refer any claim involving a potential catastrophe event to the designated catastrophe handling resource. Follow catastrophe routing instructions once assigned.  
Catastrophe claims may require coordinated resources and consistent communications. Early routing supports efficient handling.  
Document the catastrophe referral and assignment status. Record any special handling direction.

**2.AZ** Identify whether the claimed loss involves business property, personal property, or mixed property interests. Obtain clarification where the reported ownership is uncertain.  
Property classification may affect investigation scope and claim routing. Assumptions can lead to incomplete evaluation.  
Document the reported property interest and ownership information. Record unresolved classification questions.

**2.BA** Record claimed contents damage separately from building damage when both are reported. Do not combine distinct damage descriptions without preserving their source.  
Separate reporting supports orderly investigation and valuation. Combined descriptions may obscure ownership or scope issues.  
Document each reported damage category in the claim file. Identify the reporting source for each category.

**2.BB** Identify reported loss of use, additional expense, or business interruption concerns without promising payment. Refer the issue for coverage review when appropriate.  
These allegations may require facts beyond physical damage. Early identification supports complete investigation.  
Document the reported impact and claimed expense category. Record the referral for review.

**2.BC** Ask whether the property has prior damage, unrepaired conditions, or ongoing maintenance concerns. Do not characterize prior conditions as excluded or unrelated during intake.  
Prior conditions may affect cause and scope analysis. Intake information is not sufficient for a coverage conclusion.  
Document the reported prior condition and source. Identify facts requiring inspection or further inquiry.

**2.BD** Identify any weather, water source, utility failure, equipment malfunction, or other reported cause. Preserve the claimant's description without selecting a cause category prematurely.  
Cause information directs early investigation and specialist referral. Premature classification can distort the claim record.  
Document the reported cause and any alternate explanation offered. Record the source of the information.

**2.BE** Refer claims involving water, mold, contamination, smoke, or environmental concerns to the appropriate internal resource when indicated. Do not provide technical conclusions outside assigned authority.  
These conditions may require specialized investigation and handling controls. Timely referral supports safe and consistent action.  
Document the condition reported and the referral made. Record instructions received from the specialist.

**2.BF** Identify any reported theft, vandalism, malicious damage, or suspicious entry condition. Request available incident information without directing law enforcement activity.  
Such losses may involve evidence, recovery opportunities, or special investigation concerns. The claimant remains responsible for reporting to public authorities where appropriate.  
Document the reported incident facts and any available report information. Record any special investigation referral.

**2.BG** Handle all claim communications courteously and without argumentative language. Do not criticize the claimant, representative, vendor, or other party in the claim file.  
Professional communication supports fair handling and preserves a reliable record. Inflammatory language can impair claim review.  
Document facts and observations using neutral terminology. Correct any material entry that is unclear or unsupported.

**2.BH** Protect personal and claim information from unnecessary disclosure. Limit access and distribution to personnel with a claim-handling need.  
Claim information may be sensitive and protected. Controlled access supports confidentiality and carrier integrity.  
Document any authorized disclosure and its purpose. Record the recipient and information shared.

**2.BI** Confirm receipt of material documents or information submitted by an authorized party. Identify any item that cannot be opened, read, or associated with the claim.  
Acknowledging submissions reduces uncertainty about whether information reached the carrier. Prompt identification of technical issues permits correction.  
Document the item received and its source. Record any notice sent concerning an unreadable or incomplete submission.

**2.BJ** Review incoming information before relying on it for claim direction. Resolve apparent contradictions through additional inquiry or referral.  
Claim decisions require reliable facts. Unresolved contradictions may affect the fairness of the outcome.

**2.BK** Do not alter, summarize, or paraphrase a claimant statement in a way that changes its meaning. Clearly identify handler summaries as summaries.

**2.BL** Explain requested access, inspection, or information in a respectful and practical manner. Escalate refusal or noncooperation without threatening unsupported consequences.  
Cooperation concerns require careful handling and fact-specific review. Threatening language can create avoidable disputes.  
Document the request, response, and any stated reason for refusal. Record the escalation for further direction.

**2.BN** Identify the need for expert review when the loss involves specialized cause, scope, valuation, or repair issues. Obtain internal approval before retaining an expert when approval is required.  
Specialized expertise may be necessary for reliable claim evaluation. Appropriate approval controls expense and scope.  
Document the reason for expert consideration and approval status. Preserve the assignment scope and resulting report.

**2.BO** Do not provide engineering, legal, construction, environmental, or medical opinions unless authorized and qualified to do so. Refer technical questions to the appropriate resource.  
Unauthorized opinions may mislead the claimant or compromise the investigation. Proper referral supports accurate handling.  
Document the question raised and referral made. Record the response provided through authorized channels.

**2.BP** Evaluate the need for an advance payment only after confirming authority and the basis for payment. Do not describe any payment as final resolution unless authorized.  
Advance payments may address an immediate covered need while investigation continues. Clear characterization protects the claim position.  
Document the payment basis, approval, and communication. Preserve the payment record.

**2.BQ** Do not issue payment, denial, settlement, or release communications without required claim authority. Escalate any file that exceeds the handler's authority.  
Authority controls promote consistent decisions and appropriate oversight. Unauthorized action may create financial or legal exposure.  
Document the authority review and approval received. Record the person authorizing the action.

**2.BR** Explain that claim investigation may continue after an inspection, estimate, or payment. Do not imply that any single activity resolves every claim issue.  
Claims may involve separate questions concerning cause, scope, valuation, or coverage. Accurate expectations reduce misunderstanding.  
Document the explanation provided to the claimant. Preserve material written communications.

**2.BS** Review the claim record before making material external communications. Ensure the communication is consistent with known facts and prior carrier statements.  
Consistent communication supports fair handling and avoids conflicting representations. File review identifies unresolved issues.  
Document the review when material correspondence is issued. Record any correction made before delivery.

**2.BT** Correct material claim record errors promptly and transparently. Do not delete or obscure a material prior entry.  
A reliable claim file must show how information and decisions developed. Transparent correction supports auditability.  
Document the correction and reason for it. Preserve the original entry where system controls permit.

**2.BU** Refer any potential conflict of interest involving claim personnel, vendors, experts, or representatives. Do not continue affected activity without management direction.  
Conflicts can compromise objective claim handling or create the appearance of unfairness. Prompt referral protects the claim process.  
Document the potential conflict and referral. Record the management direction received.

**2.BV** Use approved communication channels for material claim correspondence. Do not transmit sensitive claim information through an unapproved channel.  
Approved channels support security, preservation, and delivery verification. Uncontrolled channels increase confidentiality risk.  
Document the channel used for material correspondence. Record any delivery confirmation available.

**2.BW** Confirm the identity of a caller before discussing material claim information. Use available verification methods appropriate to the information requested.  
Identity verification protects claim information from unauthorized disclosure. It also supports accurate communication records.  
Document the verification performed when material information is released. Record any limitation on information provided.

**2.BX** Identify claim handling barriers such as displacement, disability, limited access, communication difficulty, or emergency conditions. Adjust communication and investigation activity within authorized practices.  
Claimants may face practical barriers after a loss. Responsive handling supports fair participation in the claim process.  
Document the barrier reported and accommodation or adjustment made. Record any resource referral.

**2.BY** Avoid expressing personal opinions about coverage, value, fault, repair quality, or claimant credibility. Limit communications to verified facts and authorized claim positions.  
Personal opinions can create inconsistent records and unnecessary disputes. Objective handling supports defensible decisions.

Early supervision supports removal of barriers and consistent handling. Unaddressed delays may increase complaint risk.

Orderly transfer prevents missed obligations and inconsistent claimant communication. A complete summary supports uninterrupted handling.

## Chapter 3 — Water Losses

**3.A** Begin mitigation promptly after discovery of covered water intrusion. Require mitigation to begin within 3 days after discovery when covered water affects insured property.

Prompt mitigation limits additional damage and preserves recoverable evidence. Delayed action may increase disputed damages.

Document the reported discovery circumstances and mitigation start activity. Record the basis for any delay determination.

**3.B** Identify the water source before making a coverage position. Separate direct water damage from damage caused by excluded water movement.

Source identification directs the handling path and prevents unsupported assumptions. Visible conditions may reflect more than one source.

Document the observed source indicators and the conclusion reached. Preserve photographs and recorded statements supporting the finding.

**3.C** Protect the property from further damage when safe to do so. Authorize reasonable emergency measures consistent with the reported conditions.

Emergency action reduces avoidable deterioration and supports fair claim handling. Unsafe conditions require qualified responders.

Document the mitigation instructions and the party receiving them. Retain invoices, reports, and images of emergency work.

**3.D** Confirm whether water entered from within or outside the insured location. Distinguish plumbing discharge from surface water, seepage, and overflow.

Entry path affects coverage analysis and scope evaluation. Do not rely solely on the insured's description when physical evidence is available.

Document the entry path findings and any unresolved indicators. Explain the evidence supporting the determination.

**3.E** Inspect affected areas before demolition whenever conditions permit. Preserve representative evidence when emergency work prevents a complete inspection.

Pre-demolition conditions may establish cause, duration, and extent. Missing evidence can complicate causation review.

Document the inspection timing and any conditions preventing fuller observation. Retain images showing affected materials and moisture patterns.

**3.F** Obtain a clear account of when the water was discovered. Ask what conditions existed immediately before and after discovery.

Timing may identify whether damage resulted from a sudden event or a continuing condition. It also supports evaluation of mitigation conduct.

Document the reported sequence of events in the claim file. Identify material differences between accounts and physical evidence.

**3.G** Determine whether the reported water event was sudden and accidental. Refer any loss involving uncertain duration, repeated leakage, or progressive deterioration.

Duration often controls the applicable coverage analysis. Repeated conditions may indicate maintenance issues rather than a discrete event.

Document the facts supporting the duration assessment. Record the reason for any referral.

**3.H** Inspect accessible plumbing components associated with the reported event. Obtain qualified findings when the source cannot be reliably identified.

A failed component may establish the initiating event and distinguish resulting damage from repair costs. Unsupported conclusions create avoidable dispute.

Document the component condition and any expert observations. Retain repair records when available.

**3.I** Separate damage caused by water from damage caused by the failed part itself. Evaluate the failed part under the applicable coverage terms.

The source repair and resulting property damage may be handled differently. Clear separation supports accurate estimating.

Document the damaged property categories and the basis for treatment. Identify any excluded repair expense separately.

**3.J** Confirm whether the water involved a drain, sewer, sump, or related system. Apply the backup and sump overflow limit when the reported facts support that handling path.

Water from these systems may be subject to distinct limitations. Classification must rest on the actual source and entry route.

Document the system involved and the evidence supporting classification. Record the applicable limit analysis in the claim file.

**3.K** Apply the sewer backup deductible when the covered loss involves qualifying sewer backup. Do not offset unrelated damage under that deductible.

The deductible applies only to damage within its intended loss category. Accurate allocation prevents incorrect settlement calculations.

Document the damage allocation and deductible application. Explain any portion handled outside the sewer backup deductible.

**3.L** Inspect sump equipment and discharge arrangements when overflow is reported. Determine whether the loss arose from equipment failure, power interruption, blockage, or an external water condition.

Sump losses may involve several contributing causes. Each cause may require separate coverage consideration.

Document the equipment observations and reported operating condition. Retain images of the sump area and related discharge path.

**3.M** Evaluate the backflow preventer condition when a drainage-related loss is reported. Refer any claim where its operation, installation, or maintenance remains uncertain.

Backflow conditions may affect causation and recovery review. Qualified evaluation may be necessary where the condition is disputed.

Document the observed backflow preventer condition and referral basis. Retain any inspection findings received.

**3.N** Determine whether water damage resulted from roof opening, roof failure, or wind-driven entry. Distinguish resulting interior damage from preexisting roof deterioration.

Roof-related water claims require a supported entry-path analysis. Interior staining alone does not establish a covered opening.

Document the roof observations and interior moisture pattern. Preserve images showing the reported entry point.

**3.O** Identify whether an appliance, fixture, or supply line failed. Obtain the make, condition, and visible failure characteristics when available.

Appliance losses often depend on the precise point of failure. Product information may also support recovery opportunities.

Document the appliance details and failure observations. Preserve the failed part when practical and safe.

**3.P** Inspect supply lines for rupture, corrosion, loosened fittings, and repeated leakage. Refer claims involving concealed or inaccessible source conditions.

Supply-line evidence may establish whether water escaped suddenly or over time. Concealed conditions require careful fact development.

Document the visible line condition and access limitations. Record any basis for specialist involvement.

**3.Q** Investigate toilet, tub, shower, and sink overflows promptly. Determine whether the overflow resulted from blockage, misuse, fixture failure, or drainage backup.

Fixture overflow can involve both direct discharge and backup conditions. The distinction affects claim classification.

Document the fixture involved and the reported cause. Retain photographs of visible blockage or damage.

**3.R** Evaluate water quality before approving cleaning scope. Treat visibly contaminated water as a health and property protection concern.

Contamination may require specialized handling and affect the extent of necessary removal. Do not direct unsafe cleaning practices.

Document the water condition and any qualified remediation recommendation. Retain sanitation and disposal records.

**3.S** Refer losses involving sewage, suspected contamination, or unsafe occupancy conditions. Do not authorize reentry until conditions are reasonably safe.

Health hazards require appropriate expertise and controlled handling. Prompt referral protects occupants and claim personnel.

Document the hazard indicators and referral action. Record communications regarding restricted access.

**3.T** Inspect for fungi, wet or dry rot, or bacteria when water has affected concealed, organic, or absorbent materials. Apply the fungi, wet or dry rot, or bacteria aggregate limit where supported by the loss facts.

Microbial conditions may arise from water exposure and may require separate coverage analysis. Visible growth is not the only relevant indicator.

Document the observed condition and scope findings. Record the limit evaluation and supporting evidence.

**3.U** Distinguish preexisting staining, rot, and deterioration from damage caused by the reported event. Do not include unrelated condition work in the covered scope.

Preexisting conditions may resemble recent water damage. Proper separation prevents overpayment and inaccurate denials.

Document the condition differences and evidence used to distinguish them. Retain representative images of each area.

**3.V** Determine whether materials can be dried, cleaned, repaired, or require removal. Use qualified mitigation findings where material condition is uncertain.

Reasonable restoration methods control indemnity cost while returning the property to its prior condition. Replacement is not presumed merely because materials became wet.

Document the restoration recommendation and basis. Retain moisture findings and work records.

**3.W** Verify moisture conditions in affected building materials before approving reconstruction. Require support for any conclusion that materials remain wet or have returned to acceptable condition.

Moisture verification helps prevent concealed damage and premature rebuilding. It also supports the scope decision.

Document moisture observations and the source of testing information. Retain reports and images showing affected areas.

**3.X** Separate emergency service charges from permanent repair charges. Review each charge for relation to the reported water event.

Emergency work may be necessary even when later repair scope is limited. Clear allocation supports accurate claim payment.

Document the service categories and reviewed invoices. Explain any charge that is not approved.

**3.Y** Review mitigation invoices for reasonable work, materials, and equipment use. Refer invoices that lack support or appear inconsistent with observed conditions.

Mitigation charges should correspond to the actual loss scope. Invoice review protects the claim from unsupported expense.

Document the invoice review and any requested clarification. Retain revised invoices and vendor responses.

**3.Z** Confirm that mitigation vendors acted within authorized scope. Do not approve elective upgrades or unrelated improvements.

Vendors may perform work beyond what the loss requires. The claim payment must remain tied to covered damage.

Document the authorized scope and completed work. Identify any noncovered or unsupported work separately.

**3.AA** Preserve damaged property when its condition may establish cause or support recovery. Release damaged items only after adequate evidence has been retained.

Discarded evidence can prevent reliable causation findings. Preservation is especially important for failed components.

Document the item preserved and its current disposition. Retain images and any custody information.

**3.AB** Evaluate whether a responsible party may have caused or contributed to the loss. Protect recovery rights before disposing of relevant evidence.

Recovery review may reduce claim cost and identify additional facts. Early action is necessary when evidence is perishable.

Document the potential responsible party and supporting facts. Record preservation and recovery actions taken.

**3.AC** Obtain statements from occupants or other knowledgeable persons when material facts remain unclear. Ask about prior leaks, repairs, and recurring conditions.

Witness information may clarify duration, source, and mitigation actions. Statements should be compared with physical evidence.

Document material statements in the claim file. Identify any material inconsistency requiring follow-up.

**3.AD** Inspect adjacent rooms and connected building areas for migration of water. Do not limit the inspection to the point where water was first observed.

Water may travel through cavities, flooring, and shared structural elements. A narrow inspection may miss resulting damage.

Document all areas inspected and the observed conditions. Explain why any inaccessible area was not inspected.

**3.AE** Evaluate damage to personal property separately from building damage. Determine whether cleaning, restoration, or replacement is appropriate for each item category.

Personal property may require different handling methods than structural materials. Separate evaluation improves scope accuracy.

Document the affected property and proposed treatment. Retain inventory information and condition images.

**3.AF** Require reasonable support for ownership and condition of damaged personal property. Consider available records, images, and credible statements.

Ownership evidence helps establish the claim scope without imposing unnecessary burden. Condition evidence may distinguish prior damage from the reported event.

Document the support reviewed and the conclusion reached. Note any unresolved ownership issue.

**3.AG** Identify property that requires specialized cleaning, handling, or evaluation. Refer items with unusual materials, contamination concerns, or uncertain restoration methods.

Specialized property may be damaged by ordinary mitigation methods. Qualified handling protects both the property and claim outcome.

Document the item condition and reason for referral. Retain specialist findings and recommendations.

**3.AH** Evaluate flooring, wall finishes, insulation, and cabinetry for water migration. Consider hidden conditions where staining or moisture patterns indicate spread.

Finish materials may conceal damage beyond the visible surface. Accurate scope requires attention to connected assemblies.

Document the affected assemblies and observed migration. Retain images before removal or repair.

**3.AI** Distinguish cosmetic staining from physical damage requiring repair. Do not approve replacement solely because discoloration is reported.

Some water effects can be addressed without replacing sound materials. The repair method must reflect actual condition.

Document the condition assessment and selected repair method. Preserve images showing staining and material integrity.

**3.AJ** Investigate claims involving frozen plumbing for signs of prior leakage, inadequate maintenance, or unrelated deterioration. Identify the point of rupture before determining scope.

Freeze-related damage may combine a sudden break with older conditions. Proper source identification supports accurate handling.

Document the rupture location and surrounding condition. Record findings concerning prior leakage or deterioration.

**3.AK** Confirm whether water was shut off after discovery when safe and practical. Ask who acted and what prevented prompt shutoff if it did not occur.

Shutting off water can materially limit additional damage. The circumstances may affect mitigation evaluation.

Document the reported shutoff action and timing circumstances. Record any safety or access barrier described.

**3.AL** Evaluate whether temporary repairs were reasonable and necessary. Do not require permanent repairs before the loss scope is adequately established.

Temporary measures can protect the property while preserving claim options. Premature permanent work may obscure the source.

Document the temporary work and its purpose. Retain invoices and images of the condition addressed.

**3.AM** Consider access limitations when evaluating concealed plumbing, wall cavities, and crawl areas. Authorize reasonable investigation necessary to establish cause and extent.

Water losses may remain hidden behind intact finishes. Limited investigation may be necessary before a reliable decision can be made.

Document the access limitation and authorized investigation. Record what the investigation established.

**3.AN** Review contractor estimates for scope consistency and causation support. Refer material differences between estimates and field observations.

Estimates should reflect documented water damage rather than generalized renovation. Significant variance may require further inspection.

Document the estimate comparison and resolution of material differences. Retain the reviewed estimate in the claim file.

**3.AO** Exclude betterment from the covered repair scope unless required to restore damaged property. Separate elective upgrades from necessary replacement work.

Water repairs can create opportunities for improvement unrelated to the loss. Indemnity does not include voluntary enhancement.

Document the betterment review and any excluded work. Explain the basis for the scope separation.

**3.AP** Determine whether code-related work is directly connected to covered repairs. Refer uncertain code requirements for appropriate review.

Code issues may affect repair cost but require verified applicability. Do not assume a requirement based on contractor assertion alone.

Document the reported code issue and supporting authority. Record the outcome of the review.

**3.AQ** Evaluate loss of use conditions when water damage affects habitability or normal occupancy. Confirm that the condition resulted from the reported loss.

Water damage may impair use even where structural damage appears limited. The cause of displacement must be established.

Document the occupancy condition and supporting facts. Retain relevant communications and expense information.

**3.AR** Coordinate access with occupants, property representatives, and authorized vendors. Maintain control of keys, entry codes, and restricted areas.

Water claims often require repeated inspection and mitigation access. Clear coordination reduces delay and dispute.

Document access arrangements and any denied entry. Record the impact of access limitations on claim handling.

**3.AS** Use neutral descriptions when documenting cause before the investigation is complete. Do not characterize a loss as covered or excluded based only on initial notice information.

Initial reports may be incomplete or inaccurate. Neutral documentation preserves the integrity of the investigation.

Document preliminary facts as reported and observed. Update the claim record when findings are confirmed.

**3.AT** Refer claims with conflicting causation opinions or materially inconsistent evidence. Obtain additional investigation when the conflict affects coverage or scope.

Conflicting evidence may prevent a reliable decision. Timely referral promotes consistent handling.

Document the conflicting information and referral reason. Retain all opinions considered.

**3.AU** Review prior water damage indicators that may affect the reported loss. Distinguish prior repairs from new damage caused by the current event.

Prior conditions can alter both scope and causation analysis. They should not be assumed to defeat a new covered loss.

Document the prior damage evidence and comparison findings. Retain images showing the condition differences.

**3.AV** Inspect exterior drainage, grading, and openings when water entered from outside. Determine whether water traveled over land, through an opening, or from a building system.

Exterior conditions may identify the actual entry path. Accurate classification depends on evidence rather than appearance alone.

Document exterior observations and the route of water travel. Retain images of relevant conditions.

**3.AW** Evaluate rain-related water damage for evidence of a covered entry event. Distinguish wind-created openings from long-term wear, deterioration, or maintenance conditions.

Rainwater may enter through several mechanisms with different coverage outcomes. The entry condition must be supported by inspection findings.

Document the weather-related observations and entry analysis. Record evidence of any opening or deterioration.

**3.AX** Identify whether water damage involves a vacant, unoccupied, or seasonally used location. Refer any occupancy condition that may affect coverage analysis.

Occupancy facts may affect discovery, mitigation, and applicable terms. They should be established without assumption.

Document the occupancy information and source of the information. Record the reason for any referral.

**3.AY** Evaluate utility interruption claims for the direct cause of water release. Determine whether the interruption affected a sump, heating system, plumbing system, or other equipment.

Utility conditions may be contributing facts rather than the immediate source of damage. The relationship must be investigated.

Document the reported interruption and resulting equipment condition. Retain available utility or service information.

**3.AZ** Confirm whether restoration work includes drying verification before closing the property damage portion. Reinspect when available evidence does not support completion.

Closing before adequate drying can leave concealed damage unresolved. Verification supports final scope accuracy.

Document the completion review and any reinspection findings. Retain final mitigation records.

**3.BA** Review all claimed damage for a direct connection to the reported water event. Do not include unrelated maintenance, renovation, or prior condition work.

A water claim must be limited to damage caused by the loss. Broad scopes create inaccurate indemnity exposure.

Document the causation basis for each material damage category. Identify excluded or unrelated work clearly.

**3.BB** Communicate material information requests promptly and clearly. Explain the purpose of requested evidence without making unsupported coverage statements.

Clear requests help the claim progress and reduce misunderstanding. The insured should understand what information remains needed.

Document each request and the response received. Record any follow-up required.

**3.BC** Explain claim decisions using the established facts and applicable handling analysis. Avoid conclusions that exceed the evidence gathered.

Clear explanations support consistent claim handling and reduce avoidable escalation. Decision language must match the documented record.

Document the factual basis for the decision. Retain the communication issued.

**3.BD** Reevaluate the claim when new evidence changes the source, duration, or scope analysis. Do not rely on an earlier conclusion that the new evidence materially undermines.

Water losses can develop as hidden conditions are discovered. Reassessment ensures the claim record remains accurate.

Document the new evidence and revised analysis. Explain any change in handling position.

**3.BE** Escalate suspected misrepresentation, inflated damage, or altered evidence through the appropriate internal process. Continue necessary property protection while the concern is reviewed.

Fraud indicators require controlled handling and complete documentation. Premature accusation can compromise the investigation.

Document the observed indicators without speculation. Preserve relevant evidence and communication records.

**3.BF** Maintain professional neutrality when interacting with mitigation vendors, contractors, and recovery parties. Do not direct work that exceeds necessary emergency protection without appropriate authority.

Claim personnel must preserve independent scope evaluation. Vendor communications can affect later disputes.

Document material vendor communications and instructions given. Retain estimates, reports, and invoices received.

**3.BG** Protect privacy when collecting images, statements, and property information. Limit claim documentation to information relevant to the water loss.

Water inspections may reveal personal or sensitive information. Relevant documentation supports handling while reducing unnecessary exposure.

Document only material claim facts and evidence. Secure collected information in the claim file.

**3.BH** Confirm that all material coverage, causation, scope, and payment issues are resolved before closure. Reopen review when unresolved evidence remains material to the outcome.

A complete closing record supports consistent service and later file review. Unresolved issues should not be obscured by closure activity.

Document the final claim analysis and disposition. Record any remaining issue and the action required.

**3.BI** Close the file with a clear record of inspections, findings, payments, and communications. Ensure the file explains how the reported water event was classified and resolved.

A complete record permits efficient later review and supports defensible claim handling. The closing summary should align with the underlying evidence.

Document the closing summary in the claim file. Retain all material evidence supporting the final disposition.

**3.BJ** Refer catastrophic water conditions that affect multiple locations or require coordinated response. Establish a consistent fact record before assigning responsibility for separate damage areas.

Widespread conditions can create overlapping scopes and inconsistent reports. Coordinated handling improves accuracy and controls duplication.

Document the affected locations and assigned handling responsibilities. Record the reason for coordinated response.

**3.BK** Evaluate structural safety before entering areas affected by prolonged water exposure. Use qualified assistance where ceilings, floors, or supports appear compromised.

Water can weaken building components and create immediate hazards. Claim investigation must not create unsafe exposure.

Document observed safety conditions and any access restriction. Retain qualified findings when obtained.

**3.BL** Preserve evidence of water lines, appliance connections, and damaged finishes before repairs conceal the condition. Obtain sufficient images to show context as well as detail.

Context images help explain how water traveled through the property. Detail images help identify the source and damaged materials.

Document the evidence preserved and where it is stored. Note any condition that prevented complete preservation.

**3.BM** Confirm that payment recommendations match the supported repair or restoration scope. Do not include charges that remain unverified or unrelated to the water event.

Payment accuracy depends on a complete connection between evidence, scope, and cost. Unsupported payment creates avoidable claim leakage.

Document the payment basis and reviewed supporting materials. Explain any withheld or deferred item.

**3.BN** Review supplements for new damage information rather than automatically accepting expanded scope. Inspect or obtain support for material additions.

Supplemental requests may identify legitimate concealed damage or unsupported expansion. Each request requires independent evaluation.

Document the supplement review and resulting decision. Retain evidence supporting approved changes.

**3.BO** Address disagreement over cause or scope through additional fact gathering before taking a final position. Refer disputes that cannot be resolved through available evidence.

Water losses may involve competing explanations that require further review. A complete investigation supports a defensible outcome.

Document the disputed issue and additional steps taken. Record the basis for referral or resolution.

Roof claims require early identification of the damaged property and the claimed result.

Document the reported area of damage, the claimed cause, and the assigned handling path.

Roof losses may involve settlement terms that differ from other covered property.

Document the coverage review and identify the provisions requiring further analysis.

Roof damage may be reported for structures that are not scheduled or otherwise insured.

Prompt mitigation reduces avoidable interior damage and preserves evidence of the roof condition.

Document the mitigation instructions, the insured response, and any reported limitations.

Roof condition, access, and causation often require direct inspection.

**4.13** Separate observed physical damage from conditions reported by the insured or contractor.

Claim evaluation must rely on supported observations rather than unsupported conclusions.

Document the source of each damage statement and identify disputed observations.

**4.14** Inspect for evidence of wind, hail, fallen objects, water entry, wear, deterioration, installation defects, and prior repairs.

**4.15** Obtain available weather information that is relevant to the reported cause of loss.

Weather information may support or contradict the reported event.

**4.16** Do not accept contractor scope, pricing, or causation conclusions without independent claim evaluation.

Contractor information may assist the investigation but does not control the coverage decision.

Document the contractor materials received and the basis for accepting or rejecting any conclusion.

**4.17** Request repair records, prior inspection reports, and prior loss information when roof condition or causation is unclear.

Prior condition may affect the evaluation of new damage and resulting loss.

**4.18** Identify prior patching, replacement areas, mismatched materials, and visible maintenance concerns during inspection.

Prior work may affect both damage assessment and reasonable repair scope.

Document the location and condition of prior work with photographs and inspection notes.

**4.19** Confirm the roof covering type, installation characteristics, and visible condition before preparing a settlement estimate.

Material type and condition may affect repair methods and scope conclusions.

Document the observed roof material and any limitations on identification.

**4.20** Obtain material samples only when needed to evaluate matching, damage, repairability, or replacement scope.

Sampling should be limited to a supported claim need and performed without unnecessary damage.

Document the reason for sampling, the sample location, and chain of custody when applicable.

**4.21** Evaluate whether damaged materials can be repaired without impairing roof performance or appearance required by the policy.

Repairability must be based on the observed damage and the available material condition.

Document the repair analysis, supporting photographs, and any expert input.

**4.22** Evaluate replacement scope by separating directly damaged materials from undamaged materials claimed for access, matching, or appearance.

Replacement decisions require a supported connection to covered physical loss.

Document the basis for each included and excluded area of replacement.

**4.23** Confirm whether interior damage resulted from a covered roof opening or from another source of water entry.

Interior damage may have a different cause than the reported roof event.

Document the water path, observed entry points, and evidence supporting the conclusion.

**4.24** Inspect attic areas, ceilings, wall surfaces, and insulation when accessible and relevant to the reported loss.

Interior observations may assist in determining water movement and duration.

Document observed staining, moisture, deterioration, and inaccessible areas.

**4.25** Distinguish recent water damage from long-term staining, repeated leakage, condensation, or maintenance-related conditions.

Visible staining alone does not establish a covered event.

Document the condition indicators and the basis for the timing conclusion.

**4.26** Refer any claim with suspected fraud, altered evidence, inconsistent loss reporting, or unexplained contractor activity to the appropriate investigation resource.

Potential misrepresentation requires controlled handling and complete preservation of facts.

Document the referral basis without making unsupported accusations in the claim file.

**4.27** Preserve damaged materials when their condition may affect causation, extent, or coverage analysis.

Discarded materials may eliminate evidence needed for claim resolution.

Document preservation instructions, material location, and any inability to retain evidence.

**4.28** Do not direct destructive testing unless the expected information is material to the claim decision.

Testing may create additional damage and should be proportionate to the issue presented.

Document the testing need, the approved scope, and the resulting findings.

**4.29** Obtain expert evaluation when causation, structural condition, repairability, or code impact cannot be reasonably resolved through ordinary adjustment.

Specialized analysis may be needed to support a fair and defensible decision.

Document the referral question, expert qualifications, and the conclusions relied upon.

**4.30** Review the expert report for consistency with observed conditions, policy terms, and other claim evidence.

Expert opinions must be evaluated within the full claim record.

Document any material difference between expert findings and other evidence.

**4.31** Apply the roof settlement floor when the applicable settlement calculation would otherwise produce a lower result.

Document the calculation inputs and the reason the roof settlement floor applies.

Roof age information supports consistent use of the applicable settlement method.

Document the source of the age information and any uncertainty affecting the calculation.

The applicable schedule addresses settlement for roof surfacing based on established roof age criteria.

Document the roof age source, the selected schedule result, and the calculation basis.

**4.34** Verify roof age using reliable installation records, permits, invoices, inspection reports, or other credible evidence.

Age assumptions may materially affect roof settlement.

Document the evidence used and explain any estimate made from incomplete records.

**4.35** Do not rely solely on contractor statements to establish roof age when stronger evidence is available.

Interested parties may not have complete installation information.

Document the contractor statement and the independent evidence considered.

**4.36** Apply the twenty-year shingle percentage only when the applicable roof settlement process calls for that treatment.

The twenty-year shingle percentage must be applied consistently with the established settlement approach.

Document the material type, age evidence, and calculation inputs used.

**4.37** Separate roof surfacing settlement from payment for other covered building components when different settlement treatment applies.

Roof losses may include components that do not share the same settlement basis.

**4.38** Consider ordinance or law additional amount only after confirming that applicable law requires the additional work.

Code-related costs must be supported by an actual requirement rather than a contractor preference.

**4.39** Obtain written code enforcement information when the claim includes a code upgrade, increased scope, or required roof system change.

Verifiable enforcement information supports accurate application of coverage.

Document the enforcing authority, the stated requirement, and the affected repair scope.

**4.40** Do not include elective upgrades, betterments, or unrelated modernization in the covered repair scope.

Covered settlement restores qualifying damage and does not fund voluntary improvement.

Document the excluded upgrade items and the explanation provided to the insured.

**4.41** Evaluate reasonable access, setup, protection, and debris handling costs that are necessary to complete covered repair work.

Roof repair pricing may require supporting operations beyond the damaged material itself.

Document the operational need for each included cost.

**4.42** Review contractor estimates for duplicate operations, unsupported quantities, unsupported material selections, and work unrelated to covered damage.

Estimate review protects against overpayment and unsupported scope expansion.

Document the estimate comparison and the basis for any revision.

**4.43** Use current carrier estimating practices and supported local pricing when preparing the claim estimate.

Consistent estimating supports fair and repeatable claim handling.

Document the estimating source, material assumptions, and labor assumptions used.

**4.44** Identify whether the insured has completed repairs, intends to repair, or has not selected a repair path.

Repair status may affect inspection availability, payment handling, and supplemental review.

Document the insured's stated repair status and available repair records.

**4.45** Review invoices, contracts, proof of payment, and completion evidence before issuing payment that depends on completed work.

Completion-related payment requires support that the applicable work was performed.

Document the records reviewed and identify any remaining information needed.

**4.46** Explain the claim decision, estimate scope, deductible application, and any settlement limitation in clear claim correspondence.

Roof claim decisions should identify the basis for payment or denial without using policy language inaccurately.

Document the correspondence issued and the delivery method.

**4.47** State excluded damage, unsupported scope, and unresolved issues with specific claim facts.

Clear explanations reduce avoidable disputes and support file quality.

Document the evidence supporting each adverse or partial decision.

**4.48** Provide the insured a reasonable opportunity to submit additional information when material facts remain incomplete.

Additional information may resolve questions about cause, age, condition, or repair cost.

Document the information requested and the response received.

**4.49** Reinspect when new evidence, completed tear-off work, disputed conditions, or supplemental damage may materially change the claim evaluation.

Roof conditions may become clearer after temporary protection or repair preparation.

Document the reason for reinspection and compare the new findings with prior observations.

**4.50** Evaluate supplemental requests using the same causation, scope, and settlement standards applied to the original claim.

Supplemental handling must remain tied to covered damage.

Document the supplemental request, supporting evidence, and decision.

**4.51** Do not reopen settled roof scope solely because a contractor presents a higher estimate without new supporting evidence.

Price differences alone do not establish additional covered damage.

Document the contractor estimate and the basis for maintaining or revising the prior decision.

**4.52** Refer any dispute involving appraisal, demand for formal review, threatened litigation, or represented insured communications for appropriate claim handling direction.

Escalated disputes require controlled communication and preservation of the file record.

Document the referral, the issue presented, and communication restrictions provided.

**4.53** Preserve all inspection notes, photographs, estimates, reports, correspondence, and payment calculations in the claim file.

Roof claims often depend on condition evidence that may not remain available.

Document file uploads promptly and identify the source of each material record.

**4.54** Maintain a clear chronology of loss reporting, inspection activity, communications, decisions, and payments.

A complete chronology supports review of claim handling and decision timing.

Document material events when they occur rather than reconstructing the record later.

**4.55** Avoid characterizing damage as wear, deterioration, defect, or excluded cause until the file contains supporting observations or expert analysis.

Cause descriptions must be grounded in the available evidence.

Document the factual indicators supporting the selected cause characterization.

**4.56** Confirm that any denial or limitation has been reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and consistency with the claim evidence.

Adverse decisions require careful support and clear communication.

Document the reviewer, the issues reviewed, and any correction made before issuance.

**4.57** Issue undisputed payment promptly when coverage and amount are established, even if other portions of the roof claim remain under review.

Partial resolution may reduce hardship while preserving investigation of disputed issues.

Document the undisputed scope, payment basis, and remaining open issues.

**4.58** Do not require the insured to begin permanent repairs before completing necessary inspection unless delay would create greater property damage.

Premature repair may impair investigation, while unnecessary delay may increase loss severity.

Document inspection availability, repair urgency, and instructions provided to the insured.

**4.59** Close the roof claim only after resolving coverage, payment, communications, recoverable items, and outstanding investigation needs.

Closure should reflect a complete claim decision rather than an incomplete file condition.

Document the final claim disposition and the basis for closure.

**4.60** Reopen a closed roof claim when new material information, supported supplemental damage, or a handling error requires further evaluation.

Reopening preserves accurate claim resolution when the prior record is incomplete or incorrect.

Document the reopening reason, the new information, and the revised handling plan.

**4.61** Conduct file review for roof claims with significant coverage issues, unusual causation, extensive scope disputes, or repeated handling activity.

Focused review promotes consistent application of roof claim procedures.

Document the review findings, corrective actions, and any coaching provided.

## Chapter 5 — Fire and Smoke

**5.1** Verify the reported fire or smoke event before extending claim handling authority. Record the reported cause, location, and affected property.

Prompt verification limits handling based on incomplete loss facts. Early confirmation also identifies conditions requiring escalation.

Document the reported event, source of report, and verified loss location in the claim file. Preserve available dispatch, incident, and witness information.

**5.2** Establish contact with the insured or authorized representative promptly after assignment. Explain the information needed to evaluate emergency conditions and property protection needs.

Prompt contact supports loss mitigation and preserves evidence. It also reduces the risk of unauthorized disposal or repairs.

Document contact attempts, completed conversations, and instructions provided. Record the name and authority of any representative acting for the insured.

**5.3** Confirm whether fire suppression activity caused water, chemical, or structural damage. Distinguish suppression damage from fire and smoke damage during the inspection.

Suppression activity may create separate damage conditions that require different handling. Clear differentiation supports an accurate scope.

Document visible suppression effects, including wet materials, residue, and compromised building components. Photograph affected areas before material removal where conditions permit.

**5.4** Refer any risk with active fire damage, unstable conditions, or unsafe access for appropriate emergency response. Do not direct entry into a location that has not been cleared for safe access.

Unsafe conditions can expose personnel and vendors to injury. Restricting access also protects the integrity of the loss scene.

Document the unsafe condition and the party notified. Record any access restriction, authority instruction, or clearance obtained.

**5.5** Confirm whether a public authority restricted access to the damaged premises. Obtain available information regarding the nature of the restriction.

Access restrictions may delay inspection, mitigation, and salvage activity. The claim file must show why normal handling activity could not proceed.

Document the authority involved, reported restriction, and available release information. Retain communications supporting any delayed inspection or repair activity.

**5.6** Identify the origin area of the fire or smoke condition without stating a cause beyond available evidence. Refer suspected intentional, criminal, or unusual loss circumstances for investigation.

Cause statements made without support can compromise later investigation. Referral protects the carrier’s ability to evaluate the loss appropriately.

Document observed origin indicators and the basis for any referral. Retain photographs, reports, and communications received from investigating authorities.

**5.7** Preserve evidence where the cause of loss remains uncertain. Do not authorize alteration of the origin area without approval from the assigned investigative resource.

Alteration can prevent later examination of material evidence. Preservation is especially important where causation may affect claim handling.

Document preservation instructions given to the insured, vendor, or other involved party. Record the condition of the origin area before any authorized disturbance.

**5.8** Identify all damaged building components and personal property visible during the inspection. Separate fire, smoke, heat, water, and impact damage in the working scope.

A clear damage description supports consistent estimating and settlement review. Separation of damage types also assists in evaluating repair methods.

Document each observed damage category in the inspection notes. Support the scope with photographs, measurements, and material descriptions.

**5.9** Inspect concealed areas when visible conditions indicate possible hidden fire, smoke, water, or heat damage. Obtain appropriate access before opening building components.

Concealed damage may expand the repair scope or create continued safety concerns. Inspection should be supported by observed conditions rather than speculation.

Document the condition supporting concealed-area inspection. Retain photographs of opened areas and describe findings in the claim file.

**5.10** Require reasonable emergency measures to protect covered property from further damage. Do not direct repairs that exceed the immediate need to stabilize the property.

Emergency work should prevent additional loss while preserving adjustment options. Broad repair direction before scope review can create avoidable disputes.

Document the emergency condition, mitigation recommendation, and party performing the work. Retain available invoices, photographs, and work descriptions.

**5.11** Review emergency service invoices for work related to the reported loss. Question charges that lack a clear connection to property protection or damage stabilization.

Emergency invoices may include work outside necessary mitigation activity. Review supports reasonable claim handling and accurate payment decisions.

Document the services reviewed and the basis for any questioned item. Retain invoices, work authorizations, photographs, and vendor communications.

**5.12** Confirm whether water extraction, drying, or dehumidification is necessary after fire suppression. Monitor the work to ensure it addresses documented moisture conditions.

Uncontrolled moisture can cause additional damage after the fire event. Drying activity should be tied to observed conditions and supported methods.

Document moisture findings, equipment placement, and progress information received. Retain drying records and photographs when available.

**5.13** Evaluate smoke migration throughout the affected structure. Include areas with odor, visible residue, or contamination indicators in the inspection scope.

Smoke can travel beyond the visible fire area through openings and mechanical systems. A complete inspection reduces the risk of overlooked damage.

Document affected rooms, pathways of migration, and observed residue. Support the evaluation with photographs and contractor findings where obtained.

**5.14** Obtain appropriate evaluation of electrical systems exposed to heat, smoke, water, or suppression chemicals. Do not authorize energizing damaged systems without qualified review.

Damaged electrical components can create continuing fire and shock hazards. Qualified review supports safe restoration and accurate damage assessment.

Document the reported condition of each affected system. Retain inspection findings, repair recommendations, and related invoices.

**5.15** Obtain appropriate evaluation of fuel, gas, and mechanical systems exposed to the loss. Refer conditions presenting a continuing hazard for immediate attention.

Heat, debris, and suppression activity may impair system function. Prompt evaluation can prevent further damage or unsafe operation.

Document the affected system, reported hazard, and party contacted. Retain service findings and any authority or utility communications.

**5.16** Identify structural components affected by heat, flame, water, or firefighting operations. Refer suspected structural instability for qualified assessment.

Structural damage may not be apparent from surface conditions alone. Qualified assessment supports safe access and proper repair scoping.

Document visible deformation, cracking, charring, or collapse conditions. Retain assessment reports, photographs, and repair recommendations.

**5.17** Verify whether damaged materials can be cleaned, restored, repaired, or require removal. Base the recommendation on the condition of the property and available expert support.

Restoration may be appropriate where it returns property to functional condition. Unsupported replacement or cleaning decisions can delay resolution.

Document the condition of the material and the basis for the selected handling approach. Retain vendor findings, samples, and photographs where relevant.

**5.18** Evaluate soot and residue conditions before approving cleaning methods. Require methods appropriate for the affected surface and contamination type.

Improper cleaning can cause additional property damage or embed residue. Method review supports effective restoration and preserves evidence of condition.

Document the cleaning recommendation and the basis for approval. Retain testing results, vendor proposals, and before-and-after photographs.

**5.19** Address odor conditions through supported inspection and remediation methods. Do not approve odor treatment based solely on a generalized vendor recommendation.

Odor can indicate continuing contamination or incomplete cleaning. Supported remediation helps confirm that treatment addresses the damaged area.

Document odor observations, affected locations, and remediation findings. Retain vendor reports, work descriptions, and completion confirmation.

**5.20** Inspect contents exposed to fire, smoke, heat, water, or debris. Identify property requiring specialized handling, cleaning, storage, or evaluation.

Contents losses may involve materials that require different restoration methods. Early identification supports preservation and accurate valuation.

Document the item description, condition, and proposed handling. Retain photographs, inventories, vendor assessments, and storage records.

**5.21** Preserve damaged property until the inspection need has concluded. Hold damaged property available for inspection for 30 days days before authorizing disposal or release.

Damaged property may be needed to confirm cause, extent, condition, or value. Retention protects the claim file from unsupported conclusions.

Document retention instructions and the party responsible for custody. Record any approved release, disposal, or salvage transfer.

**5.22** Identify property with potential salvage value before authorizing disposal. Coordinate salvage handling so that evidence preservation and claim evaluation remain protected.

Salvage may offset claim costs when property cannot be restored. Premature disposal can eliminate value and impair examination.

Document the item condition, salvage assessment, and disposition decision. Retain communications with the insured, vendor, and salvage resource.

**5.23** Do not release salvage to a purchaser until ownership and claim handling authority are clear. Obtain appropriate acknowledgment for any transfer of damaged property.

Unclear transfer activity can create disputes over ownership or claim credit. Written acknowledgment supports proper file control.

Document the transfer terms, receiving party, and property description. Retain release records, sale information, and proof of transfer.

**5.24** Identify hazardous materials, contaminated debris, or unsafe personal property during inspection. Refer handling of hazardous conditions to qualified resources.

Fire losses may create hazardous residue, damaged chemicals, or unsafe debris. Specialized handling may be needed to protect persons and property.

Document the hazardous condition and the referral made. Retain reports, disposal records, and communications regarding access restrictions.

**5.25** Review debris handling separately from repair activity. Evaluate the debris removal additional amount under applicable claim handling authority.

Debris costs can arise from necessary clearing, demolition, and disposal work. Separate review prevents unsupported inclusion in repair scope.

Document the debris condition, work required, and basis for the handling decision. Retain estimates, invoices, photographs, and disposal records.

**5.26** Verify whether a fire response charge was assessed in connection with the reported event. Obtain the available charge notice and identify the issuing authority.

Response-related charges may require separate review from direct property damage. The file should show the source and nature of the assessment.

Document the reported fire response charge and supporting notice. Record the issuing authority, property connection, and disposition of the request.

Occupancy can affect the scope of damage, mitigation needs, and investigative handling. Accurate use information supports proper claim evaluation.

Document reported occupancy, observed conditions, and any discrepancy. Retain photographs, statements, and available supporting records.

**5.28** Identify whether the loss affects a residence, business operation, or mixed-use location. Coordinate handling activity with the property’s operational needs where appropriate.

Operational properties may require specialized mitigation, access control, and contents handling. Clear identification supports efficient vendor direction.

Document the observed use and operational impacts reported. Record any special access, security, or preservation requirements.

Fire damage can leave openings that permit further loss. Prompt security measures help preserve the remaining property.

Document the exposure condition and security measures taken. Retain photographs, invoices, and communications supporting the work.

Exterior openings can allow weather damage after the reported event. Temporary protection should preserve the property without predetermining permanent repairs.

Document the opening, protection method, and reason permanent work was deferred. Retain photographs and vendor work descriptions.

**5.31** Verify damage to heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning systems exposed to smoke or soot. Do not approve system cleaning or replacement without support for the affected condition.

Mechanical systems can distribute contamination beyond the fire area. Supported evaluation helps distinguish necessary remediation from routine maintenance.

Document system observations, vendor findings, and recommended work. Retain inspection reports, photographs, and invoices.

Some damaged items may appear intact while containing internal damage. Qualified evaluation supports repairability and value decisions.

Document the item, observed condition, and evaluation result. Retain diagnostic reports, photographs, and repair or replacement recommendations.

Estimates should reflect documented damage and necessary repair methods. Unsupported line items can delay settlement and create unnecessary cost.

**5.51** Reinspect property when new information, completed mitigation, or disputed scope requires further review. Limit reinspection to issues material to the claim decision.

Reinspection may be necessary when conditions change or earlier access was limited. Focused review supports efficient resolution.

Document the reason for reinspection and findings obtained. Retain updated photographs, measurements, and scope notes.

**5.52** Confirm that mitigation, cleaning, repair, and salvage activity have been reviewed before final claim disposition. Resolve material inconsistencies in the file before completing the handling decision.

Final review helps identify unsupported costs, missing records, or unresolved scope issues. A complete file supports consistent claim outcomes.

Document the final review and any issue resolved. Retain the basis for the completed claim decision.

**5.53** Refer disputes involving cause, scope, valuation, ownership, or repair method when available information does not support resolution. Maintain objective communications while the matter is under review.

Unresolved disputes require careful handling to avoid unsupported decisions. Referral ensures that appropriate resources evaluate material differences.

Document the disputed issue, information considered, and referral made. Record subsequent findings and the resulting claim action.

**5.54** Close the claim file only after documenting the loss facts, damage evaluation, communications, payments, and disposition of damaged property. Confirm that retained evidence and outstanding handling tasks have been addressed.

A complete closing record supports later review and consistent claim administration. It also preserves the basis for the handling outcome.

Document the closing review and any remaining administrative action. Retain final correspondence, payment records, inspection materials, and disposition records.

## Chapter 6 — Theft and Mysterious Disappearance

**6.A** Confirm that the reported property was in the claimant’s care, custody, or control at the time of loss. Obtain a clear description of the property and its usual location.

Theft coverage depends on identifying property that is subject to the claimed coverage. Unclear ownership or possession may indicate an unrelated loss.

Document the ownership information, usual location, and source of the claimant’s knowledge. Preserve statements that identify competing ownership interests.

**6.B** Obtain the claimant’s account of the discovery of the loss and the circumstances surrounding the report. Require the claimant to identify who had access to the premises or property.

A prompt and consistent account assists in distinguishing theft from unexplained absence. Access information may identify a responsible party or recovery source.

Document the reported discovery circumstances and all identified persons with access. Record material inconsistencies without characterizing the claimant’s intent.

**6.C** Require a complete inventory of claimed personal property within 30 days. Direct the claimant to include descriptions, claimed values, and supporting ownership information.

A complete inventory permits evaluation of the claimed loss and application of applicable limits. Delayed or incomplete inventories may impair the investigation.

Document the date the inventory was requested and the date it was received. Identify missing information and record follow-up efforts.

**6.D** Verify that the reported event involves theft, attempted theft, or mysterious disappearance before extending coverage analysis. Do not treat an unsupported absence of property as established theft.

The cause of loss controls the applicable coverage review. Property may be misplaced, abandoned, transferred, damaged, or removed by an authorized person.

Document the facts supporting the reported cause of loss. Identify facts that remain unverified or disputed.

**6.E** Obtain any available law-enforcement report or incident reference associated with the claimed loss. Confirm whether the claimant reported the matter to the appropriate authority.

External reporting may provide information about suspects, recovered property, forced entry, or similar losses. The absence of a report may require additional claim inquiry.

Document the reporting authority, report status, and information obtained. Record the claimant’s explanation when no report is available.

**6.F** Inspect the reported location when physical conditions may establish theft, entry, removal, or damage. Photograph relevant conditions before repairs or cleanup alter the evidence.

Physical evidence may corroborate or contradict the reported sequence of events. Inspection may also identify preservation, mitigation, or recovery issues.

Document the inspection findings and retain photographs in the claim file. Note any condition that could not be inspected and explain why.

**6.G** Obtain information regarding locks, alarms, cameras, access controls, and other protective features at the reported location. Determine whether those features were operating as represented.

Protective features may establish the opportunity for theft or identify available evidence. System records may also clarify the time and manner of entry.

Document the reported protective features and the condition of each feature. Preserve available footage, alarm records, and access records.

**6.H** Request available receipts, invoices, photographs, appraisals, manuals, serial numbers, and other ownership evidence. Match the evidence to each material claimed item.

Ownership evidence supports valuation and assists in identifying recoverable property. General assertions of ownership may require additional verification.

Document the evidence received and the items it supports. Record any claimed item for which supporting evidence is unavailable.

**6.I** Confirm whether the claimed property has identifying characteristics that can be reported for recovery efforts. Obtain serial numbers, markings, model information, and distinctive features where available.

Identifying information assists law enforcement, recovery vendors, and salvage handling. It also reduces the risk of duplicate or unrelated claims.

Document all available identifying information in the claim file. Record the source of each identifier.

**6.J** Evaluate whether the claimed property was left in a location that creates a custody or entrustment issue. Obtain the identity of any person or organization that possessed the property.

Losses involving entrusted property may involve facts that differ from a conventional theft loss. The manner of transfer may affect recovery and coverage analysis.

Document the circumstances of possession and transfer. Preserve communications concerning permission, return, or access.

**6.K** Investigate losses involving employees, household members, tenants, guests, contractors, or other persons with authorized access. Obtain statements addressing authority to remove or use the property.

Authorized access may affect whether the event constitutes theft or another form of loss. These facts may also identify a subrogation opportunity.

Document the relationship of each person with access to the claimant and property. Record the facts supporting or disputing authority to remove the property.

**6.L** Separate damage caused during a theft event from property that is merely missing. Evaluate each claimed item under the cause of loss supported by the facts.

The manner of loss may affect valuation, deductible application, and coverage treatment. Combining unlike losses may obscure the investigation.

Document the cause of loss assigned to each claimed item. Explain any item that remains under investigation.

**6.M** Review the claimant’s prior statements, application information, and available claim history for material inconsistencies. Refer material discrepancies for supervisory review before making an adverse coverage determination.

Inconsistencies may result from memory, valuation uncertainty, or inaccurate reporting. They may also indicate the need for expanded investigation.

Document the inconsistent information and the source of each statement. Record the claimant’s explanation and the outcome of the review.

**6.N** Preserve evidence of forced entry, tampering, damage, or removal until the investigation is complete. Do not authorize disposal of material evidence without confirming that it is no longer needed.

Evidence may be necessary to establish the cause of loss or pursue recovery. Premature disposal can prevent meaningful review.

Document preserved evidence and its location. Record the basis for releasing or disposing of any evidence.

**6.O** Obtain a detailed statement when the loss circumstances are unclear, disputed, or unusual. Ask the claimant to describe events in sequence using the claimant’s own words.

A detailed statement may clarify possession, discovery, access, and reporting facts. It also provides a record for later comparison.

Document the statement and identify the person who provided it. Preserve any recorded statement in accordance with claim handling practice.

**6.P** Review whether the claimed loss involves property used for business, trade, rental, or income-producing activity. Obtain facts concerning the property’s actual use.

Property use may affect the applicable coverage analysis and available valuation support. Business use may also identify records that verify ownership.

Document the reported use of the property and the supporting information. Identify any use that remains uncertain.

**6.Q** Evaluate claimed jewelry, watches, gems, and similar articles for the precious stones theft limit. Obtain appraisals, photographs, purchase evidence, and descriptions of each article.

Articles of this type may be subject to specialized theft treatment. Accurate identification is necessary before applying any applicable limitation.

Document each article separately and retain the supporting valuation evidence. Record the basis for applying the precious stones theft limit.

**6.R** Evaluate claimed weapons and related property for the special limit on theft of firearms. Obtain make, model, serial information, and ownership evidence where available.

Firearm claims require clear identification because recovery and reporting obligations may apply. Specialized limit treatment depends on the nature of the claimed property.

Document each firearm and the available identifying information. Record the basis for applying the special limit on theft of firearms.

**6.S** Evaluate claimed silverware, goldware, serviceware, and similar property for the flatware and goldware limit. Obtain descriptions that distinguish ordinary household goods from specialty articles.

Specialty articles may require separate valuation and limit analysis. General household descriptions may not be sufficient to support the claimed value.

Document the description and valuation support for each claimed article. Record the basis for applying the flatware and goldware limit.

**6.T** Evaluate claimed cash, coins, bullion, negotiable items, and similar property for the currency and bullion limit. Obtain the claimant’s explanation of the source, storage, and amount claimed.

These items are difficult to verify after a reported theft or disappearance. Specialized limit treatment may apply based on the property type.

Document the claimant’s explanation and available corroborating evidence. Record the basis for applying the currency and bullion limit.

**6.U** Distinguish between property that is stolen and property that may be recovered, returned, or replaced by another party. Do not finalize valuation while material recovery information remains unresolved.

Potential recovery affects the extent of the covered loss. The claimant must not receive payment for property that has been restored without appropriate adjustment.

Document all recovery inquiries and reported recovery status. Update the claim file when property is located or returned.

**6.V** Notify the claimant to report recovered property promptly. Evaluate recovered property for condition, value, and effect on the open claim.

Recovered property may reduce the loss or create a salvage handling issue. Condition at recovery may establish additional damage from the theft event.

Document the recovery report, property condition, and disposition. Record any claim adjustment resulting from recovery.

**6.W** Obtain information about online listings, pawn activity, resale activity, or marketplace communications when those sources may assist recovery. Preserve available screenshots and contact information.

Stolen property may be offered for sale through channels that provide identifying evidence. Prompt preservation may prevent loss of relevant information.

Document the source reviewed and the information located. Record any referral made for recovery or law-enforcement follow-up.

**6.X** Review whether the claimant has reported similar losses involving the same property, location, or circumstances. Refer repeated or patterned losses for enhanced review.

Repeated loss activity may identify an unresolved cause, inaccurate inventory, or potential fraud concern. It may also reveal a continuing exposure condition.

Document the prior-loss information considered and its relevance. Avoid unsupported conclusions based solely on claim frequency.

**6.Y** Obtain information concerning recent moves, renovations, storage changes, relationship changes, or other events affecting possession of the property. Confirm whether the property may have been relocated.

Property is often reported missing after being moved, packed, stored, or transferred. These circumstances may resolve a disappearance without a theft finding.

Document the events affecting possession and the locations searched. Record the claimant’s basis for concluding that the property was stolen or missing.

**6.Z** Require reasonable search efforts when the facts suggest that property may be misplaced rather than stolen. Ask the claimant to identify locations, persons, and records checked.

A mysterious disappearance requires sufficient investigation before payment is considered. Search efforts may identify the property or clarify its last known location.

Document the search steps reported by the claimant. Record any information that narrows the time or place of disappearance.

**6.AA** Review storage arrangements when the property was kept away from the reported premises. Obtain the identity of the storage provider and the terms of access.

Off-premises storage may involve separate custody, security, and recovery considerations. Storage records may establish possession and timing.

Document the storage location, access arrangements, and available records. Preserve communications with the storage provider.

**6.AB** Obtain information from carriers, bailees, repair facilities, movers, and other custodians when they may possess relevant facts. Confirm whether the property was accepted, transferred, or released.

Third-party custodians may provide independent evidence of possession and loss circumstances. Their records may also identify responsible parties.

Document all third-party contacts and information received. Record any recovery or subrogation potential identified.

**6.AC** Evaluate whether the claimant has a claim against another party for the reported loss. Protect recovery rights before issuing payment where practical.

Theft losses may arise from negligent security, unauthorized release, conversion, or other actionable conduct. Failure to preserve recovery rights may increase claim severity.

Document the potentially responsible party and the facts supporting recovery. Record actions taken to protect the carrier’s interests.

**6.AD** Do not waive investigation requirements merely because the claimant provides a plausible account. Verify material facts through available independent evidence.

A credible presentation does not replace claim verification. Independent evidence supports consistent handling and defensible claim decisions.

Document the verification completed and the evidence relied upon. Identify material facts that could not be independently confirmed.

**6.AE** Refer claims involving suspected staging, false ownership, inflated value, or fabricated theft for special investigation review. Continue ordinary claim handling while protecting the integrity of the investigation.

Fraud indicators require coordinated handling and careful documentation. Premature accusations may create unnecessary claim handling risk.

Document objective indicators and the source of each indicator. Avoid conclusory fraud language in routine claimant communications.

**6.AF** Maintain neutral communications while investigating disputed theft or disappearance facts. Do not state that a claim is denied until the coverage and factual review is complete.

Claimant communications must accurately reflect the status of the investigation. Premature conclusions can impair fact development and create avoidable disputes.

Document material communications with the claimant. Preserve copies of requests, responses, and explanations provided.

**6.AG** Request clarification when claimed values appear unsupported, inconsistent, or disproportionate to the available evidence. Ask for evidence that reasonably supports the claimed valuation.

Valuation uncertainty may be resolved through receipts, comparable property, appraisals, or other reliable information. Unsupported values should not be accepted without review.

Document the valuation information requested and received. Record the valuation method used for each material item.

**6.AH** Apply appropriate condition adjustments to recovered, damaged, used, or partially repaired property. Do not value property as undamaged when the evidence establishes prior wear or damage.

Condition affects actual loss and available recovery. Prior damage must be separated from damage associated with the reported event.

Document the observed condition and the evidence supporting any adjustment. Retain photographs or repair information when available.

**6.AI** Obtain repair information when a theft event includes damage to doors, windows, locks, cabinets, safes, or other property. Separate entry damage from unrelated maintenance conditions.

Entry damage may corroborate the reported theft and establish covered repair needs. Existing deterioration may require separate handling.

Document the damaged condition and the repair support received. Identify damage attributed to wear, neglect, or prior events.

**6.AJ** Confirm whether any alleged stolen property was leased, financed, borrowed, rented, or subject to another person’s interest. Obtain contact information for the interested party.

Property interests may affect payment, recovery, and ownership determinations. Another party may possess records that verify the property and its value.

Document all identified interests and the information obtained. Record any payment or recovery consideration arising from those interests.

**6.AK** Investigate reported theft from vehicles by confirming the location, security condition, and property placement. Obtain available photographs, witness information, and law-enforcement information.

Vehicle theft losses may involve questions concerning entry, visibility, custody, and timing. These facts assist in evaluating the reported event.

Document the vehicle condition and the reported placement of property. Preserve available evidence of entry or damage.

**6.AL** Investigate reported theft from common areas, shared premises, or communal storage locations by identifying access controls and persons with access. Obtain information from property management where available.

Shared locations may create broad access and uncertain possession facts. Management records may assist in establishing security conditions and timing.

Document the shared-location conditions and management information received. Record identified access concerns and unanswered questions.

**6.AM** Evaluate alleged disappearance during travel by obtaining the itinerary, transportation details, lodging information, and last known possession. Request available reports made to transportation or lodging providers.

Travel losses may involve multiple custodians and changing possession. Independent records may clarify where the property was last accounted for.

Document the reported travel circumstances and all provider contacts. Record evidence establishing the last known possession.

**6.AN** Evaluate losses involving mailed, shipped, or delivered property by obtaining tracking information, delivery records, and packaging details. Confirm whether another party accepted delivery.

Transit records may establish whether property was delivered, misdirected, damaged, or stolen. Delivery acceptance may identify the appropriate recovery path.

Document shipment details and all available tracking information. Record the delivery status and any responsible party information.

**6.AO** Confirm whether the claimant has made a report to a financial institution, payment provider, or issuer when negotiable property is involved. Obtain information regarding cancellation, replacement, or reimbursement.

Negotiable property may be subject to separate recovery or replacement processes. Available reimbursement can affect the claimed loss.

Document the actions taken by the claimant and the status of any reimbursement. Record any duplication concern identified.

**6.AP** Evaluate allegations of identity-related theft separately from claims for tangible personal property. Obtain a clear description of the direct property loss being presented.

Identity-related events may involve financial harm that differs from theft of tangible property. The claim must be evaluated on the actual property and loss facts.

Document the tangible property claimed and the evidence supporting the loss. Record related circumstances without expanding the claimed property beyond the facts.

**6.AQ** Obtain witness information when another person observed the property, the alleged theft, or the discovery of loss. Contact witnesses when their information is material to the claim decision.

Witness accounts may corroborate timing, possession, access, and discovery facts. They may also identify conflicting evidence requiring further review.

Document witness contact efforts and the information provided. Identify whether the witness has a relationship to the claimant.

**6.AR** Review surveillance material carefully and preserve the original source when available. Do not rely solely on edited clips or unsupported descriptions of footage.

Surveillance material may establish entry, removal, timing, or access. Edited material may omit facts necessary for a fair evaluation.

Document the source, date received, and content of reviewed footage. Record limitations affecting the reliability or completeness of the material.

**6.AS** Confirm whether the claimant took reasonable steps to protect remaining property after discovering the loss. Obtain information regarding changed locks, repairs, or other protective actions.

Post-loss protection may reduce further loss and preserve evidence. It may also affect the condition of the premises during inspection.

Document protective actions reported by the claimant. Record any continuing condition that requires attention.

**6.AT** Do not direct repairs that could destroy theft evidence before necessary inspection or documentation is complete. Permit reasonable emergency action needed to protect property from further damage.

Claim handling must balance evidence preservation with mitigation needs. Emergency conditions may require prompt action despite incomplete investigation.

Document the condition requiring action and the steps authorized. Retain photographs or other evidence obtained before repairs.

**6.AU** Evaluate each claimed item for actual existence at the time of loss. Do not assume that a general household inventory establishes possession of a specific item.

Item-specific evidence supports accurate claim valuation and reduces duplicate payment risk. General descriptions may not establish that a particular item was present.

Document the evidence supporting the existence of each material item. Record items that remain unsupported after reasonable inquiry.

**6.AV** Confirm whether claimed property was previously sold, gifted, discarded, donated, exchanged, or transferred. Obtain details of any prior disposition discussed by the claimant.

Prior disposition may explain an alleged disappearance without a theft event. It may also identify an ownership issue.

Document the reported disposition history and supporting information. Record any inconsistency requiring additional investigation.

**6.AW** Review communications among household members, custodians, and other involved persons when those communications may clarify possession or removal. Obtain relevant messages in their original form where practical.

Contemporaneous communications may establish timing, consent, or knowledge of the property’s whereabouts. Summaries may omit material context.

Document the communications reviewed and their source. Preserve relevant content in a manner that retains context.

**6.AX** Refer losses involving antiques, collectibles, artwork, manuscripts, or unique property when specialized valuation is needed. Obtain available provenance, appraisal, and condition information.

Unique property may not be reliably valued through ordinary replacement information. Specialized review may be necessary to determine the supported loss.

Document the characteristics that require specialized valuation. Record the valuation evidence and reviewer conclusions.

**6.AY** Evaluate whether damaged packaging, containers, cases, or safes support the reported removal of property. Inspect those items when they may provide material evidence.

Containers may show forced access, handling, or prior damage. Their condition may also identify a different cause of loss.

Document the condition of the container and all observed markings. Retain photographs and repair information when available.

**6.AZ** Confirm that any payment reflects only property supported by the investigation and applicable coverage analysis. Do not include speculative, unverified, or duplicate items.

Accurate payment requires a clear connection between the claimed property, the reported event, and the supporting evidence. Unsupported payment increases claim severity and recovery risk.

Document the basis for each payment component. Identify excluded, deferred, or unresolved items and the reason for their status.

**6.BA** Provide a clear written explanation when claim information is insufficient to complete the theft or disappearance review. Identify the information needed without suggesting that coverage is available.

Clear requests assist the claimant in providing usable information and create an accurate record of claim handling. Neutral language preserves the integrity of the investigation.

Document the information requested and the response received. Record follow-up efforts when the requested information remains outstanding.

**6.BB** Reassess the claim when new evidence changes the reported cause of loss, ownership, valuation, or recovery potential. Update the claim position promptly when the facts support revision.

Theft and disappearance claims may develop as property is located or additional evidence becomes available. Claim decisions must reflect the most reliable information.

Document the new evidence and its effect on the evaluation. Record the reason for any revised claim decision.

**6.BC** Close the claim only after documenting the cause-of-loss evaluation, ownership review, valuation support, recovery inquiry, and claimant communications. Confirm that material investigation items have been resolved or appropriately addressed.

A complete claim file supports consistent handling and later review. Unresolved matters should be clearly identified rather than left unexplained.

Document the closing rationale and the evidence supporting the final disposition. Record any remaining recovery or follow-up responsibility.

## Chapter 7 — Weather: Wind, Hail and Named Storms

**7.A** Open a weather claim when reported wind, hail, or named storm conditions may have caused direct physical loss. Confirm the reported peril before assigning the loss category.

Weather claims require prompt peril identification. Early classification supports consistent handling.

Document the reported weather event and the initial loss category. Record the source of the weather information.

**7.B** Obtain the reported date of loss from the insured or representative. Compare the reported timing with available weather data.

Loss timing may affect coverage review and cause analysis. A clear date of loss also supports claim routing.

Document the reported date of loss and any conflicting information. Note the source of each reported date.

**7.C** Verify the risk location before evaluating weather conditions. Confirm that the weather data applies to the insured premises.

Weather conditions may vary across a local area. Location verification prevents reliance on unrelated storm activity.

Document the verified risk location. Retain the weather search result used for the location review.

**7.D** Assign a qualified field resource when the reported damage requires inspection. Select a resource familiar with wind, hail, and named storm damage.

Weather-related damage may be concealed or misidentified. Qualified inspection supports accurate cause findings.

Document the assignment decision and the selected field resource. Record any stated qualifications relevant to the loss.

**7.E** Advise the insured to protect covered property from further damage when conditions permit. Do not direct repairs that could alter material evidence without adequate documentation.

Reasonable mitigation may reduce avoidable additional damage. Preserving evidence supports cause and scope evaluation.

Document mitigation guidance provided to the insured. Record any emergency measures reported or observed.

**7.F** Review the claim report for indications of wind-driven rain. Distinguish reported water entry from reported roof or exterior damage.

Wind-driven rain claims often require close review of the path of entry. Exterior damage may not establish covered water damage without a supported causal connection.

Document the reported path of water entry. Identify the observed or alleged exterior opening.

**7.G** Inspect roof coverings for displaced, creased, fractured, punctured, or missing materials. Identify whether the observed condition is consistent with the reported weather event.

Roof damage may result from weather, wear, installation conditions, or prior repair activity. Cause analysis must address the condition actually observed.

Document roof observations with photographs and measurements. Record the basis for the cause conclusion.

**7.H** Inspect roof penetrations, flashing, vents, and edge materials when wind damage is alleged. Evaluate whether these components show weather-related displacement or failure.

Wind forces may affect roof components differently than field materials. Detailed inspection may identify the actual source of interior damage.

Document all inspected roof components. Photograph material conditions supporting the findings.

**7.I** Evaluate hail damage by identifying impact marks, fractures, bruising, spatter, or displaced granules where applicable. Do not characterize cosmetic irregularities as functional damage without support.

Hail damage may be difficult to distinguish from age-related deterioration or mechanical marks. Functional impact must be supported by observed conditions.

Document the material type and observed impact characteristics. Retain photographs that show the claimed hail effects.

**7.J** Compare reported hail damage with available storm information for the risk location. Refer unsupported hail reports for further review.

A reported hail event does not establish damage to the insured property. Weather confirmation assists in evaluating plausibility and timing.

Document the hail information reviewed. Record the reason for any referral.

**7.K** Inspect exterior elevations, windows, doors, screens, and shutters when windborne debris is reported. Identify the direction and likely source of any debris impact.

Debris damage may indicate wind conditions or a separate maintenance issue. Source identification supports causation analysis.

Document the damaged elevation and the reported debris source. Photograph impact points and nearby debris.

**7.L** Evaluate fence, awning, carport, and detached structure damage separately from building damage. Confirm whether the damaged property is included in the reported loss.

Weather events may damage multiple property categories with different coverage considerations. Separate evaluation prevents unsupported scope expansion.

Document each damaged property category. Identify the relationship of each item to the reported claim.

**7.M** Inspect trees, branches, and other vegetation when they are alleged to have caused damage. Determine whether the vegetation contacted covered property.

Vegetation may create damage through impact, abrasion, or water entry. The contact mechanism must be supported before attributing damage to the storm.

Document the location and condition of the vegetation. Photograph contact points and resulting property damage.

**7.N** Evaluate interior staining, dampness, and microbial conditions after determining the reported source of water. Do not assume that interior damage resulted from wind or hail.

Interior conditions may arise from long-term leakage, plumbing sources, or prior damage. Cause analysis must connect the observed condition to the reported event.

Document the condition of affected interior materials. Record the evidence supporting the identified water source.

**7.O** Separate new weather-related damage from prior damage, wear, deterioration, and deferred maintenance. Refer material cause disputes for further review.

Weather claims may include conditions unrelated to the reported event. Clear separation supports an accurate loss determination.

Document the basis for separating conditions. Identify the evidence relied upon for each conclusion.

**7.P** Request repair records, inspection reports, and photographs when prior damage is indicated. Use the information to evaluate condition before the reported event.

Prior records may clarify whether claimed damage existed before the loss. Records may also identify incomplete repairs or recurring leakage.

Document records requested and received. Note any material gap in the available history.

**7.Q** Review the claim file for prior weather losses involving the same property. Compare prior damage locations with the current reported damage.

Prior claims may affect cause analysis and repair scope. Similar damage may indicate unresolved conditions or recurring exposure.

Document the prior claim information reviewed. Record any overlap identified between prior and current damage.

**7.R** Suspend binding when forecast landfall is within 48 hours. Apply the suspension consistently to risks exposed to the forecast storm.

Binding controls reduce adverse selection during an approaching named storm. Consistent application protects underwriting integrity.

Document the forecast information supporting the binding action. Record the effective suspension decision in the claim file when relevant.

**7.S** Identify whether the reported event is a named storm before applying named storm handling procedures. Use the carrier’s recognized storm designation.

Named storm classification may affect deductible review and claim routing. Accurate classification avoids inconsistent handling.

Document the storm designation used. Record the source supporting the designation.

**7.T** Review the minimum windstorm and hail deductible percentage when wind or hail is alleged. Confirm the applicable deductible treatment before estimating payment.

Deductible application is a material claim handling decision. Early review reduces avoidable estimate revisions.

Document the deductible review and the basis for the applied treatment. Record any issue requiring escalation.

**7.U** Review the hurricane deductible cap when a named storm loss is reported. Refer any uncertainty regarding the applicable limitation.

Named storm deductible handling may require specialized review. A documented analysis supports consistent claim outcomes.

Document the deductible issue and the disposition. Retain supporting information used in the review.

**7.V** Consider the hurricane occurrence tail when evaluating damage reported after storm conditions have passed. Confirm whether the reported damage remains connected to the named storm event.

Storm-related damage may be discovered after immediate weather conditions end. Cause analysis must remain tied to supported storm effects.

Document the reported discovery circumstances. Record the evidence linking damage to the event.

**7.W** Review the seacoast deductible ceiling for coastal weather losses. Refer the file when the applicable deductible treatment is unclear.

Coastal exposures may require specialized deductible analysis. Early referral reduces inconsistent claim handling.

Document the deductible review. Record the reason for any referral.

**7.X** Preserve material evidence before authorizing removal of damaged roofing or exterior components. Obtain adequate photographs and descriptions when emergency work is necessary.

Removed materials may be necessary to evaluate cause and scope. Evidence preservation supports later review.

Document the condition of materials before removal. Retain photographs, invoices, and contractor statements.

**7.Y** Authorize reasonable emergency services only after confirming the reported need. Limit emergency work to protection, stabilization, and prevention of further damage.

Emergency work may be necessary while cause and scope remain under review. Controlled authorization preserves the carrier’s ability to evaluate the loss.

Document the services authorized and the reason for authorization. Retain invoices and photographs of completed work.

**7.Z** Review contractor estimates for weather-related repairs before accepting the stated scope. Identify items that address wear, upgrades, or unrelated conditions.

Contractor estimates may combine covered and noncovered work. Itemized review supports accurate payment decisions.

Document estimate differences and the basis for any adjustment. Retain the reviewed estimate in the claim file.

**7.AA** Obtain an itemized repair estimate when the reported damage involves multiple building components. Require clear separation of labor, materials, and related work.

Itemized estimates allow comparison with observed damage. Clear scope detail supports accurate evaluation.

Document the estimate received and the components reviewed. Record any requested clarification.

**7.AB** Evaluate matching concerns after establishing covered direct physical damage. Do not include undamaged materials without a supported basis.

Matching issues may affect repair planning and valuation. The claim file must distinguish covered repair needs from elective replacement preferences.

Document the damaged materials and adjacent undamaged materials. Record the basis for the repair scope decision.

**7.AC** Confirm whether temporary repairs altered the reported damage. Obtain photographs or other evidence created before the temporary work when available.

Temporary repairs may obscure the original condition. Pre-repair evidence supports reliable cause findings.

Document the temporary work performed. Retain all available pre-repair photographs and invoices.

**7.AD** Inspect attic spaces, crawl spaces, and concealed areas when the reported damage suggests an opening in the building envelope. Evaluate whether observed conditions support recent weather intrusion.

Concealed areas may provide evidence of water pathways or long-term leakage. Inspection may confirm or contradict reported storm damage.

Document all accessible concealed-area observations. Note any area that could not be inspected.

**7.AE** Evaluate the direction of wind effects when exterior damage is reported. Compare damaged elevations with the reported storm path when available.

Wind direction may help distinguish storm effects from unrelated damage. Directional evidence can also identify likely points of entry.

Document observed directional indicators. Record the weather information used in the comparison.

**7.AF** Inspect gutters, downspouts, and drainage paths when overflow or water intrusion is alleged. Determine whether blockage, detachment, or storm debris contributed to the loss.

Drainage conditions may affect the source and duration of water exposure. The condition must be evaluated independently from the reported weather event.

Document drainage observations and reported maintenance history. Photograph relevant blockage or damage.

**7.AG** Evaluate siding, trim, soffit, fascia, and exterior finishes for wind-related displacement. Distinguish storm damage from age-related loosening or installation defects.

Exterior finish conditions may develop gradually. Supported cause findings require attention to fastening, exposure, and material condition.

Document the affected elevations and components. Photograph conditions supporting the determination.

**7.AH** Inspect detached items for anchoring, attachment, and prior condition when wind damage is reported. Determine whether the reported failure is consistent with storm forces.

Improper attachment may affect the cause of loss. Inspection must address both weather effects and the condition of the item.

Document observed attachment conditions. Record the basis for the weather damage conclusion.

**7.AI** Obtain a recorded statement when material facts regarding storm timing, damage discovery, or mitigation remain unclear. Limit questions to information necessary to resolve the claim.

Weather losses may involve conflicting accounts of damage timing. A focused statement may clarify causation and notice issues.

Document the reason for the statement. Retain the statement and summarize material facts obtained.

**7.AJ** Communicate claim status promptly when weather conditions delay inspection or repair activity. Explain the operational reason without making unsupported coverage statements.

Storm conditions may affect access, safety, and contractor availability. Clear communication helps manage expectations.

Document material communications with the insured or representative. Record any stated access or safety limitation.

Prompt protective action may reduce continuing moisture exposure and secondary contamination. The claim file must show whether the insured acted reasonably after discovery.

Document the mitigation instruction, the reported discovery information, and the insured’s response.

**9.F** Verify whether emergency services are needed to stop active water intrusion or protect the premises. Authorize reasonable emergency measures when supported by the reported facts.

Emergency action may prevent additional property damage and reduce unsafe conditions. Authorization must remain limited to necessary protective work.

Document the condition requiring emergency action and the scope of authorized work.

**9.G** Require reasonable preservation of damaged materials when practical. Do not authorize disposal that prevents evaluation of cause, extent, or coverage unless conditions require immediate removal.

Removed materials may provide relevant evidence concerning source and duration of moisture exposure. Preservation supports defensible claim handling.

Document materials preserved, materials removed, and the reason preservation was not practical when applicable.

**9.H** Obtain a clear chronology of discovery, moisture exposure, repairs, mitigation, and cleanup. Separate reported facts from assumptions or conclusions.

Chronology assists in evaluating sudden events, ongoing conditions, and post-discovery conduct. A complete timeline also identifies missing information.

Document the reported chronology and the source for each material event.

**9.I** Determine whether the moisture condition resulted from an identifiable accidental discharge, overflow, leak, seepage, condensation, weather event, or construction condition. Do not assume causation from the presence of growth.

Coverage depends on the cause of loss and the applicable policy terms. Growth alone does not establish a covered event.

Document the causation analysis and the evidence supporting or contradicting each reported cause.

**9.J** Obtain repair records, invoices, service reports, and prior maintenance information relevant to the reported moisture source. Request only information material to the claim decision.

Maintenance and repair history may show whether damage arose from a recent event or an existing condition. Relevant records may also identify prior loss activity.

Document records requested, records received, and material gaps in the information.

**9.K** Review prior claim information available to the carrier when the reported condition may involve recurring damage. Compare prior reported locations, causes, and repairs to the current loss.

Recurring conditions may affect the investigation of cause, duration, and prior damage. Prior claim information must not replace current inspection findings.

Document the prior claim review and any relevant comparison to current conditions.

**9.L** Separate direct physical damage from cleaning, testing, mitigation, repair, replacement, and preventive work. Evaluate each claimed item under the applicable coverage terms.

Mold-related invoices often combine covered and noncovered work. Clear allocation supports accurate payment and explanation.

Document the claimed work categories and the basis for any allocation.

**9.M** Review the applicable fungi, wet or dry rot, or bacteria aggregate limit before evaluating payment exposure. Apply the limit only after confirming that the claim involves the conditions addressed by that coverage provision.

The fungi, wet or dry rot, or bacteria aggregate limit may affect available payment for covered loss. The file must show a reasoned application of the applicable policy language.

Document the applicable coverage provision, the claim conditions reviewed, and the limit analysis.

**9.N** Do not represent that mold, fungi, wet or dry rot, or bacteria is covered or excluded before completing the cause-of-loss review. Explain that coverage remains subject to investigation and policy terms.

Premature coverage statements may create confusion and inconsistent claim handling. Clear reservation language protects the investigation.

Document material coverage communications and the information provided to the insured.

**9.O** Refer any claim involving disputed causation, substantial contamination, bodily injury allegations, habitability concerns, or unusual remediation scope. Obtain claim leadership guidance before issuing a final coverage position when the file presents unresolved material issues.

Complex conditions may require coordinated claim, legal, technical, or vendor review. Referral promotes consistent carrier decisions.

Document the referral reason, the guidance received, and actions taken from that guidance.

**9.P** Use qualified experts when the condition cannot be reasonably evaluated through inspection and available records. Define the assignment scope before retaining the expert.

Expert involvement should address a specific unresolved claim issue. An unfocused assignment may produce unnecessary cost or incomplete analysis.

Document the assignment purpose, expert qualifications, and requested deliverables.

**9.Q** Review expert reports for consistency with observed conditions, photographs, records, and reported facts. Seek clarification when conclusions are unsupported or outside the assignment scope.

Expert opinions assist the claim evaluation but do not replace claim judgment. Unsupported conclusions must not be adopted without further review.

Document the report review, clarification requests, and the basis for reliance on any opinion.

**9.R** Distinguish testing performed for claim investigation from testing performed for health, regulatory, or restoration purposes. Do not require testing unless it is material to the coverage or damage evaluation.

Testing may identify conditions but may not determine the covered cause of loss. Unnecessary testing can delay mitigation and increase expense.

Document the reason testing was requested or declined and the relevance of the results.

**9.S** Evaluate odor complaints separately from visible damage. Determine whether the odor is tied to a reported covered cause, a preexisting condition, or an unverified source.

Odor may indicate moisture, microbial activity, construction materials, or unrelated conditions. The presence of odor does not establish covered property damage.

Document odor observations, reported locations, and the investigation findings.

**9.T** Identify affected building materials and determine whether they can be cleaned, repaired, or require removal. Base the determination on condition, contamination concerns, and reasonable repair practice.

Material handling affects both mitigation cost and the extent of direct physical damage. The file should distinguish necessary work from elective improvement.

Document affected materials, recommended treatment, and the basis for the repair or replacement decision.

**9.U** Evaluate whether claimed work improves the property beyond its condition immediately before the reported loss. Do not allow betterment without applying the applicable claim handling direction.

Remediation and reconstruction may include upgrades unrelated to covered damage. Betterment must be separated from restoration work.

Document the pre-loss condition, the proposed improvement, and the handling decision.

**9.V** Confirm that mitigation vendors are addressing the reported moisture condition and not performing unrelated renovation work. Review estimates for scope, necessity, and connection to the loss.

Vendor estimates may include work beyond reasonable emergency or remediation needs. Scope review supports appropriate claim payment.

Document estimate review notes, approved scope, and excluded or deferred work.

**9.W** Require invoices to identify labor, materials, equipment, testing, disposal, and other material charges. Request clarification when charges cannot be tied to the claim scope.

Itemized billing supports evaluation of reasonable and necessary costs. Generalized invoices may prevent reliable allocation.

Document invoice review and any clarification received from the vendor or insured.

**9.X** Evaluate remediation recommendations in light of the observed damage and documented moisture source. Do not approve work solely because it is recommended by a vendor.

Recommendations may reflect vendor practice, risk preference, or incomplete site information. Claim approval requires an independent coverage and reasonableness review.

Document the recommendation reviewed and the basis for approval, modification, or denial.

**9.Y** Confirm whether the reported condition affects personal property, building property, or both. Evaluate each property category under the applicable coverage terms.

Different property categories may involve different valuation, responsibility, and coverage issues. Clear classification supports accurate adjustment.

Document the affected property category and the basis for the classification.

**9.Z** Protect personal property from further damage when reasonable handling can reduce loss. Obtain an inventory before disposal or disposal authorization when practical.

Personal property may be damaged by water, contamination, handling, or prolonged storage. Inventory information supports valuation and disposition decisions.

Document protective actions, inventory information, and the reason for any disposal.

**9.AA** Evaluate whether damaged contents can be cleaned, restored, or require replacement. Consider material type, condition, contamination concerns, and reasonable restoration practice.

Contents restoration may reduce claim cost while preserving usable property. Replacement may be appropriate when restoration cannot return property to a reasonable condition.

Document the restoration evaluation and the basis for the disposition of material contents.

**9.AB** Confirm ownership and responsibility for damaged property when occupancy or tenancy is involved. Do not assume the occupant is responsible for all affected property.

Property ownership may affect payment direction and investigation needs. Clear responsibility also reduces duplicate payment risk.

Document ownership information, occupancy status, and any unresolved responsibility issue.

**9.AC** Coordinate with interested parties when the loss involves a landlord, tenant, property manager, lender, or condominium association. Limit communications to information relevant to the claim.

Multiple interested parties may hold different responsibilities for repairs and mitigation. Coordination helps prevent conflicting work and payment instructions.

Document material communications, authority provided, and any dispute affecting claim handling.

**9.AD** Identify whether the property remains safely usable during mitigation and repair. Refer habitability or safety concerns for appropriate claim leadership review.

Unsafe conditions may require prompt attention beyond ordinary repair coordination. Claim personnel must not make unsupported health or safety determinations.

Document reported safety concerns, observed conditions, and referral actions.

**9.AE** Do not provide medical, environmental, or legal advice to an insured or occupant. Direct questions outside the claim scope to an appropriate qualified professional.

Claims personnel must remain within the carrier’s claim handling role. Advice outside that role may create improper reliance.

Document the request for advice and the response provided.

**9.AF** Use neutral language when communicating about possible health effects. Do not state that a reported condition caused illness unless supported by appropriate evidence and authorized claim guidance.

Health allegations may arise from conditions that are not medically established in the claim file. Neutral communication preserves fair handling.

Document reported health allegations without characterizing causation beyond the available information.

**9.AG** Refer bodily injury allegations associated with mold, fungi, wet or dry rot, or bacteria for claim leadership review. Preserve all related communications and evidence.

Bodily injury allegations may create additional exposure and require coordinated handling. Early referral helps protect the carrier’s position.

Document the allegation, referral, and preservation actions.

**9.AH** Evaluate whether the insured completed reasonable temporary repairs to stop the reported source of moisture. Distinguish temporary protection from permanent repair or renovation.

Temporary repairs may reduce further damage while the claim remains under investigation. Permanent work may require separate scope and coverage review.

Document the reported repairs, invoices, photographs, and the purpose of the work.

**9.AI** Do not deny a claim solely because mitigation began before inspection. Evaluate whether the work was reasonable under the reported circumstances and whether evidence remains available.

Prompt mitigation may be necessary to prevent further damage. The absence of pre-mitigation inspection does not eliminate the need for a complete investigation.

Document the reason mitigation began, available evidence, and remaining investigation steps.

**9.AJ** Obtain photographs showing the affected area before, during, and after mitigation when available. Request images that show both close conditions and the surrounding location.

Photographs may establish extent, progression, and connection to the reported source. Context images help verify the affected building area.

Document photographs received, their source, and any limitations in the visual evidence.

**9.AK** Record moisture readings, material condition, and other relevant field observations when obtained. Identify who collected the information and the location observed.

Field observations may support or challenge reported moisture migration. The reliability of the information depends on its source and context.

Document the observation record and any limitation affecting its reliability.

**9.AL** Investigate concealed damage when visible conditions reasonably indicate moisture migration beyond the exposed area. Limit intrusive investigation to work reasonably necessary for the claim evaluation.

Concealed conditions may affect scope, causation, and mitigation needs. Unnecessary destructive work may create avoidable damage.

Document the reason for intrusive investigation, the areas opened, and the results.

**9.AM** Evaluate whether access limitations prevented complete inspection or mitigation. Coordinate reasonable access efforts with the insured or responsible property party.

Unavailable areas may delay confirmation of extent and source. The file must show the carrier’s efforts to obtain necessary access.

Document access requests, responses, missed access opportunities, and remaining limitations.

**9.AN** Identify preexisting staining, deterioration, leakage, or prior repairs that may affect the damage evaluation. Do not treat all observed conditions as resulting from the reported event.

Preexisting conditions may be unrelated to the reported loss or may show an earlier source of moisture. Separating conditions supports accurate payment.

Document the evidence of preexisting condition and the basis for any allocation.

**9.AO** Evaluate repeated leakage, seepage, or maintenance-related conditions based on available facts. Refer unresolved coverage interpretation issues before issuing a final denial or limitation.

Repeated moisture conditions may require careful analysis of cause and duration. The decision must be supported by the policy terms and claim evidence.

Document the facts considered, the interpretation issue, and referral guidance.

**9.AP** Confirm whether weather contributed to the reported moisture condition. Obtain relevant loss information without assuming weather was the sole cause.

Weather may combine with construction defects, drainage issues, or mechanical failure. A complete cause evaluation must address all material contributors.

Document weather-related information and the basis for the causation finding.

**9.AQ** Evaluate roof, wall, window, foundation, plumbing, appliance, and drainage conditions when relevant to the reported source. Do not expand the inspection beyond issues material to the claim.

Building components may direct moisture into areas where mold or related conditions develop. Source identification requires examination of plausible pathways.

Document the components reviewed and the findings relevant to moisture migration.

**9.AR** Distinguish water damage caused by an event from damage caused by faulty workmanship, wear, deterioration, or design conditions. Seek technical support when the distinction cannot be reasonably made from available evidence.

Construction-related conditions may create ongoing moisture exposure without a covered accidental event. Clear causation analysis is necessary before a coverage decision.

Document the evidence supporting the identified cause and any technical opinion obtained.

**9.AS** Review mitigation and repair estimates for duplication of work. Do not approve overlapping charges from separate vendors without a supported explanation.

Multiple vendors may bill for similar services, materials, or handling. Duplicate charges increase the risk of inaccurate payment.

Document the estimate comparison and resolution of overlapping items.

**9.AT** Verify whether remediation work includes demolition, containment, cleaning, drying, disposal, reconstruction, or other services. Match each service to observed conditions and the approved scope.

Different services may be necessary at different stages of the loss. Scope matching prevents payment for unsupported work.

Document the approved service categories and the evidence supporting each category.

**9.AU** Confirm that temporary containment or protective measures are reasonably related to the reported condition. Reevaluate those measures if the claim facts materially change.

Protective measures may be appropriate while source and extent remain under review. Continued measures require ongoing claim support.

Document the purpose of the protective measure and the basis for continued approval.

**9.AV** Evaluate whether relocation, storage, or access-related expenses are claimed in connection with the reported condition. Refer unclear expense categories for claim leadership review.

Related expenses may involve separate coverage questions and factual requirements. Prompt review avoids unsupported commitments.

Document the claimed expense, the claimed necessity, and the handling guidance obtained.

**9.AW** Issue clear written communications when requesting information, authorizing work, reserving rights, or explaining a claim decision. Use language consistent with the evidence available at the time.

Written communication creates the claim record relied upon by the carrier and interested parties. Clear statements reduce misunderstanding about scope and coverage.

Document all material written communications in the claim file.

**9.AX** Explain information requests in a manner that identifies their connection to the reported loss. Avoid requesting broad information that does not assist the investigation.

Focused requests support efficient claim handling and reduce unnecessary burden. The file should show why requested information was material.

Document the purpose of each material information request and the response received.

**9.AY** Follow up on missing material information before making a final decision when reasonable efforts may obtain it. Do not delay a decision for information that is not material.

A complete record supports accurate coverage and valuation decisions. Unnecessary delay may impair fair claim handling.

Document follow-up efforts, unresolved information, and the effect on the claim decision.

**9.AZ** Evaluate depreciation, repairability, and valuation in accordance with the applicable policy terms and claim handling direction. Do not use remediation invoices as the sole evidence of property value.

Remediation cost may not equal the value of damaged property or the cost of covered repair. Valuation must reflect the applicable claim basis.

Document the valuation method, supporting information, and calculation rationale.

**9.BA** Confirm that payment recipients have a documented interest in the covered property or approved work. Resolve payment direction issues before issuing funds when ownership is unclear.

Incorrect payment direction may delay repairs and create duplicate payment exposure. Payment records must support the recipient selected.

Document payment authorization, recipient interest, and any required consent.

**9.BB** Preserve a complete claim file containing inspection records, photographs, estimates, invoices, communications, expert materials, and coverage analysis. Maintain the file so another claim professional can understand the handling decision.

Mold-related claims may involve evolving facts and later review. A complete file supports consistent carrier handling.

Document material file updates as information is received or decisions are made.

**9.BC** Reinspect when material new information suggests the source, extent, or condition differs from the prior evaluation. Do not reinspect solely to repeat a completed review without a claim-related reason.

Changed conditions may affect scope and coverage analysis. Targeted reinspection helps resolve material discrepancies.

Document the reason for reinspection, findings, and any resulting change in claim handling.

**9.BD** Close the claim only after completing the coverage, causation, damage, mitigation, and payment review appropriate to the reported facts. Identify any unresolved matter that remains open after claim closure activity.

Premature closure may leave material claim issues unaddressed. A documented closure review supports finality and consistency.

Document the closure rationale, final communications, and any remaining handling responsibility.

**9.BE** Reopen the claim when credible new information may materially affect coverage, scope, valuation, or payment. Review the new information without assuming the prior decision was incorrect.

New evidence may clarify conditions that were not reasonably available during initial handling. Reopening allows the carrier to evaluate material information fairly.

Document the reopening reason, information received, and the resulting claim decision.

## Chapter 10 — Loss of Use and Additional Living Expense

**10.A** Confirm that a covered direct physical loss has made the residence premises unfit for normal use before considering loss of use. Do not treat inconvenience, preference, or elective relocation as loss of use.

Document the condition preventing normal use and the facts establishing the covered loss.

**10.B** Identify whether the claim presents additional living expense, fair rental value, or both. Apply only the loss of use category supported by the occupancy and rental facts.

Document the occupancy status, intended use, and claimed loss of use category.

**10.C** Establish the date the residence premises became unfit for normal use. Use the covered damage condition rather than the date an insured chooses to leave.

Document the basis for the unfit-for-use date in the claim file.

**10.D** Limit additional living expense to the period reasonably required to repair or replace the damaged residence premises. Additional living expense may be paid for no longer than 24 months.

Document the repair status, the anticipated completion date, and the applicable loss of use end date.

**10.E** Measure additional living expense by the increase in necessary living costs. Do not reimburse ordinary household expenses that would have continued without the loss.

Document the insured’s normal expense and the increased expense attributable to displacement.

**10.F** Require the insured to support additional living expense with reliable records. Receipts, invoices, payment confirmations, and comparable records may establish the incurred cost.

Document the records reviewed and any unsupported expense excluded from payment.

**10.G** Evaluate temporary housing for reasonableness based on the household’s pre-loss living arrangement. Do not approve accommodations materially greater than reasonably necessary for the displaced household.

Document the pre-loss household composition and the basis for the selected temporary housing.

**10.H** Consider available housing near the damaged residence premises when evaluating lodging expense. A more distant location may be reasonable when local housing is unavailable or unsuitable.

Document the housing search information and the reason for the selected location.

**10.I** Evaluate lodging expense separately from meals and incidental charges. Do not assume all charges on a lodging statement are necessary additional living expense.

Document the lodging statement review and the charges accepted or declined.

**10.J** Allow the increased cost of meals only when displacement creates an actual additional expense. Offset meal costs by the ordinary grocery or meal expense that the household would otherwise incur.

Document the claimed meal expense and the ordinary expense used for comparison.

**10.K** Review transportation expense for a direct connection to the covered displacement. Do not pay travel that is personal, recreational, or unrelated to temporary living arrangements.

Document the travel purpose, route, and basis for the additional cost.

**10.L** Evaluate utility expense based on the temporary residence and the insured’s ordinary utility obligation. Do not duplicate utility charges already included in lodging costs.

Document the utility charges and the review for duplication.

**10.M** Consider pet-related expense only when necessary to maintain the displaced household’s ordinary living arrangement. Do not pay elective pet services or costs unrelated to displacement.

Document the pet expense, the necessity of the service, and the basis for payment.

**10.N** Evaluate storage expense when temporary removal of personal property is reasonably necessary because of the covered loss. Do not continue storage payment after the need for storage ends.

Document the property stored, the reason for storage, and the date the need ceased.

**10.O** Review moving expense for necessity and reasonableness. Do not pay repeated moves caused by preference, delay, or a voluntary change in plans.

Document the move, the reason for relocation, and the invoice or payment record.

**10.P** Address cleaning, laundry, and similar household costs as additional living expense only when the loss creates an actual increase. Apply the same ordinary-expense comparison used for other living costs.

Document the increased cost and the ordinary cost excluded from the payment calculation.

**10.Q** Do not pay for upgrades, luxury accommodations, or discretionary services as additional living expense. Loss of use restores necessary living conditions and does not improve the insured’s pre-loss standard of living.

Document the basis for excluding any upgraded or discretionary charge.

**10.R** Apply the Coverage D limit as a percentage of Coverage A as shown in the applicable claim coverage information. Do not alter the available loss of use limit based on hardship or repair delay.

Document the available loss of use limit and the source used to confirm it.

**10.S** Reserve for known and reasonably anticipated additional living expense when exposure is supported by the claim facts. Update the reserve as housing, repair, and occupancy information changes.

Document the reserve basis and each material reserve adjustment.

**10.T** Obtain repair status from reliable claim handling sources before extending loss of use payments. Do not rely solely on an insured’s estimate of completion.

Document the repair status source and the anticipated date normal use may resume.

**10.U** Evaluate contractor delay to determine whether it affects the period reasonably required to repair or replace. Distinguish covered loss-related delay from delay caused by elective changes or unrelated work.

Document the cause of delay and its effect on the loss of use period.

**10.V** Evaluate permit, inspection, and authority delay when the delay is necessary to complete covered repair or replacement. Exclude delay attributable solely to voluntary redesign or expansion.

Document the authority requirement, the delay information, and the related repair activity.

**10.W** Consider material availability when it affects covered repair or replacement. Do not extend payment for delays caused by selection of nonstandard or elective materials.

Document the material issue, the contractor explanation, and the effect on completion.

**10.X** Review mitigation efforts throughout the loss of use period. The insured must take reasonable steps to reduce additional living expense when practical alternatives are available.

Document mitigation discussions, available alternatives, and the insured’s response.

**10.Y** Refer any claim involving prolonged displacement, disputed habitability, or substantial additional living expense for supervisory review. Early review supports consistent handling and reserve control.

Document the referral, the issue presented, and the supervisory direction received.

**10.Z** Coordinate loss of use handling with dwelling repair activity. Do not authorize continued additional living expense without confirming that covered repair remains active and necessary.

Document the repair coordination and the current condition of the residence premises.

**10.AA** Confirm whether the insured occupied the damaged residence premises at the time of loss. Occupancy facts determine whether additional living expense or fair rental value analysis applies.

Document the occupancy evidence and any conflicting information resolved.

**10.AB** Evaluate fair rental value using the rental value of the portion of the residence premises rented to others or held for rental. Do not use speculative rental income.

Document the rental arrangement, rental value support, and any excluded income claim.

**10.AC** Require evidence of an actual rental arrangement when fair rental value is claimed. A stated intent to rent without supporting facts does not establish lost rental value.

Document the lease, payment history, listing information, or other rental evidence reviewed.

**10.AD** Consider vacancy and tenant turnover when evaluating fair rental value. Do not assume uninterrupted rental occupancy where the facts show an expected vacancy or change in tenancy.

Document the vacancy history, tenant communications, and rental occupancy findings.

**10.AE** Distinguish lost rental value from repair cost, tenant damage, and business interruption claims. Each claimed expense or income loss must be evaluated under the appropriate coverage analysis.

Document the classification of each claimed loss of use item.

**10.AF** Address tenant relocation communications promptly when rental occupancy is affected by covered damage. Clear communication may reduce unnecessary lodging and rental disputes.

Document material tenant communications and any assistance authorized.

**10.AG** Do not pay duplicate loss of use benefits for the same living expense or rental period. Review available payment sources and claim file records before issuing payment.

Document the duplication review and the basis for any reduction or denial.

**10.AH** Consider recoveries from other sources when they reimburse the same additional living expense. Do not offset payments that address unrelated loss or expense.

Document the other-source information and the treatment applied.

**10.AI** Evaluate civil authority restrictions only when the restriction directly affects use of the residence premises. Do not treat general travel difficulty or neighborhood concern as a qualifying restriction.

Document the authority action, the affected area, and the impact on residence use.

**10.AJ** Verify that a civil authority restriction arises from conditions connected to the covered loss before considering loss of use payment. The claim file must show the required causal connection.

Document the restriction source and the facts linking it to the covered loss.

**10.AK** Consider partial use of the residence premises when determining additional living expense. Reduced convenience does not alone establish that the entire household must relocate.

Document the usable areas, unavailable areas, and the reason for any relocation.

**10.AL** Evaluate health and safety conditions using reliable evidence. Do not direct an insured to remain in a residence premises that presents an unsafe condition related to the covered loss.

Document the condition evidence, any expert opinion, and the handling decision.

**10.AM** Do not determine habitability solely from visible damage. Consider essential services, sanitation, access, structural condition, and other conditions affecting normal residential use.

Document the habitability assessment and the facts supporting the conclusion.

**10.AN** Address restoration of essential services promptly when it may end or reduce additional living expense. Timely restoration may permit the insured to resume normal use of the residence premises.

Document service interruption, restoration efforts, and the effect on occupancy.

**10.AO** Evaluate temporary repairs that may make the residence premises suitable for normal use. Do not continue displacement payment when reasonable temporary measures restore safe occupancy.

Document the temporary repair assessment and the resulting occupancy decision.

**10.AP** Review invoices for recurring charges that continue after the insured returns home. Additional living expense ends when the covered displacement no longer causes the increased cost.

Document the return-to-home date and the final expense review.

**10.AQ** Obtain current contact information for the insured during displacement. Prompt communication supports payment accuracy and timely resolution of housing issues.

Document contact information updates and material communication attempts.

**10.AR** Issue additional living expense payments based on verified incurred expense unless claim circumstances support another approved payment method. Do not advance funds without establishing the loss of use need and payment basis.

Document the payment method, supporting information, and approval rationale.

**10.AS** Explain the need for receipts and prompt expense submission to the insured. Clear instructions reduce avoidable delays and unsupported payment requests.

Document the instructions provided and the method of communication.

**10.AT** Review claimed expenses at regular claim contacts while the insured remains displaced. Ongoing review prevents accumulation of unsupported or unnecessary charges.

Document each expense review and any updated payment direction.

**10.AU** Escalate disputes concerning temporary housing necessity, cost, or duration when material facts remain unresolved. Do not delay undisputed payment while resolving the disputed portion.

Document the disputed issue, the undisputed amount handled, and the escalation action.

**10.AV** Maintain a clear calculation for each additional living expense payment. The file must show the claimed cost, ordinary cost, approved increase, and payment disposition.

Document the calculation in the claim financial record.

**10.AW** Identify taxes, fees, deposits, and similar charges within temporary housing costs. Pay only charges that are necessary, nonrefundable, and attributable to covered displacement.

Document the charge type, refund status, and payment determination.

**10.AX** Review refundable deposits separately from incurred living expense. A refundable deposit is not additional living expense merely because it is required to secure housing.

Document the deposit terms and the reason for any payment decision.

**10.AY** Consider reasonable accessibility needs when evaluating temporary housing. A suitable temporary residence may require accommodations necessary for a household member’s documented condition.

Document the accommodation need and the basis for the selected housing expense.

**10.AZ** Protect confidential information received in support of loss of use handling. Use only the information necessary to evaluate coverage, occupancy, expense, and payment.

Document sensitive records in the appropriate claim file location.

**10.BA** Close the loss of use exposure only after confirming that no supported additional living expense or fair rental value remains. A complete closing review reduces the risk of omitted payment or duplicate payment.

Document the final occupancy status, final payment review, and closure rationale.

## Chapter 11 — Proof of Loss, Appraisal and Suit

**11.A** Require a signed proof of loss when the claim file requires the insured to state the claimed amount and supporting facts. Identify the requested information clearly in the claim correspondence.

A complete proof of loss establishes the insured’s position and permits evaluation of the reported damage. It also separates claimed facts from carrier findings.

Document the request, delivery method, and response status in the claim file. Retain the submitted proof of loss and all supporting materials.

**11.B** Apply the proof of loss period as stated in the applicable coverage terms. Do not extend or shorten that requirement without documented claim authority.

The proof of loss period controls the timing of the insured’s formal claim submission. Consistent handling preserves defenses and avoids misleading communications.

Document the controlling requirement and the date the request was issued. Record any approved accommodation and the basis for it.

**11.C** Send the proof of loss request to the insured and any authorized representative shown in the claim file. Use language that identifies the loss, requested statement, and required supporting information.

Clear delivery reduces disputes over whether the insured received the request. It also creates a reliable record of the carrier’s instructions.

Document the recipient, delivery method, and transmission result. Preserve a copy of the issued request in the claim file.

**11.D** Request only information material to the reported loss, claimed amount, ownership, valuation, or coverage evaluation. Do not use the proof of loss process to obtain unrelated personal information.

A focused request supports prompt adjustment and reduces avoidable friction. Material requests are easier to enforce and evaluate.

Document the reason each material category was requested. Note any item withdrawn or narrowed during claim handling.

**11.E** Review a submitted proof of loss promptly for completeness, signature, claimed amount, and supporting facts. Identify deficiencies without treating the submission as accepted proof of coverage.

Early review permits efficient correction and protects the carrier from unintended admissions. Completeness review is separate from coverage determination.

Document the review findings and any deficiencies communicated to the insured. Preserve the reviewed version with the claim notes.

**11.F** Acknowledge receipt of a proof of loss without agreeing that the claimed amount is owed. State that the carrier will continue its investigation and evaluation.

Receipt acknowledgment prevents uncertainty about delivery while preserving the carrier’s position. It should not be phrased as acceptance of liability.

Document the acknowledgment and its delivery method in the claim file. Retain the correspondence with the submitted proof.

**11.G** Return an incomplete proof of loss for correction when the missing information prevents meaningful evaluation. Describe the deficiency specifically and request a revised submission.

Specific deficiency notices give the insured a fair opportunity to cure. Vague objections create unnecessary disputes and delay.

Document each identified deficiency and the related request for correction. Retain copies of all revised submissions.

**11.H** Do not reject a proof of loss solely because its presentation differs from a preferred carrier format. Evaluate whether the submission supplies the material information needed to adjust the claim.

The proof of loss requirement concerns substance as well as execution. Technical objections should not replace substantive claim evaluation.

Document the information accepted from the submission and any material information still needed. Explain any continued deficiency in the claim notes.

**11.I** Request supporting estimates, invoices, photographs, inventories, or ownership evidence when needed to evaluate the claimed loss. Tailor the request to the property and damage at issue.

Supporting records assist in testing scope, value, condition, and ownership. They also support a defensible payment or denial decision.

Document the requested records and the reason they are material. Record receipt, review, and any unresolved gaps.

**11.J** Require the insured to separate damaged property from undamaged property when practical and material to the evaluation. Do not require separation when doing so would create an unreasonable safety or preservation concern.

Separation may assist inspection, valuation, and salvage evaluation. Safety and loss mitigation remain controlling considerations.

Document the instruction given and any condition preventing compliance. Record photographs or inspection observations supporting the handling decision.

**11.K** Evaluate the proof of loss against inspection findings, reported facts, and available claim evidence. Refer material inconsistencies for further investigation before making a payment decision.

Inconsistencies may result from mistake, incomplete information, changed conditions, or potential misrepresentation. They must be resolved on evidence rather than assumption.

Document the inconsistency, investigative steps, and outcome. Preserve records supporting the final evaluation.

**11.L** Do not treat a proof of loss as a waiver of the insured’s right to dispute the carrier’s evaluation. Maintain a clear distinction between the insured’s claim presentation and the carrier’s determination.

The proof of loss records the insured’s asserted amount and facts. It does not resolve all coverage, valuation, or causation issues.

Document the carrier’s independent evaluation separately from the insured’s submission. Use claim notes that identify disputed issues clearly.

**11.M** Do not state that a proof of loss establishes coverage, causation, or the amount payable unless authorized by the coverage determination. Reserve all unresolved claim issues in carrier communications.

A signed statement may support evaluation without resolving the claim. Premature statements can create avoidable disputes about the carrier’s position.

Document the communication review and any required reservation language. Escalate ambiguous correspondence before release.

**11.N** Issue payment for an undisputed covered amount when authorized, even if the insured submits a broader proof of loss. Do not delay an otherwise payable amount solely because another portion remains disputed.

Prompt payment of an undisputed amount supports fair claim handling. It also narrows the issues requiring further evaluation.

Document the basis for the undisputed amount and the issues remaining open. State clearly that payment does not resolve unresolved portions of the claim.

**11.O** Refer a proof of loss containing material misstatements, altered records, or unexplained valuation changes for special investigation review. Do not accuse the insured of fraud without supporting facts and required authority.

Potential misrepresentation must be evaluated carefully and consistently. Referral preserves evidence and permits appropriate investigation.

Document the observed facts rather than conclusions or labels. Preserve original records and identify all referral activity in the claim file.

**11.P** Obtain a clear withdrawal statement when the insured withdraws a proof of loss or reduces the claimed amount. Confirm whether the withdrawal affects the entire claim or only a stated portion.

A withdrawal may affect evaluation, payment, appraisal, and suit handling. The file must show the insured’s intended scope.

Document the withdrawal communication and the claim issues affected. Retain any revised proof or written confirmation.

**11.Q** Preserve the proof of loss and related correspondence as claim record material. Do not discard drafts, attachments, or delivery evidence that may bear on the handling history.

Proof of loss records may become material in appraisal, litigation, or coverage review. A complete file supports the carrier’s position.

Document the location of electronically stored materials and any original records retained. Maintain the record according to claim file controls.

**11.R** Explain that appraisal addresses the amount of loss and does not decide coverage questions unless claim authority determines otherwise. Keep coverage analysis separate from appraisal communications.

Appraisal is a valuation process, not a substitute for coverage investigation. Clear issue framing reduces improper expansion of the appraisal process.

Document the issues identified as valuation matters and the issues retained for coverage evaluation. Preserve all communications defining the appraisal scope.

**11.S** Evaluate any demand for appraisal promptly and confirm whether the dispute concerns the amount of loss. Refer demands involving unresolved coverage, causation, or condition issues for claim authority review.

A demand may contain both appraisal issues and nonappraisal issues. Early analysis protects the carrier’s rights while allowing proper valuation handling.

Document the demand, date received, and initial issue analysis. Record the authority decision and resulting response.

**11.T** Respond to a valid appraisal demand in writing through the assigned claim representative or authorized counsel. State the carrier’s position on appraisal without conceding unresolved coverage issues.

Written response creates a clear record of the carrier’s election and reservations. It also reduces later disagreement about the scope of the process.

Document the response, delivery method, and any stated reservation. Retain proof of transmission in the claim file.

**11.U** Select a competent and impartial appraiser when appraisal proceeds. Confirm that the selected appraiser has no conflict that would impair independent judgment.

The appraiser’s role requires credible valuation expertise and fair consideration of the loss. Conflicts may undermine the appraisal result.

Document the appraiser’s qualifications, conflict review, and appointment communication. Retain any disclosures received from the appraiser.

**11.V** Apply the days to select an appraiser after a demand for appraisal as stated in the applicable coverage terms. Refer any inability to meet that requirement for immediate claim authority direction.

Timely appraiser selection preserves the appraisal process and avoids unnecessary procedural disputes. Delayed action may impair the carrier’s position.

Document receipt of the demand, selection activity, and notice of the carrier’s appraiser. Record the reason for any referral or delayed response.

**11.W** Instruct the carrier-appointed appraiser to value the loss independently and within the authorized scope. Do not direct the appraiser to reach a predetermined amount.

Independent appraisal supports a credible determination of loss value. Outcome-driven instructions undermine the process and the carrier’s position.

Document the scope instruction and any materials provided to the appraiser. Preserve communications concerning valuation issues.

**11.X** Provide the carrier-appointed appraiser with relevant inspection records, estimates, photographs, and valuation materials. Do not provide privileged claim advice or unrelated investigative materials without authority approval.

The appraiser requires reliable factual information to evaluate the amount of loss. Controlled disclosure protects claim handling information and preserves appropriate privileges.

Document the materials transmitted and the reason they were provided. Identify any withheld material and the authority supporting that decision.

**11.Y** Require the appraisers to agree on an umpire within 15 days. Refer any failure to obtain agreement for direction on the available appointment process.

An agreed umpire supports orderly resolution when the appraisers cannot reach a joint valuation. Prompt escalation prevents the appraisal from becoming inactive.

Document the appraisers’ selection efforts and all umpire communications. Record the referral and resulting appointment action.

**11.Z** Review proposed umpire candidates for apparent conflicts before consenting to an appointment. Raise material concerns promptly and through the authorized appraisal channel.

An umpire must be capable of acting fairly on valuation differences. Conflict review protects the integrity of any resulting award.

Document the candidate review, identified concerns, and consent or objection. Retain disclosures and appointment communications.

**11.AA** Distinguish an appraisal award from a coverage determination in all claim communications. Do not describe an award as resolving issues not submitted to appraisal.

An appraisal award may establish value while leaving other claim defenses or conditions unresolved. Accurate communication preserves the carrier’s position.

Document the award scope and any issues remaining for claim evaluation. Use clear claim notes when applying the award.

**11.AB** Review an appraisal award for facial completeness, signatures, stated loss items, and apparent scope issues before issuing payment. Refer an irregular award for claim authority or legal review.

Award review ensures that payment is based on a usable and authorized valuation determination. It also identifies issues requiring prompt response.

Document the award review and the basis for any referral. Preserve the executed award and related correspondence.

**11.AC** Pay the amount supported by a valid appraisal award when coverage and claim conditions support payment. Do not withhold payment based solely on dissatisfaction with the valuation result.

Appraisal is intended to resolve valuation disputes through the selected process. Payment handling must remain consistent with unresolved coverage issues.

Document the award amount, covered application, and any deductions or limitations applied. Explain any amount not paid through authorized claim correspondence.

**11.AD** Apply the payment after agreement period as stated in the applicable coverage terms. Treat an appraisal award requiring payment as an agreement only when claim authority confirms that treatment.

Payment timing depends on the controlling claim circumstances and the applicable coverage terms. Careful classification avoids inaccurate timing calculations.

Document the payment trigger and the basis for the timing determination. Record approval of any exception or reservation.

**11.AE** Do not reopen settled valuation issues merely because later estimates differ, unless authorized claim authority identifies a valid basis. Review new information for materiality before changing the carrier’s position.

Finality supports efficient claim resolution and reliable appraisal handling. Material new facts may require further evaluation.

Document the new information, materiality review, and authority decision. Preserve all revised estimates and supporting evidence.

**11.AF** Refer disputes over causation, coverage, policy conditions, or excluded property that arise during appraisal for claim authority review. Do not ask appraisers to decide issues outside their assigned valuation role.

Appraisal cannot cure an unresolved coverage analysis. Proper referral keeps valuation and coverage functions distinct.

Document the disputed issue and the instruction given to the appraiser. Record the coverage review outcome separately from the appraisal file.

**11.AG** Maintain professional and neutral communications with the insured during appraisal. Do not use appraisal correspondence to pressure acceptance of unrelated claim positions.

Appraisal should narrow valuation disagreement without impairing fair claim handling. Neutral communications reduce avoidable escalation.

Document substantive communications and any issues raised by the insured. Escalate complaints or allegations requiring management review.

**11.AH** Coordinate appraisal activity with mortgagees, lienholders, and other interested parties when their interests may affect payment handling. Do not disclose claim information beyond the authority provided in the claim file.

Interested parties may have payment rights or claim-related interests requiring controlled handling. Coordination prevents payment errors and confidentiality concerns.

Document the interest identified and the communication authority relied upon. Retain notices and payment instructions in the claim file.

**11.AI** Preserve all appraisal correspondence, estimates, awards, and appointment records in the claim file. Identify whether each record originated from the carrier, insured, appraiser, or umpire.

A complete appraisal record supports later payment, dispute, and litigation handling. Source identification assists in evaluating the evidentiary record.

Document storage location and access controls for appraisal materials. Maintain originals when available.

**11.AJ** Refer a threatened action against the insurer to the designated claim authority upon receipt of a demand, complaint, summons, or similar notice. Do not provide legal conclusions to the insured.

Early referral permits preservation of defenses and coordinated response. Informal threats may also require review when they identify a material dispute.

Document the notice received, source, and date of receipt. Preserve the original communication and record the referral.

**11.AK** Apply the years within which an action against the insurer must be brought as stated in the applicable coverage terms. Refer any potential time-bar issue before communicating a position to the insured.

Suit limitations may affect claim handling, defense, and settlement decisions. Incorrect communication can create waiver or estoppel concerns.

Document the controlling claim analysis and the authority review. Record all communications concerning the potential limitation issue.

**11.AL** Preserve all claim materials when suit is anticipated or filed. Suspend routine destruction or alteration of potentially relevant claim records.

Litigation exposure requires careful preservation of evidence. Incomplete records can impair defense and claim evaluation.

Document the preservation action and the materials identified. Record instructions provided to personnel with relevant records.

**11.AM** Coordinate with authorized counsel after suit is filed or when claim authority directs counsel involvement. Provide a complete factual claim file and identify unresolved issues.

Counsel requires accurate claim history to evaluate pleadings, defenses, and settlement posture. Timely coordination prevents inconsistent positions.

Document the referral, materials provided, and counsel instructions received. Maintain privileged communications according to carrier controls.

**11.AN** Do not negotiate directly with a represented insured on matters assigned to counsel without authorization. Route substantive communications through the approved channel.

Direct contact may create procedural or ethical concerns. Controlled communication protects the carrier and supports coordinated defense handling.

Document representation information and the communication routing decision. Preserve all authorized communications.

**11.AO** Continue appropriate claim investigation after suit is filed unless counsel or claim authority directs otherwise. Do not assume litigation eliminates the need for accurate claim evaluation.

Litigation may reveal additional facts affecting exposure, payment, or settlement. Ongoing investigation must remain coordinated and controlled.

Document investigative activity and any counsel consultation. Identify new evidence and its effect on claim evaluation.

**11.AP** Refer any request for examination, recorded statement, inspection, or document production received after suit is filed to authorized counsel. Do not issue unilateral litigation-related demands without direction.

Post-suit discovery and investigation must be coordinated with the defense strategy. Uncontrolled requests may create procedural issues.

Document the request, referral, and resulting instruction. Preserve all responsive communications in the claim file.

**11.AQ** Evaluate settlement opportunities based on the supported claim value, coverage position, litigation posture, and authority limits. Do not allow appraisal status alone to control settlement evaluation.

Settlement analysis requires consideration of all material claim factors. A reasoned evaluation supports fair and consistent resolution.

Document the settlement analysis and authority obtained. Record material changes affecting the evaluation.

**11.AR** Maintain a clear chronology of proof of loss, appraisal, payment, and suit activity. Update the chronology when a material event occurs.

These claim events often affect one another and may become disputed later. A reliable chronology supports supervisory and legal review.

Document each material event with its source and related correspondence. Correct errors promptly without deleting the original record.

**11.AS** Use reservation language when communicating about unresolved coverage, valuation, causation, or condition issues. Obtain claim authority approval for nonstandard reservation language.

Reservations preserve the carrier’s ability to complete a full evaluation. Imprecise language can create misunderstanding or unintended commitment.

Document the approved language and its delivery. Record the issues reserved in the claim notes.

**11.AT** Do not state that appraisal is mandatory, binding, or unavailable without confirming the applicable coverage terms and claim authority position. Refer unusual appraisal provisions for review.

Appraisal obligations and effects depend on the controlling claim circumstances. Accurate statements avoid procedural disputes.

Document the provision review and authority guidance. Retain the communication stating the carrier’s position.

**11.AU** Identify partial agreements reached during proof of loss or appraisal handling. Confirm in writing which property, scope, valuation, or payment issues remain disputed.

Partial agreements may permit payment and narrow further handling. Clear confirmation prevents later disagreement about what was resolved.

Document the agreed matters and unresolved matters separately. Retain written confirmation from the involved parties.

**11.AV** Escalate any allegation that the carrier delayed proof of loss, appraisal, payment, or suit handling improperly. Review the file chronology before responding.

Delay allegations require fact-based review and controlled communications. Early escalation permits correction where warranted.

Document the allegation, chronology review, and management direction. Preserve related communications and response drafts.

**11.AW** Protect confidential claim information during proof of loss, appraisal, and suit handling. Limit disclosure to persons with a valid claim-related need for the information.

Claim materials may contain personal, financial, and investigative information. Controlled disclosure supports privacy and claim integrity.

Document authorized disclosures and the recipient’s role. Record any reported privacy concern and resulting action.

**11.AX** Close proof of loss, appraisal, and suit tasks only after confirming that all assigned actions are complete or appropriately transferred. Do not close an activity merely because correspondence has become inactive.

Unresolved tasks can impair payment, defense, and customer service. Clear task disposition supports accurate claim file management.

Document task completion, transfer, or reason for continued pendency. Identify the responsible claim owner for open matters.

**11.AY** Refer any conflict between proof of loss information, appraisal findings, and litigation allegations for supervisory review. Do not resolve material conflicts through unsupported claim notes.

Conflicting positions may affect coverage, valuation, credibility, and defense strategy. Supervisory review promotes a consistent carrier response.

Document the conflicting information and the review outcome. Preserve records supporting the final handling direction.

**11.AZ** Apply claim authority requirements before compromising a proof of loss dispute, accepting an appraisal outcome outside ordinary handling, or resolving a suit-related claim issue. Obtain approval before making a binding commitment.

Authority controls protect the carrier from unauthorized settlement or coverage action. They also ensure appropriate review of material exposure.

Document the authority requested, granted, and used. Retain approval records with the related claim activity.

**11.BA** Communicate claim decisions in plain, accurate language that distinguishes facts, valuation, and coverage conclusions. Do not use internal claim shorthand in correspondence to the insured.

Clear communication supports fair handling and reduces misunderstanding. Internal terminology may not accurately convey the carrier’s position externally.

Document the communication review and delivery. Retain the final issued version in the claim file.

**11.BB** Notify claim authority when an appraisal, proof of loss dispute, or suit issue creates potential extra-contractual exposure. Provide the factual basis without speculation.

Potential handling exposure requires prompt management awareness. Fact-based reporting permits informed direction and corrective action.

Document the referral and the facts provided. Record resulting instructions and follow through.

**11.BC** Use only authorized experts, vendors, appraisers, and counsel for assigned claim activities. Refer any request to use an unapproved participant for claim authority review.

Authorized participants support quality control, confidentiality, and consistent claim handling. Unapproved engagement may create avoidable cost or procedural concerns.

Document the participant selection and authorization. Retain engagement communications and work product.

**11.BD** Review payments issued after proof of loss, appraisal, or suit activity for correct payees, claim allocation, and stated purpose. Correct payment errors promptly through authorized procedures.

Payment accuracy protects the insured, interested parties, and the carrier. Clear allocation also supports later claim reconciliation.

Document payment review findings and any correction action. Retain payment records with the related claim correspondence.

**11.BE** Report material proof of loss, appraisal, and suit developments to the assigned supervisor according to claim handling controls. Escalate developments that could alter the carrier’s exposure or position.

Supervisory awareness supports timely direction and consistent handling. Material developments may require revised authority or strategy.

Document the report and any supervisory instruction. Update the claim plan to reflect the resulting direction.

**11.BF** Complete the claim file with a final summary of proof of loss, appraisal, payment, and suit activity when those issues are resolved. Identify the basis for the final claim outcome and any matters reserved or transferred.

A complete summary supports audit, complaint response, and future claim review. It also demonstrates that the carrier handled the matter through an organized process.

Document the final summary in the claim file and confirm record retention status. Refer any unresolved issue before final closure.

## Chapter 12 — State Prompt Payment Standards

**12.1** Open a prompt-payment diary when a property claim is received. Assign the diary to the handling file without delay.  
Prompt handling begins with a reliable receipt record. An unrecorded receipt event can impair deadline control.  
Document compliance by preserving the intake time stamp. Record the assigned diary owner in the claim file.  

**12.2** Identify the applicable state prompt-payment standard at claim intake. Apply the controlling standard throughout handling.  
Prompt-payment obligations depend on the claim’s governing jurisdiction. Incorrect jurisdiction selection can create avoidable compliance exposure.  
Document compliance by recording the jurisdiction determination. Retain the basis for the determination in the claim file.  

**12.3** Review the reported loss information for completeness upon receipt. Request material information that is reasonably needed for handling.  
Early identification of missing information supports efficient investigation. Unsupported requests can delay handling without advancing resolution.  
Document compliance by recording the information reviewed. Preserve each request and the stated handling purpose.  

**12.4** Acknowledge receipt of the claim within 15 days. Use the approved acknowledgement communication.  
A prompt acknowledgement confirms that the claim entered the handling process. It also establishes a clear communication record.  
Document compliance by retaining the acknowledgement and its delivery record. Record the receipt event that started the acknowledgement obligation.  

**12.5** Provide accurate claim contact information in the acknowledgement. Direct the claimant to the assigned handling channel.  
Clear contact information reduces misdirected communications. It also supports prompt collection of claim information.  
Document compliance by preserving the communication sent. Record the contact information provided to the claimant.  

**12.6** Do not represent that coverage is available before completing the required review. Do not imply denial before the file supports that position.  
Premature coverage statements can mislead the claimant. They can also complicate later claim communications.  
Document compliance by recording the basis for each coverage communication. Preserve material communications in the claim file.  

**12.7** Review the policy information needed to evaluate the reported loss. Confirm that the policy data matches the claim file.  
Accurate policy information is necessary for a reliable coverage evaluation. File mismatches can delay disposition.  
Document compliance by recording the policy information reviewed. Note any discrepancy and the action taken.  

**12.8** Identify the claimed property and reported damage clearly. Separate reported facts from unverified conclusions.  
A clear loss description supports focused investigation. It also prevents unsupported assumptions from entering the file.  
Document compliance by recording the reported damage description. Identify the source of material claim information.  

**12.9** Evaluate whether emergency measures are needed to protect covered property. Communicate appropriate mitigation expectations without directing unnecessary work.  
Reasonable mitigation can limit further damage. Improper direction can create disputes about responsibility and cost.  
Document compliance by recording the mitigation discussion. Preserve material photographs, invoices, and communications received.  

**12.10** Assign the claim to personnel with authority to handle the reported issues. Escalate assignments that require specialized review.  
Appropriate assignment supports timely and accurate handling. Delayed escalation can affect prompt-payment compliance.  
Document compliance by recording the assignment decision. Note the reason for any specialized referral.  

**12.11** Establish a current loss chronology from available information. Update the chronology as material events occur.  
A current chronology helps identify pending obligations. It also supports supervisory review of file progress.  
Document compliance by recording material claim events in the chronology. Preserve supporting communications and evidence.  

**12.12** Review all claimant communications when received. Respond to material communications through the assigned handling channel.  
Unaddressed communications can leave needed issues unresolved. Timely responses support an orderly investigation.  
Document compliance by retaining incoming and outgoing communications. Record the handling action taken for each material issue.  

**12.13** Request only information that is relevant to coverage, valuation, or loss adjustment. State the purpose of each material request.  
Focused requests promote efficient claim resolution. Overbroad requests may create unnecessary delay.  
Document compliance by preserving each request. Record why the requested information was needed.  

**12.14** Avoid repetitive requests for information already in the claim file. Review the file before requesting replacement materials.  
Repeated requests frustrate claimants and delay investigation. File review reduces unnecessary handling activity.  
Document compliance by recording the pre-request file review. Identify any unavailable item that required a new request.  

**12.15** Review submitted information promptly after receipt. Identify whether additional material information is actually needed.  
Prompt review prevents information from remaining unused in the file. It also supports a timely coverage evaluation.  
Document compliance by recording the receipt and review of submitted information. State the reason for any additional request.  

**12.16** Treat a claim as ready for decision when requested material information is available. Do not delay disposition for immaterial items.  
A decision should rest on information needed to resolve the claim. Waiting for unnecessary items can impair compliance.  
Document compliance by recording the readiness determination. Identify the material information supporting the decision.  

**12.17** Monitor the accept or deny deadline throughout claim handling. Escalate the file when the deadline is at risk.  
Active deadline monitoring reduces avoidable prompt-payment failures. Escalation allows management to remove handling barriers.  
Document compliance by recording each deadline review. Preserve the escalation record and resulting direction.  

**12.18** Accept or reject the claim within 30 days after receiving the requested items. Issue a clear written disposition.  
A timely disposition provides the claimant with a definitive coverage position. Clear communication reduces uncertainty and dispute risk.  
Document compliance by retaining the disposition communication and delivery record. Record the receipt of the requested items supporting the decision.  

**12.19** State the coverage basis for an acceptance in clear claim-specific language. Identify material conditions affecting payment or adjustment.  
A clear acceptance explains the carrier’s handling position. It also supports accurate payment processing.  
Document compliance by retaining the acceptance communication. Record the coverage analysis supporting the acceptance.  

**12.20** State the coverage basis for a denial in clear claim-specific language. Identify the material facts supporting the denial.  
A supported denial helps the claimant understand the disposition. It also demonstrates that the file received a reasoned review.  
Document compliance by retaining the denial communication. Record the coverage analysis and supporting evidence.  

**12.21** Do not use conclusory denial language without claim-specific support. Explain the applicable coverage position accurately.  
Unsupported conclusions can create confusion and complaint exposure. Specific analysis promotes consistent claim handling.  
Document compliance by preserving the final denial rationale. Identify the policy information and facts relied upon.  

**12.22** Explain any partial acceptance or partial denial clearly. Distinguish accepted damage from damage not accepted.  
Mixed dispositions require careful communication to avoid misunderstanding. Clear separation supports accurate valuation and payment.  
Document compliance by retaining the disposition communication. Record the basis for each accepted and denied component.  

**12.23** Refer a file for coverage review when material facts create uncertainty. Do not allow referral status to suspend ordinary claim activity.  
Coverage review can improve decision quality. Unmanaged referral activity can create unnecessary delay.  
Document compliance by recording the referral reason. Preserve the reviewer’s guidance and resulting handling action.  

**12.24** Refer a file for supervisory review when prompt-payment compliance is at risk. Follow supervisory direction promptly.  
Supervisory involvement can resolve obstacles affecting disposition. Delayed action after escalation can worsen compliance exposure.

**12.27** Review inspection findings for consistency with the reported loss. Resolve material discrepancies through appropriate investigation.  
Unresolved discrepancies can affect coverage and valuation decisions. Focused follow-up supports a defensible claim file.  
Document compliance by recording the discrepancy identified. Preserve the information supporting its resolution.

Document compliance by recording payment deadline monitoring. Preserve escalation notes and corrective actions.  

**12.38** Confirm that approved payment instructions match the accepted claim disposition. Correct material discrepancies before release.  
Payment instructions must reflect the carrier’s coverage position. Mismatched instructions can result in inaccurate payment.  
Document compliance by recording the payment instruction review. Preserve the approved payment authorization.

Controlled payment processing supports accuracy and auditability. Unapproved methods can impair delivery verification.  
Document compliance by recording the payment method used. Preserve the payment transaction record.

Clear payment communication helps the claimant understand the transaction. It also reduces inquiries caused by unexplained payments.

Supplemental reports may change the claim adjustment. Prompt review prevents supported issues from remaining unresolved.

**14.O** Evaluate whether the association has asserted responsibility for common elements or shared systems. Do not treat an assertion as conclusive without supporting facts.

Association responsibility positions can affect handling direction and payment coordination. The reported position may change as facts develop.

**14.P** Evaluate whether the unit owner has asserted responsibility for interior finishes, improvements, or betterments. Confirm the claimed interest before evaluating the repair demand.

**14.Q** Identify damage to shared plumbing, electrical, heating, cooling, or drainage systems. Refer technical cause questions for appropriate evaluation when needed.

**14.R** Confirm whether the reported loss affects neighboring units or common areas. Expand the investigation when facts indicate a broader impact.

A localized report may conceal damage to adjacent property interests. Prompt expansion limits avoidable delay and inconsistent claim treatment.

**14.T** Confirm the party authorized to receive payment for association-owned property. Do not issue payment based solely on an invoice recipient.

Payment authority may differ from repair coordination authority. Verification reduces the risk of misdirected funds.

Document the payment recipient, asserted authority, and verification source. Record any restriction or condition applied to payment handling.

**14.U** Confirm the party authorized to receive payment for unit-owned property. Address conflicting payment instructions before releasing funds.

Unit owners, lenders, and associations may assert competing interests in payment proceeds. Early review prevents avoidable reissue activity.

Document the payment instructions received and the basis for accepting them. Record unresolved conflicts and handling direction.

**14.V** Review estimates for duplicated repair items across unit and association scopes. Remove overlap before approving payment.

Condominium losses frequently generate separate estimates for connected property areas. Duplicate scope can inflate the claim and delay repairs.

Document the estimate comparison and each overlap identified. Record the disposition of duplicate or transferred repair items.

**14.W** Require a clear scope description for all claimed repairs. Do not approve generalized restoration charges without support.

A defined scope permits comparison with observed damage and other repair demands. It also supports later payment review.

Document the accepted repair scope and the evidence supporting it. Record any scope item deferred pending additional information.

**14.X** Address code-related repair demands only after confirming their connection to covered damage and required restoration. Do not accept a contractor statement as sufficient support.

Code issues may arise from preexisting conditions or elective improvements. Careful review prevents unsupported expansion of the repair scope.

Document the claimed code requirement and the facts supporting its relevance. Record the basis for accepting or declining the related work.

**14.Y** Review association assessments tied to the reported loss before discussing payment. Confirm the stated basis, purpose, and damaged property interest.

An assessment may include expenses unrelated to the claimed event. Proper review distinguishes a loss-related demand from general association expense.

Document the assessment demand and the stated purpose of the charge. Record the evidence used to connect or disconnect the assessment from the loss.

**14.Z** Confirm the loss assessment additional coverage limit before accepting an assessment-based demand. Apply the applicable handling position consistently throughout the claim.

Assessment demands may create expectations that differ from direct property damage handling. Early review supports accurate communications and payment control.

Document the loss assessment additional coverage limit considered during the review. Record the handling decision and supporting claim facts.

**14.AA** Confirm the assessment endorsement amount when an assessment endorsement affects the reported demand. Do not rely on an unsupported statement that an endorsement applies.

Endorsement-related handling requires a clear connection between the demand and the reported loss. Accurate review prevents payment beyond the applicable claim position.

Document the assessment endorsement amount considered in the claim evaluation. Record the facts supporting the endorsement-related decision.

**14.AB** Confirm the condominium dwelling default limit when evaluating unit property that may be subject to a default handling position. Escalate uncertainty before communicating a definitive amount.

Default limit handling can affect expectations for unit repair and settlement. Consistent review reduces avoidable disputes.

Document the condominium dwelling default limit considered during evaluation. Record the facts supporting the default handling position.

**14.AC** Obtain a complete damage statement from the unit owner when the owner seeks payment for interior property. Require the statement to identify the claimed items and locations.

Owner descriptions may omit common-area damage or include unrelated property. A focused statement improves estimate accuracy.

Document the owner’s damage statement and any supporting photographs. Record material differences between the statement and inspection findings.

**14.AD** Obtain a complete damage statement from the association when the association seeks payment. Require the statement to identify the affected property and claimed repairs.

Association repair demands may include shared property not visible during the initial inspection. Clear identification supports appropriate scope review.

Document the association’s damage statement and claimed repair scope. Record unresolved differences between the association statement and observed conditions.

**14.AE** Review all contractor communications for scope changes, source opinions, and repair recommendations. Do not adopt a contractor opinion without evaluating its factual support.

Contractor conclusions may be influenced by repair preferences or incomplete site access. Independent review supports sound claim handling.

Document material contractor opinions and the evidence cited. Record the basis for accepting, modifying, or rejecting each opinion.

**14.AF** Refer suspected fraud, misrepresentation, or intentional loss activity through the designated claim process. Do not accuse a claimant or association representative of wrongdoing.

Condominium claims can involve multiple parties and competing financial interests. Controlled referral protects the investigation and claim communications.

Document the facts creating the concern without using conclusory language. Record the referral and any direction received.

**14.AG** Identify prior damage, repairs, and known conditions that may affect the current claim. Do not assume prior conditions are unrelated without review.

Prior conditions may explain visible damage or complicate cause analysis. Accurate history improves scope and responsibility decisions.

Document available information concerning prior damage and repairs. Record how prior conditions were considered in the current evaluation.

**14.AH** Investigate signs of repeated leakage, recurring backup, or ongoing moisture conditions. Treat recurring conditions separately from sudden reported damage.

Recurring conditions may involve maintenance concerns or prior unreported damage. Clear differentiation supports a defensible claim position.

Document the indicators of recurrence and their locations. Record the basis for separating recurring damage from the reported event.

**14.AI** Confirm whether temporary protective measures remain necessary after inspection. Communicate promptly when additional protection is needed.

Unprotected property may sustain further damage while responsibility issues remain under review. Timely action limits avoidable severity.

Document recommended protective measures and the party notified. Record whether the measures were completed or declined.

**14.AJ** Monitor repair activity when ongoing work may affect cause evidence or scope accuracy. Request updated information when conditions materially change.

Repair progress can reveal concealed damage or eliminate evidence needed for evaluation. Active monitoring supports accurate claim decisions.

Document material repair developments and resulting scope changes. Record updated photographs, invoices, and communications in the claim file.

**14.AK** Evaluate personal property claims separately from building damage claims. Do not include movable property in a structural repair scope.

Personal property may belong to occupants, associations, or service providers. Separate evaluation prevents incorrect payment direction.

Document the claimed personal property, ownership assertion, and observed condition. Record the basis for including or excluding each item.

**14.AL** Evaluate improvements and betterments based on the claimant’s demonstrated interest in the damaged property. Do not presume ownership from possession alone.

Occupants may use improvements without having responsibility for their repair. Clear interest review supports proper claim handling.

Document the claimed improvement and the asserted ownership basis. Record evidence supporting the accepted property interest.

**14.AM** Identify damage caused by an association contractor or vendor when alleged. Preserve the allegation without accepting liability before investigation.

Vendor involvement may create recovery potential or competing responsibility positions. Early evidence preservation supports later review.

Document the alleged vendor involvement and available supporting facts. Record communications with the association concerning the allegation.

**14.AN** Identify potential recovery opportunities arising from another party’s acts or omissions. Preserve relevant evidence and avoid compromising recovery rights.

Condominium losses often involve contractors, unit occupants, or shared system maintenance. Timely recovery review supports carrier interests.

Document the potential responsible party and factual basis. Record evidence preserved and any recovery referral made.

**14.AO** Communicate claim decisions in clear terms that identify the damaged interest and basis for the decision. Avoid language that assigns responsibility beyond the established facts.

Clear communication reduces misunderstanding among unit owners and associations. Precise language also protects the claim file from inconsistent positions.

Document material claim communications and the information provided. Record any question or disagreement raised in response.

**14.AP** Do not direct repairs outside the established claim scope. Explain when requested work requires further review.

Association representatives may seek broader work to coordinate building repairs. Scope control prevents unintended commitments.

Document requested work outside the accepted scope and the response provided. Record the reason for approving, deferring, or declining the request.

**14.AQ** Review repair invoices against the accepted scope before payment. Question charges for unapproved work, unsupported materials, or unrelated locations.

Invoice review confirms that payment follows the documented loss evaluation. It also identifies scope changes requiring further investigation.

Document the invoice comparison and payment recommendation. Record all adjustments and the reason for each adjustment.

**14.AR** Confirm that repairs address the reported damage rather than elective upgrades. Distinguish restoration needs from preference-driven changes.

Association projects may combine loss repairs with broader improvement activity. Clear separation prevents payment for nonloss work.

Document the accepted restoration work and any excluded upgrade component. Record the evidence used to make the distinction.

**14.AS** Evaluate debris removal demands by location and relation to covered damage. Do not approve building-wide cleanup charges without a loss-specific basis.

Debris activity may serve both damaged and undamaged property areas. Location-based review supports accurate allocation.

Document the debris condition, removal scope, and claimed charges. Record the basis for accepting or limiting payment.

**14.AT** Evaluate access-related expenses only when they are necessary to inspect, mitigate, or repair reported damage. Require a clear explanation of the access need.

Access work may be claimed for convenience rather than loss-related necessity. Proper review avoids unsupported expense.

Document the access condition and work performed. Record the factual basis for accepting or declining the charge.

**14.AU** Confirm whether damaged common property remains in service during repairs. Coordinate handling when loss conditions affect building operations or resident safety.

Operational impacts can influence access, mitigation, and repair sequencing. Timely coordination reduces disruption and further damage.

Document reported operational impacts and the party managing them. Record material claim actions taken in response.

**14.AV** Identify all available photographs, video, and electronic communications relevant to the loss. Preserve material evidence in the claim file.

Digital evidence may capture conditions that change before inspection. Early preservation supports later review of cause and scope.

Document the source and content of preserved digital evidence. Record any relevant evidence reported unavailable.

**14.AW** Obtain statements from persons with direct knowledge of the loss when their information may affect cause or scope. Keep questions focused on observed facts.

Association personnel, occupants, and vendors may provide differing accounts. Fact-focused statements assist in resolving material discrepancies.

Document the substance of each statement and the person’s claimed knowledge. Record material conflicts requiring further investigation.

**14.AX** Escalate disputes involving competing repair responsibility when available facts do not support a clear handling position. Do not resolve the dispute through unsupported compromise.

Responsibility disputes can affect payment direction and recovery potential. Escalation promotes consistent handling of material conflicts.

Document the disputed issue, parties involved, and facts gathered. Record the direction received and claim action taken.

**14.AY** Maintain respectful communications with association representatives, unit owners, occupants, and vendors. Do not permit disagreement over responsibility to delay necessary loss investigation.

Condominium losses can create tension among parties with different interests. Professional communication supports access and information exchange.

Document material communication concerns and steps taken to maintain claim progress. Record any conduct affecting investigation or repair activity.

**14.AZ** Reevaluate the claim when new evidence changes the reported cause, damaged property interest, or repair scope. Update the handling position promptly.

Condominium claim facts may develop as access improves and repairs expose concealed damage. Reevaluation prevents reliance on outdated assumptions.

Document the new evidence and its effect on the claim evaluation. Record all resulting changes to scope, payment, or responsibility handling.

**14.BA** Confirm that all material claim decisions are supported by facts in the claim file. Do not rely on undocumented oral conclusions.

A complete file permits consistent review and defensible claim handling. It also supports continuity when claim ownership changes.

Document the evidence supporting each material decision. Record the rationale in clear claim notes.

**14.BB** Maintain separate records for unit-owner demands, association demands, and vendor submissions. Do not merge communications that concern different property interests.

Separate records reduce confusion over scope, authority, and payment direction. They also improve review of competing claims.

Document the source and subject of each material submission. File supporting materials under the related claimed interest.

**14.BC** Confirm that payment communications identify the property interest and repair scope being addressed. Avoid broad statements that could be interpreted as approval of unrelated work.

Specific payment communication limits misunderstanding among claim participants. It also helps prevent duplicate or unauthorized demands.

Document the payment communication and identified scope. Record any reservation, limitation, or requested follow-up.

**14.BD** Close the claim only after resolving or documenting all known property interests, payments, and outstanding investigation items. Do not close while material responsibility issues remain unaddressed.

Condominium claims may remain active after visible repairs appear complete. A complete closure review reduces reopened claim activity.

Document the closure review and all remaining issues, if any. Record the basis for closure and final claim status.

**14.BE** Reopen the investigation when credible new information indicates additional loss-related damage or an unresolved property interest. Review the new information without assuming the prior decision controls.

Later-discovered conditions may affect the accepted scope or payment direction. Prompt review supports fair and consistent claim handling.

Document the reopening trigger and new evidence received. Record the revised investigation steps and resulting decision.

**14.BF** Protect confidential claim information when communicating among unit owners, associations, property managers, and vendors. Share only information necessary for authorized claim handling.

Condominium losses involve multiple parties who may not share the same property interest. Controlled communication reduces improper disclosure risk.

Document material information requests and the response provided. Record the basis for any limitation on disclosure.

## Chapter 15 — Dwelling Property Losses

**15.A** Open the dwelling loss file under the applicable coverage and assign the reported location. Identify the claimant and all known interested parties.  

A complete intake establishes the handling path and preserves coverage issues. Early identification reduces avoidable delay.  

Document the reported cause, date of discovery, location, and contact information in the claim file. Record any immediate safety or habitability concern.  

**15.B** Verify that the damaged property is the insured dwelling location before extending coverage consideration. Resolve conflicting location information before authorizing material payments.  

Location verification connects the loss to the applicable risk. Unresolved location discrepancies can affect ownership, occupancy, and valuation.  

Document the source used to confirm the insured location. Record any discrepancy and the action taken to resolve it.  

**15.C** Establish the dwelling's occupancy status at the time of loss and during the relevant loss circumstances. Obtain facts from the claimant and available physical evidence.  

Occupancy may affect the cause investigation and available coverage. Reliable occupancy facts also support mitigation and loss of use handling.  

Document the claimant's occupancy statement and any supporting observations. Record signs of regular habitation, temporary absence, vacancy, or unauthorized occupancy.  

**15.D** Apply the vandalism exclusion where the dwelling has been vacant for 60 days. Confirm the vacancy facts before issuing a coverage position.  

Vacancy can materially affect the coverage analysis for malicious damage. The file must support the conclusion reached.  

Document the evidence of vacancy and the source of each fact. Record the vandalism findings and the resulting coverage decision.  

**15.E** Identify the efficient cause of loss before determining coverage. Distinguish the initiating event from resulting damage.  

Accurate cause analysis prevents payment for excluded damage and prevents denial of covered resulting damage. The investigation must address the actual mechanism of loss.  

Document the reported cause, observed damage pattern, and expert findings when obtained. Explain how the facts support the cause determination.  

**15.F** Preserve available evidence when the cause, extent, or responsibility for the loss is uncertain. Direct the claimant not to discard material evidence without inspection when practical.  

Evidence preservation supports coverage analysis, recovery activity, and dispute resolution. Lost evidence may prevent a reliable determination.  

Document the preservation instructions given and the claimant's response. Retain photographs, inspection notes, and relevant physical evidence information in the file.  

**15.G** Instruct the claimant to protect the dwelling from further damage after the loss. Authorize reasonable emergency measures when supported by the reported conditions.  

Prompt mitigation limits avoidable deterioration and reduces the ultimate loss. Emergency action must remain tied to protection of covered property.  

Document the mitigation instructions, emergency authorizations, and observed conditions. Record the reasonableness of incurred emergency charges.  

**15.H** Review emergency repairs for necessity and relation to the reported damage. Do not treat emergency work as proof that all resulting repairs are covered.  

Emergency work may be necessary before the full cause and scope investigation is complete. Coverage remains subject to the applicable terms and facts.  

Document the emergency condition, work performed, vendor information, and invoices received. Note any portion requiring further investigation or allocation.  

**15.I** Determine the scope of direct physical damage caused by the reported event. Separate damaged property from undamaged, preexisting, or unrelated conditions.  

A defensible scope reflects observable damage and supported causation. Broad estimates without damage support can create unsupported indemnity.  

Document the inspection findings with photographs, measurements where available, and a detailed estimate. Explain material scope differences between inspections or estimates.  

**15.J** Evaluate exclusions and limitations that may apply to the reported cause of loss. Do not issue a final coverage position until material facts have been developed.  

Coverage analysis requires consideration of the full factual record. A partial investigation can produce an inaccurate outcome.  

Document each potentially applicable coverage issue and the facts supporting or defeating it. Record the basis for the final determination.  

**15.K** Consider whether covered resulting damage exists when an excluded condition contributes to the loss. Distinguish the excluded condition from separate damage caused by a covered event.  

Some loss presentations involve more than one mechanism of damage. Proper separation protects the carrier and the claimant from an overbroad result.  

Document the sequence of damage and the basis for any allocation. Identify the evidence supporting coverage for resulting damage or denial of excluded damage.  

**15.L** Separate wear, deterioration, maintenance deficiencies, and construction defects from sudden accidental damage. Do not include non-loss improvements in the repair scope.  

Long-term conditions may resemble recent loss damage. A careful distinction prevents the claim payment from funding deferred maintenance.  

Document the condition of affected components and any indicators of age, wear, or deficient upkeep. Explain the treatment of each disputed item.  

**15.M** Investigate concealed damage when the reported event may have affected building components not visible during the initial inspection. Authorize reasonable access when necessary to determine scope.  

Concealed damage may require limited investigation before a reliable estimate can be completed. Access should remain focused on the reported loss.  

Document the reason for exploratory work and the findings obtained. Retain photographs showing conditions before and after access work.  

**15.N** Identify the source of water when water damage is reported. Distinguish water originating inside the dwelling from water originating outside the dwelling.  

Water claims require fact-specific cause analysis. The source and path of water often determine the coverage outcome.  

Document the reported source, observed water path, and inspection findings. Record any expert opinion relied upon for the water origin determination.  

**15.O** Investigate freezing losses by determining the condition of the dwelling and the affected system. Obtain available facts concerning heat, occupancy, and maintenance.  

Freeze damage may involve preventable conditions, hidden defects, or sudden system failure. The file must show a reasonable investigation of the relevant facts.  

Document the claimant's account, service records when available, and physical evidence. Record the basis for the coverage decision.  

**15.P** Evaluate plumbing losses by identifying the failed component and the resulting property damage. Separate repair of the failed component from covered damage caused by the failure when required by the coverage terms.  

Plumbing claims frequently include both source repair and resulting damage. Clear allocation supports accurate indemnity.  

Document the failed component, cause of failure, and resulting damage. Identify how the estimate treats source repair and resulting repairs.  

**15.Q** Inspect roof damage for storm-created openings, age-related deterioration, installation defects, and unrelated conditions. Do not assume that interior staining establishes covered roof damage.  

Roof and interior damage may arise from different causes. The damage pattern must support the claimed pathway of water intrusion.  

Document roof observations, interior conditions, and available weather information. Retain photographs of affected and representative unaffected areas.  

**15.R** Investigate wind damage by identifying displaced, broken, or otherwise altered building materials. Distinguish wind damage from deterioration, improper installation, and mechanical damage.  

Wind damage can be obscured by preexisting condition issues. A focused inspection supports an accurate scope and coverage determination.  

Document the damaged materials, observed failure pattern, and condition of adjoining materials. Explain any difference between the claimed damage and the supported damage.  

**15.S** Evaluate hail damage through physical inspection and, when appropriate, specialized review. Do not rely solely on reported weather conditions to establish property damage.  

Weather occurrence does not establish covered physical damage. The dwelling must show damage consistent with the reported event.  

Document the observed impact marks, material condition, and inspection conclusions. Record the basis for accepting or declining claimed hail damage.  

**15.T** Investigate theft losses by identifying the property allegedly taken and the circumstances of disappearance. Obtain available information concerning forced entry, access, ownership, and recovery efforts.  

Theft claims require reliable evidence of loss and ownership. The investigation must distinguish theft from unexplained disappearance or prior disposal.  

Document the claimant's description of the missing property and all supporting information obtained. Record law enforcement information when available.  

**15.U** Investigate vandalism by identifying intentional damage and separating it from accidental damage, neglect, or deterioration. Confirm whether the damage occurred during the claimed loss circumstances.  

Intentional damage may present unique cause and vacancy concerns. The file must support the conclusion that the damage was malicious and occurred as alleged.  

Document the damage pattern, entry conditions, claimant statements, and any available incident information. Record the basis for the vandalism determination.  

**15.V** Handle fire losses with attention to origin, cause, safety, and preservation of damaged property. Refer suspicious fire circumstances for specialized investigation.  

Fire losses can involve significant property damage, safety hazards, and recovery potential. Prompt coordination protects the investigation and the claim outcome.  

Document origin and cause information, fire service findings, and any referral made. Retain photographs and reports received in the claim file.  

**15.W** Evaluate smoke damage by identifying the source of smoke and the affected building components. Distinguish smoke residue from ordinary dirt, cooking residue, or preexisting staining.  

Smoke can migrate beyond the visible area of origin. Proper assessment prevents both under-scoping and unsupported cleaning charges.  

Document the source, affected areas, odor observations, and cleaning recommendations. Record the basis for the approved scope.  

**15.X** Investigate collapse conditions before authorizing substantial demolition or rebuilding work. Determine the damaged structure, precipitating event, and extent of structural impairment.  

Collapse allegations may involve sudden damage, long-term deterioration, or construction issues. The cause and condition of the structure must be established before coverage is determined.  

Document structural observations, safety actions, and expert findings. Record the facts supporting the collapse analysis.  

**15.Y** Evaluate damage from falling trees, limbs, and other objects by identifying the object and the point of impact. Separate impact damage from unrelated landscape, maintenance, or improvement work.  

Impact losses may create hidden structural damage and emergency access needs. The scope should address actual damage caused by the object.  

Document the object, impact location, removal needs, and resulting dwelling damage. Retain photographs showing the object and damaged property when available.  

**15.Z** Review increased repair costs arising from code, permit, or authority requirements only after confirming that the requirement applies to the covered repair. Do not include elective upgrades as loss costs.  

Required work may differ from betterment selected by the claimant or contractor. The file must distinguish mandated work from discretionary improvement.  

Document the authority requirement, repair condition, and support for the added cost. Record the basis for any allowance or denial.  

**15.AA** Separate dwelling building damage from personal property damage and detached property damage. Assign each damaged item to the appropriate coverage consideration.  

Accurate classification prevents duplication and preserves applicable limits. Mixed estimates can obscure the proper payment basis.  

Document the classification of each material damage item. Explain any allocation affecting the valuation or payment calculation.  

**15.AB** Verify ownership or insurable interest when the dwelling ownership is unclear or disputed. Obtain reliable information before releasing payment to a disputed party.  

Ownership issues can affect payment direction and recovery rights. A premature payment may create duplicate payment exposure.  

Document the ownership information obtained and any unresolved interest. Record the payment direction and basis for that direction.  

**15.AC** Identify mortgagee, lienholder, or other financial interest information that affects payment handling. Coordinate payment issuance with the applicable claim obligations.  

Interested parties may have a recognized financial interest in covered dwelling damage. Proper handling reduces payment disputes and protects claim closure.  

Document the known financial interests and payment instructions. Retain evidence supporting the payee determination.  

**15.AD** Determine the appropriate valuation method before preparing the final dwelling estimate. Apply the valuation basis consistently to comparable damage items.  

Valuation affects the amount payable and the claimant's understanding of the settlement. Inconsistent treatment creates avoidable disputes.  

Document the valuation method used and the information supporting it. Explain material estimate adjustments and depreciation decisions.  

**15.AE** Review repair estimates for reasonable materials, labor, and scope related to the covered damage. Challenge unsupported, duplicate, or unrelated charges.  

A supported estimate reflects necessary work to restore covered damage. Estimate review protects claim accuracy without directing contractor selection.  

Document the estimate reviewed, material revisions, and the basis for each adjustment. Record communications concerning disputed scope or pricing.  

**15.AF** Issue undisputed payment when the covered amount can be determined without awaiting resolution of unrelated matters. Do not delay payment solely because a separate issue remains under investigation.  

Timely payment supports fair claim handling and reduces additional loss exposure. The file must still preserve any unresolved coverage issue.  

Document the undisputed amount, payment basis, and unresolved issue. Record the explanation provided to the claimant.  

**15.AG** Evaluate supplemental requests against the original scope, newly discovered damage, and applicable coverage terms. Do not approve a supplement without support for causation and necessity.  

Supplements may reflect legitimate concealed damage or unsupported expansion of the repair scope. Consistent review maintains estimate integrity.  

Document the supplemental request, inspection findings, and disposition. Explain the reason for approval, adjustment, or denial.  

**15.AH** Evaluate additional living expense and rental loss under the applicable loss of use limit. Confirm that the claimed expense or lost rental value resulted from the covered dwelling damage.  

Loss of use handling requires a connection between the covered loss and the claimed inability to occupy or rent. Ordinary living costs and unrelated vacancy conditions are not claim expenses.  

Document the displacement facts, claimed expenses, rental information, and occupancy status. Record the basis for each payment decision.  

**15.AI** Review temporary housing needs for reasonableness and relation to the damaged dwelling's condition. Consider available accommodations that meet the claimant's practical needs without creating unnecessary expense.  

Temporary housing should respond to the actual loss of use caused by covered damage. The claim file must support the chosen accommodation.  

Document the dwelling condition, claimant needs, housing options considered, and approved arrangement. Record communications concerning housing decisions.  

**15.AJ** Monitor repair progress when loss of use benefits depend on restoration of the dwelling. Address avoidable delays caused by the claimant, contractor, or claim handling activity.  

Extended repairs can increase claim expense and create disputes over responsibility for delay. Active monitoring supports timely restoration.  

Document material repair milestones, delay reasons, and actions taken. Record any impact on loss of use handling.  

**15.AK** Preserve subrogation potential whenever another party may have caused or contributed to the dwelling loss. Do not waive recovery rights without appropriate authority.  

Recovery may offset claim costs and protect the carrier's financial interest. Early preservation prevents loss of evidence and recovery opportunities.  

Document the potentially responsible party, supporting facts, and preservation actions. Record referrals and information transmitted for recovery review.  

**15.AL** Manage salvageable property to prevent unnecessary disposal and to protect any remaining value. Obtain claimant agreement before disposing of property when required by claim handling procedures.  

Salvage handling can affect indemnity, recovery, and claimant satisfaction. Disposal without adequate documentation can create disputes.  

Document the condition, disposition decision, and any salvage proceeds. Retain photographs and disposal information in the claim file.  

**15.AM** Refer claims presenting material inconsistencies, questionable ownership, altered evidence, or suspected misrepresentation for further review. Continue necessary claim handling without accusing the claimant of fraud.  

Special investigation protects the carrier while preserving fair treatment of the claimant. Unsupported accusations create unnecessary exposure.  

Document the specific facts supporting the referral and the actions taken. Record communications in neutral, factual language.  

**15.AN** Communicate the coverage decision, payment basis, and remaining claim requirements clearly to the claimant. Use plain claim language and avoid statements beyond the supported file facts.  

Clear communication reduces misunderstanding and supports prompt resolution. The claimant must understand the action taken and any information still needed.  

Document all material communications, including the subject discussed and any commitments made. Retain copies of claim correspondence in the file.

## Chapter 16 — Ordinance or Law

**16.A** Confirm that the reported damage resulted from a covered direct physical loss. Do not open ordinance or law handling for maintenance, wear, or voluntary improvement.

Ordinance or law coverage responds only when the covered loss creates the applicable condition. A governmental requirement alone does not establish coverage.

Document the loss facts, the reported code issue, and the source of the requirement. Retain the available authority communication in the claim file.

**16.B** Identify the damaged building component before evaluating any ordinance or law expense. Separate damaged property from undamaged property.

The extent of direct physical damage controls the ordinance or law analysis. Clear separation prevents unsupported upgrade allowances.

Document the affected component, observed damage, and condition of adjacent property. Record the basis for any distinction between damaged and undamaged work.

**16.C** Obtain the applicable authority’s requirement before allowing increased construction cost. Do not rely solely on contractor advice.

A contractor may identify a potential issue without establishing a mandatory requirement. The authority having jurisdiction determines enforcement.

Document the authority contact, the requirement stated, and the enforcement status. Retain written directives, notices, or permit comments when available.

**16.D** Confirm that the requirement applies to the insured location and planned repair scope. Do not assume that a general code provision applies.

Code provisions vary by occupancy, construction, location, and scope of work. Applicability must be established for the specific loss.

Document the property characteristics considered and the stated basis for applicability. Record any authority interpretation obtained during adjustment.

**16.E** Distinguish repair requirements from replacement requirements. Do not characterize elective replacement as a required ordinance or law cost.

The required method of restoration may differ from the method preferred by the insured. Only mandatory work supports the ordinance or law evaluation.

Document the available repair options and the authority’s stated requirement. Identify the basis for selecting the required method.

**16.F** Evaluate whether the damaged portion may be repaired under the applicable requirement. Refer the matter when enforcement requires broader action.

Partial repair may remain permissible despite substantial visible damage. Broader action requires a documented enforcement basis.

Document the damaged area, proposed repair, and authority response. Record the reason for any referral.

**16.G** Separate demolition expense caused by the covered loss from demolition undertaken for convenience. Allow only the supported demolition scope.

Demolition may be necessary to comply with an enforced requirement. Convenience demolition does not become covered merely because repairs are underway.

Document the demolition purpose, affected materials, and authority direction. Retain contractor scope descriptions that identify required removal.

**16.H** Confirm that any undamaged portion claimed for demolition is subject to an applicable requirement. Do not extend demolition coverage based on anticipated future work.

Undamaged property may require removal only when enforcement compels that action. Planned renovation does not establish a covered demolition obligation.

Document the undamaged portion at issue and the requirement requiring removal. Record the evidence distinguishing mandatory work from planned improvement.

**16.I** Review notices of violation, condemnation communications, and permit conditions promptly. Refer unclear enforcement positions for coverage review.

Authority communications may contain conditions that materially affect the claim. Prompt review reduces unsupported construction commitments.

Document the communication received, the issuing authority, and the action required. Record the referral outcome and handling direction.

**16.J** Verify that the requirement was in force when it was applied to the repair. Do not rely on informal statements about prior practice.

The applicable requirement must be enforceable against the loss repair. Informal expectations may not establish an enforceable obligation.

Document the authority’s confirmation of enforceability. Retain the available code citation or written explanation.

**16.K** Do not authorize code-related work before confirming the applicable coverage position. Permit necessary emergency protection subject to claim handling authority.

Premature authorization can create expense beyond the supported claim scope. Emergency measures may still be necessary to protect property from further damage.

Document the reason for any emergency action and the work performed. Record the coverage review completed before broader work proceeds.

**16.L** Permit emergency repair expense without prior approval up to $2,500. Require the insured to preserve invoices, photographs, and damaged materials when practicable.

Emergency work may prevent additional damage and protect the premises. The claim file must still support the nature and necessity of the expense.

Document the reported emergency condition and the repair performed. Record the invoices received and the inspection findings.

## Chapter 18 — Subrogation and Salvage

**18.A** Open a subrogation review when facts indicate that a party outside the insured may have caused or contributed to covered damage.

Prompt review preserves recovery opportunities and prevents avoidable impairment of carrier rights.

Document the apparent responsible party, known facts, and the basis for the review in the claim file.

**18.B** Notify the subrogation unit within 10 days after identifying a recoverable loss.

Timely notice allows the subrogation unit to evaluate liability, preservation needs, and recovery strategy before evidence changes.

Document the referral date, the loss facts provided, and the subrogation unit contact in the claim file.

**18.C** Preserve all known recovery rights when adjusting a loss with a potential third-party cause.

An unauthorized release, settlement, or waiver may prejudice the carrier's ability to recover claim payments.

Document the recovery-rights review and any action taken to preserve those rights in the claim file.

**18.D** Do not authorize the insured to release a potentially responsible party without subrogation unit review.

A release may extinguish the carrier's recovery interest even when the insured believes the matter is resolved.

Document the proposed release, the subrogation unit direction, and the communication to the insured in the claim file.

**18.E** Obtain the insured's account of how the loss occurred when a third party may be responsible.

The insured's initial account may identify responsible parties, witnesses, contracts, or circumstances that support recovery.

Document the insured's statement and identify any unresolved causation issues in the claim file.

**18.F** Identify all known parties with potential responsibility for the loss.

Recovery may depend on recognizing property owners, contractors, manufacturers, operators, or other involved parties.

Document each identified party, available contact information, and the basis for potential responsibility in the claim file.

**18.G** Secure available evidence before repairs, cleanup, disposal, or salvage activity alters the loss condition.

Physical evidence may be necessary to establish cause, defect, negligence, or the extent of damage.

Document the condition of the property, preservation instructions, and any evidence secured in the claim file.

## Chapter 20 — Appendix: Form Provision Cross-Reference

**20.A** Identify the controlling form provision before evaluating a reported property loss. Apply the provision language as issued for the risk.

Form wording controls the claim obligation and prevents unsupported handling assumptions. Do not substitute customary practice for applicable wording.

Document the controlling provision in the claim file. Record the basis for selecting that provision.

**20.B** Confirm that the reported event falls within the form’s covered cause of loss grant. Refer uncertain cause-of-loss issues for coverage review.

The coverage grant establishes the starting point for all subsequent analysis. A loss description alone does not establish coverage.

Document the reported cause of loss and the applicable grant. Preserve supporting statements, reports, and observations.

**20.C** Identify exclusions that may affect the reported damage. Evaluate each exclusion against the known facts.

Exclusions may limit or remove coverage otherwise suggested by the coverage grant. Early identification supports accurate investigation direction.

Document each potentially applicable exclusion and its factual basis. Record the disposition of each exclusion considered.

**20.D** Apply exceptions to exclusions only when the facts support their operation. Do not infer an exception from incomplete evidence.

An exception may restore coverage for damage otherwise excluded. The exception must be read with the exclusion it modifies.

Document the exclusion, the asserted exception, and the facts supporting the decision. Retain materials used to resolve the issue.

**20.E** Confirm the insured property described in the claim matches covered property under the applicable form. Refer ownership or property-description conflicts for review.

Covered property provisions define the property to which the form responds. Unverified property status can produce an incorrect payment decision.

Document the property description and the basis for covered-property status. Note any unresolved ownership or location issue.

**20.F** Separate covered property from property subject to a limitation or exclusion. Do not combine unlike property interests in the loss evaluation.

Property categories may carry different coverage treatment. Proper separation preserves the form’s intended application.

Document the property categories used in the evaluation. Explain any allocation of damage between categories.

**20.G** Verify the covered location before applying building or business personal property provisions. Refer location discrepancies for coverage review.

Location provisions may restrict coverage to described premises or other qualifying locations. Address verification before approving location-dependent expenses.

Document the reported loss location and the evidence supporting coverage there. Record any variance from the scheduled or described location.

**20.H** Evaluate property away from the described premises under the applicable off-premises provision. Confirm the property remained subject to the insured’s covered interest.

Off-premises treatment may differ from premises-based coverage. The facts must support both the property interest and the location condition.

Document the property’s location when damaged and its connection to the insured. Preserve records establishing custody, control, or ownership.

**20.I** Apply vacancy-related provisions when the loss facts indicate reduced occupancy or use. Refer disputed vacancy conditions for coverage review.

Vacancy language may change coverage treatment for specified loss circumstances. The operational status of the premises requires factual support.

Document observations, statements, and records relevant to occupancy status. Record the provision applied and the resulting handling decision.

**20.J** Evaluate protective safeguard provisions when a required safeguard may have been impaired. Determine whether the safeguard requirement applies to the reported loss.

Safeguard provisions may condition coverage on maintenance of specified protection. The connection between the safeguard condition and the loss must be developed.

Document the applicable safeguard condition and the evidence of its status. Record the investigation of impairment, if any.

**20.K** Identify any deductible provision applicable to the loss. Apply the deductible only after determining covered loss treatment.

Deductible application follows coverage analysis and valuation. Applying it prematurely can obscure disputed coverage issues.

Document the deductible provision selected and the basis for its application. Show the deductible treatment in the claim payment calculation.

**20.L** Apply special deductibles only when their stated triggering conditions are established. Do not use a special deductible based solely on a preliminary loss label.

Special deductibles may depend on the cause, location, or nature of damage. The claim facts must support the applicable trigger.

Document the triggering facts and the special deductible provision used. Retain supporting weather, incident, or inspection information.

**20.M** Confirm the valuation provision before estimating covered damage. Use the valuation method required for the property category involved.

Valuation provisions determine the measure of covered loss. A repair estimate does not alone establish the proper valuation basis.

Document the selected valuation provision and the property category. Retain estimates, invoices, appraisals, and valuation support.

**20.N** Evaluate replacement cost treatment only when the form conditions for that treatment are satisfied. Refer disputed completion or replacement issues for coverage review.

Replacement cost provisions may require facts beyond the existence of physical damage. The claim file must support the treatment selected.

Document the replacement cost analysis and the evidence supporting eligibility. Record any withheld amount and the reason for withholding it.

Ordinance or law coverage may address costs not included in ordinary repair scope. The enforcement requirement must be established in the file.

**20.R** Apply debris removal provisions to covered debris arising from covered direct physical loss. Separate debris work from ordinary cleanup or improvement work.

Debris removal coverage is tied to the underlying loss and covered property. Clear categorization avoids payment for unrelated site work.

Document the debris source, removal scope, and associated covered damage. Retain invoices and itemized vendor descriptions.

**20.S** Evaluate preservation of property expenses under the applicable provision. Confirm that the expense was incurred to protect covered property from further damage.

Preservation provisions support reasonable protective action after a covered event. They do not replace ordinary maintenance obligations.

Document the protective measure, timing, and reason it was taken. Retain invoices, photographs, and mitigation reports.

**20.T** Require reasonable mitigation of further damage after notice of a covered loss. Do not deny otherwise covered damage solely because mitigation remains under investigation.

The insured’s duties and the carrier’s coverage determination require separate analysis. Timely mitigation protects the property and supports accurate measurement of loss.

Document mitigation instructions, observed conditions, and responsive actions. Record any claimed increase in damage and its supporting facts.

**20.U** Evaluate fungus-related provisions when the reported damage includes fungal growth, wet rot, dry rot, or associated conditions. Distinguish direct damage from remediation activity.

Fungus-related provisions may contain specialized coverage treatment and limitations. The cause and extent of the condition must be developed before payment evaluation.

Document the reported condition, causation evidence, and remediation scope. Retain inspection findings and laboratory material when obtained.

**20.V** Apply water-related provisions according to the reported source and path of water. Do not classify water damage without investigating the source.

Water claims may involve distinct grants, exclusions, or limitations. Accurate source identification is necessary for correct form application.

Document the water source, route of entry, and affected property. Preserve photographs, plumbing findings, and witness statements.

**20.W** Evaluate seepage, leakage, overflow, and discharge provisions using the specific facts of the loss. Refer unresolved source issues for coverage review.

Similar damage patterns may arise from materially different causes. The form treatment depends on the established source and occurrence.

Document the event narrative and source investigation. Record the provision selected and the reasons for selection.

**20.X** Review flood-related language whenever surface water, overflow, runoff, or rising water is reported. Do not rely on a contractor’s terminology alone.

Flood-related coverage analysis requires the actual movement and origin of water. Labels used in estimates may not reflect form terminology.

Document weather information, site conditions, and water movement evidence. Identify the form language considered in the analysis.

**20.Y** Evaluate earth movement provisions when the loss includes settling, sinking, shifting, collapse, or ground disturbance. Develop facts addressing the initiating event.

Earth movement provisions can apply even when resulting damage appears to be structural or water-related. Cause analysis must precede coverage classification.

Document site observations, expert findings, and reported ground conditions. Record the basis for the earth movement determination.

**20.Z** Apply collapse provisions only after identifying the structural condition and cause of collapse. Do not treat all structural damage as collapse.

Collapse language may require a specific type of failure or cause. The file must distinguish deterioration, movement, and abrupt structural failure.

Document the observed structural condition and the causation evidence. Preserve engineering reports and inspection photographs.

**20.AA** Review the concealment, misrepresentation, or fraud provision when material claim information appears unreliable. Refer suspected intentional conduct for special investigation handling.

Material false information can affect the carrier’s obligations and claim resolution. A concern alone does not establish a provision violation.

Document the questioned information and the basis for concern. Preserve statements, records, and verification results.

**20.AB** Obtain sufficient information to evaluate the insured’s duties after loss. Apply the duties provision consistently with the facts developed.

Post-loss duties support investigation, valuation, and protection of recovery rights. Requests must relate to the loss and the applicable form requirement.

Document each requested item and the reason it is needed. Record receipt, follow-up, and any unresolved compliance issue.

**20.AC** Address notice obligations promptly after receiving a reported loss. Report each loss within 60 days after discovery when the applicable provision requires that timing.

Notice provisions allow timely investigation and preservation of evidence. The effect of delayed notice depends on the applicable form language and claim facts.

Document the reported discovery information and the notice received. Record any coverage review requested because of late notice.

**20.AD** Evaluate cooperation obligations when the insured’s actions limit claim investigation. Make reasonable efforts to obtain needed information before escalating the issue.

Cooperation requirements permit the carrier to investigate fairly and efficiently. The file should show the information sought and the insured’s response.

Document contact efforts, requested information, and responses received. Record any claimed prejudice arising from noncooperation.

**20.AE** Apply examination requirements when the form permits an examination under oath or similar inquiry. Coordinate the request through approved claim handling procedures.

An examination may be necessary when material facts cannot be resolved through ordinary investigation. Use the process only for a defined claim purpose.

Document the reason for the examination request and the information sought. Retain notices, attendance records, and resulting materials.

**20.AF** Request a signed proof of loss when required by the applicable form or needed to resolve material claim facts. Address the proof of loss period according to the controlling provision.

A proof of loss supports a formal statement of the amount and basis of claim. The request should identify the information necessary for evaluation.

Document the request, the form used, and the response received. Record any deficiency and the follow-up action taken.

**20.AG** Evaluate appraisal provisions when a covered amount dispute cannot be resolved through ordinary adjustment. Do not use appraisal to decide coverage issues.

Appraisal addresses valuation disputes within the scope allowed by the form. Coverage interpretation remains subject to separate review.

Document the disputed amount and efforts made to resolve it. Record the basis for invoking or declining appraisal.

**20.AH** Preserve subrogation rights when another party may be responsible for the loss. Do not authorize a release that impairs recovery without review.

Subrogation provisions protect the carrier’s ability to pursue responsible parties. Early preservation may be necessary before repairs or disposal remove evidence.

Document responsible-party information and preservation steps. Retain correspondence concerning releases, repairs, and recovery potential.

**20.AI** Evaluate transfer of rights provisions when the insured proposes an assignment or transfer related to the claim. Refer disputed assignment validity for coverage review.

Assignment issues may affect who may receive payment or direct claim activity. The form language and applicable facts must be considered together.

Document the asserted transfer and all supporting records. Record the review outcome before changing payment direction.

**20.AJ** Confirm mortgagee or loss payee status before issuing payment to an additional interest. Use the applicable form provision to determine payment treatment.

Additional interests may have rights that differ from the named insured’s rights. Payment handling must reflect the status shown by the claim file.

Document the additional interest, supporting endorsement information, and payment rationale. Retain lien, mortgage, or loss payee records.

**20.AK** Apply mortgageholder provisions separately from the insured’s claim obligations when the form requires separate treatment. Refer conflicts between insured and mortgageholder interests for review.

Mortgageholder language may preserve or modify rights following certain insured acts or omissions. The claim decision must identify the interest being evaluated.

Document communications with the mortgageholder and the applicable provision. Record the basis for any separate payment determination.

**20.AL** Evaluate other insurance provisions when another source may cover the same property damage. Obtain available information about the concurrent coverage.

Other insurance language may affect allocation and recovery handling. Do not assume another carrier’s position establishes this carrier’s obligation.

Document known other insurance and the information source. Record allocation analysis and recovery activity.

**20.AM** Apply loss payment provisions after coverage and valuation are established. Issue payment only to parties entitled under the applicable form.

Loss payment language governs the manner and direction of payment. Correct payment handling reduces duplicate payment and interest disputes.

Document the payee determination and payment basis. Retain payment records and supporting authority.

**20.AN** Evaluate pair, set, or part provisions when damaged property has reduced value beyond the item physically damaged. Do not assume full set replacement is required.

Pair and set treatment depends on the relationship between the damaged item and the remaining property. The valuation must be supported by the actual loss of value.

Document the affected property and the claimed loss of value. Retain valuation support for repair, replacement, or diminished value.

**20.AO** Apply recovered property provisions when property is found after payment or settlement activity. Coordinate recovery handling before disposing of the property.

Recovered property may affect the final amount owed or the carrier’s salvage interest. Prompt handling avoids duplicate recovery or uncompensated loss.

Document the recovery date, property condition, and disposition. Record any adjustment to the claim payment.

**20.AP** Evaluate salvage provisions when damaged property retains residual value. Preserve salvage opportunities without delaying necessary mitigation.

Salvage value may reduce the net covered loss or support recovery after payment. The carrier must maintain clear control over salvage disposition.

Document the salvage assessment and disposition authority. Retain bids, receipts, and transfer records.

**20.AQ** Review business income provisions when direct physical loss affects operations. Confirm the claimed interruption arises from covered property damage.

Business income coverage requires both an operational impact and the form’s required loss connection. Revenue reduction alone does not establish covered loss.

Document the interruption cause, affected operations, and supporting financial records. Record the provision used in the evaluation.

**20.AR** Evaluate extra expense provisions separately from business income loss. Confirm the expense was incurred to avoid or minimize an otherwise covered operational interruption.

Extra expense may be covered even when it does not appear in ordinary repair scope. The expense purpose and reasonableness must be established.

Document the extra expense, operational purpose, and supporting invoices. Record the analysis of avoided or reduced interruption.

**20.AS** Apply civil authority provisions only when the claimed interruption results from an applicable authority action. Obtain the order or equivalent evidence before approving the claim.

Civil authority treatment depends on the reason for the authority action and its relationship to covered damage. General access difficulty does not alone establish coverage.

Document the authority action and the affected operations. Retain the order, public notice, or other supporting record.

**20.AT** Evaluate ingress or egress provisions when access to the insured location is impaired. Distinguish access impairment from ordinary customer decline.

Ingress or egress coverage may require a qualifying condition outside the insured premises. The file must establish the reason access was prevented or limited.

Document the access condition and its operational effect. Preserve photographs, statements, and public authority information.

**20.AU** Apply utility services provisions when interruption of a utility may have caused loss or damage. Identify the affected utility and the point of interruption.

Utility service provisions may vary according to the service type and damaged property. The interruption source is necessary to evaluate coverage.

Document the utility information, interruption cause, and resulting damage. Retain utility records and repair findings.

**20.AV** Review electronic data provisions when the claim includes data loss, restoration, or software-related expense. Separate physical damage from data-related cost.

Electronic data coverage may have specialized treatment not applicable to tangible property loss. The claim file must identify the type of loss being claimed.

Document the data condition, restoration work, and claimed expense. Retain vendor records and technical findings.

**20.AW** Evaluate valuable papers and records provisions when the claim includes reconstruction or reproduction costs. Confirm the claimed work relates to covered records.

Records-related provisions may address restoration costs differently from the value of the information itself. Proper classification supports accurate payment handling.

Document the records affected and the reconstruction scope. Retain invoices, descriptions, and restoration support.

**20.AX** Apply accounts receivable provisions when the loss affects records needed to collect receivables. Verify that the claimed inability to collect arises from the covered event.

Accounts receivable losses require support for both the damaged records and the resulting collection impact. Claimed balances must be reconciled to available business records.

Document the damaged records, receivable information, and collection efforts. Retain accounting records and customer correspondence.

**20.AY** Evaluate property of others provisions when the insured reports damage to property owned by another party. Confirm custody, control, and the insured’s responsibility for the property.

Property of others coverage depends on the form language and the insured’s relationship to the damaged property. Ownership alone does not determine handling.

Document the owner, property description, and basis for the insured’s interest. Retain agreements, receipts, and owner statements.

**20.AZ** Apply personal property of officers, partners, or employees provisions only when the form extends coverage to that property. Confirm the property’s business connection.

Personal property associated with individuals may not receive the same treatment as insured business property. The claimed use and ownership require verification.

Document the owner, business use, and property description. Record the provision analysis and payment decision.

**20.BA** Evaluate newly acquired property provisions when the loss involves property not identified in the current risk description. Confirm the acquisition and intended use.

Newly acquired property treatment depends on the form language and facts surrounding the acquisition. Do not assume coverage from a reported purchase alone.

Document acquisition records, property use, and loss location. Record the basis for applying or declining the provision.

**20.BB** Apply newly constructed property provisions only after confirming the construction status and covered interest. Refer incomplete ownership or occupancy facts for review.

Construction-related property may present distinct valuation and coverage issues. The property status at the time of loss must be established.

Document construction status, ownership evidence, and damage findings. Retain permits, contracts, and inspection material when available.

**20.BC** Evaluate property in transit provisions when damage occurred during movement or delivery. Identify the party responsible for transportation and custody.

Transit treatment may depend on where the property was located and who controlled it. Delivery records often provide material claim evidence.

Document shipment details, custody information, and damage observations. Retain bills of lading, delivery records, and carrier correspondence.

**20.BD** Review spoilage provisions when temperature change, power interruption, or equipment failure is reported. Determine the immediate cause of the spoiled property.

Spoilage losses may involve distinct causes and specialized form conditions. Product condition and timing must be supported by reliable evidence.

Document the affected goods, temperature information, and cause findings. Retain inventory, disposal, and vendor records.

**20.BE** Apply pollutant-related provisions when cleanup, discharge, release, or contamination is alleged. Refer environmental uncertainty for specialized coverage review.

Pollution-related language may restrict coverage or impose conditions on payment. The source, substance, and response activity require careful documentation.

Document the alleged substance, release information, and remediation scope. Preserve reports, notices, and environmental invoices.

**20.BF** Identify the legal action deadline when evaluating correspondence that threatens or initiates litigation. Refer any waiver, extension, or deadline dispute for coverage review.

Legal action provisions affect the carrier’s response to suit-related claim activity. Claim handling actions must not create an unsupported representation concerning legal rights.

Document litigation-related communications and the applicable provision review. Preserve notices, service materials, and referral records.

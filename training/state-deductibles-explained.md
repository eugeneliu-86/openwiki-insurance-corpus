# State Wind and Hail Deductibles Explained







## L.1 — Learning Objectives

**L.1.1** You will learn to identify when a wind or hail deductible may apply to a covered loss. You will also learn why the applicable deductible can depend on the policy language and the location of the insured property.

**L.1.2** You will learn to distinguish a wind loss from a hail loss when reviewing a claim. This helps you match the reported damage to the deductible terms that govern the loss.

**L.1.3** You will learn to read deductible wording closely rather than relying on the name of the deductible alone. Similar labels can operate differently under different policy forms.

**L.1.4** You will learn to recognize that a policy may contain a separate deductible for wind, hail, or both perils. You should confirm whether the separate deductible replaces the standard deductible or applies alongside it.

**L.1.5** You will learn to identify the event or conditions that trigger a special wind or hail deductible. The trigger may depend on the cause of loss, an official weather event, or other policy-defined circumstances.

**L.1.6** You will learn to compare the declarations with the policy provisions and endorsements. The declarations may show the selected deductible, while the policy language explains how it applies.

**L.1.7** You will learn to recognize the importance of endorsements when handling wind and hail claims. An endorsement can change the deductible that would otherwise apply under the base policy.

**L.1.8** You will learn to determine whether the insured location is subject to state-specific wind or hail deductible rules. Geographic location can affect the deductible options available and the wording used in the policy.

**L.1.9** You will learn to identify percentage-based deductibles and understand that they are calculated from a policy value identified by the contract. You should verify the relevant policy value before calculating the insured’s responsibility.

**L.1.10** You will learn to distinguish deductible calculation from claim valuation. The deductible affects the amount payable after covered damage has been evaluated under the policy.

**L.1.11** You will learn to review whether multiple damaged items are subject to a single deductible or separate deductibles. The answer depends on the policy’s treatment of the occurrence, property, and covered loss.

**L.1.12** You will learn to assess whether wind and hail damage resulted from the same weather event. This analysis can matter when deciding how the deductible applies to the reported damage.

**L.1.13** You will learn to separate covered wind or hail damage from damage caused by other conditions. Accurate cause-of-loss analysis supports correct deductible handling and clear claim documentation.

**L.1.14** You will learn to recognize situations where a loss involves more than one cause. In those situations, you should follow the policy language carefully and document the basis for the deductible decision.

**L.1.15** You will learn to explain deductible application in plain language to an insured. A clear explanation should describe what deductible applies and how it affects the payment, without promising coverage before the review is complete.

**L.1.16** You will learn to document the facts supporting your deductible determination. Useful documentation includes the reported weather conditions, damage observations, policy wording, and relevant claim records.

**L.1.17** You will learn to identify questions that require escalation. Ask for guidance when the declarations, endorsements, policy terms, or loss facts do not clearly support a deductible conclusion.

**L.1.18** You will learn to avoid treating all storm damage claims the same way. Each claim requires a review of the applicable policy, the property location, the cause of loss, and the facts of the event.

**L.1.19** You will learn that deductible analysis is part of the broader coverage review. A deductible decision should be consistent with the policy terms and the supported cause of loss.

**L.1.20** You will learn to communicate your reasoning clearly for claim file review. Clear reasoning helps the insured, the claim team, and the insurer understand how the deductible decision was reached.

**L.1.21** By the end of this material, you should be able to approach wind and hail deductible questions with confidence and care. Your goal is to apply the policy language accurately while explaining the result in a way that is easy to follow.

## L.2 — Key Concepts

**L.2.1 Q:** What is a wind and hail deductible? Use the term as the portion of a covered wind or hail loss that remains with the insured.

**L.2.2 A:** It is a loss-sharing feature that can apply when wind or hail causes covered damage. You should identify it before estimating what may be payable.

**L.2.3 Q:** Does a wind and hail deductible always match the deductible for other causes of loss? Do not assume that it does.

**L.2.4 A:** Wind and hail may have their own deductible treatment. The applicable wording controls which deductible applies.

**L.2.5 Q:** Why does the cause of loss matter? The deductible analysis starts with the cause of damage.

**L.2.6 A:** You need to decide whether wind, hail, or another cause produced the damage. That decision affects the deductible that may apply.

**L.2.7 Q:** Can wind and hail occur in the same event? They often appear together in a storm loss.

**L.2.8 A:** When both are present, focus on the applicable deductible language rather than treating each condition separately by default. The loss facts still need to support the cause-of-loss decision.

**L.2.9 Q:** Is a wind and hail deductible simply a charge added after a loss? It is not a separate fee.

**L.2.10 A:** It is the insured’s retained part of a covered loss. You apply it as part of the claim calculation.

**L.2.11 Q:** What should you review before discussing the deductible with an insured? Start with the facts of the reported damage.

**L.2.12 A:** Confirm what happened, what property was damaged, and what caused the damage. Then match those facts to the applicable deductible treatment.

**L.2.13 Q:** Does the deductible determine whether damage is covered? Keep coverage and deductible analysis distinct.

**L.2.14 A:** Coverage determines whether the loss qualifies for payment. The deductible determines how much of an otherwise covered loss remains with the insured.

**L.2.15 Q:** What if the reported damage has more than one possible cause? Avoid choosing a deductible too quickly.

**L.2.16 A:** Gather facts that help separate wind or hail damage from wear, maintenance issues, and other causes. Your deductible conclusion should follow the supported cause-of-loss finding.

**L.2.17 Q:** Can roof damage create a wind and hail deductible issue? It can.

**L.2.18 A:** Roof claims often require careful review because the reported condition may have several possible explanations. Describe the observed damage clearly before applying deductible treatment.

**L.2.19 Q:** Can exterior damage create the same issue? Yes, it can.

**L.2.20 A:** Siding, windows, fencing, and other exterior property may show storm-related damage. The relevant question is whether the reported damage resulted from a covered wind or hail event.

**L.2.21 Q:** Should you rely only on an insured’s description of the storm? Listen carefully, but investigate the facts.

**L.2.22 A:** The insured’s report is important evidence. It should be considered with observations, available weather information, and other claim evidence.

**L.2.23 Q:** What is the underwriter’s role in deductible review? You help make sure the deductible is set and communicated appropriately.

**L.2.24 A:** You should recognize when wind and hail deductible treatment may differ from other loss treatment. Clear communication reduces surprises when a claim occurs.

**L.2.25 Q:** What is the adjuster’s role in deductible review? You apply the deductible after evaluating the reported loss.

**L.2.26 A:** Your file should show why the wind and hail deductible applies. Explain the reasoning in plain language that connects the loss facts to the result.

**L.2.27 Q:** Why is clear communication important? Deductibles can be confusing during a stressful claim.

**L.2.28 A:** Tell the insured what deductible applies and why it applies. Avoid technical language when a direct explanation will do.

**L.2.29 Q:** Should you describe a wind and hail deductible as a penalty? No.

**L.2.30 A:** A deductible is a feature of the insurance arrangement, not a punishment for reporting a loss. Use neutral language when explaining it.

**L.2.31 Q:** Can a deductible apply when damage is minor? It may, if the loss is otherwise covered.

**L.2.32 A:** The deductible analysis does not depend on whether the damage appears large or small. The payable result depends on the covered loss and the applicable deductible.

**L.2.33 Q:** What happens when the covered damage does not exceed the deductible? Explain the result plainly.

**L.2.34 A:** There may be no payment for that loss. The claim handling should still document the damage, cause of loss, and deductible reasoning.

**L.2.35 Q:** Should you promise payment before applying the deductible? Do not make that promise.

**L.2.36 A:** First determine the covered amount and the deductible treatment. Then communicate the claim result accurately.

**L.2.37 Q:** Can a storm event involve damage that is not covered? Yes, that is possible.

**L.2.38 A:** A storm report alone does not establish coverage for every observed condition. Evaluate each claimed item under the applicable coverage and cause-of-loss analysis.

**L.2.39 Q:** Does every damaged item receive separate deductible treatment? Do not assume that it does.

**L.2.40 A:** Consider the reported loss as a whole under the applicable wording. Separate treatment may require support from the facts and the coverage terms.

**L.2.41 Q:** What should you do when the loss facts are incomplete? Identify what is missing.

**L.2.42 A:** Ask focused questions about the event, the timing of the discovery, and the location of the damage. Good fact gathering supports a sound deductible decision.

**L.2.43 Q:** Why does timing matter in a wind or hail claim? It can help connect damage to an event.

**L.2.44 A:** Timing may also help identify whether a condition existed before the reported storm. Record the information without guessing.

**L.2.45 Q:** Should you use weather conditions as the only evidence of damage? No.

**L.2.46 A:** Weather information can support the investigation, but it does not replace inspection findings. Match the reported damage to the available evidence.

**L.2.47 Q:** What if a contractor says damage is storm-related? Treat that statement as information to evaluate.

**L.2.48 A:** Consider the contractor’s observations along with the rest of the claim evidence. Do not let a conclusion replace your own documented analysis.

**L.2.49 Q:** How should you explain a disputed deductible? Stay calm and specific.

**L.2.50 A:** Describe the reported cause of loss, the applicable deductible treatment, and the resulting impact on payment. Invite questions about the explanation.

**L.2.51 Q:** What does a deductible ceiling mean? It limits how high the applicable deductible may be.

**L.2.52 A:** A windstorm and hail deductible may not exceed ten (10) percent when the applicable state rule sets that ceiling. You should recognize that a stated deductible can be subject to that limit.

**L.2.53 Q:** Does a deductible ceiling decide whether a loss is covered? No.

**L.2.54 A:** A ceiling addresses the permissible deductible level. Coverage still depends on the facts and the applicable coverage terms.

**L.2.55 Q:** Why should an underwriter understand deductible limits? Deductible selection can affect claim expectations.

**L.2.56 A:** You should avoid presenting a deductible as available if it exceeds an applicable limit. Clear setup at issuance supports clearer claim handling later.

**L.2.57 Q:** Why should an adjuster understand deductible limits? The applied deductible must be appropriate.

**L.2.58 A:** If the stated deductible raises a concern, pause and seek appropriate guidance before finalizing the calculation. Do not correct the issue through assumption.

**L.2.59 Q:** Can different property types affect deductible treatment? They can.

**L.2.60 A:** Consider the property involved and the applicable coverage arrangement. Do not transfer a deductible conclusion from one risk to another without support.

**L.2.61 Q:** Can location affect wind and hail deductible treatment? It may.

**L.2.62 A:** State requirements and the applicable coverage wording can affect the analysis. Confirm the relevant location before reaching a conclusion.

**L.2.63 Q:** Should you treat every storm report the same way? No.

**L.2.64 A:** Similar weather reports can produce different damage facts. Evaluate the actual reported loss rather than relying on a label such as storm claim.

**L.2.65 Q:** What is a good claim note about a deductible? It is clear, factual, and easy to follow.

**L.2.66 A:** State the reported cause of loss, the deductible selected, and the reason it applies. Avoid vague phrases that do not explain the decision.

**L.2.67 Q:** What is a good underwriting note about a deductible? It records the basis for the deductible treatment.

**L.2.68 A:** Note the information that supports the selected approach and any communication provided to the insured. Clear notes help others understand the file.

**L.2.69 Q:** Should you use the word storm as if it always means wind or hail? Be careful.

**L.2.70 A:** Storm can describe many weather conditions. Identify the specific cause that produced the claimed damage.

**L.2.71 Q:** What if wind damage and water damage are both reported? Analyze each cause carefully.

**L.2.72 A:** Do not assume that all damage from the same event receives the same treatment. The applicable cause-of-loss analysis may differ by damage type.

**L.2.73 Q:** Can hail damage be visible without wind damage? Yes, it can.

**L.2.74 A:** Hail may leave distinct signs on exposed surfaces. Your conclusion should rest on the evidence observed during the investigation.

**L.2.75 Q:** Can wind damage exist without obvious hail damage? Yes, it can.

**L.2.76 A:** Wind may affect materials, openings, or exterior features without hail being involved. Apply the deductible based on the supported cause of loss.

**L.2.77 Q:** What if the insured asks why the deductible is different from another claim? Explain that deductible treatment can depend on the loss facts.

**L.2.78 A:** Focus on the applicable deductible and the reported cause of damage for the current claim. Do not speculate about another claim’s facts.

**L.2.79 Q:** Should you use absolute statements when the facts are still developing? Avoid them.

**L.2.80 A:** Explain what is known, what is being reviewed, and what remains to be determined. This keeps communication accurate and fair.

**L.2.81 Q:** What is the main training habit to remember? Separate the cause-of-loss decision from the payment calculation.

**L.2.82 A:** Determine whether wind or hail caused covered damage before applying the deductible. Then explain the result in language the insured can understand.

**L.2.83 Q:** What is the practical goal of careful deductible handling? Reach a supported and clearly explained outcome.

**L.2.84 A:** Good work combines accurate fact gathering, careful application of the deductible, and respectful communication. That approach helps both the insured and the claim file.

## L.3 — Worked Examples

**L.3.1** **Q:** What is the starting point for a wind or hail deductible review? **A:** Start by identifying the cause of loss and the deductible that applies to that cause.

**L.3.2** **Q:** Why does the cause of loss matter? **A:** Wind, hail, hurricane, and other causes can trigger different deductible treatment.

**L.3.3** **Q:** What should you confirm before discussing the deductible? **A:** Confirm that the reported damage is connected to the weather event being reviewed.

**L.3.4** **Q:** Does every weather claim use the same deductible? **A:** No. The applicable deductible depends on the coverage language and the reported facts.

**L.3.5** **Q:** What if wind and rain both damage the property? **A:** Separate the damage by cause when the facts and coverage language support doing so.

**L.3.6** **Q:** What if hail damages the roof and wind damages exterior siding? **A:** Review whether the same windstorm and hail deductible applies to both causes.

**L.3.7** **Q:** What should you do when the insured calls all storm damage “wind damage”? **A:** Ask follow-up questions instead of relying only on the insured’s description.

**L.3.8** **Q:** What facts can help identify hail damage? **A:** Look for impact marks, damaged roofing materials, and reports of hail in the area.

**L.3.9** **Q:** What facts can help identify wind damage? **A:** Look for displaced materials, fallen objects, and damage patterns consistent with wind force.

**L.3.10** **Q:** Can a deductible apply even when the damage seems minor? **A:** Yes. The deductible applies before payment when covered damage exceeds it.

**L.3.11** **Q:** What should you explain when covered damage does not exceed the deductible? **A:** Explain that there may be covered damage, but no payment is due after applying the deductible.

**L.3.12** **Q:** Should you describe the deductible as a coverage exclusion? **A:** No. A deductible is generally the amount applied before a covered loss is paid.

**L.3.13** **Q:** What if the insured asks why a wind deductible applies instead of the all-other-perils deductible? **A:** Explain that weather-related causes can have their own deductible treatment.

**L.3.14** **Q:** What should you avoid when explaining deductible selection? **A:** Avoid guessing which deductible applies before confirming the cause of loss.

**L.3.15** **Q:** How should you handle a claim involving wind-driven rain? **A:** Determine whether the wind created an opening or otherwise caused the water entry.

**L.3.16** **Q:** What if rain enters through an existing opening? **A:** Review the cause of the opening and the applicable coverage limitations.

**L.3.17** **Q:** Can the deductible decision depend on timing? **A:** Yes. Timing can matter when a named storm designation affects deductible treatment.

**L.3.18** **Q:** When does the named storm period end after the designation ends? **A:** A named storm period continues after the designation ends for seventy-two (72) hours.

**L.3.19** **Q:** Should you assume that every storm with a name triggers the named storm deductible? **A:** No. Confirm the event status and the applicable coverage language.

**L.3.20** **Q:** What if the loss occurs near the end of a named storm event? **A:** Document the reported timing carefully and apply the named storm provisions as written.

**L.3.21** **Q:** What records can help establish when damage occurred? **A:** Weather reports, photographs, repair invoices, and witness statements can help.

**L.3.22** **Q:** What if the insured cannot identify the exact time of damage? **A:** Use available evidence to determine the most reasonable timing of the loss.

**L.3.23** **Q:** Should you rely only on a weather report to determine causation? **A:** No. Weather information supports the analysis, but property evidence also matters.

**L.3.24** **Q:** What if more than one storm affected the property? **A:** Determine whether the damage is attributable to a particular event or to separate events.

**L.3.25** **Q:** Why does event identification matter? **A:** It helps you decide whether the damage should be treated as a single loss or separate losses.

**L.3.26** **Q:** What if repairs were made before the inspection? **A:** Request photographs, invoices, damaged materials, and a clear description of the repairs.

**L.3.27** **Q:** Can temporary repairs affect the deductible? **A:** Temporary repairs do not change the deductible, but they can help show the nature of the damage.

**L.3.28** **Q:** What should you do if the insured reports wind damage and interior water damage? **A:** Review the exterior damage, the water path, and the sequence of events.

**L.3.29** **Q:** What if a fallen tree damages the roof during a wind event? **A:** Determine whether wind caused the tree to fall and which deductible applies to the resulting loss.

**L.3.30** **Q:** Does a tree claim always use the wind deductible when wind is present? **A:** No. The direct cause of damage and the applicable coverage wording control.

**L.3.31** **Q:** What if wind damages a fence but not the dwelling? **A:** Review the damaged property category and the deductible that applies to the covered loss.

**L.3.32** **Q:** Can outbuildings be subject to the same wind deductible as the dwelling? **A:** They can be, but you should confirm the applicable coverage terms.

**L.3.33** **Q:** What if only personal property is damaged by wind? **A:** Identify the damaged property and apply the deductible to the covered loss.

**L.3.34** **Q:** How should you handle damaged contents that were moved after the storm? **A:** Ask where the items were located at the time of loss and document their condition.

**L.3.35** **Q:** What if an insured reports damaged jewelry after a wind loss? **A:** Review the jewelry and watches limit separately from the deductible analysis.

**L.3.36** **Q:** Does a special property limit eliminate the deductible? **A:** No. A property limit and a deductible address different parts of the claim.

**L.3.37** **Q:** What if wind causes a power outage and food spoils? **A:** Identify the direct cause of the spoilage and review the relevant coverage provisions.

**L.3.38** **Q:** What if hail damages solar equipment? **A:** Confirm whether the equipment is covered property and apply the appropriate deductible.

**L.3.39** **Q:** What if hail damage is visible but the insured has not repaired it? **A:** Inspect the condition, document the damage, and estimate covered repairs.

**L.3.40** **Q:** Can prior unrepaired damage affect the claim review? **A:** Yes. Separate prior damage from damage caused by the reported event.

**L.3.41** **Q:** What if the roof already had wear before the storm? **A:** Distinguish wear from sudden storm-caused damage before applying the deductible.

**L.3.42** **Q:** Should you apply a wind deductible to ordinary deterioration? **A:** No. A deductible applies only after you identify covered loss.

**L.3.43** **Q:** What if wind exposes a preexisting construction defect? **A:** Separate the storm damage from damage caused by the defect.

**L.3.44** **Q:** What if the insured says a contractor found hail damage? **A:** Review the evidence independently and document the observed damage.

**L.3.45** **Q:** Why should you avoid adopting a contractor’s conclusion without review? **A:** The claim decision must be based on the available facts and coverage language.

**L.3.46** **Q:** What if a contractor estimates repairs above the deductible? **A:** Confirm that the estimate addresses covered damage before calculating payment.

**L.3.47** **Q:** Can repair costs include improvements unrelated to storm damage? **A:** They can, so separate storm repairs from elective upgrades.

**L.3.48** **Q:** What if matching materials are unavailable? **A:** Evaluate the covered repair needs and apply the deductible after determining the covered amount.

**L.3.49** **Q:** Does the deductible apply before or after the covered repair amount is determined? **A:** Determine the covered amount, then apply the deductible.

**L.3.50** **Q:** What if the loss includes emergency mitigation work? **A:** Review whether the work was reasonable and related to preventing further covered damage.

**L.3.51** **Q:** Can mitigation expenses be considered with the storm damage? **A:** They may be considered when they are covered and properly documented.

**L.3.52** **Q:** What if water enters through damage caused by hail? **A:** Document the hail damage, the opening, and the resulting water path.

**L.3.53** **Q:** What if water backs up during a storm? **A:** Review the cause of the backup and the drain backup sublimit.

**L.3.54** **Q:** Does a drain backup sublimit determine the wind deductible? **A:** No. It is a separate coverage consideration that may affect the payment calculation.

**L.3.55** **Q:** What if a storm causes both roof damage and sewer backup? **A:** Analyze each cause of damage before combining any covered amounts.

**L.3.56** **Q:** Why is it important to separate causes of loss? **A:** Different causes can have different deductibles, limits, or coverage requirements.

**L.3.57** **Q:** What if the insured reports damage from wind and flood? **A:** Separate the wind-caused damage from the flood-caused damage before evaluating payment.

**L.3.58** **Q:** Can flood damage be included under a wind and hail deductible? **A:** Do not assume so. Identify the cause of each damaged item or building component.

**L.3.59** **Q:** What if a storm surge damages the property? **A:** Document the water source and distinguish surge damage from wind damage.

**L.3.60** **Q:** What if wind damages a window and later rain damages interior finishes? **A:** Evaluate the wind damage and the resulting water damage as part of the same reported event.

**L.3.61** **Q:** What if the window was already cracked before the storm? **A:** Determine whether the storm caused additional damage or merely exposed prior damage.

**L.3.62** **Q:** How should you explain a deductible to an insured? **A:** Use plain language and explain that it is applied to the covered loss amount.

**L.3.63** **Q:** What should you avoid saying to an insured about deductible recovery? **A:** Do not promise payment of the deductible unless the facts and coverage support it.

**L.3.64** **Q:** What if a mortgagee asks about claim payment? **A:** Address the claim payment process while keeping the deductible calculation clear.

**L.3.65** **Q:** Can a deductible be waived because the insured is upset? **A:** No. Apply the deductible consistently under the applicable coverage terms.

**L.3.66** **Q:** What if the insured cannot afford repairs after the deductible is applied? **A:** Acknowledge the concern, but do not alter the deductible based on financial hardship.

**L.3.67** **Q:** What if a claim involves a replacement cost calculation? **A:** Complete the covered loss calculation before applying the deductible.

**L.3.68** **Q:** What if depreciation affects the payment calculation? **A:** Address depreciation under the applicable loss settlement terms, then apply the deductible correctly.

**L.3.69** **Q:** Can recoverable depreciation change the deductible? **A:** No. The deductible remains part of the claim calculation.

**L.3.70** **Q:** What if repairs cost less than the deductible but replacement cost is higher? **A:** Base the claim decision on the covered loss calculation and applicable settlement terms.

**L.3.71** **Q:** What if the property is insured below its replacement value? **A:** Review the eighty percent condition as part of the loss settlement analysis.

**L.3.72** **Q:** Does the eighty percent condition replace the deductible? **A:** No. It is separate from the deductible and may affect the covered payment calculation.

**L.3.73** **Q:** What if the insured selected a deductible that does not meet underwriting requirements? **A:** Review the minimum Section I deductible before completing the analysis.

**L.3.74** **Q:** What if the selected wind deductible appears unusually low? **A:** Confirm that it meets the minimum windstorm and hail deductible percentage.

**L.3.75** **Q:** Should an adjuster change a selected deductible during claim handling? **A:** No. Apply the deductible that governs the reported loss.

**L.3.76** **Q:** What if the insured disputes the deductible shown in the claim file? **A:** Explain the basis for the applied deductible and document the discussion.

**L.3.77** **Q:** What if the reported loss date differs from the date damage was discovered? **A:** Investigate when the damage most likely occurred and document the supporting evidence.

**L.3.78** **Q:** Can delayed reporting prevent you from applying the deductible? **A:** No. The deductible analysis still begins with the cause and timing of loss.

**L.3.79** **Q:** What if multiple areas of the property have different damage patterns? **A:** Consider whether the patterns support a common weather event or separate causes.

**L.3.80** **Q:** What if wind damages the roof and hail damages an exterior fixture? **A:** Review whether both damages fall within the same deductible treatment.

**L.3.81** **Q:** What if the insured claims a storm caused cracks in masonry? **A:** Inspect the cracks and consider whether the damage pattern supports storm causation.

**L.3.82** **Q:** What if wind loosens roofing materials but no water enters? **A:** Evaluate the direct physical damage and apply the deductible to covered repairs.

**L.3.83** **Q:** What if a tarp prevents later rain damage? **A:** Document the mitigation efforts and separate avoided damage from actual damage.

**L.3.84** **Q:** What if the insured makes permanent repairs before inspection? **A:** Obtain documentation that shows the condition before and during repair.

**L.3.85** **Q:** Why are photographs important in wind and hail claims? **A:** They can support causation, scope, timing, and the deductible decision.

**L.3.86** **Q:** What if photographs show damage but not the cause? **A:** Combine the photographs with inspection findings and weather information.

**L.3.87** **Q:** What if neighbors report similar hail damage? **A:** Treat that information as supporting context rather than proof of this property’s damage.

**L.3.88** **Q:** What if a public adjuster presents a large estimate? **A:** Review the estimate carefully and identify the items supported by covered damage.

**L.3.89** **Q:** Should you apply the deductible to unsupported estimate items? **A:** No. Apply the deductible after determining the covered loss amount.

**L.3.90** **Q:** What if the estimate includes code-related work? **A:** Review whether the code-related work is covered before including it in the loss calculation.

**L.3.91** **Q:** What if debris removal is needed after wind damage? **A:** Determine whether the debris removal is related to covered property damage.

**L.3.92** **Q:** What if a detached structure is damaged by hail? **A:** Identify the structure, confirm coverage, and apply the applicable deductible.

**L.3.93** **Q:** What if tenant property is damaged in a rented residence? **A:** Identify who owns the damaged property and review the applicable coverage.

**L.3.94** **Q:** What if the insured says the storm damaged landscaping? **A:** Confirm the cause of damage and review the applicable property coverage.

**L.3.95** **Q:** What if a weather event damages both covered and excluded property? **A:** Separate the covered damage from damage that is not covered.

**L.3.96** **Q:** What is the clearest way to document the deductible decision? **A:** State the cause of loss, the applicable deductible, and the covered loss calculation.

**L.3.97** **Q:** What should your claim notes show when more than one deductible is considered? **A:** Show why each deductible was considered and why the selected deductible applies.

**L.3.98** **Q:** What if the deductible question remains unclear after the inspection? **A:** Gather additional facts rather than making assumptions.

**L.3.99** **Q:** What is the main habit to build when handling wind and hail deductibles? **A:** Identify the cause, document the timing, calculate covered damage, and then apply the correct deductible.

## L.4 — Common Mistakes

**L.4.1** **Q:** Is wind damage always subject to the same deductible as other property damage? **A:** No. You should identify the cause of loss and the policy’s applicable deductible wording before selecting a deductible.

**L.4.2** **Q:** Can you apply a wind deductible just because strong weather was reported nearby? **A:** No. You need facts showing that wind caused the claimed damage. Weather reports can support your analysis, but they do not replace an inspection.

**L.4.3** **Q:** Is hail damage automatically handled under a wind deductible? **A:** Not always. Read the deductible provision carefully because the policy may address wind and hail together or differently.

**L.4.4** **Q:** Can you use the deductible shown on an estimate without checking the policy? **A:** No. An estimate may repeat information provided by the insured or contractor. Confirm the applicable deductible from the policy information available to you.

**L.4.5** **Q:** Does the largest deductible on the declarations always control? **A:** No. The deductible that applies depends on the loss and the policy wording. Do not assume that a prominently displayed deductible applies to every claim.

**L.4.6** **Q:** Can a contractor decide which deductible applies? **A:** No. A contractor may describe damage, but the insurer determines coverage and deductible application. Keep the coverage decision separate from repair pricing.

**L.4.7** **Q:** Is a named storm enough to trigger a special deductible? **A:** Not by itself. You must confirm that the policy language connects the event to the special deductible. Match the facts of loss to the wording rather than the storm’s public name.

**L.4.8** **Q:** Can you assume a wind deductible applies everywhere in the state? **A:** No. Territory matters when the policy uses location-based deductible provisions. Verify the insured location rather than relying on the mailing address.

**L.4.9** **Q:** Does a coastal address automatically mean the highest wind deductible applies? **A:** No. Coastal descriptions can be informal and imprecise. Use the territory information recognized by the policy.

**L.4.10** **Q:** What is a common mistake when reviewing a county-based wind cap? **A:** A common error is treating the cap as though it applies to every location. For a tier one county, the wind cap is five (5) percent.

**L.4.11** **Q:** Can you select a deductible before confirming the damaged building location? **A:** No. A policy can insure property at more than one location with different terms. Tie the claimed damage to the correct insured premises.

**L.4.12** **Q:** Does a policy mailing address prove where the damage occurred? **A:** No. The mailing address may differ from the insured property address. Confirm the location through the policy and claim facts.

**L.4.13** **Q:** Can you apply a percentage deductible to the repair estimate? **A:** Usually, you should not start with the estimate. Review the policy language that identifies the value used to calculate the deductible.

**L.4.14** **Q:** Is the deductible based on what the insured paid for the property? **A:** Not necessarily. Purchase price and policy values can serve different purposes. Follow the deductible provision instead of using a value from outside the policy.

**L.4.15** **Q:** Can you calculate the deductible from the dwelling limit without checking endorsements? **A:** No. Endorsements can affect how a deductible applies. Review all applicable policy changes before completing your calculation.

**L.4.16** **Q:** Does an increased replacement cost payment change the deductible? **A:** Not automatically. The deductible is determined under its own policy wording. Do not revise it merely because the settlement amount changes.

**L.4.17** **Q:** Can recoverable depreciation be used to reduce the deductible? **A:** No. Depreciation and deductibles are separate parts of the claim calculation. Explain each adjustment clearly so the insured can follow the settlement.

**L.4.18** **Q:** Is a deductible waived when damage appears severe? **A:** Not simply because the damage is extensive. Apply any waiver only when the policy specifically provides it. Severity alone does not create a waiver.

**L.4.19** **Q:** Can you waive a deductible because the insured has been a customer for a long time? **A:** No. Customer history does not change the policy terms. Apply the deductible consistently and document the basis for your decision.

**L.4.20** **Q:** Does emergency mitigation eliminate the deductible? **A:** No. Necessary mitigation may be covered, but it does not usually remove the deductible. Handle mitigation charges under the policy while preserving the deductible analysis.

**L.4.21** **Q:** Can you apply the deductible separately to every repair invoice? **A:** Usually, no. Consider whether the invoices relate to the same occurrence and covered loss. Splitting a single loss can create an incorrect deductible result.

**L.4.22** **Q:** Does each damaged building receive a separate deductible? **A:** Do not assume so. Review the policy language and the facts involving the insured locations. The answer depends on how the policy addresses buildings and occurrences.

**L.4.23** **Q:** Can separate storms be treated as one loss because the damage looks similar? **A:** No. Determine whether the damage arose from the same event or separate events. Your file should show the facts supporting that conclusion.

**L.4.24** **Q:** Can later-discovered damage be assigned to an earlier storm without investigation? **A:** No. Later discovery does not establish the date or cause of damage. Compare inspection findings, weather information, and prior condition evidence.

**L.4.25** **Q:** Is missing documentation a reason to guess which deductible applies? **A:** No. Ask for available policy records and confirm the facts through reliable sources. If uncertainty remains, document it and follow claim handling procedures.

**L.4.26** **Q:** Can you rely only on a weather app to determine wind causation? **A:** No. Weather information is useful context, but the physical evidence must also support the conclusion. Consider damage patterns and competing causes.

**L.4.27** **Q:** Does wind-driven rain always mean wind damage is covered? **A:** No. The cause of the opening that allowed water entry may matter. Investigate whether a covered cause created the opening.

**L.4.28** **Q:** Can you call all roof damage hail damage because hail occurred in the area? **A:** No. Roof conditions can result from wear, installation issues, tree contact, or other causes. Identify the observed damage before reaching a cause conclusion.

**L.4.29** **Q:** Does interior water staining prove a wind claim? **A:** No. Staining shows that water entered, not necessarily how or when it entered. Trace the source and evaluate the condition of the building envelope.

**L.4.30** **Q:** Can a prior roof claim be ignored when reviewing a new claim? **A:** No. Prior damage and repairs may affect causation and valuation. Review prior claim information when it is relevant to the current damage.

**L.4.31** **Q:** Is it safe to tell the insured that the deductible will be refunded later? **A:** No. Do not make promises that the policy does not support. Explain that the deductible is applied according to the policy and claim facts.

**L.4.32** **Q:** Can you describe the deductible as a penalty? **A:** No. A deductible is a policy term that affects the amount payable for a covered loss. Use neutral language that explains the calculation without assigning blame.

**L.4.33** **Q:** Should you discuss the deductible before confirming coverage? **A:** You can explain that a deductible may apply, but avoid presenting a final result too early. Confirm coverage, cause, location, and policy terms before making the final determination.

**L.4.34** **Q:** Can you deny a claim solely because the expected repair cost appears below the deductible? **A:** No. You still need to evaluate coverage and document the loss. A covered loss may produce no payment after the deductible, but the coverage review still matters.

**L.4.35** **Q:** Does a payment below the deductible mean there was no claim? **A:** No. The claim should still be handled and documented appropriately. The deductible affects payment, not whether the reported event should be evaluated.

**L.4.36** **Q:** Can an insured avoid the deductible by paying a contractor directly? **A:** No. Who pays the contractor does not change the deductible provision. Apply the deductible to the covered loss calculation.

**L.4.37** **Q:** Can a contractor absorb the deductible and change the claim payment? **A:** No. Contractor arrangements do not alter the policy deductible. Focus on the covered amount and the deductible that applies under the policy.

**L.4.38** **Q:** Does the deductible apply before you decide whether work is reasonable and necessary? **A:** No. Evaluate the covered scope and reasonable repair cost before completing the settlement calculation. An unsupported estimate should not control the result.

**L.4.39** **Q:** Can matching issues be used to avoid applying the deductible? **A:** No. Matching concerns and deductible application are separate questions. Address each under the applicable policy language and claim facts.

**L.4.40** **Q:** Does ordinance or law coverage as a percentage of Coverage A change the wind deductible by itself? **A:** No. Treat that coverage as a separate coverage question unless the policy states otherwise. Do not combine its limit structure with the deductible calculation without support.

**L.4.41** **Q:** Can the microbial remediation limit be treated as part of the wind deductible? **A:** No. That limit addresses a different coverage issue. Keep the deductible analysis separate from any limitation that may affect remediation work.

**L.4.42** **Q:** Does the assessment coverage limit increase what is payable for wind damage? **A:** Not by itself. Determine whether the claimed assessment is covered and how the applicable deductible works. Do not use a separate coverage limit as a shortcut for claim payment.

**L.4.43** **Q:** Can the sworn proof deadline be ignored because the loss involved a major storm? **A:** No. The sworn proof deadline remains an important policy condition. Explain the requirement clearly and document communications with the insured.

**L.4.44** **Q:** Does the years within which an action against the insurer must be brought affect the deductible calculation? **A:** No. That condition concerns a different claim issue. Keep it separate from your analysis of coverage and deductible application.

**L.4.45** **Q:** Can the nonrenewal notice period be used to decide whether a deductible applies? **A:** No. The nonrenewal notice period concerns policy administration rather than loss settlement. Do not confuse policy status questions with deductible questions.

**L.4.46** **Q:** Can you assume an endorsement is irrelevant because it has a general title? **A:** No. Read the endorsement language and identify whether it changes deductibles, covered causes of loss, or valuation. Titles are helpful labels, not a substitute for review.

**L.4.47** **Q:** Does a mortgagee decide whether the wind deductible applies? **A:** No. Mortgagee interests may affect payment handling, but they do not rewrite the deductible provision. Resolve deductible application under the policy terms.

**L.4.48** **Q:** Can an insured’s memory of selecting a deductible replace the issued policy? **A:** No. The issued policy and applicable endorsements control the coverage analysis. Treat recollections as information to investigate, not as final policy evidence.

**L.4.49** **Q:** Is a renewal declarations page always enough to determine the deductible for an earlier loss? **A:** No. Use the policy version in force for the date of loss. Later policy changes may not apply to an earlier event.

**L.4.50** **Q:** Can you use a later endorsement to interpret an earlier policy term? **A:** Be careful. A later endorsement may show that the policy changed rather than clarify the earlier wording. Review the policy materials applicable to the loss.

**L.4.51** **Q:** Does a deductible apply to excluded damage? **A:** No payment calculation is needed for damage that is not covered. Decide coverage before applying the deductible. Clear sequencing avoids confusing explanations.

**L.4.52** **Q:** Can you apply a wind deductible to unrelated water damage found during the inspection? **A:** No. Separate damage by cause when the facts support doing so. The deductible analysis should follow the covered cause of each claimed damage item.

**L.4.53** **Q:** Can a claim note simply say that the deductible was applied? **A:** No. Your note should explain why that deductible applied and what policy information you reviewed. A clear file helps others understand the decision.

**L.4.54** **Q:** Should you hide the deductible explanation in technical language? **A:** No. Explain the outcome in plain language while remaining accurate. The insured should understand why the payment differs from the repair estimate.

**L.4.55** **Q:** Can you tell an insured that every neighbor has the same wind deductible? **A:** No. Deductibles can vary by policy, location, and applicable endorsements. Discuss only the insured’s own policy and claim.

**L.4.56** **Q:** Does the same weather event guarantee the same outcome for every insured? **A:** No. Damage, coverage, policy wording, and deductibles may differ. Evaluate each claim using its own facts and policy terms.

**L.4.57** **Q:** Can you decide causation from photographs alone? **A:** Not always. Photographs can be valuable, but they may not show age, prior condition, or all relevant damage patterns. Use them with inspection findings and other evidence.

**L.4.58** **Q:** Is it acceptable to change the deductible after payment without explaining why? **A:** No. Any revision should be supported by policy information or corrected claim facts. Communicate the reason for the change and document the file.

**L.4.59** **Q:** Can you treat a deductible dispute as proof that the claim is covered? **A:** No. A disagreement about the deductible does not resolve coverage. Address the coverage decision and deductible decision separately.

**L.4.60** **Q:** Does an insured’s financial hardship remove the deductible? **A:** No. Handle the claim with empathy, but do not alter policy terms based on hardship. You can explain the settlement clearly and discuss available claim process options.

**L.4.61** **Q:** Can you close a wind claim without confirming that the deductible explanation is accurate? **A:** No. Review the policy wording, cause of loss, insured location, and settlement calculation before closing. A final accuracy check prevents common deductible errors.

**L.4.62** **Q:** What is the best way to avoid deductible mistakes? **A:** Slow down and separate the questions of coverage, cause, location, valuation, and deductible application. When your reasoning is clear, your claim explanation will be clearer too.

## L.6 — Knowledge Check

**L.6.1** **Q:** What is the main purpose of a wind or hail deductible? How should you explain it to an insured?

**L.6.2** **A:** It is the portion of a covered wind or hail loss that remains the insured’s responsibility. Explain that it affects the loss payment, not whether coverage exists.

**L.6.3** **Q:** Does a wind deductible always apply to every property loss? What should you review before answering?

**L.6.4** **A:** No. Review the cause of loss, the policy wording, and any applicable endorsement before deciding which deductible applies.

**L.6.5** **Q:** Why should you identify the cause of damage carefully? Can visible damage alone answer the question?

**L.6.6** **A:** The cause of loss determines whether the wind or hail deductible may apply. Visible roof damage may have more than one possible cause.

**L.6.7** **Q:** What should you do when an insured reports wind and hail damage together? Should you assume separate deductibles apply?

**L.6.8** **A:** Do not assume that separate deductibles apply. Read the deductible language and match it to the reported event.

**L.6.9** **Q:** How does the location of the damaged property matter? Is the insured’s mailing address enough?

**L.6.10** **A:** The covered location can control the applicable deductible treatment. Confirm the location shown in the policy rather than relying only on a mailing address.

**L.6.11** **Q:** What if a policy covers property in more than one state? Can you apply the same approach everywhere?

**L.6.12** **A:** No. State-specific rules and policy provisions can differ. Confirm the deductible that applies at the location of the loss.

**L.6.13** **Q:** Why should you review endorsements before discussing a deductible? Can the declarations alone settle the issue?

**L.6.14** **A:** Endorsements can change how the deductible applies. The declarations are important, but they may not tell the complete story.

**L.6.15** **Q:** What is a practical way to explain a percentage deductible? Should you call it a flat-dollar deductible?

**L.6.16** **A:** Explain that the deductible is based on the policy value identified by the applicable wording. Do not describe it as a flat-dollar deductible unless the policy says it is one.

**L.6.17** **Q:** What should you do when the insured asks for the exact deductible amount? Can you estimate from memory?

**L.6.18** **A:** Use the declarations, endorsements, and applicable valuation basis. Do not estimate when the policy record can provide the answer.

**L.6.19** **Q:** How should you handle damage that occurred during more than one weather event? Can you combine all damage automatically?

**L.6.20** **A:** Investigate the reported events and the damage evidence. Separate events may require separate coverage and deductible analysis.

**L.6.21** **Q:** What information helps establish the event that caused damage? Is the insured’s description useful?

**L.6.22** **A:** The insured’s description is useful, but it should be considered with inspection findings and available weather information. Document the facts that support your conclusion.

**L.6.23** **Q:** What if the insured says the roof was already damaged? Does that end the claim review?

**L.6.24** **A:** No. Prior damage may affect the scope of covered damage, but you still need to evaluate the reported loss. Distinguish preexisting conditions from new damage.

**L.6.25** **Q:** Why does the condition of the property matter in a hail claim? Can all marks be treated as hail damage?

**L.6.26** **A:** Property condition can help explain whether damage is new, old, accidental, or caused by wear. Do not classify every mark as hail damage without support.

**L.6.27** **Q:** What should you say when an insured asks whether wind damage is covered? Can you answer before reviewing exclusions?

**L.6.28** **A:** Explain that coverage depends on the full policy language and the facts of the loss. Review exclusions, limitations, and conditions before giving a coverage position.

**L.6.29** **Q:** How should you discuss the deductible with an upset insured? Is technical language the best approach?

**L.6.30** **A:** Use clear language and explain how the deductible affects the covered payment. Avoid promising an outcome before the claim review is complete.

**L.6.31** **Q:** What is the difference between a deductible and a coverage limit? Should you use the terms interchangeably?

**L.6.32** **A:** A deductible is the insured’s share of a covered loss. A coverage limit is the most available coverage under the applicable policy provision.

**L.6.33** **Q:** How does a single plant limit affect your review of commercial property? What should you confirm?

**L.6.34** **A:** Confirm whether the reported property is subject to a single plant limit. Then apply the policy language to the facts of the reported loss.

**L.6.35** **Q:** When should you look for a renewal deductible notice? Is it relevant to an underwriting file?

**L.6.36** **A:** A renewal deductible notice may be relevant when confirming how deductible changes were communicated. Review the available file materials before describing the renewal history.

**L.6.37** **Q:** Why might business days to accept or reject a claim after receiving requested items matter? Should you track claim activity?

**L.6.38** **A:** This concept can affect claim handling expectations after requested information is received. Maintain a clear record of requests, receipts, and claim decisions.

**L.6.39** **Q:** What does inspection trigger roof age mean for underwriting review? Should it replace an inspection?

**L.6.40** **A:** Inspection trigger roof age can help identify when further property review may be needed. It does not replace careful evaluation of the actual property condition.

**L.6.41** **Q:** What should you verify before relying on a roof inspection? Is every inspection report equally useful?

**L.6.42** **A:** Confirm the property address, the inspection scope, and the observations recorded. Consider whether the report addresses the claimed area of damage.

**L.6.43** **Q:** Can a contractor’s estimate determine the applicable deductible? What is its proper role?

**L.6.44** **A:** A contractor’s estimate may help identify claimed repairs and pricing. It does not replace the policy review needed to determine the deductible.

**L.6.45** **Q:** What if repair costs appear lower than the deductible? Should you deny coverage without further review?

**L.6.46** **A:** Complete the coverage analysis and document the covered damage. If the covered amount does not exceed the applicable deductible, explain the payment result clearly.

**L.6.47** **Q:** How should you handle a claim involving interior water damage after wind? Does the roof damage decide everything?

**L.6.48** **A:** Review the path of water entry and the applicable policy conditions. Interior damage may require its own factual and coverage analysis.

**L.6.49** **Q:** Why is documentation important when applying a wind or hail deductible? Can a short note be enough?

**L.6.50** **A:** Your file should show the reported cause, policy language reviewed, and basis for the deductible decision. Clear documentation helps another reviewer understand your reasoning.

**L.6.51** **Q:** What should you do if the declarations and endorsement appear inconsistent? Can you choose the result that seems most convenient?

**L.6.52** **A:** Escalate the issue through the appropriate underwriting or claims process. Do not resolve conflicting policy language by assumption.

**L.6.53** **Q:** Can you rely on a prior claim decision for a new wind or hail claim? What may be different?

**L.6.54** **A:** A prior claim can provide background, but the current policy and current facts control the new review. Confirm that the relevant wording has not changed.

**L.6.55** **Q:** What if the insured believes the deductible is unfair? Should you debate the choice of deductible?

**L.6.56** **A:** Acknowledge the concern and explain the deductible shown in the applicable policy record. Keep the discussion focused on the policy terms and the current loss.

**L.6.57** **Q:** Why should you avoid calling all storm damage wind damage? Can storm reports be broad?

**L.6.58** **A:** Storm reports can describe conditions without identifying the exact cause of every item of damage. Match the claimed damage to the evidence available for that property.

**L.6.59** **Q:** What should you do when damage appears limited to an older part of the roof? Does age alone prove prior damage?

**L.6.60** **A:** Age alone does not prove when damage occurred. Use inspection evidence and claim facts to distinguish deterioration from sudden damage.

**L.6.61** **Q:** How should you explain a deductible to a mortgage lender or other interested party? Should you disclose more than necessary?

**L.6.62** **A:** Provide information only as permitted by the policy, claim authority, and privacy requirements. Explain the deductible in the context of the covered loss payment.

**L.6.63** **Q:** What is the best response when you are unsure which deductible applies? Should you guess to keep the file moving?

**L.6.64** **A:** Pause and verify the policy language, location, and cause of loss. Escalate questions when the available record does not support a clear answer.

**L.6.65** **Q:** How can you make a deductible explanation easier to understand? Should you begin with policy jargon?

**L.6.66** **A:** Start with the practical result for the reported claim. Then explain the policy basis in plain language and invite questions.

**L.6.67** **Q:** What should your claim note show after you explain the deductible? Is a verbal conversation enough?

**L.6.68** **A:** Record what you explained, the policy materials reviewed, and any remaining questions. A clear note supports consistent follow-up.

**L.6.69** **Q:** What is the key habit to remember when handling wind and hail deductibles? Is speed more important than accuracy?

**L.6.70** **A:** Accuracy comes from matching the policy wording to the reported facts. A careful review protects both the insured and the company.

**L.6.71** **Q:** When should you ask for help from a supervisor or specialist? Is escalation a sign of failure?

**L.6.72** **A:** Ask for help when the policy language, damage evidence, or state treatment is unclear. Escalation is a sound professional step when it supports an accurate decision.

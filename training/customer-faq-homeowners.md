# Customer FAQ — Homeowners







## L.1 — Learning Objectives

**L.1.1** You will learn how to recognize the questions homeowners ask most often about insurance protection. You will focus on clear answers that help customers understand what happens next.

**L.1.2** You will learn to listen for the concern behind the question. A customer may ask about coverage while really seeking reassurance, guidance, or help reporting a loss.

**L.1.3** You will learn to use plain language without losing important meaning. Your goal is to make insurance terms easier to understand, not to make promises that the coverage does not support.

**L.1.4** You will learn to explain that coverage depends on the applicable terms, facts, and circumstances. You will avoid assuming an outcome before the information has been reviewed.

**L.1.5** You will learn how to distinguish general information from a decision about a specific claim or coverage request. This helps you answer confidently while staying within your role.

**L.1.6** You will learn to explain property coverage in a way that connects to the customer’s home and belongings. You will help the customer understand that damaged property, the cause of damage, and the coverage wording all matter.

**L.1.7** You will learn to discuss deductibles in clear, customer-friendly terms. You will explain that a deductible can affect what the customer receives after a covered loss.

**L.1.8** You will learn to explain coverage limits without suggesting that every loss will be paid in full. You will help customers understand that limits and applicable terms can affect payment.

**L.1.9** You will learn to address exclusions carefully and respectfully. You will explain that some causes of loss, property, or circumstances may not be covered.

**L.1.10** You will learn to describe customer responsibilities after a loss. You will emphasize the importance of protecting property, sharing accurate information, and cooperating with the claim process.

**L.1.11** You will learn how endorsements can change standard coverage. You will avoid assuming that every customer has the same protection.

**L.1.12** You will learn to identify when a question involves a possible claim. You will guide the customer toward the appropriate reporting process without deciding coverage yourself.

**L.1.13** You will learn to ask useful follow-up questions about what happened, what was damaged, and whether anyone was injured. Good questions help you gather information without leading the customer to an answer.

**L.1.14** You will learn to explain the difference between property concerns and liability concerns. You will recognize when a customer’s question may involve injury, damage to someone else’s property, or a legal demand.

**L.1.15** You will learn to communicate with empathy when a customer is upset or under stress. You can acknowledge the situation while remaining accurate and professional.

**L.1.16** You will learn to avoid language that guarantees payment, coverage, or a claim result. You will use careful wording when facts still need to be reviewed.

**L.1.17** You will learn to explain why documentation can be helpful. Photos, receipts, estimates, and other records may support the handling of a claim.

**L.1.18** You will learn to recognize when information is missing or unclear. When needed, you will seek help rather than guessing.

**L.1.19** You will learn to handle questions about updates and next steps with transparency. Customers should understand what information is needed and who will review it.

**L.1.20** You will learn to protect customer information during every interaction. You will share details only through appropriate channels and with authorized parties.

**L.1.21** You will learn to document customer communications clearly. Accurate notes help others understand the question, the information provided, and any follow-up needed.

**L.1.22** By the end of this material, you should be able to answer common homeowners questions with clarity, care, and appropriate caution. You will know when to provide general guidance and when to involve the right insurance professional.

## L.2 — Key Concepts

**L.2.1** **Q:** What does homeowners insurance generally protect?

**A:** Homeowners insurance can help with covered damage to your home, belongings, and certain legal responsibilities. Coverage depends on the policy wording and the facts of the loss.

**L.2.2** **Q:** Does every loss qualify for coverage?

**A:** No. A loss must fit within the policy’s coverage grant and must not be excluded or limited.

**L.2.3** **Q:** Why does the cause of damage matter?

**A:** The cause helps determine whether the policy responds. You should describe what happened as clearly as you can when reporting a claim.

**L.2.4** **Q:** What is a covered loss?

**A:** A covered loss is damage or liability that meets the policy requirements. Coverage may still be subject to deductibles, limits, conditions, and exclusions.

**L.2.5** **Q:** What is an exclusion?

**A:** An exclusion identifies a type of loss, property, cause, or circumstance the policy does not cover. Exclusions are important because they can prevent coverage even when damage is significant.

**L.2.6** **Q:** What is a policy limit?

**A:** A policy limit is the most the insurer will pay for covered property or a covered type of loss. Limits can apply differently to the home, personal property, and special coverage items.

**L.2.7** **Q:** What is a deductible?

**A:** A deductible is the amount you are responsible for before insurance pays a covered property claim. The deductible usually applies to covered damage rather than liability claims.

**L.2.8** **Q:** Is the deductible taken from every claim payment?

**A:** Not always. Whether it applies depends on the coverage involved and the policy wording.

**L.2.9** **Q:** What is the dwelling?

**A:** The dwelling is generally the home structure shown in the policy. It can include attached structures when the policy treats them as part of the home.

**L.2.10** **Q:** Are attached structures part of the dwelling?

**A:** They often are, but the policy wording controls. You should not assume that every structure connected to the home is treated the same way.

**L.2.11** **Q:** What are other structures?

**A:** Other structures are buildings or structures on the property that are separate from the dwelling. Examples may include certain sheds, fences, and detached garages.

**L.2.12** **Q:** Is every structure on my property covered?

**A:** No. Coverage can depend on the structure’s use, ownership, location, and condition.

**L.2.13** **Q:** What is personal property?

**A:** Personal property is the movable property you own or use. It may include furniture, clothing, electronics, and household items.

**L.2.14** **Q:** Are all belongings covered in the same way?

**A:** No. Some categories of property can have special limits, valuation rules, or coverage restrictions. Your claim handling may depend on the type of item involved.

**L.2.15** **Q:** Why should I keep records of my belongings?

**A:** Records can help show ownership, condition, and value after a loss. Photos, receipts, and inventories can make the claim process easier.

**L.2.16** **Q:** What should I do after property is damaged?

**A:** Protect the property from further damage if it is safe to do so. Report the loss promptly and keep damaged items available for review when possible.

**L.2.17** **Q:** Can I throw away damaged property?

**A:** Avoid disposing of damaged property until the insurer has had a chance to inspect it or has told you otherwise. If disposal is necessary for safety, keep photos and records if you can.

**L.2.18** **Q:** Should I make temporary repairs?

**A:** You should take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage when it is safe. Keep receipts and records for emergency work and temporary repairs.

**L.2.19** **Q:** Does temporary repair mean permanent repair?

**A:** No. Temporary work is meant to protect property from further damage. Permanent repairs should be discussed with the insurer when practical.

**L.2.20** **Q:** What information should I provide when reporting a claim?

**A:** Provide a clear description of what happened, where it happened, and what was damaged. Share any relevant photos, invoices, reports, or contact information you have.

**L.2.21** **Q:** Why might an adjuster inspect the damage?

**A:** An adjuster may inspect to understand the cause, scope, and extent of the loss. The inspection also helps identify covered property and needed repairs.

**L.2.22** **Q:** What if I disagree with the adjuster’s assessment?

**A:** Tell the claims representative what you disagree with and provide supporting information. Estimates, photos, invoices, and expert opinions may help explain your position.

**L.2.23** **Q:** Can I choose a contractor?

**A:** You may be able to choose a contractor, subject to the policy and claim process. You remain responsible for understanding the contractor’s work, pricing, and agreement.

**L.2.24** **Q:** Does the insurer guarantee a contractor’s work?

**A:** You should not assume that a contractor is guaranteed simply because the contractor is suggested during a claim. Ask questions about the contractor’s role and any available service arrangements.

**L.2.25** **Q:** What is replacement cost?

**A:** Replacement cost generally concerns the cost to repair or replace damaged property with similar property. Payment may depend on policy conditions and the work completed.

**L.2.26** **Q:** What is actual cash value?

**A:** Actual cash value generally reflects the value of property after considering age, wear, and condition. It may be less than the cost of buying new replacement property.

**L.2.27** **Q:** Why does valuation matter?

**A:** Valuation affects how a covered loss is calculated. Different property and coverages may use different valuation methods.

**L.2.28** **Q:** Does insurance pay for upgrades?

**A:** Coverage usually focuses on repairing covered damage rather than improving undamaged property. An upgrade may create costs that are not covered.

**L.2.29** **Q:** What if building rules affect repairs?

**A:** Building requirements can affect how repairs must be completed. Whether related costs are covered depends on the policy wording and the specific circumstances.

**L.2.30** **Q:** What is loss of use coverage?

**A:** Loss of use coverage may help when a covered loss makes the home unsuitable for normal living. The policy wording determines what expenses and circumstances qualify.

**L.2.31** **Q:** Can I move out after any damage to my home?

**A:** You should make living arrangements based on safety and the condition of the home. Ask the claims representative how the policy may respond before assuming expenses are covered.

**L.2.32** **Q:** Are hotel costs always covered?

**A:** No. Hotel costs must relate to a covered loss and meet the policy requirements. The reason for leaving the home matters.

**L.2.33** **Q:** What is personal liability coverage?

**A:** Personal liability coverage may protect you if you are legally responsible for bodily injury or property damage to others. Coverage depends on the event, the claim, and the policy terms.

**L.2.34** **Q:** Does liability coverage mean I am automatically at fault?

**A:** No. Liability coverage involves reviewing whether you have legal responsibility. A claim can be investigated even when fault is uncertain.

**L.2.35** **Q:** What should I do if someone says I caused damage?

**A:** Report the matter to the insurer promptly. Do not make promises to pay or admit responsibility before the claim has been reviewed.

**L.2.36** **Q:** What is medical payments coverage?

**A:** Medical payments coverage may help with certain medical expenses after someone is injured in connection with the insured location or your activities. It does not necessarily determine legal fault.

**L.2.37** **Q:** Does medical payments coverage apply to every injury?

**A:** No. The policy contains requirements, exclusions, and conditions. The facts surrounding the injury must be reviewed.

**L.2.38** **Q:** What happens if police or emergency services respond to a loss?

**A:** Keep any reports, notices, or invoices you receive. These records can help the insurer understand what occurred.

**L.2.39** **Q:** Does homeowners insurance cover a fire department service charge?

**A:** The most we will pay is $500 (five hundred dollars). This coverage addresses a charge billed by a fire department for responding to a covered loss.

**L.2.40** **Q:** Should I send a fire department invoice to the insurer?

**A:** Yes, provide the invoice or notice as soon as you can. Keep a copy for your records.

**L.2.41** **Q:** What if the damage was caused by water?

**A:** Water losses require careful review because coverage can depend heavily on the source and manner of water entry. Explain whether the water came from inside the home, outside the home, or another source.

**L.2.42** **Q:** Are flood losses covered by homeowners insurance?

**A:** Do not assume that water damage caused by flooding is covered. Coverage for flooding is often handled differently from other water damage.

**L.2.43** **Q:** What if a pipe leaks?

**A:** Report the damage and take reasonable steps to stop further leaking if it is safe. Coverage can depend on the cause, duration, and resulting damage.

**L.2.44** **Q:** Does insurance cover wear and tear?

**A:** Wear and tear is generally a maintenance concern rather than sudden accidental damage. A claim review may separate covered resulting damage from excluded maintenance issues.

**L.2.45** **Q:** What if mold is found after a loss?

**A:** Report the condition promptly and take reasonable steps to reduce further damage when safe. Mold-related coverage can involve special conditions and limitations.

**L.2.46** **Q:** What is maintenance?

**A:** Maintenance is the routine care needed to keep the home and its systems in working condition. Insurance is not a substitute for upkeep and repairs caused by ordinary deterioration.

**L.2.47** **Q:** Why does the condition of my home matter?

**A:** Property condition can help explain the cause and extent of damage. It may also affect whether a problem developed suddenly or over time.

**L.2.48** **Q:** What if my roof is damaged?

**A:** Report the damage and protect the home from further damage if it is safe. The cause of the roof damage and the condition of the roof can affect coverage.

**L.2.49** **Q:** Does homeowners insurance cover storm damage?

**A:** It may cover damage caused by certain storm events, subject to the policy terms. The type of storm damage and the damaged property both matter.

**L.2.50** **Q:** What if a tree falls on my home?

**A:** Report the damage and take steps to protect the home from additional damage if it is safe. Coverage can depend on what caused the tree to fall and what property was damaged.

**L.2.51** **Q:** Is damage from animals covered?

**A:** Animal damage can be treated differently depending on the animal and the nature of the damage. Provide details about what you observed and when you noticed it.

**L.2.52** **Q:** What if property is stolen?

**A:** Report the theft to law enforcement when appropriate and notify the insurer promptly. Provide a list of missing property and any records showing ownership or value.

**L.2.53** **Q:** Why is a police report useful after theft?

**A:** A report can document the event and identify information relevant to the claim. The insurer may request a copy or report details.

**L.2.54** **Q:** Are valuables handled differently from ordinary belongings?

**A:** Some valuable items may have special coverage rules or limits. Tell the insurer about the type of item, its value, and any available proof of ownership.

**L.2.55** **Q:** What if my belongings are damaged away from home?

**A:** Personal property coverage may extend beyond the insured location in some situations. The policy wording and circumstances determine whether coverage applies.

**L.2.56** **Q:** Does homeowners insurance cover property owned by guests?

**A:** Coverage for property belonging to others can depend on the circumstances and policy wording. Tell the insurer who owns the property and why it was at your home.

**L.2.57** **Q:** What if I rent part of my home to someone else?

**A:** Renting can affect coverage and may create additional risks. Tell the insurer about the arrangement so the policy can be reviewed accurately.

**L.2.58** **Q:** What if I run a business from home?

**A:** Business activities may not be covered in the same way as personal activities. Inform the insurer about business use of the home or property.

**L.2.59** **Q:** Can a home-based business affect a claim?

**A:** Yes. The use of the property and the property involved can affect coverage. Clear information about the business activity helps with the review.

**L.2.60** **Q:** What if I move?

**A:** Tell the insurer before or as soon as possible after changing your residence. A new location can change the risks, property details, and coverage needs.

**L.2.61** **Q:** Why should I update my policy after home improvements?

**A:** Improvements can change the cost to repair or replace the home. Updating the insurer helps keep property information accurate.

**L.2.62** **Q:** What if I buy expensive personal property?

**A:** Keep purchase records, photos, appraisals, and other ownership documents. Some property may need special attention under the policy.

**L.2.63** **Q:** What should I do if I receive legal papers?

**A:** Send the papers to the insurer promptly and keep copies. Do not ignore a lawsuit, demand, or other legal notice.

**L.2.64** **Q:** Can I settle a claim directly with someone who says I damaged their property?

**A:** Contact the insurer before agreeing to pay or settling the matter. A private agreement may affect the insurer’s ability to investigate or respond.

**L.2.65** **Q:** What is a reservation of rights letter?

**A:** It is a notice explaining that the insurer is investigating while reserving the right to deny some or all coverage. It does not necessarily mean the claim has been denied.

**L.2.66** **Q:** What is a coverage decision?

**A:** A coverage decision explains whether the policy applies to the claim or a portion of the claim. It should identify the relevant policy reasoning.

**L.2.67** **Q:** Why might part of my claim be covered and another part not covered?

**A:** Different damage can have different causes, coverages, limits, or exclusions. The insurer may evaluate each part of the loss separately.

**L.2.68** **Q:** What is depreciation?

**A:** Depreciation reflects the effect of age, use, and condition on property value. It can affect claim payment depending on the policy valuation method.

**L.2.69** **Q:** Why should I save receipts after a loss?

**A:** Receipts can support claimed expenses and help show what you paid. Keep records organized and provide them when requested.

**L.2.70** **Q:** Can I begin cleanup before the claim is inspected?

**A:** You may need to act quickly to prevent further damage or address safety concerns. Take photos and keep records before cleanup whenever practical.

**L.2.71** **Q:** What if the home is unsafe after a loss?

**A:** Put safety first and follow directions from emergency officials or qualified professionals. Tell the insurer about unsafe conditions and emergency actions taken.

**L.2.72** **Q:** Do I need to cooperate with the claim investigation?

**A:** Yes. The policy generally requires you to provide information, documents, and access reasonably needed to evaluate the claim.

**L.2.73** **Q:** What does cooperation mean during a claim?

**A:** Cooperation means responding honestly and promptly to reasonable claim requests. It also means preserving relevant information and allowing appropriate inspections.

**L.2.74** **Q:** What if I do not have every document requested?

**A:** Tell the insurer what you have and explain what is unavailable. Other records or sources may help support the claim.

**L.2.75** **Q:** Why must I be truthful in a claim?

**A:** Accurate information helps the insurer investigate and evaluate coverage fairly. Incorrect or incomplete information can delay the claim and may affect coverage.

**L.2.76** **Q:** Can I ask questions during the claim process?

**A:** Yes. Ask the claims representative to explain requests, decisions, or next steps that are unclear.

**L.2.77** **Q:** What should I do if I receive a claim payment?

**A:** Review the payment explanation and compare it with the damage and claim information. Ask questions promptly if you do not understand what the payment addresses.

**L.2.78** **Q:** Can additional damage be reported after the initial claim?

**A:** Yes, report newly discovered damage promptly. Explain how and when you found it, and provide supporting photos or records if available.

**L.2.79** **Q:** What is the best way to help a homeowners claim move forward?

**A:** Report the loss promptly, protect property from further damage, and provide clear information. Keep records, ask questions, and stay in contact with the claims representative.

## L.3 — Worked Examples

**L.3.1** **Q:** A customer says water came through a roof opening after wind damaged the roof. **A:** Start by separating the wind damage from the resulting interior water damage.

**L.3.2** **Q:** The customer reports a stain on the ceiling but cannot identify when it began. **A:** Ask what they observed, what they did to protect the property, and whether there is visible damage nearby.

**L.3.3** **Q:** A homeowner asks whether a leaking pipe is covered. **A:** Explain that the source of the leak and the resulting damage may be evaluated differently.

**L.3.4** **Q:** Water entered through a backed-up drain. **A:** Check whether water backup coverage applies before describing the claim outcome.

**L.3.5** **Q:** The insured says the sump overflowed during heavy rain. **A:** The deductible for a sump overflow claim is $500 (five hundred dollars).

**L.3.6** **Q:** The customer asks about the water backup and sump overflow sublimit. **A:** Explain that this policy term can affect how a covered water-related loss is settled.

**L.3.7** **Q:** A customer reports damage from water that entered through an open window. **A:** Ask why the window was open and whether wind or another covered event caused the opening.

**L.3.8** **Q:** A frozen pipe bursts while the homeowner is away. **A:** Gather details about heating, occupancy, and steps taken to maintain the home.

**L.3.9** **Q:** A customer finds mold after discovering a slow leak. **A:** Focus on the cause of the moisture and the timeline of the reported damage.

**L.3.10** **Q:** The insured says a dishwasher leaked onto the kitchen floor. **A:** Confirm what failed and whether the damage resulted from a sudden event or ongoing seepage.

**L.3.11** **Q:** A washing machine hose comes loose and damages flooring. **A:** Record the cause of the release and the materials affected by the water.

**L.3.12** **Q:** A customer says a toilet overflow damaged a bathroom. **A:** Ask whether the water remained within the plumbing system or backed up from elsewhere.

**L.3.13** **Q:** Water enters the basement after surface water collects near the foundation. **A:** Review the path of the water before discussing possible coverage.

**L.3.14** **Q:** The homeowner says a contractor told them the roof has old damage. **A:** Treat the contractor's statement as information to investigate, not as a coverage decision.

**L.3.15** **Q:** A tree falls onto the dwelling during a storm. **A:** Confirm what the tree struck and whether the customer has taken reasonable steps to prevent further damage.

**L.3.16** **Q:** A fallen tree blocks the driveway but does not damage the home. **A:** Explain that removal concerns are evaluated based on the facts and applicable policy terms.

**L.3.17** **Q:** Wind damages siding on part of the home. **A:** Document the damaged area and avoid promising that unrelated materials will be replaced.

**L.3.18** **Q:** The customer asks whether matching materials will be available. **A:** Explain that availability can affect repair planning but does not decide coverage by itself.

**L.3.19** **Q:** Hail dents a metal roof. **A:** Obtain clear information about the condition of the roof before and after the reported event.

**L.3.20** **Q:** A customer reports wind damage after a named storm. **A:** Identify the applicable deductible terms before discussing payment expectations.

**L.3.21** **Q:** The homeowner asks about the named storm deductible minimum. **A:** Explain that it is a policy term that may apply when a named storm causes covered damage.

**L.3.22** **Q:** A storm damages a fence and outdoor furniture. **A:** Separate damage to the dwelling from damage to property kept outside.

**L.3.23** **Q:** Lightning damages several electronic items. **A:** Ask what happened at the time of the event and whether the items can be inspected.

**L.3.24** **Q:** A power surge damages appliances. **A:** Gather information about the affected equipment and any evidence of the surge.

**L.3.25** **Q:** The insured says lightning caused a fire. **A:** Treat the fire damage and the cause of the fire as related facts that both need support.

**L.3.26** **Q:** Smoke damages the home after a nearby fire. **A:** Confirm where the fire occurred and what property was affected by smoke.

**L.3.27** **Q:** A customer reports soot throughout the house. **A:** Ask about the source of the soot and whether emergency cleaning was needed.

**L.3.28** **Q:** A kitchen fire damages cabinets and cookware. **A:** Document the damaged building materials separately from damaged personal property.

**L.3.29** **Q:** Firefighters damaged a door while entering the home. **A:** Record why entry was needed and the damage caused during the response.

**L.3.30** **Q:** A customer says their home is unsafe to occupy after a fire. **A:** Explain that occupancy concerns should be evaluated promptly using the reported conditions.

**L.3.31** **Q:** The insured asks whether temporary living arrangements are available. **A:** Explain that this depends on the loss facts and the applicable policy provisions.

**L.3.32** **Q:** A customer moves personal property after a fire. **A:** Ask them to preserve records and photographs of the property when possible.

**L.3.33** **Q:** Theft is reported after a forced entry. **A:** Ask what signs of entry were found and whether law enforcement was notified.

**L.3.34** **Q:** The customer cannot provide receipts for stolen items. **A:** Explain that photographs, records, and other supporting information may help document ownership.

**L.3.35** **Q:** Jewelry is reported stolen from a bedroom. **A:** Identify the items carefully and review any applicable special limits.

**L.3.36** **Q:** A customer asks about the precious stones theft limit. **A:** Explain that this term may affect how theft of certain jewelry items is handled.

**L.3.37** **Q:** A bicycle is stolen from an unlocked garage. **A:** Gather the facts about where the bicycle was kept and how the theft was discovered.

**L.3.38** **Q:** Tools are missing from a detached structure. **A:** Confirm whether the tools were personal property and whether the structure was secured.

**L.3.39** **Q:** A customer says a guest stole property from the home. **A:** Avoid assumptions and collect details about the guest's access and the missing property.

**L.3.40** **Q:** The insured reports vandalism at a vacant home. **A:** Ask about the condition of the home and the events leading up to the discovery.

**L.3.41** **Q:** A wall is damaged by a vehicle. **A:** Confirm who owned or operated the vehicle and how the impact occurred.

**L.3.42** **Q:** A customer backed into their own garage door. **A:** Explain that vehicle damage can require careful review of the policy facts.

**L.3.43** **Q:** A neighbor's vehicle damages the insured's fence. **A:** Obtain the neighbor's contact and insurance information without directing the customer to settle privately.

**L.3.44** **Q:** A delivery vehicle damages landscaping. **A:** Document the location, visible damage, and any available information about the vehicle.

**L.3.45** **Q:** A customer says their dog bit a visitor. **A:** Gather facts about the event, the injured person, and the dog's history as reported.

**L.3.46** **Q:** A visitor slips on an icy walkway. **A:** Ask about the weather, the condition of the walkway, and what the homeowner knew.

**L.3.47** **Q:** A guest falls down interior stairs. **A:** Document the condition of the stairs and any reported reason for the fall.

**L.3.48** **Q:** A child is injured while using a trampoline. **A:** Report the circumstances accurately and avoid making liability conclusions during the initial conversation.

**L.3.49** **Q:** A customer asks whether they should speak with an injured person's attorney. **A:** Tell them to report the matter promptly and avoid discussing fault beyond the known facts.

**L.3.50** **Q:** The homeowner admits they may have caused the accident. **A:** Acknowledge the report without agreeing that the homeowner is legally responsible.

**L.3.51** **Q:** A customer asks why a deductible applies. **A:** Explain that a deductible is the portion of a covered loss handled by the insured under the policy terms.

**L.3.52** **Q:** The customer asks about the all-other-perils deductible minimum. **A:** Explain that this policy term should be identified before discussing the claim settlement.

**L.3.53** **Q:** The homeowner wants repairs started immediately. **A:** Encourage reasonable emergency steps while asking them to preserve evidence of the damage.

**L.3.54** **Q:** A contractor offers to begin work before the claim is reported. **A:** Tell the customer to keep records and avoid signing documents they do not understand.

**L.3.55** **Q:** The insured asks whether they may choose a contractor. **A:** Explain that they may discuss repair choices while the claim is being reviewed.

**L.3.56** **Q:** A contractor says the insurer must pay for upgrades. **A:** Explain that repair recommendations do not determine what the policy covers.

**L.3.57** **Q:** A customer receives an assignment request from a contractor. **A:** Encourage them to read the document carefully and ask questions before signing.

**L.3.58** **Q:** A repair estimate includes work unrelated to the reported loss. **A:** Separate loss-related damage from maintenance and improvement work.

**L.3.59** **Q:** The insured says the home had prior damage in the same area. **A:** Ask what prior damage existed and whether it had been repaired.

**L.3.60** **Q:** A customer cannot remember the date of loss. **A:** Ask about weather events, discovery dates, and other details that may help establish the timeline.

**L.3.61** **Q:** The customer asks whether maintenance is covered. **A:** Explain that maintenance issues and accidental loss are not the same thing.

**L.3.62** **Q:** A roof has worn shingles and a fresh opening. **A:** Review the reported event and distinguish visible wear from new damage.

**L.3.63** **Q:** The insured reports cracking in a foundation wall. **A:** Gather information about when the cracking was noticed and whether any event preceded it.

**L.3.64** **Q:** A customer says the floor is settling. **A:** Ask about the condition, any nearby water issues, and whether the change was sudden.

**L.3.65** **Q:** A wall shows signs of rot. **A:** Focus on the moisture source and the duration suggested by the available evidence.

**L.3.66** **Q:** The customer reports damage caused by insects. **A:** Explain that the cause of damage matters and should be documented carefully.

**L.3.67** **Q:** An animal damages wiring in the attic. **A:** Confirm the type of animal, the location of the damage, and whether there was resulting loss.

**L.3.68** **Q:** A pet damages a customer's furniture. **A:** Explain that property damage claims depend on the facts and applicable policy language.

**L.3.69** **Q:** A sewer line issue causes water to appear inside the home. **A:** Ask where the water originated and what plumbing systems were involved.

**L.3.70** **Q:** A customer says the city sewer system caused the loss. **A:** Record the customer's report without deciding who is responsible.

**L.3.71** **Q:** The insured asks whether they should discard damaged property. **A:** Tell them to preserve property when practical and photograph it before disposal.

**L.3.72** **Q:** Wet carpeting must be removed to prevent further damage. **A:** Ask the customer to keep photographs, invoices, and details about the emergency work.

**L.3.73** **Q:** A customer wants to clean smoke damage before inspection. **A:** Encourage documentation before cleaning whenever conditions safely allow.

**L.3.74** **Q:** The insured has no photographs from before the loss. **A:** Explain that other records may still help establish the condition and ownership of property.

**L.3.75** **Q:** A customer has only an estimate from a contractor. **A:** Treat the estimate as supporting information rather than a final claim decision.

**L.3.76** **Q:** The homeowner disagrees with the scope of repairs. **A:** Listen to the concern and identify the specific damage they believe was missed.

**L.3.77** **Q:** The customer says the payment will not complete repairs. **A:** Ask what repair work remains and what documentation supports the concern.

**L.3.78** **Q:** The insured asks how replacement cost works. **A:** Explain that settlement can depend on the policy terms, the damaged property, and the repair information.

**L.3.79** **Q:** A customer asks whether depreciation applies. **A:** Explain that the claim handling approach depends on the applicable coverage and facts.

**L.3.80** **Q:** The homeowner wants cash instead of repairs. **A:** Explain that payment questions should be handled using the policy terms and documented loss information.

**L.3.81** **Q:** A customer asks about the coinsurance threshold. **A:** Explain that it is a policy concept that may be relevant when evaluating the amount of insurance carried.

**L.3.82** **Q:** The insured says the home was recently renovated. **A:** Ask what was changed and whether the renovation affects the damaged area.

**L.3.83** **Q:** A customer added a home office before the loss. **A:** Gather details about how the space was used and what property was damaged.

**L.3.84** **Q:** Business equipment is damaged in the home. **A:** Identify the equipment and ask how it was used before making coverage statements.

**L.3.85** **Q:** A customer stores another person's property in the garage. **A:** Confirm who owns the property and why it was at the residence.

**L.3.86** **Q:** The homeowner rents part of the residence to someone else. **A:** Gather the occupancy facts because they may affect the claim review.

**L.3.87** **Q:** A customer reports damage at a seasonal residence. **A:** Ask about occupancy, maintenance, and when the damage was discovered.

**L.3.88** **Q:** The home is under renovation when the loss occurs. **A:** Identify the construction activity and whether the work relates to the damaged area.

**L.3.89** **Q:** A customer says a contractor caused the damage. **A:** Record the contractor's role and obtain available information about the work performed.

**L.3.90** **Q:** A neighbor says the insured caused damage to their property. **A:** Tell the insured to report the allegation and avoid promising payment.

**L.3.91** **Q:** The customer asks whether a claim will raise their premium. **A:** Explain that claim reporting and future pricing are separate matters that should not be predicted during claim intake.

**L.3.92** **Q:** The insured worries that reporting damage will lead to cancellation. **A:** Acknowledge the concern and focus on collecting accurate claim information.

**L.3.93** **Q:** A customer says they will repair the damage themselves. **A:** Ask them to document the damage and keep records of materials and work.

**L.3.94** **Q:** The homeowner has already completed repairs. **A:** Request photographs, invoices, and a clear explanation of the original damage.

**L.3.95** **Q:** A customer is upset because the claim is taking time. **A:** Explain what information is still needed and avoid giving promises that cannot be supported.

**L.3.96** **Q:** The insured does not understand a coverage decision. **A:** Use plain language to explain the reported facts, the policy basis, and any next steps.

**L.3.97** **Q:** A customer wants to appeal a decision. **A:** Explain the available review process without suggesting what the outcome will be.

**L.3.98** **Q:** The customer asks what information should be provided after a loss. **A:** Encourage clear photos, repair records, ownership information, and a description of what happened.

**L.3.99** **Q:** A homeowner is unsure whether damage is worth reporting. **A:** Explain that you can gather the facts without promising coverage or payment.

**L.3.100** **Q:** What is the best approach when facts are incomplete? **A:** Be clear about what is known, identify what still needs review, and avoid guessing.

## L.4 — Common Mistakes

**L.4.1** **Q:** Is it safe to assume every type of water damage is covered?

**A:** No. You should identify where the water came from and how it entered the home before discussing coverage. A slow leak, a backup, floodwater, and sudden discharge can be treated very differently.

**L.4.2** **Q:** Can I describe all water damage as flooding?

**A:** Avoid using flood as a catchall term. You should explain that flood coverage and coverage for water damage from inside the home may be separate issues.

**L.4.3** **Q:** Does a homeowners policy cover poor maintenance?

**A:** Usually, maintenance problems are not what the policy is designed to cover. You should separate the cost to correct wear, deterioration, or neglect from damage caused by a covered event.

**L.4.4** **Q:** Is an old roof automatically a reason to deny a roof claim?

**A:** No. Roof age may matter, but you still need to identify the actual cause of loss. A claim can involve both preexisting wear and new storm damage.

**L.4.5** **Q:** Can I promise full roof replacement after storm damage?

**A:** Do not promise a result before the damage, policy language, and repair requirements are reviewed. Replacement may depend on the covered cause of loss and the condition of the damaged property.

**L.4.6** **Q:** Does matching always mean the insurer must replace undamaged materials?

**A:** No. Matching concerns can be important, but they do not automatically create coverage for all undamaged materials. You should review the policy and applicable repair requirements before setting expectations.

**L.4.7** **Q:** Can I assume a tree claim is covered because the tree fell during a storm?

**A:** Not automatically. You should determine what caused the tree to fall and what property it damaged. Tree removal and damage to insured structures may be handled differently.

**L.4.8** **Q:** Is damage from a fallen tree the same as the cost to remove the tree?

**A:** No. The damage caused by the tree and the cost of removing debris are separate coverage questions. Explain that each part of the loss needs its own review.

**L.4.9** **Q:** Does homeowners insurance cover every sewer or drain backup?

**A:** No. Backup losses often require careful review of the source of the water and any applicable endorsement. Do not tell a customer that a backup is covered until the policy has been checked.

**L.4.10** **Q:** Should I tell a customer to wait for an adjuster before taking any action?

**A:** No. You should encourage reasonable steps to protect the property from further damage. The customer should keep damaged items when practical and document emergency work.

**L.4.11** **Q:** Can I tell a customer to throw away all damaged property right away?

**A:** No. Disposing of property too early can make it harder to evaluate the loss. Ask the customer to preserve items when safe and practical, while still taking reasonable protective action.

**L.4.12** **Q:** Is every repair made after a loss automatically reimbursable?

**A:** No. Emergency measures may be necessary, but the policy review still determines what is covered. The customer should keep invoices, photographs, and repair information.

**L.4.13** **Q:** Does an estimate prove that all work on the estimate is covered?

**A:** No. An estimate shows what a contractor proposes, not what the policy necessarily pays for. You should compare the estimate with the observed damage and the policy terms.

**L.4.14** **Q:** Can a contractor decide coverage for the customer?

**A:** No. Contractors can provide useful repair information, but they do not interpret the insurance contract for the insurer. You should avoid adopting a contractor’s coverage conclusion without review.

**L.4.15** **Q:** Does a mortgage lender receive claim payment in every situation?

**A:** Not always. Lender involvement can depend on the policy, the type of payment, and the lender’s interest in the property. Explain that the payment process may require coordination.

**L.4.16** **Q:** Can I tell a customer that the claim check will be made out only to them?

**A:** Do not make that promise before reviewing the policy and mortgage information. Other parties may have an interest in certain payments.

**L.4.17** **Q:** Is personal property covered anywhere the customer takes it?

**A:** Coverage away from home can be available, but it is not unlimited and may be subject to conditions. Ask where the property was located and what happened to it.

**L.4.18** **Q:** Does a home inventory guarantee payment for every listed item?

**A:** No. An inventory can help identify lost property and support a claim, but it does not replace policy review. The customer may still need to provide ownership and value information.

**L.4.19** **Q:** Can I accept a list of damaged belongings without asking for more detail?

**A:** You should request clear descriptions, supporting records when available, and information about the condition of the items. Vague descriptions can delay a fair evaluation.

**L.4.20** **Q:** Does a receipt always establish the current value of an item?

**A:** No. A receipt may show a purchase, but the valuation process can depend on the policy and the item’s condition. Do not confuse original cost with the amount payable.

**L.4.21** **Q:** Are valuables automatically covered under the same terms as ordinary household property?

**A:** Not necessarily. Some categories of property can have special limitations or separate coverage options. You should ask whether the customer owns items that may need special attention.

**L.4.22** **Q:** Can I tell a customer that jewelry is fully covered without reviewing the loss details?

**A:** No. You should identify the type of loss, the item involved, and any special policy treatment. A broad promise can create an avoidable misunderstanding.

**L.4.23** **Q:** Is a home business covered just because it operates from the residence?

**A:** Not always. Business property, business income, and liability from business activity can be treated differently from personal household exposures. Ask what work is being performed and how the property is used.

**L.4.24** **Q:** Does occasional work from home mean there is no insurance concern?

**A:** Not necessarily. The nature of the activity matters more than the customer’s informal description of it. You should ask whether clients visit, inventory is stored, or business equipment is used.

**L.4.25** **Q:** Is an injury on the property automatically covered by liability insurance?

**A:** No. Liability coverage depends on the facts, policy terms, and possible exclusions. Do not admit responsibility or promise payment before the claim is investigated.

**L.4.26** **Q:** Should I tell a customer to apologize to an injured visitor?

**A:** You can encourage concern for the visitor’s wellbeing, but avoid directing the customer to make statements about fault. The customer should report the incident and preserve relevant information.

**L.4.27** **Q:** Does a dog bite claim always have the same coverage result?

**A:** No. The animal, the circumstances, and policy provisions can all matter. Ask focused questions rather than assuming that every animal-related loss is handled alike.

**L.4.28** **Q:** Can I say that a trampoline is prohibited whenever a customer mentions one?

**A:** No. You should not make assumptions about eligibility or coverage without reviewing the applicable policy terms. Explain that certain property features may affect underwriting or liability exposure.

**L.4.29** **Q:** Does a vacant home have the same risk as an occupied home?

**A:** No. A home that is not regularly occupied can have different concerns, such as unnoticed leaks or delayed reporting. Encourage customers to tell the insurer when occupancy changes.

**L.4.30** **Q:** Is a home vacant just because the customer is traveling?

**A:** Not necessarily. You should ask whether the home remains furnished, maintained, and regularly attended. Avoid labeling the situation before you know the facts.

**L.4.31** **Q:** Can I assume renovation work is covered under the existing policy without discussion?

**A:** No. Renovation can change the property’s value, condition, and exposure to loss. The customer should report major work before relying on existing coverage.

**L.4.32** **Q:** Does hiring a contractor transfer all responsibility away from the homeowner?

**A:** No. Contractor involvement does not eliminate the homeowner’s need to protect the property and report changes. The customer should also verify that the contractor is appropriate for the work.

**L.4.33** **Q:** Is damage caused by construction always the contractor’s responsibility?

**A:** Not automatically. Responsibility can depend on contracts, facts, insurance arrangements, and the cause of damage. Do not direct the customer to a single party before the loss is reviewed.

**L.4.34** **Q:** Can I tell a customer that mold is always excluded?

**A:** No. Microbial issues require careful review of the cause of loss and the policy language. You should avoid broad statements about the microbial remediation limit before confirming how it applies.

**L.4.35** **Q:** Is mold damage always covered when it follows water damage?

**A:** No. The underlying water event, the timing of discovery, and the policy language can affect the outcome. Explain that the source and progression of damage matter.

**L.4.36** **Q:** Does a building code requirement automatically become part of the repair payment?

**A:** No. Code-related costs can require separate analysis. You should review ordinance or law coverage as a percentage of Coverage A without promising that every upgrade is covered.

**L.4.37** **Q:** Can a customer upgrade damaged property and expect the policy to pay for the upgrade?

**A:** No. A customer may choose improved materials or features, but that choice does not automatically make the added cost payable. Separate necessary repair from elective improvement.

**L.4.38** **Q:** Is an assessment from an association always covered?

**A:** No. You should identify why the assessment was issued and what property or loss it concerns. Do not promise payment based only on the fact that an assessment exists.

**L.4.39** **Q:** Should I describe association assessments as unlimited coverage?

**A:** No. Explain that policy treatment can be limited and fact-specific. The assessment coverage limit should be reviewed before giving the customer an expectation.

**L.4.40** **Q:** Does a power outage automatically create a covered food spoilage claim?

**A:** Not always. You should determine what caused the outage and whether the policy addresses the resulting loss. A utility interruption may be handled differently from damage originating at the residence.

**L.4.41** **Q:** Can I tell a customer to make permanent repairs before the damage is documented?

**A:** Encourage reasonable emergency protection, but do not encourage unnecessary destruction of evidence. Photographs, receipts, and damaged materials can help support the claim review.

**L.4.42** **Q:** Does reporting a claim mean the insurer has accepted coverage?

**A:** No. Reporting starts the claim process, but it does not decide the outcome. Be clear that investigation and policy review are still required.

**L.4.43** **Q:** Can I tell a customer that a claim will not affect future underwriting?

**A:** No. Claim history can be relevant to underwriting decisions, but you should not predict a specific result. Keep the discussion focused on accurate reporting and current coverage.

**L.4.44** **Q:** Does a policy renewal guarantee that the terms will remain unchanged?

**A:** No. Policy terms and underwriting decisions can change. The customer should read communications carefully, including any nonrenewal notice period.

**L.4.45** **Q:** Can I ignore a customer’s address change until renewal?

**A:** No. A move, seasonal use, rental arrangement, or occupancy change can affect the risk immediately. Encourage prompt updates whenever the property situation changes.

**L.4.46** **Q:** Is renting out part of the home the same as having a guest stay over?

**A:** No. Rental activity can create different property and liability concerns. Ask whether money changes hands and whether the arrangement is ongoing.

**L.4.47** **Q:** Can a customer rely on verbal advice instead of reading claim communications?

**A:** No. Important claim requests and decisions should be communicated clearly and documented. Encourage the customer to read notices and ask questions promptly.

**L.4.48** **Q:** Does sending photos eliminate the need for further claim information?

**A:** No. Photos can be helpful, but they may not show the cause, extent, or history of the damage. Additional records or inspection may still be needed.

**L.4.49** **Q:** Can I tell a customer to wait indefinitely before responding to a claim request?

**A:** No. Delays can interfere with investigation and payment. Make sure the customer understands the importance of the sworn proof deadline.

**L.4.50** **Q:** Is a sworn statement the same as an informal description of the loss?

**A:** No. A sworn submission can have specific requirements and consequences. You should avoid telling the customer that an informal message is an automatic substitute.

**L.4.51** **Q:** Can a customer wait until a dispute becomes urgent before seeking help?

**A:** That can create problems. You should remind the customer that years within which an action against the insurer must be brought may affect available options.

**L.4.52** **Q:** Does a disagreement with an estimate mean the customer should stop communicating?

**A:** No. The customer should explain the disagreement, provide support, and continue responding to reasonable requests. Productive communication often helps clarify the remaining issues.

**L.4.53** **Q:** Can I use the word denial before the coverage decision is complete?

**A:** Avoid premature labels. You should distinguish between a request for information, a pending investigation, a partial payment, and a final coverage decision.

**L.4.54** **Q:** Is a partial payment proof that the entire claim has been resolved?

**A:** No. A payment may address only part of the reported damage or expense. Explain what the payment addresses without suggesting that all issues are settled.

**L.4.55** **Q:** Can I tell a customer that every claim requires the same documents?

**A:** No. The needed information depends on the type of loss and the facts involved. Ask for records that help establish ownership, cause, damage, and expenses.

**L.4.56** **Q:** Does being helpful mean promising a coverage outcome?

**A:** No. You can be clear, respectful, and responsive without making commitments before review. Accurate expectations are more helpful than quick assumptions.

**L.4.57** **Q:** What is the most common mistake to avoid when discussing a homeowners claim?

**A:** Do not assume that a familiar loss has a familiar answer. Listen carefully, gather the facts, and explain that coverage depends on the policy and the specific circumstances.

## L.6 — Knowledge Check

**L.6.1** **Q:** What is the purpose of a knowledge check? How should you use it during training?

**L.6.2** **A:** Use it to confirm that you can identify the issue before giving an answer. It also helps you spot when you need more claim or underwriting facts.

**L.6.3** **Q:** What should you ask before discussing possible coverage? What details are most useful?

**L.6.4** **A:** Ask what happened, what was damaged, and what the customer is seeking. Clear facts help you separate a covered cause of loss from an excluded or limited situation.

**L.6.5** **Q:** Should you promise payment when a customer reports damage? Why not?

**L.6.6** **A:** Do not promise payment based only on an initial report. Explain that coverage depends on the reported facts and the applicable policy terms.

**L.6.7** **Q:** How should you respond when a customer asks whether sudden damage is covered? What should you avoid assuming?

**L.6.8** **A:** Start by identifying the event that caused the damage. Do not assume that sudden damage is covered without considering the source and circumstances.

**L.6.9** **Q:** What is the difference between damage and the cause of damage? Why does that distinction matter?

**L.6.10** **A:** Damage describes what was harmed, while the cause explains why it happened. Coverage decisions often depend more on the cause than on the visible damage.

**L.6.11** **Q:** How should you handle a report involving water damage? What facts should you gather?

**L.6.12** **A:** Ask where the water came from and how it entered the property. Also ask whether the condition developed gradually or resulted from a sudden event.

**L.6.13** **Q:** Can wear, deterioration, or neglect create a coverage concern? How should you explain that concern?

**L.6.14** **A:** These conditions can affect how a loss is evaluated. Explain that maintenance-related concerns may be treated differently from accidental direct physical loss.

**L.6.15** **Q:** What should you do when a customer reports roof damage? What facts may affect the review?

**L.6.16** **A:** Ask what caused the damage and whether there were prior signs of wear or leakage. You should also understand the roof age declination point when reviewing underwriting concerns.

**L.6.17** **Q:** How should you explain a claim involving wind-driven rain? What is the key coverage question?

**L.6.18** **A:** Focus on how the water entered the home. The key question is whether a covered event created the opening or condition that allowed the water inside.

**L.6.19** **Q:** What should you do if the reported loss occurred during a named storm? What should you avoid saying?

**L.6.20** **A:** Confirm the reported timing and location of the loss. Avoid making assumptions about how the named storm label affects coverage without reviewing the relevant facts.

**L.6.21** **Q:** How would you explain hours after a named storm designation ends that the named storm period lasts? Why is careful wording important?

**L.6.22** **A:** Describe it as a policy-defined timing concept connected to the storm designation. Be careful not to substitute your own interpretation for the applicable claim handling guidance.

**L.6.23** **Q:** What is a deductible? How should you describe it to a customer?

**L.6.24** **A:** A deductible is the part of a covered loss that remains the customer’s responsibility. Explain it plainly without suggesting that every reported loss will result in payment.

**L.6.25** **Q:** Should you calculate a payment before the damage is evaluated? Why not?

**L.6.26** **A:** No, because the loss facts and covered damage must be established first. Early estimates can create confusion if the final evaluation differs.

**L.6.27** **Q:** What should you do when a customer asks about damage to personal property? What information matters?

**L.6.28** **A:** Ask what property was damaged, where it was kept, and how it was affected. The location and cause of damage can be important to the coverage review.

**L.6.29** **Q:** How should you respond when a customer cannot provide a complete inventory? What is a helpful next step?

**L.6.30** **A:** Encourage the customer to describe the damaged property as clearly as possible. Photographs, receipts, and other available records may help support the review.

**L.6.31** **Q:** What should you say when a customer asks whether temporary repairs are appropriate? What should be documented?

**L.6.32** **A:** Explain that reasonable steps to prevent further damage may be appropriate. The customer should retain information showing what was done and why it was needed.

**L.6.33** **Q:** How should you discuss emergency services after a loss? What should you make clear?

**L.6.34** **A:** Explain that emergency work may be reviewed as part of the claim. Make clear that the service provider’s invoice is not itself a coverage decision.

**L.6.35** **Q:** What is a fire response charge? How should you address a customer question about it?

**L.6.36** **A:** Treat a fire response charge as a separate reported expense that may need review. Ask for available billing details and avoid guaranteeing reimbursement.

**L.6.37** **Q:** What should you do when a customer reports theft? What details should you seek?

**L.6.38** **A:** Ask what property is missing and how the loss was discovered. You should also clarify whether there are records, photographs, or other supporting information.

**L.6.39** **Q:** How should you handle a report of vandalism? What distinction may matter?

**L.6.40** **A:** Identify the damaged property and the apparent cause of the damage. Separate intentional damage by another party from damage caused by a maintenance issue.

**L.6.41** **Q:** What should you do if a customer reports damage from an animal? What should you avoid assuming?

**L.6.42** **A:** Ask what type of animal caused the damage and what was affected. Do not assume that all animal-related damage is handled the same way.

**L.6.43** **Q:** How should you explain a liability claim? What information is important?

**L.6.44** **A:** Explain that liability claims involve an allegation that the customer may be legally responsible for injury or damage. Gather the reported facts without admitting fault or promising a result.

**L.6.45** **Q:** What should you do when an injured person contacts you directly? How should you communicate?

**L.6.46** **A:** Be courteous and collect the information needed to identify the claim. Do not discuss liability conclusions or suggest that payment will be made.

**L.6.47** **Q:** What does prompt payment period mean? How should you describe it?

**L.6.48** **A:** Describe the prompt payment period as a claim handling concept that addresses timely action. Avoid stating a deadline unless the applicable guidance provides it.

**L.6.49** **Q:** How should you respond when a customer is frustrated by a claim delay? What is a useful approach?

**L.6.50** **A:** Acknowledge the concern and explain what information or review step is still needed. Give a clear update without promising an outcome that has not been determined.

**L.6.51** **Q:** What is the safest way to answer a coverage question when facts are incomplete? What should your answer include?
